Shelter and Provision

A campaign over waiting lists that the 2024 safeguard power turned into a party

Shelter and ProvisionS&P

18 seatsof 10017.9% of the voteLeft

Housing, provisioning, tenant organisation, direct democracy

Formed in the spring of 2024 as a campaign over housing waiting lists, and grown by the shortages of the following winter. It was classified under the 2024 safeguard power in September of that year while polling at nineteen per cent, which discredited the power and made the campaign a party. It signed the joint standing decision of 3 February 2026.

Ideology

The party holds that a right stated without a quantity is a right that cannot be claimed, and that the whole of social provision should therefore be expressed in figures that a person can hold an authority to. It is a single-subject party by origin.

Its direct democracy is procedural and follows from its origin in tenant organisation. It holds that decisions about a dwelling, a street or a block belong to the people who live there, and that an administrative decision taken elsewhere should be capable of being replaced by a decision of the assembly of residents.

Position on the Constitution

The party supported the draft. Its reservation is that Article 65 states a right whose content is left to organic law, and that until the quantities are fixed the right is enforceable in principle and unmeasurable in practice.

It proposes no amendment to the Constitution and has proposed none since its foundation.

Programme

The housing minimum core, in quantities

Article 65(1) gives every person a right to housing that is adequate, secure, habitable, accessible and affordable, of a size and quality fixed as the minimum core, and situated within reach of work, care, education and transport. Article 41(5) leaves those quantities to organic law, and Article 333(1) makes the level furnished in the year before commencement operate as the core until they are fixed.

The party wants the quantities adopted before the first plan takes effect: floor area per person, habitability standards, and a maximum travel time to each of the four things Article 65(1) names.

"The article says within reach of work, care, education and transport. Within reach is not a measurable outcome. Give us minutes and we will hold the institutions to them."

The two open periods

Article 65(4) makes homelessness a violation of the minimum core, remediable within a period that organic law fixes. Article 65(5) makes occupation of a long-vacant dwelling a defence to any proceeding for possession, over a period that organic law fixes. Neither period has been fixed.

The party proposes thirty days for the first and ninety days for the second, and holds that a long period in either place would leave the article without effect.

Allocation and the waiting lists

Where provision falls short of the need registered, Article 113 requires published rules applying urgency, duration of wait and the drawing of lots, anonymised public lists, and each applicant told their own position. The party wants that machinery operating before the next shortage.

It also wants the Audit Office to examine, on its own initiative under Article 250(1), whether Article 113(3) is being observed, that paragraph voiding any preference given by office, function, contribution or acquaintance.

Decisions taken where people live

The party wants communal assemblies of residents used wherever Article 169 permits them, neighbourhood assemblies convened on the schedule Article 172 allows, and the reallocation protections of Article 185 applied by a decision of the assembly of residents in each case.

Relations outside the Republic

The party seeks no affiliation with a European party family and takes no position on the party politics of the Union, holding that these are matters for states within it. It supports Ukraine and takes no position favourable to Russia.

Public opinion

The figures below come from the quarterly survey published by communal press at Lindoma. They have no official standing, and the Electoral Commission publishes no survey of its own.

Forty-four per cent of respondents describe the party favourably and 31 per cent unfavourably. Its support is strongest in Lindoma, Merrisoma and Thurioma, among respondents under thirty-five, and among households that have applied for a reallocation within the last three years.

The same survey records the mean wait for a reallocation falling from fourteen months in 2025 to seven in 2026. The projection reduces the party's share for that reason, and keeps the greater part of it because 58 per cent of respondents say the housing quantities should have been fixed by now.

Officers and candidates

The names below are published under paragraph 3 of Resolution 61 of 2026, which sends an individual name to the annexes where it is necessary to establish a fact. Who stands for election is such a fact, and a voter cannot exercise the preference Article 120(5) gives without it. Naming candidates and party officers is therefore consistent with the depersonalisation of the official record, which reaches decisions of state and the persons who took them. The annex explains the rule and this exception.
