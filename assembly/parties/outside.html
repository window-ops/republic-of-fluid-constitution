Parties outside parliament

Three lists that returned no member in 2025, and one complaint between them

Current Chamber Projection How it works Parties
These lists stood in September 2025 and returned no member. Under Article 120(4) no legal threshold applies, and in a chamber of two hundred a share near a quarter of one per cent is enough for a seat. None of them reached it. Their names and figures are published here because a register that lists only the lists which won seats does not show the full range of candidacies put before the voters.

What they have in common

All three argue that the established left has entrenched itself. Each of the three supported the Constitution at the referendum, and none of them objects to a particular article.

The objection concerns three decisions taken alongside the text: four parties holding seventy-nine of a hundred seats registered a joint standing decision on 3 February 2026, obtained a resolution of the chamber instructing the Office of Democratic Integrity to report against it, and then adopted Resolution 61 on the naming of persons in the record. Every step was lawful and every step was taken by the same four parties.

The answer the four give is that the decisions bind only the signatories, create no power, and are drafted against themselves. The answer the three give is that a commitment which creates no power also creates no remedy, and that the safeguard power of 2024 showed what an undertaking without a penalty produces.

What the projection assumes about them

The projection for the first Assembly puts the lists winning no seat at 0.4 per cent between them, below the quotient at which the two hundredth seat is won. This is an uncertain assumption in the projection and none of the three has contested an election in a chamber of two hundred, where a seat is won on about half the share a seat required in 2025. If one of them reaches a quarter of one per cent, it takes a seat from the ninth party. See the projection.
