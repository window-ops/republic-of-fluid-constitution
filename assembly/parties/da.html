Docklands AllianceDA

3 seatsof 1003.2% of the voteCentre left to left

Regionalism, internationalism, maritime economy

Speaks for Lindoma and Lutoliluri. It is the loudest voice in the chamber for the open-frontier objective of Article 296, and the corridor from Lutoliluri to Sluland handles the greater part of the Republic's remaining external exchange.

Ideology

The party holds that the Republic is a maritime state whose contact with the world runs through two ports, and that its politics should follow from that fact. Its internationalism is practical: it treats the movement of people and goods through the corridor as the ordinary condition of the coast and the closure of the southern land border as the exception.

Its regionalism rests on the same ground as that of the Highlands Federation, from the opposite geography. A port bears costs on behalf of the whole territory and should be compensated for them as of right.

Position on the Constitution

The party supported the draft and proposes no amendment.

Like the Highlands Federation it has widened its programme for the first Assembly, on the same reasoning: two sub-regions set a ceiling, and the questions of allocation between territories are national.

Programme

The open frontier acted upon

Article 296 states the objective, and Article 296(3) requires the Republic to seek an opening agreement with any state upon reciprocity, concluding it where it imposes no obligation contrary to Titles II, III or IV.

The party wants a standing programme of such approaches, with the date of each approach and the answer received published as they occur.

"The objective has been in the text since July and in the annual report since October. Neither document records a single approach to a single state. We would like a list with dates on it."

Docklands Alliance, Programme for the first Assembly, section 1

Equalisation for coastal burdens

The party supports the early publication of the allocation formula and wants it to recognise the burdens a port bears on behalf of the whole territory: dredging, corridor maintenance, rescue capacity, and the housing pressure that follows the traffic. Article 181 makes compensation for burdens borne on behalf of others owed as of right.

Communal competence at the waterfront

The party holds the same position as the Highlands Federation on Articles 167 and 171. It adds that spatial planning under Article 184 determines the development of a port town, and that the commune adopts that plan after a public inquiry.

Rescue without exception

Article 297 obliges the Republic to assist anyone in distress at sea and protects rescuers from prosecution. The party wants the station capacity stated in the plan and the obligation excluded from the subject matter of any agreement on movement.

Article 297Article 289

The corridor and the railway

The line from Lutoliluri inland is the route in the worst condition in the Republic, and the party has made its renewal a condition of its support for the first plan. Article 69 places mobility within the guarantee of essential provision.

Article 69Article 107

Relations outside the Republic

The party maintains informal contact with port-city and maritime networks abroad and seeks no formal affiliation with a Union party family, on the same ground as the Highlands Federation. It supports Ukraine, opposes the parties of the far right in Europe, and takes no position favourable to Russia.

Public opinion

The figures below come from the quarterly survey published by communal press at Lindoma. They have no official standing, and the Electoral Commission publishes no survey of its own.

Within Lindoma and Lutoliluri, 47 per cent of respondents describe the party favourably. Across the Republic, 8 per cent say they would consider voting for it.

The projection raises its share by three tenths of a point and more than doubles its seats, the second effect coming from the size of the chamber. The railway question is recorded as its strongest issue outside the two sub-regions.

Officers and candidates

The names below are published under paragraph 3 of Resolution 61 of 2026, which sends an individual name to the annexes where it is necessary to establish a fact. Who stands for election is such a fact, and a voter cannot exercise the preference Article 120(5) gives without it. Naming candidates and party officers is therefore consistent with the depersonalisation of the official record, which reaches decisions of state and the persons who took them. The annex explains the rule and this exception.
