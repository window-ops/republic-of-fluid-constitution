# CONSTITUTION OF THE FREE REPUBLIC OF REFORMED LUCES APORIA

**Adopted 11 September 1994. Consolidated text, with the Amendments appended.**

---

## PREAMBLE

The people of Reformed Luces Aporia, having brought to an end the rule of a single party over the offices of the State, having endured the Stoppage of 1990 to 1993 and the exhaustion of the Renewal before it, and resolved that the wealth of the country shall be held in common and governed by consent, adopt this Constitution.

We preserve the social ownership won in 1946 and abandon the direction of it by decree. The land, the water and the air of this country belong to those who come after us as much as to us. We remain among the peoples who made the union at Lindoma, and we seek peace with every state that will have peace with us.

---

# TITLE I: FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

**Article 1: The Republic**
1. Reformed Luces Aporia is a free, sovereign, unitary and indivisible republic.
2. Its official name shall be the Free Republic of Reformed Luces Aporia. The short name Reformed Luces Aporia shall have equal force in law.
3. The Republic is a democratic socialist republic, founded on political pluralism, on the social ownership of the principal means of production, and on the rule of law.
4. The republican form of the State shall not be revised.

**Article 2: Sovereignty**
1. Sovereignty belongs to the people and is exercised in the forms established by this Constitution.
2. The people exercise sovereignty through the Assembly of the Republic, through referendum and popular initiative, through the organs of local self-government, and through the bodies drawn by lot in the cases this Constitution provides.
3. No party, association, organ or person shall claim sovereignty or exercise public power outside the procedures of this Constitution. The assumption of power outside those procedures is usurpation, no obedience shall be owed to its author, and prosecution for it is subject to no time limit.

**Article 3: Supremacy of the Constitution**
1. This Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic and binds every organ of the State, every unit of local self-government and every holder of public function.
2. A law, decree, regulation or administrative act which conflicts with this Constitution has no effect, and only the conflicting part of it fails.
3. The rights declared in Title II may be invoked before any court without implementing legislation.

**Article 4: Legality and publicity of the law**
1. Public power shall be exercised only on a legal basis and within its limits.
2. A law, decree or regulation shall enter into force only on publication in the Official Gazette, and no sooner than the fifteenth day after publication except where the law itself provides a shorter period for reasons stated in it.
3. Secret laws, secret decrees and secret regulations are prohibited. An act not published shall bind nobody.
4. The retroactive operation of a law is prohibited, except where it is favourable to the person concerned.

**Article 5: Political pluralism**
1. Parties, trade unions, cooperative federations and associations shall be formed freely, shall govern themselves democratically, and shall publish their statutes and their accounts.
2. No party shall be accorded a leading role in the State, in the economy, in the armed forces, in education or in any enterprise, and no such role shall be established by law.
3. Membership of a party shall not be a condition of appointment to public office, of admission to education, or of employment.
4. A party may be dissolved only by judgment of the Constitutional Court, on proof that it pursues by force the abolition of democratic government or of the equal dignity of persons, and the judgment shall state the acts proved.

**Article 6: Prohibition of political policing**
1. No organ of the State shall collect, hold or process information on a person by reason of that person's opinions, party membership, trade union activity, religion or lawful association.
2. Bodies exercising the functions of the former State Protection Service are abolished and shall not be re-established under any name.
3. The archives of that Service pass to the National Archive. Every person shall have access to the file kept on them and to the identity of those who informed on them, subject only to the protection of persons still living from danger to life.
4. Surveillance of communications and of the person shall be conducted only by judicial warrant, for an offence defined by law, and for a stated period.

**Article 7: Human dignity**
1. Human dignity is inviolable and is the foundation of the rights declared in this Constitution.
2. No emergency, no economic plan, no majority and no international obligation shall authorise its violation.

**Article 8: Territory**
1. The territory of the Republic comprises its land, inland waters, territorial sea, seabed, subsoil and airspace.
2. The territory is inalienable. No part of it shall be ceded, leased to the exclusive use of a foreign power, or made the site of a foreign military installation.
3. The capital of the Republic is Sluland. The seat of an organ of the State may be fixed elsewhere by law.
4. The Republic maintains the maritime corridor to the Republic of Fluid as a permanent way of exchange, and shall keep it open in peace and in war.

**Article 9: Language, symbols and the calendar**
1. The official language of the Republic is Aporean. The law shall provide for the use of the languages of national minorities before the organs of the State, in education and in the courts of the districts in which they are spoken.
2. The flag, the arms and the anthem of the Republic shall be established by organic law.
3. The eleventh of September, the day of the adoption of this Constitution, is the national day. The first of May and the eighteenth of March, the day of the liberation of 1946, are days of the Republic.

**Article 10: Citizenship**
1. Citizenship shall be acquired and lost according to organic law.
2. No citizen shall be deprived of citizenship, expelled from the territory, or surrendered to a foreign state for an offence of a political character.
3. A citizen of a member state of the union to which Article 18 refers shall enter, reside, work and receive care in the Republic on the footing of a citizen of the Republic.

**Article 11: Economic order**
1. The economy of the Republic rests on the social ownership of the principal means of production, on the enterprise of the working collective, and on planning by agreement between enterprises, districts and the organs of the State.
2. Social ownership takes three forms: property of the whole people, administered by public establishments, meaning bodies created by law to provide a service to the population; the enterprise of the working collective, which its members administer for as long as they work in it; and the property of the districts and communes.
3. Private enterprise is lawful in the trades, in agriculture, in repair, in catering, in transport of goods, in cultural production and in such other activities listed by law, subject to a ceiling of employed persons fixed by organic law and to the same taxes, standards and conditions of labour as apply to enterprises in social ownership.
4. Land, watercourses, aquifers, coastline, seabed, forests, mineral deposits and the radio spectrum are the property of the whole people. They shall not be alienated, and their use shall be granted only as a revocable and conditional concession.
5. The employment of persons for the appropriation of their surplus labour beyond the ceiling established under paragraph 3 is prohibited, and no market shall be established in the means of production.

**Article 12: Currency, prices and the direction of policy**
1. The monetary unit of the Republic is the marek. It shall be issued by the Bank of the Republic alone.
2. The Bank of the Republic shall be accountable to the Assembly of the Republic, which shall appoint and may remove its Council. The Bank shall not lend to a foreign state, and shall not accept an instruction from a foreign body.
3. Prices in the socially owned sector shall be established by agreement between producing and using enterprises within the framework of the plan, and the law shall list the goods and services whose prices the Government fixes.
4. Housing, health care, education at every level, water, the care of children and of the aged, and public transport shall be provided free of charge at the point of use, and shall be met from the plan.
5. It is a standing objective of the Republic to reduce the range of goods and services allocated by money and to extend the range provided free of charge, at the pace that the productive capacity of the country allows. The Assembly of the Republic shall report on the progress of this objective every four years.

**Article 13: Labour**
1. Labour is the source of the wealth of the Republic and the ground of the claim of each person on its product.
2. Forced labour is prohibited. The direction of a person to a place of work against their will is prohibited.
3. Every working collective shall govern the enterprise in which it works, elect and remove its management, and decide on the disposal of the enterprise's revenues within the limits of the law and of the plan.
4. Income shall correspond to labour performed. Income derived from the bare holding of a productive asset is prohibited.

**Article 14: The natural environment**
1. The Republic shall protect the land, the water, the air, the coast and the living species of the territory, and shall repair the damage done to them.
2. The State, the districts and the enterprises shall conduct their activity with regard to the capacity of the natural environment to sustain it.
3. Every person has the duty to preserve the natural environment and the right to information on its condition.

**Article 15: Secularity**
1. The Republic adopts no religion. Religious communities are separate from the State, are equal before it, and shall govern themselves.
2. No public office shall carry a religious qualification, and no religious qualification shall be required for the exercise of a right.
3. Public education shall transmit verified knowledge and the methods by which it is tested.

**Article 16: Local self-government**
1. The territory is divided into communes and districts, which govern themselves through elected councils.
2. Every matter of local concern falls to the commune, except where this Constitution or an organic law confers it on the district or on the State.
3. The resources of the communes and districts shall correspond to the functions assigned to them.

**Article 17: Organs of the Republic and their separation**
1. Legislative power belongs to the Assembly of the Republic, executive power to the Government, and judicial power to the courts.
2. The President of the Republic represents the Republic and exercises the functions this Constitution confers.
3. The Constitutional Court, the Court of Accounts, the Electoral Commission and the Commission on Information and Data exercise their functions independently of the Government.
4. An organ shall not assume a function conferred on another, and a function shall not be delegated except on the authority of this Constitution.

**Article 18: International relations**
1. The Republic is a founding member of the Democratic Socialist Bloc, established at Lindoma in 1994, and shall remain a member so long as that union rests on social ownership, ecological limits, democratic self-government and the equality of its members.
2. A treaty shall bind the Republic only on ratification by the Assembly of the Republic and publication, and shall then have the force of law and prevail over a contrary provision of a law.
3. The Republic shall not conclude a treaty that submits it to a foreign or arbitral jurisdiction in a dispute with a company, that permits a foreign military installation, or that restricts the amendment of this Constitution.
4. The Republic renounces war as an instrument of policy, recognises no acquisition of territory obtained by force, and grants asylum to persons persecuted for their opinions, their origin or their activity in defence of rights.

**Article 19: Armed forces and public order**
1. The armed forces are subordinate to the civil power, serve the defence of the territory, and shall not be employed in a political dispute or against a lawful assembly.
2. A member of the armed forces or of the police shall not belong to a party while in service.
3. The Republic shall neither possess nor accept on its territory a nuclear, chemical or biological weapon.
4. The armed forces shall act beyond the frontier only on a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic.

**Article 20: Access to public information**
1. Every person has the right of access to information held by an organ of the State, by a district, by a commune or by an enterprise in social ownership.
2. Refusal shall be reasoned, shall rest on a ground stated in an organic law, and shall be subject to appeal to a court.
3. The plan, the budget, the accounts of enterprises in social ownership, and the register of concessions granted under Article 11(4) shall be published.

# TITLE II: RIGHTS, LIBERTIES AND DUTIES

## Chapter 1: General provisions

**Article 21: Equality**
1. All persons are equal before the law and are entitled to its equal protection.
2. No person shall be favoured or prejudiced by reason of origin, nationality, language, sex, birth, age, disability, health, property, occupation, religion, opinion, party membership or family situation.
3. Measures adopted to remove an established inequality shall not be held contrary to this Article.
4. Privileges of birth, hereditary titles and hereditary offices are abolished.

**Article 22: Scope and limitation of rights**
1. The rights declared in this Title bind the Assembly of the Republic, the Government, the administration, the courts, the districts, the communes and the enterprises.
2. A right may be limited only by a law, only where the limitation is necessary for the protection of another right or of a public interest stated in this Constitution, and only to the extent required.
3. A limitation shall be general in its terms and shall not touch the essential content of the right.
4. Where a right is claimed to have been infringed, the organ concerned shall bear the burden of showing that the conditions of paragraph 2 are satisfied.

**Article 23: Foreigners, stateless persons and asylum**
1. Foreigners and stateless persons lawfully in the territory enjoy the rights declared in this Title, except those which this Constitution reserves to citizens.
2. Asylum shall be granted to a person persecuted for their opinions, their origin, their belonging to a group, or their activity in defence of rights.
3. Extradition shall be refused where the offence is of a political character, where the person would face the death penalty, or where they would face torture or an inhuman punishment.
4. A person shall not be expelled to a territory in which their life or liberty would be in danger.

**Article 24: National minorities**
1. Persons belonging to a national minority have the right to preserve their language, their culture and their religion, to be taught in their language, and to use it before the organs of the State in the districts in which they reside in significant numbers.
2. The law shall provide for their representation in the councils of those districts.
3. Assimilation by measure of the State is prohibited.

**Article 25: Remedy and reparation**
1. Every person whose right has been infringed has the right to a remedy before a court, decided within a reasonable time.
2. The State shall make reparation for damage caused by the unlawful act of a public organ or of its agents, and shall recover from the agent at fault.
3. A person unlawfully deprived of liberty or convicted in error has the right to compensation and to the public rectification of the record.

## Chapter 2: Life, integrity and liberty

**Article 26: Life**
1. The life of the person is inviolable.
2. The death penalty is abolished and shall not be re-established, including in time of war.

**Article 27: Integrity of the person**
1. The physical and mental integrity of the person is inviolable.
2. Torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment are prohibited absolutely.
3. A medical or scientific intervention on a person requires their free and informed consent, given in the forms laid down by law.
4. A statement obtained by torture, by threat or by deception shall be inadmissible in any proceeding.

**Article 28: Liberty and security of the person**
1. No person shall be deprived of liberty except in the cases and by the procedure established by law.
2. A person arrested shall be informed at once of the reasons and of their rights, and shall be brought before a judge within forty-eight hours, failing which they shall be released.
3. Detention before judgment shall be exceptional, shall rest on a reasoned judicial decision, and shall be subject to a maximum fixed by law.
4. Every person deprived of liberty has the right to inform a person of their choice, to counsel from the first hour, and to a medical examination.
5. Detention for debt is prohibited. Detention on the ground of illness, poverty, homelessness or want of employment is prohibited.

**Article 29: Fair trial and defence**
1. Every person has the right to a hearing in public before an independent and impartial court established by law.
2. The accused shall be presumed innocent until convicted by a final judgment.
3. The accused has the right to be informed of the charge, to time and facilities for defence, to counsel provided free of charge where they lack means, to interpretation, to examine witnesses, and to appeal.
4. No person shall be tried twice for the same act, nor compelled to testify against themselves or against a member of their family.
5. Extraordinary courts are prohibited. A civilian shall not be tried by a military court.

**Article 30: Legality of offences and penalties**
1. An act shall be punished only where it constituted an offence defined by law at the time of its commission, and only with the penalty then prescribed.
2. A penalty shall be proportionate to the offence. Penalties shall serve the protection of society and the restoration of the person, and no penalty shall be perpetual and without review.
3. A penalty shall not extend to the family of the convicted person, and shall not entail the general forfeiture of goods.
4. Deprivation of liberty shall not entail the loss of the right to vote.

**Article 31: Inviolability of the home**
1. The home is inviolable. Entry, search or seizure requires a reasoned judicial warrant, except in pursuit of a person taken in the act or in case of danger to life.
2. A search shall be conducted in the presence of the occupier or of witnesses, and a record shall be given to them.

**Article 32: Correspondence and personal data**
1. The secrecy of correspondence and of communication is guaranteed. Interception requires a reasoned judicial warrant, for a stated offence and a stated period.
2. Every person has the right to know what data on them is held by an organ of the State or by an enterprise, to obtain the correction of error, and to obtain the erasure of data collected without a legal basis.
3. Data collected for one purpose shall not be used for another, and the general numbering of persons across unconnected registers is prohibited.

**Article 33: Freedom of movement**
1. Every person lawfully in the territory has the right to move within it and to choose their residence.
2. Every citizen has the right to leave the territory and to return to it. A passport shall be refused only by reasoned decision subject to appeal to a court.
3. The exit visa and the internal permit of residence are abolished and shall not be re-established.

## Chapter 3: Conscience, expression and association

**Article 34: Conscience and religion**
1. Freedom of conscience, of religion and of belief is guaranteed, in private and in public, alone and in community.
2. No person shall be required to declare their religion or belief.
3. Conscientious objection to armed service is recognised, and the law shall provide a civil service of equal duration.

**Article 35: Expression and information**
1. Every person has the right to express and to publish their opinions, and to seek and receive information.
2. Censorship is prohibited. A publication shall not be seized or a broadcast suspended except by judicial decision.
3. Liability for expression shall arise only after publication and only in the cases the law defines. Insult to the State, to its symbols or to the holder of an office shall not be an offence.
4. Scientific research and artistic creation are free.

**Article 36: Media**
1. A monopoly of the State over the press, broadcasting or telecommunication is prohibited.
2. The ownership and the funding of a medium shall be public, and the law shall prevent the concentration of media in few hands.
3. The public broadcasting service shall be independent of the Government, shall be governed by a council appointed by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds, and shall give access to the parties and associations on equal terms.
4. Journalists have the right to protect their sources and shall not be compelled to disclose them.

**Article 37: Assembly**
1. Every person has the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, in public or in private, without prior authorisation.
2. Notice may be required for an assembly on a public way, and a failure of notice shall not of itself render the assembly unlawful.
3. An assembly shall be dispersed only where it has ceased to be peaceful, by decision of the responsible authority, and by the least force sufficient.

**Article 38: Association**
1. Every person has the right to form associations and to join them or abstain from joining them.
2. An association becomes a person in law by registration, so that it may own property, make contracts and appear in court in its own name. Registration which shall be refused only by reasoned decision subject to appeal to a court.
3. An association shall be dissolved only by judicial decision.
4. Secret associations and associations of a military character are prohibited.

**Article 39: Petition and access to the organs**
1. Every person has the right to address a petition to any organ of the State and to receive a reasoned reply within the period laid down by law.
2. Every person has the right to be heard before a decision of the administration affecting them, and to receive the reasons for it in writing.

## Chapter 4: Political rights

**Article 40: Suffrage**
1. Every citizen of eighteen years has the right to vote and to stand for election.
2. The vote is universal, equal, direct, secret and free. Voting is not compulsory.
3. No condition of turnout shall be attached to the validity of an election or of a referendum.
4. Residents who are not citizens shall vote and stand in elections to the councils of the commune after three years of lawful residence.

**Article 41: Access to public office**
1. Every citizen has the right of access to public office on conditions of equality, by competition or by election.
2. Appointment and promotion in the administration shall be made on merit established by public procedure.
3. The holder of a public office shall declare their interests and their assets, and the declaration shall be published.

**Article 42: Referendum and popular initiative**
1. A referendum shall be held on the demand of the Assembly of the Republic, of one third of its members, or of a number of citizens fixed by organic law.
2. A number of citizens fixed by organic law may lay a proposal of law before the Assembly of the Republic, which shall consider it and vote on it within six months.
3. A referendum shall not be held on a matter that would abolish a right declared in this Title.

**Article 43: Participation in local affairs**
1. Residents have the right to participate in the decisions of their commune, through the assembly of the commune, through consultation on plans of land use, and through the local referendum.
2. A decision to site an installation harmful to health or to the environment shall be taken after a public hearing in the commune concerned, whose opinion shall be published together with the decision.

## Chapter 5: Economic and social rights

**Article 44: Work**
1. Every person has the right to work, to choose their occupation, and to be assisted by the State in obtaining employment corresponding to their qualification.
2. Dismissal shall be permitted only on a ground defined by law and shall be subject to appeal to a court.
3. Dismissal on the ground of trade union activity, of the exercise of a right, or of pregnancy is void.
4. Equal work is paid equally, and the law shall fix a minimum income and a maximum ratio between the highest and the lowest income in any enterprise.

**Article 45: Conditions of labour**
1. The working week shall not exceed forty hours. The law shall reduce it as the productivity of labour allows.
2. Every worker has the right to a daily and a weekly rest, to paid annual leave of no fewer than twenty-four working days, and to public holidays.
3. Every worker has the right to conditions of work that protect their health, to information on the hazards of their work, and to withdraw from work presenting a serious and immediate danger without loss of income.
4. The employment of children below the age at which compulsory education ends is prohibited.

**Article 46: Strike**
1. The right to strike is guaranteed to every worker, including in enterprises in social ownership and in the public service.
2. The law shall provide for a minimum service in health, in water, in electricity and in emergency communication, and that minimum service shall not be set at a level which makes the strike ineffective.
3. The closure of a workplace by its management in answer to a strike, called a lock-out, is prohibited. A worker shall not be dismissed or penalised for participation in a lawful strike.

**Article 47: Trade unions**
1. Workers have the right to form trade unions freely, to join the union of their choice, and to be represented by it.
2. Trade unions shall be independent of the State, of the parties and of the management of enterprises.
3. Collective agreements concluded between unions and enterprises or their associations have the force of law between the parties, and the law shall provide for their extension to a branch.

**Article 48: Self-management in the enterprise**
1. The working collective of an enterprise in social ownership shall constitute a workers' council, elected by secret ballot from among its members.
2. The council shall adopt the statute of the enterprise, appoint and remove its director, decide on the plan of the enterprise, and dispose of its revenues within the limits of the law.
3. In an enterprise in private ownership employing more than the number of persons fixed by organic law, the workers shall elect a committee entitled to information on the accounts of the enterprise and to consultation before a change in the organisation of work.

**Article 49: Housing**
1. Every person has the right to a dwelling adequate to the size of their household, secure in tenure, and served with water, sanitation, heating and electricity.
2. Dwellings shall be allocated from the public stock according to need, on criteria published by the commune, in the order of a register open to inspection by the applicants.
3. A person shall not be evicted from their dwelling without a judicial decision and without the offer of alternative accommodation.
4. The purchase of dwellings for letting at a profit, and the holding of dwellings vacant, shall be limited by law, and the commune may take a vacant dwelling into the public stock on compensation.

**Article 50: Health**
1. Every person has the right to the protection of their health and to care provided free of charge at the point of use by the public health service.
2. The public health service shall be general, shall serve the whole territory, and shall be financed from the plan.
3. Preventive medicine, the medicine of work and the health of mothers and children are duties of the State.
4. A charge shall not be imposed for treatment, for a medicine on the list established by law, or for an examination.

**Article 51: Education**
1. Every person has the right to education. Education is free of charge at every level, including higher education, and in the establishments of the State alone.
2. Education is compulsory to the age fixed by law and shall not be shorter than nine years.
3. Higher education shall be open on the basis of ability established by public examination.
4. Universities govern themselves, and the freedom of teaching and of research is guaranteed.
5. Private establishments of education may be founded on the conditions laid down by law and shall receive no public funds.

**Article 52: Social security**
1. Every person has the right to social security in old age, in sickness, in disability, in unemployment, in the loss of a supporter, and in maternity and paternity.
2. Benefits shall be sufficient to maintain the recipient and their household in dignity, and shall be adjusted at least annually to the movement of prices.
3. The right acquired to a pension shall not be reduced.
4. Every person without means has the right to a subsistence income and to shelter.

**Article 53: Family and children**
1. The family is protected by the State. Marriage rests on the free consent and the equality of the spouses.
2. Children born within and outside marriage have equal rights, and any mention of the circumstances of birth in a public register is prohibited.
3. Parents have the right and the duty to maintain and educate their children, and shall be assisted in it by the State by leave from work, by allowance and by public establishments for the care of children.
4. Children have the right to protection against neglect, violence and exploitation, and shall be heard in decisions affecting them according to their age.
5. Young persons, aged persons and persons with disability have the right to the particular measures of protection and of participation that the law establishes.

**Article 54: Culture, science and creation**
1. Every person has the right to take part in cultural life and to enjoy the benefit of scientific progress.
2. The author of a work of the mind has the right to be named as its author and to object to its distortion. These rights cannot be sold or surrendered.
3. Knowledge produced with public funds shall be published and shall be open to use without charge.
4. The cultural and natural heritage of the territory shall be protected, and its removal from the territory is prohibited.

## Chapter 6: Environment and consumption

**Article 55: Environment**
1. Every person has the right to an environment that does not injure their health, and the duty to preserve it.
2. Every person has the right to information on the condition of the environment and on emissions affecting the place where they live or work, and the right to bring proceedings for the cessation of a harm and for its repair.
3. A project liable to affect the environment significantly shall be preceded by a public assessment, whose conclusions shall be published before the decision is taken.

**Article 56: Consumption and public services**
1. Users of goods and services have the right to safety, to truthful information on what is provided to them, to the repair of goods, and to redress.
2. Goods sold in the Republic shall be repairable, and the parts and the information necessary for repair shall be available for the useful life of the good.
3. Users of a public service have the right to be consulted on its organisation and to complain to an organ independent of the service.

## Chapter 7: Duties

**Article 57: Duties**
1. Every person shall observe the Constitution and the laws, contribute to public burdens according to their means, and respect the rights of others.
2. Every citizen shall serve in the defence of the territory on the conditions laid down by law, and Article 34(3) governs.
3. Every person shall preserve the natural environment, the public property and the cultural heritage.
4. A duty shall not be established except by law, and the invocation of a duty shall not justify the denial of a right.

# TITLE III: THE ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL ORDER

## Chapter 1: The plan

**Article 58: Objectives**
1. Economic activity in the Republic serves the satisfaction of the needs of the population, full employment, the reduction of inequality between persons and between districts, and the protection of the natural environment.
2. Economic policy shall be conducted so as to reduce the dependence of the Republic on supplies it cannot replace.
3. The State shall not conduct economic policy in a manner that transfers to households the cost of the failure of an enterprise or of a public establishment.

**Article 59: Nature of the plan**
1. The plan of the Republic is adopted for five years and revised annually, in each case by a law.
2. The plan states the objectives of production, of investment, of employment and of provision, the resources allocated to them, and the enterprises and establishments responsible.
3. The plan binds the organs of the State, the districts, the communes, the public establishments and the enterprises in social ownership. It binds a private enterprise only in relation to a contract it has concluded under the plan.

**Article 60: Preparation of the plan**
1. The plan is prepared by agreement between the enterprises, the communes, the districts and the Office of the Plan, in rounds of negotiation opened by the Government and concluded before the plan is laid before the Assembly of the Republic.
2. Enterprises state the quantities they can produce and the inputs they require. Communes and districts state the needs of their population. Trade unions and associations of users are heard.
3. Where agreement is not reached, the Office of the Plan proposes a settlement, and the Assembly of the Republic decides.
4. The drafts, the variants and the objections recorded in the negotiation shall be published with the plan.

**Article 61: Content and accounts of the plan**
1. The plan is expressed in monetary values and, for the goods and resources listed by law, in physical units.
2. The plan shall include balances for energy, for water, for the principal materials and for labour.
3. The plan shall state the effect of the planned activity on the natural environment, in the terms the law prescribes.

**Article 62: Effect of the plan on enterprises**
1. An enterprise which has agreed a task in the plan shall perform it, and shall be entitled to the inputs allocated to it for that purpose.
2. Where an allocation is not delivered, the enterprise is released from the corresponding task and may claim compensation from the party in default.
3. An enterprise may produce beyond its task in the plan and dispose of the surplus, on the conditions laid down by law.

**Article 63: Districts and communes in the plan**
1. Districts and communes adopt their own plans, which shall conform to the plan of the Republic in the matters it regulates.
2. The transfers to districts and communes shall be established by the plan on criteria fixed by law, published in advance, and equal in their application.

## Chapter 2: Enterprises

**Article 64: The enterprise in social ownership**
1. An enterprise in social ownership is a person in law. Its assets belong to the whole people, and the enterprise administers them on its own responsibility.
2. The assets of the enterprise shall not be sold, mortgaged or transferred except on the conditions laid down by law and with the approval of the organ that established it.
3. The enterprise is governed by the workers' council under Article 48.

**Article 65: Establishment and dissolution**
1. An enterprise in social ownership is established by the Assembly of the Republic, by a district or by a commune, and its founding act states its purpose and its assets.
2. An enterprise may be merged, divided or dissolved by the organ that established it, after consultation of its workers' council and of the trade union.
3. On dissolution, the assets return to the founding organ and the workers have the right to transfer to other employment and to income during the period of transfer.

**Article 66: Accounts and audit**
1. Every enterprise in social ownership shall keep accounts in the form the law prescribes and publish them annually.
2. The accounts are audited by the Court of Accounts or by an auditor it approves.
3. The workers' council, the trade union and any resident of the commune in which the enterprise operates may inspect the published accounts and put questions to the director, who shall reply in writing.

**Article 67: Losses and support**
1. An enterprise in social ownership bears its own losses out of its reserves.
2. Where an enterprise cannot meet its obligations, the founding organ may grant support out of the funds of the plan, for a stated period and against a programme of recovery, or may proceed to dissolution under Article 65.
3. The grant of support and the programme of recovery shall be published.

**Article 68: Public establishments**
1. Health, education, water, sanitation, the postal service, the railways and the supply of electricity are conducted by public establishments serving the whole territory.
2. These activities shall not be transferred to a private enterprise, nor conducted for profit, nor made the object of a concession to a foreign person.

**Article 69: Private enterprise**
1. A private enterprise may be established in the activities listed by law, and shall be registered.
2. The number of persons it employs shall not exceed the ceiling fixed by organic law, and the ceiling shall be the same for every activity.
3. The private enterprise is subject to the same standards of safety, of health, of conditions of labour and of environmental protection as the enterprise in social ownership.
4. A private enterprise shall not be established in banking, in insurance, in the extraction of minerals, in the generation of electricity, in the railways, in the press of national circulation, in health or in education.

**Article 70: Cooperatives and agriculture**
1. Agricultural land is worked by cooperatives of producers, by public farms and by family holdings.
2. Membership of a cooperative is voluntary, and a member may withdraw with the land and the equipment they brought into it, on the conditions laid down by law.
3. The area of a family holding shall not exceed the ceiling fixed by organic law.

**Article 71: Sole and principal suppliers**
1. An enterprise which is the sole or the principal supplier of a good or a service shall not use that position to impose conditions on a user, on a supplier or on a commune.
2. Agreements between enterprises to divide a market or to fix a price outside the plan are void.

## Chapter 3: Finance

**Article 72: The budget**
1. The budget of the Republic is adopted by a law for each budget year, which runs from 1 January to 31 December, before that year begins, and states all revenue and all expenditure.
2. Expenditure shall not be undertaken outside the budget. A fund outside the budget may be established only by a law which states its purpose, its resources and the date of its expiry.
3. The accounts of execution of the budget are laid before the Assembly of the Republic and published within six months of the end of that year.

**Article 73: Taxation**
1. A tax, a contribution or a charge shall be established, altered or abolished only by a law which states the taxable event, the base, the rate and the persons liable.
2. Taxation is progressive in relation to income and to wealth, and shall not reduce a person below the subsistence income under Article 52(4).
3. The privileges of exemption granted to a person or to an enterprise individually are prohibited.

**Article 74: Public borrowing**
1. The Republic may borrow only by authority of a law which states the amount, the purpose and the term.
2. Borrowing shall be applied to investment and shall not be applied to current expenditure.
3. The Republic shall not borrow from a foreign state, a foreign bank or an international financial institution except by a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic, and shall not accept a condition touching its internal order.

**Article 75: The Bank of the Republic**
1. The Bank of the Republic issues the marek, holds the accounts of the State, and conducts the credit of the Republic within the framework of the plan.
2. The Council of the Bank is appointed by the Assembly of the Republic for a term of six years and reports to it twice a year in public sitting.
3. The Bank shall not grant credit to the State to cover a deficit of the budget.

**Article 76: Credit to enterprises**
1. Enterprises obtain credit for investment from the Bank of the Republic, on application stating the purpose, the term and the expected return, and in the order of priority established by the plan.
2. The conditions of credit shall be published, and shall be the same for every applicant of the same class.
3. Credit unpaid at term shall be recovered, and the Bank shall report annually to the Assembly of the Republic on the amounts unrecovered, by enterprise.

**Article 77: Savings and household credit**
1. Households may hold savings in the Bank of the Republic and in the savings institutions established by law, and their savings are guaranteed by the Republic.
2. Credit to households is granted for the construction and repair of dwellings, for study and for the establishment of a private enterprise under Article 69.
3. Interest on savings shall not exceed the rate fixed by law, and income from interest is subject to tax.

**Article 78: Prices**
1. The law lists the goods and services whose prices are fixed by the Government, and the Government publishes the reasons for each price it fixes.
2. Other prices are agreed between the parties within the framework of the plan.
3. Where a good in general use is in short supply, the Government may establish rationing by an order limited to twelve months, renewable by a law, and shall publish the quantities available and the criteria of distribution.

## Chapter 4: Labour and incomes

**Article 79: Incomes**
1. Income in an enterprise in social ownership is determined by its workers' council, within the minimum and the ratio established under Article 44(4) and within the funds the enterprise has earned.
2. The Government, the trade unions and the associations of enterprises shall conclude an annual agreement on the movement of incomes, which is laid before the Assembly of the Republic.
3. Income in the public service and in the public establishments is fixed by law on a published scale.

**Article 80: Employment**
1. The State, the districts and the communes shall organise the employment service, publish the vacancies known to it, and provide training and retraining free of charge.
2. Where an enterprise reduces its workforce, it shall notify the employment service and the trade union in advance, and the workers concerned have priority for training and for vacancies in the district.
3. A person seeking employment receives the benefit under Article 52 for the period laid down by law, which shall not be conditioned on the acceptance of employment below their qualification or outside their district.

## Chapter 5: Land, resources and the environment

**Article 81: Concessions over natural resources**
1. The use of land, water, forests, minerals, the coast and the seabed is granted by concession, for a stated period, against stated conditions of restoration, and against payment to the Republic.
2. A concession is revoked where its conditions are broken, and the holder shall restore the site at their own cost.
3. The register of concessions, with their conditions and their terms, is public.

**Article 82: Spatial planning**
1. Land use is governed by the spatial plan of the commune, which conforms to the plan of the district and to the plan of the Republic.
2. A spatial plan is adopted after a public inquiry, and the objections received and the reasons for rejecting them are published with it.
3. Building in a floodplain, on an unstable slope or on land subject to coastal retreat is prohibited, and the law shall provide for the removal of existing building and for the compensation of the occupiers.

**Article 83: Expropriation**
1. Property may be taken for a public purpose stated by law, by a decision subject to appeal to a court, and against prior and full compensation.
2. Property shall not be taken for the benefit of a private enterprise.

**Article 84: Protection of the environment and of resources**
1. The Government shall publish annually the condition of the air, of the water, of the soil and of the forests, and the quantities of the principal materials extracted, imported, used and discarded.
2. Emissions and discharges are subject to limits fixed by law, and the enterprise causing a harm shall pay for its repair.
3. Waste shall be reduced, materials shall be returned to use, and the deposit of untreated waste is prohibited from the date the law fixes.

**Article 85: Energy and water**
1. The generation and distribution of electricity, the supply of water and the district heating networks are held in social ownership and shall not be alienated.
2. The Republic shall reduce its consumption of fossil fuels on the timetable the plan establishes.
3. A supply of water sufficient for personal and domestic use shall not be cut off for non-payment.

## Chapter 6: External economic relations

**Article 86: Foreign trade**
1. Trade with the member states of the union under Article 18 is conducted by agreement concluded within that union, and settled in the manner the agreement provides.
2. Trade with other states is conducted by enterprises under licence granted by the Government, which states the goods, the quantities and the term.
3. The register of licences is public.

**Article 87: Trade agreements**
1. The Republic may conclude trade agreements, which are ratified and published under Article 18(2).
2. A trade agreement shall not require the Republic to open to private or foreign enterprise an activity reserved by Article 68 or Article 69(4), to grant an exclusive right over knowledge, or to submit a dispute with a company to a foreign or arbitral jurisdiction.
3. Accession to an international economic organisation requires a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic, and withdrawal from it requires a law adopted by simple majority.

**Article 88: Foreign investment**
1. A foreign person may participate in an enterprise in the Republic only under licence, which states the term, the technique to be transferred, and the conditions of employment.
2. A foreign person shall not acquire land, mineral deposits, dwellings, the press, a bank or a public establishment.
3. The transfer abroad of the earnings of a licensed participation is permitted within the limits the licence states.

**Article 89: Foreign exchange**
1. All foreign exchange of the Republic passes through the external account held at the Bank of the Republic.
2. The Bank publishes quarterly the balance of the external account and the composition of the reserves.
3. The marek shall not be issued against a foreign currency held as reserve, and the Republic shall not adopt the currency of another state or of a union of states.

**Article 90: Assistance to other peoples**
1. The Republic provides assistance in goods, in technique and in training to peoples in want, on the scale the plan establishes.
2. Assistance creates no debt and is granted on no condition touching the internal affairs of the recipient.

# TITLE IV: THE ASSEMBLY OF THE REPUBLIC

## Chapter 1: Composition and election

**Article 91: Nature of the Assembly**
1. The Assembly of the Republic represents the people and exercises legislative power.
2. The Assembly sits in one chamber.
3. The Assembly meets in permanent session and adjourns only by its own decision.

**Article 92: Composition**
1. The Assembly consists of one hundred and twenty members, elected for four years.
2. Sixty members are elected in single-member constituencies, one for each constituency, by the candidate who receives the most votes.
3. Sixty members are elected from lists presented in the districts, and the seats are distributed among the lists in proportion to the votes they receive.
4. A list receiving less than three per cent of the votes cast in the Republic takes no seat from the distribution under paragraph 3.
5. Constituencies are drawn by the Electoral Commission so that the number of residents in each departs by no more than five per cent from the average, and are revised after each census.

**Article 93: Candidature**
1. A citizen entitled to vote may stand for election.
2. Candidates are presented by parties, by associations and by groups of voters, and a candidature presented by a group of voters is subject to no condition additional to those applying to a party.
3. The public funds granted to a candidature and the private contributions received by it are published before the poll, and a contribution from a foreign source is prohibited.

**Article 94: Incompatibility**
1. The office of member of the Assembly is incompatible with a judicial office, with membership of the Constitutional Court, of the Court of Accounts or of the Electoral Commission, with service in the armed forces or the police, and with the direction of an enterprise.
2. A member appointed to the Government retains their seat, and their vote in the Assembly is exercised by them alone.
3. A member shall declare their interests and their assets on taking office and on leaving it, and the declarations are published.

**Article 95: Mandate**
1. A member represents the whole people and is bound by no instruction.
2. A member shall not be prosecuted for a vote given or an opinion expressed in the exercise of their office.
3. A member may be arrested or prosecuted for another act only with the authorisation of the Assembly, which shall be refused only where the proceeding appears to be brought to prevent the exercise of the mandate. Authorisation is not required where the member is taken in the act of a serious offence.
4. A member may resign. A seat becomes vacant on resignation, on death, on a final conviction carrying disqualification, and on the loss of a condition of eligibility.

**Article 96: Filling of vacancies**
1. A vacancy in a seat under Article 92(3) passes to the next candidate on the same list.
2. A vacancy in a seat under Article 92(2) is filled by a by-election within ninety days, unless fewer than six months of the term remain.

## Chapter 2: Functioning

**Article 97: Sittings and publicity**
1. Sittings of the Assembly are public. The Assembly may sit in closed session by a decision taken in public and stating the reason.
2. The record of every sitting, the text of every proposal and the result of every vote, by member, are published within forty-eight hours.
3. Every person has the right to attend a public sitting and to consult the record without charge.

**Article 98: Officers and organisation**
1. The Assembly elects its President and its other officers at its first sitting, for the duration of the legislature, and may remove them by the same majority.
2. The Assembly adopts its rules of procedure, which are published and are subject to review by the Constitutional Court.
3. Groups formed by members according to the rules of procedure receive means for their work in proportion to their number, and a group of the opposition presides over the committee on the budget and over the committee on the plan.

**Article 99: Committees**
1. The Assembly constitutes standing committees, whose composition reflects the composition of the Assembly.
2. A committee may summon a minister, a director of a public establishment or of an enterprise in social ownership, and an officer of the administration, who shall appear and answer.
3. A committee may require the production of a document, and a refusal shall be reasoned in writing and may be referred to the Constitutional Court.

**Article 100: Committees of inquiry**
1. A committee of inquiry is constituted at the demand of one third of the members of the Assembly.
2. The committee has the powers of a court to summon witnesses and to require documents, and its conclusions are published.
3. Its conclusions bind no court and decide nothing in advance in a criminal proceeding.

**Article 101: Quorum and majorities**
1. The Assembly deliberates validly when the majority of its members are present, and decides by the majority of those voting, except where this Constitution requires another majority.
2. An organic law is adopted, amended or repealed by the majority of the members of the Assembly.
3. A vote by which the Assembly may remove a person from office is taken by secret ballot. Every other vote is taken openly.

## Chapter 3: Legislation

**Article 102: Laws and organic laws**
1. The Assembly legislates by law.
2. An organic law is a law which this Constitution requires for a stated matter. It is adopted by the majority under Article 101(2), takes precedence over an ordinary law in the matter it regulates, and is submitted to the Constitutional Court before promulgation.
3. This Constitution requires an organic law for citizenship, for the electoral system, for the ceilings under Articles 69 and 70, for the organisation of the courts, for the status of judges, for the armed forces and the police, for the state of emergency, for the grounds on which access to public information may be refused, and for the organisation of local self-government.

**Article 103: Initiative**
1. A proposal of law may be laid by a member of the Assembly, by the Government, by a district council, or by citizens under Article 42(2).
2. The Government alone may lay the proposal of the budget and the proposal of the plan.
3. A proposal is accompanied by a statement of its reasons, of its cost, and of its effect on the natural environment.

**Article 104: Procedure**
1. A proposal is examined in committee, which reports to the Assembly, and is then voted in general terms, article by article, and as a whole.
2. An amendment which is unconnected with the subject of the proposal is inadmissible, and the President of the Assembly rules on admissibility subject to appeal to the Assembly.
3. A proposal rejected by the Assembly may be laid again after six months.
4. A proposal which has not been voted at the end of the legislature lapses.

**Article 105: Promulgation and publication**
1. A law adopted by the Assembly is sent to the President of the Republic, who promulgates it within fifteen days and orders its publication.
2. The President may, once, return a law to the Assembly with reasons stated in writing. Where the Assembly adopts it again by the majority of its members, the President promulgates it within eight days.
3. The President may, before promulgation, refer a law to the Constitutional Court, which decides within thirty days. Promulgation is suspended during that period.
4. Where the President neither promulgates nor returns nor refers a law within the periods stated, the President of the Assembly orders its publication.

**Article 106: Delegated legislation**
1. The Assembly may authorise the Government by law to make decrees having the force of law in a stated matter, for a stated period, and within limits the law defines.
2. The Assembly shall not delegate the matters reserved to an organic law, taxation, criminal offences and penalties, or the restriction of a right declared in Title II.
3. A decree made under a delegation is laid before the Assembly within thirty days and lapses if the Assembly does not confirm it within sixty days.

**Article 107: Powers of the Assembly**
1. The Assembly adopts the plan and the budget, ratifies treaties, declares a state of emergency, decides the use of the armed forces beyond the frontier, and grants amnesty.
2. The Assembly elects the Prime Minister, and elects or approves the holders of the offices this Constitution states.
3. The Assembly receives the reports of the Court of Accounts, of the Electoral Commission, of the Commission on Information and Data and of the Bank of the Republic, and debates each in public sitting.
4. The Assembly may set aside a regulation of the Government which exceeds the law, by a resolution stating the excess.

## Chapter 4: Term and dissolution

**Article 108: Term**
1. The Assembly is elected for four years, and its term runs from its first sitting.
2. Elections are held between thirty and sixty days before the end of the term, and the new Assembly meets within thirty days of the poll.
3. The term is extended only during a state of emergency or an armed attack, by a law adopted by two thirds of the members, for a period no longer than six months at a time, and elections are held within ninety days of the end of the emergency.

**Article 109: Dissolution**
1. The President of the Republic may dissolve the Assembly on the proposal of the Government, after consulting the President of the Assembly and the groups.
2. The Assembly shall not be dissolved within twelve months of its first sitting, during a state of emergency, or while a motion of censure is pending.
3. Elections are held within sixty days of the dissolution.
4. The Assembly may dissolve itself by a decision taken by two thirds of its members.

**Article 110: Continuity**
1. A dissolved Assembly continues to exercise its functions until the new Assembly meets, and during that period it shall not adopt an organic law, revise the Constitution, or ratify a treaty.
2. Where the Assembly stands dissolved and a matter admits of no delay, its standing committee, composed in proportion to the outgoing Assembly, decides subject to confirmation by the new Assembly.

# TITLE V: THE PRESIDENT OF THE REPUBLIC

**Article 111: Office**
1. The President of the Republic represents the Republic, safeguards the observance of this Constitution, and exercises the functions this Constitution states.
2. The President exercises no function of government and gives no instruction to the administration.

**Article 112: Election**
1. The President is elected by direct universal suffrage for four years.
2. The candidate receiving more than half the votes cast is elected. Where no candidate does so, a second poll is held after fourteen days between the two candidates receiving the most votes, and the candidate receiving more votes is elected.
3. No person shall serve more than two terms, whether consecutive or not.
4. A candidate shall be a citizen of thirty-five years entitled to vote, and shall be presented by ten thousand voters or by a group in the Assembly.

**Article 113: Incompatibility and oath**
1. The office of President is incompatible with any other public or private office, with membership of the Assembly, and with the direction of a party.
2. The President takes office before the Assembly, swearing to observe the Constitution and the laws.

**Article 114: Functions**
1. The President promulgates laws under Article 105, appoints the Prime Minister elected by the Assembly, appoints and dismisses ministers on the proposal of the Prime Minister, and dissolves the Assembly under Article 109.
2. The President accredits and receives ambassadors, signs treaties for ratification by the Assembly, and appoints the holders of the offices this Constitution or an organic law assigns to the President.
3. The President is the head of the armed forces, and exercises that function on the decision of the Assembly and through the Government.
4. The President may refer a law or a treaty to the Constitutional Court before promulgation or ratification.
5. The President may address the Assembly, and the address is not debated in the President's presence.
6. The President grants individual pardons on the proposal of the Government, and shall not grant a pardon for an offence committed by a member of the Government or by a holder of public office in the exercise of that office.

**Article 115: Countersignature**
1. An act of the President other than those stated in paragraph 2 requires the countersignature of the Prime Minister, who assumes responsibility for it before the Assembly.
2. The promulgation of a law, the return of a law to the Assembly, a reference to the Constitutional Court, an address to the Assembly, and the appointment of the Prime Minister elected by the Assembly require no countersignature.

**Article 116: Vacancy and substitution**
1. Where the office is vacant or the President is prevented from acting, the President of the Assembly exercises the functions of the office, and shall not dissolve the Assembly or return a law during that period.
2. An election is held within sixty days of the vacancy, and the person elected serves a full term.

**Article 117: Responsibility**
1. The President is not answerable for acts done in the exercise of the office, except for a serious violation of the Constitution or a criminal offence.
2. Proceedings are opened by the Assembly on the demand of one third of its members and by the decision of two thirds, and are decided by the Constitutional Court.
3. Where the Constitutional Court finds the charge proved, the President is removed from office, and remains liable to the ordinary courts for any offence.

# TITLE VI: THE GOVERNMENT

## Chapter 1: Formation and responsibility

**Article 118: Composition and function**
1. The Government consists of the Prime Minister and the ministers.
2. The Government conducts the general policy of the Republic, executes the laws and the plan, directs the administration, and has at its disposal the police under Article 19.
3. The Government is collectively answerable to the Assembly of the Republic.

**Article 119: Formation**
1. The Assembly elects the Prime Minister by a majority of its members, on candidatures presented by groups or by twenty members.
2. The President appoints the Prime Minister so elected, and appoints the ministers on the Prime Minister's proposal.
3. The Government takes office on presenting its programme to the Assembly, and the programme is published.
4. Where the Assembly does not elect a Prime Minister within sixty days of its first sitting or of the resignation of a Government, the President dissolves it and orders elections.

**Article 120: Censure**
1. A motion of censure is presented by one fifth of the members of the Assembly and shall name a candidate for the office of Prime Minister.
2. The motion is voted no earlier than the third day after its presentation. Where it is carried by a majority of the members of the Assembly, the Prime Minister named in it is elected and the outgoing Government is dismissed.
3. Where the motion is rejected, its signatories shall not present another within six months.
4. The Prime Minister may put the question of confidence to the Assembly. Where confidence is refused by a majority of the members, the Prime Minister resigns, and Article 119 applies.

**Article 121: Resignation and continuation**
1. The Government resigns on the election of a new Assembly, on the resignation or death of the Prime Minister, and on a vote of censure.
2. A Government which has resigned continues to conduct current affairs until a new Government takes office, and during that period it shall not lay a proposal of law, make a decree under a delegation, or appoint to an office for a fixed term.

**Article 122: Incompatibility and conduct of ministers**
1. The office of minister is incompatible with any other public or private office, with the direction of an enterprise, and with a professional activity.
2. A minister declares their interests and their assets on taking office and on leaving it, and the declarations are published.
3. For two years after leaving office, a former minister shall not take employment or a paid engagement with an enterprise which was the object of a decision they took.
4. A minister is answerable before the ordinary courts for an offence committed in the exercise of the office, and no authorisation of the Assembly is required for the proceeding.

## Chapter 2: Powers and acts

**Article 123: Acts of the Government**
1. The Government makes regulations for the execution of the laws, which state the law they execute and shall not add to it, restrict a right, or create an offence.
2. Regulations are published in the Official Gazette and take effect under Article 4(2).
3. A regulation may be annulled by the administrative court on the application of any person concerned.

**Article 124: Ministries and their number**
1. The number of ministries, their names and the distribution of business among them are established by law.
2. A minister directs the ministry assigned to them, and answers to the Assembly for it.
3. A body created to exercise a function of a ministry outside the ministry shall be created by law, shall publish its accounts, and shall answer to a committee of the Assembly.

**Article 125: The Office of the Plan**
1. The Office of the Plan prepares the plan under Article 60, monitors its execution, and publishes quarterly the departures from it.
2. The Director of the Office is appointed by the Assembly for six years and may be removed by it by a majority of its members.
3. The Office publishes the data on which the plan rests, in a form which permits its calculations to be repeated.

**Article 126: Duty to inform**
1. The Government answers questions put by members of the Assembly, in writing within thirty days and orally in the sitting reserved for that purpose each week.
2. The Government shall not withhold from a committee of the Assembly information relating to the execution of the plan, the budget or a contract concluded by an organ of the State.
3. The Government publishes every contract concluded by an organ of the State above the value fixed by law, with its price and its term.

## Chapter 3: The administration

**Article 127: Principles of the administration**
1. The administration serves the population, acts on the basis of law, and treats like cases alike.
2. An act of the administration affecting a person states its reasons and the means of contesting it, and takes effect on notification to that person.
3. Where the administration fails to decide within the period fixed by law, the person concerned may apply to the administrative court as though the application had been refused.
4. Procedures before the administration are free of charge, except for charges fixed by law and published.

**Article 128: Public service**
1. Officers of the administration are recruited by public competition, are appointed for an indefinite period, and may be dismissed only on grounds and by procedure established by law.
2. An officer shall not be appointed, promoted, transferred or dismissed by reason of party membership or of opinion.
3. An officer shall refuse an unlawful instruction, shall state the refusal in writing, and shall not be penalised for it.
4. An officer who reports a breach of the law within the administration is protected from dismissal, transfer and disciplinary proceedings for that reason.

**Article 129: Delegation and contracting**
1. A function which involves the exercise of public authority over persons shall not be delegated to a private person.
2. Where the administration contracts for goods or services, it does so by public procedure, publishes the criteria in advance, and publishes the reasons for the award.
3. A contract concluded in breach of this Article is void, and the officer responsible answers for the loss.

**Article 130: Territorial administration**
1. The Government maintains services in the districts for the execution of the laws in matters reserved to the Republic.
2. These services shall not exercise a function assigned to a district or a commune, and shall not review the merits of a decision of a district or a commune.

# TITLE VII: LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT

**Article 131: Units of local self-government**
1. The units of local self-government are the commune and the district. Every part of the territory belongs to one commune and one district.
2. A commune is created, merged or abolished by law, after a referendum in the population concerned, whose result the Assembly of the Republic shall follow.
3. The seat and the boundaries of a district are established by law.

**Article 132: Competence of the commune**
1. The commune decides every matter of local concern which this Constitution or an organic law has not assigned to the district or to the Republic.
2. The commune is responsible for the spatial plan, the local roads, water and sanitation, the collection of waste, the public stock of dwellings, the schools of the first level, the care of children and of the aged, the local cultural institutions and the public spaces.
3. A function may be assigned to the commune by law only together with the resources to perform it.

**Article 133: Competence of the district**
1. The district is responsible for the schools of the second level, the hospitals of the district, the roads of the district, public transport between communes, the plan of the district, and the coordination of the communes within it.
2. The district shall not review the merits of a decision taken by a commune within the commune's competence.

**Article 134: Organs of the commune**
1. The commune is governed by a council elected for four years by direct universal suffrage, and by a mayor elected in the same manner and for the same term.
2. The mayor executes the decisions of the council, directs the administration of the commune, and answers to the council, which may put questions and require documents.
3. The council adopts the budget, the spatial plan and the local regulations, and the mayor shall not adopt them in its place.
4. The mayor may be removed before the end of the term by a referendum held on the demand of the council by a majority of its members or on the demand of a number of residents fixed by organic law.

**Article 135: Organs of the district**
1. The district is governed by a council elected for four years by direct universal suffrage, which elects its president from among its members.
2. The president of the district executes the decisions of the council and answers to it.

**Article 136: Participation of residents**
1. The commune assembly, open to all residents, meets at least twice a year, receives the accounts of the mayor, and may put any matter within the competence of the commune to the council, which shall consider it and reply in public sitting.
2. A local referendum is held on the demand of a number of residents fixed by organic law, and its result binds the council in a matter within the competence of the commune.
3. The sittings of the council and of the district council are public, and their decisions, budgets and contracts are published.

**Article 137: Resources**
1. Communes and districts have their own revenue, consisting of local taxes and charges established within the limits of the law, of the revenue of their property and enterprises, and of transfers from the plan.
2. Transfers are distributed by published criteria which take account of population, of the cost of service in the territory, and of the difference in resources between units, so as to reduce that difference.
3. A commune or a district may borrow only for investment, within the limit fixed by law, and with the approval of the Government where the limit is exceeded.

**Article 138: Property of local units**
1. Communes and districts own property, which serves their functions and the use of their residents.
2. Property serving a public use shall not be sold or mortgaged.
3. A commune or a district may establish an enterprise for a service to its residents, and the enterprise answers to the council.

**Article 139: Supervision**
1. The Government supervises the lawfulness of the acts of communes and districts, and shall not supervise their expediency.
2. Where the Government considers an act unlawful, it may suspend it by a reasoned decision and shall apply within thirty days to the administrative court, which decides within ninety days. The suspension lapses if the application is not made.
3. A council may be dissolved only by a law, on the ground of a grave and persisting breach of the Constitution or of the law established by a judgment, and elections are held within sixty days.

**Article 140: Cooperation**
1. Communes may cooperate by agreement for the joint performance of their functions, and may create joint bodies, which answer to the councils that created them.
2. Cooperation between communes shall not create a further tier of government, and a joint body shall not be given a general competence.

**Article 141: Defence of local self-government**
1. A commune or a district may apply to the Constitutional Court against an act of the Republic which infringes its competence or removes the resources corresponding to its functions.

# TITLE VIII: THE COURTS

## Chapter 1: Principles

**Article 142: Judicial power**
1. Justice is administered in the name of the people by the courts, which are independent and subject only to the Constitution and the law.
2. An organ of the State, a party or a person shall not give an instruction to a court, and shall not comment on a pending case in a manner intended to influence it.
3. A court decision is reasoned, is pronounced in public, and is binding on the organs of the State.
4. Extraordinary and special courts are prohibited, and Article 29(5) governs.

**Article 143: Access to justice**
1. Every person has the right to bring a case before a court, and a right shall not be denied for want of a procedure.
2. Court fees shall be proportionate and shall be remitted for a person without means.
3. Legal assistance is provided free of charge to a person without means, in criminal matters and in matters concerning dwelling, employment, social security and family.
4. Proceedings are conducted in Aporean, and in the language of a national minority in the districts under Article 24, with interpretation provided free of charge.

**Article 144: Publicity**
1. Hearings are public. A court may exclude the public by a reasoned decision to protect a minor, the private life of a party, or a witness in danger, and shall pronounce the judgment in public.
2. Judgments are published, with the names of natural parties removed where the law so provides.

## Chapter 2: Organisation

**Article 145: The courts**
1. The courts are the communal courts, the district courts, the courts of appeal, the Supreme Court, and the administrative courts.
2. The Supreme Court secures the uniform application of the law, decides appeals in the cases the law states, and publishes the principles it establishes.
3. The administrative courts decide disputes between persons and the administration, including the annulment of a regulation under Article 123(3).
4. The organisation, the seats and the jurisdiction of the courts are established by organic law.

**Article 146: Lay participation**
1. In criminal proceedings for an offence punishable by more than five years of deprivation of liberty, the court sits with lay judges drawn by lot from the register of voters of the district.
2. Service as a lay judge is a public duty, is compensated, and is a ground of excused absence from work.
3. Lay judges deliberate and vote on the facts and on the penalty together with the professional judges.

**Article 147: Enforcement**
1. The organs of the State shall execute a judgment addressed to them within the period it states.
2. Failure to execute a judgment is an offence, and the court may impose a periodic penalty on the organ and name the officer responsible.

## Chapter 3: Judges and the Judicial Council

**Article 148: Status of judges**
1. Judges are appointed for an indefinite period and shall not be transferred, suspended, retired or removed except by decision of the Judicial Council on grounds established by organic law.
2. A judge shall not belong to a party, exercise another paid office, or take part in a political campaign.
3. A judge shall not be held liable for a decision, except for an act done deliberately to cause harm, established by a criminal judgment.
4. The remuneration of judges is established by law and shall not be reduced during their service.

**Article 149: The Judicial Council**
1. The Judicial Council appoints, promotes, transfers and disciplines judges, and proposes to the Assembly the budget of the courts.
2. The Council consists of fifteen members: eight judges elected by the judges of each level by secret ballot, four persons elected by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds from among lawyers and teachers of law, and three residents drawn by lot from the register of voters, who serve for two years.
3. The President of the Supreme Court presides over the Council, which decides by a majority of its members and states the reasons of its decisions.
4. Decisions of the Council concerning a judge are subject to appeal to the Supreme Court sitting in plenary.

**Article 150: Selection and training**
1. Judges are selected by public competition, are trained in the school of the judiciary, and are appointed by the Judicial Council on the results of that competition.
2. The criteria of promotion are published, and a decision of promotion states how the candidate satisfied them.

## Chapter 4: Prosecution

**Article 151: The public prosecution**
1. The public prosecution investigates offences, brings and conducts criminal proceedings, and defends the public interest in the cases the law states.
2. Prosecutors act on the law and on the evidence. The Government shall not give an instruction to prosecute or to discontinue in an individual case, and a general instruction of policy is published.
3. The Prosecutor General is appointed by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds for six years, is not eligible for a second term, and may be removed by the same majority on grounds established by organic law.
4. A decision not to prosecute is reasoned, is notified to the injured person, and may be contested before a court.

**Article 152: Investigation**
1. An investigative measure restricting a right requires a judicial warrant under Articles 31 and 32.
2. A person under investigation has the right to counsel, to know the acts alleged, and to consult the file, subject to a delay ordered by a judge for a stated period where disclosure would defeat the investigation.
3. Evidence obtained in breach of the Constitution is inadmissible, and Article 27(4) governs.

## Chapter 5: The Constitutional Court

**Article 153: Composition**
1. The Constitutional Court consists of nine judges appointed for nine years, who are not eligible for a second term.
2. Three are elected by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds, three are appointed by the President of the Republic, and three are elected by the plenary of the Supreme Court.
3. One third of the Court is renewed every three years.
4. Judges of the Court are chosen from among jurists of at least fifteen years of practice or teaching, shall not belong to a party, and hold no other office.
5. The Court elects its President from among its members for three years.

**Article 154: Jurisdiction**
1. The Constitutional Court decides on the conformity of laws, decrees, regulations, treaties and rules of procedure with this Constitution.
2. It decides disputes over competence between organs of the Republic, and between the Republic and a commune or a district under Article 141.
3. It decides on the dissolution of a party under Article 5(4), on the validity of an election or a referendum on appeal from the Electoral Commission, and on proceedings against the President under Article 117.
4. It decides individual applications under Article 155.
5. It gives an opinion, before ratification, on the conformity of a treaty with this Constitution, and where the opinion is negative the treaty is ratified only after revision of the Constitution.

**Article 155: Individual application**
1. Every person who alleges that a right declared in Title II has been infringed by an act of a public organ may apply to the Constitutional Court after exhausting other remedies.
2. The Court may waive the requirement of exhaustion where the matter is of general importance or where delay would cause irreparable harm.
3. The application is free of charge, requires no representation by a lawyer for its admission, and the Court assists an applicant without means to put the application in order.

**Article 156: Effects of decisions**
1. A provision found contrary to this Constitution ceases to have effect on publication of the decision, or on the later date the Court fixes, which shall not exceed twelve months.
2. Decisions of the Court are final and bind every organ and every court.
3. A criminal conviction founded on a provision found contrary to this Constitution is reopened on the application of the convicted person.
4. Where the Court finds that the absence of a provision infringes this Constitution, it states the finding and fixes a period within which the Assembly of the Republic shall legislate.

# TITLE IX: INDEPENDENT BODIES

## Chapter 1: Common provisions

**Article 157: Independence**
1. The bodies established by this Title exercise their functions independently, receive no instruction, and are answerable to the Assembly of the Republic alone.
2. Their members are elected by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds, for a fixed term, and are not eligible for a second term.
3. A member may be removed before the end of the term only by the same majority, on grounds established by organic law, and by a reasoned decision.
4. The budget of each body is laid before the Assembly by the body itself, and the Government shall not alter it before it is laid.

**Article 158: Reports and access**
1. Each body reports annually to the Assembly of the Republic, in public sitting, and publishes the report.
2. Each body may require from an organ of the State, a district, a commune, a public establishment and an enterprise in social ownership any document within its field, and a refusal is reasoned in writing and may be referred to the administrative court.

## Chapter 2: The Court of Accounts

**Article 159: Function**
1. The Court of Accounts audits the execution of the budget and of the plan, the accounts of the organs of the State, of the districts, of the communes, of the public establishments and of the enterprises in social ownership, and the use of public funds by any person who receives them.
2. The Court examines the regularity of expenditure and the results obtained for it, and publishes its findings with the reply of the body audited.
3. The Court reports on the accounts of execution of the budget before the Assembly votes on them.

**Article 160: Composition and powers**
1. The Court consists of nine members elected for nine years, one third renewed every three years, chosen from persons qualified in accounting, in audit or in public finance.
2. Members of the Court hold the guarantees of independence of judges, and Article 148 applies to them.
3. Where the Court finds an unlawful expenditure, it refers the matter to the public prosecution and may order the officer responsible to repay, subject to appeal to the Supreme Court.

## Chapter 3: The Electoral Commission

**Article 161: Function**
1. The Electoral Commission organises elections and referendums, maintains the register of voters, draws constituencies under Article 92(5), registers candidatures, and publishes the results.
2. The Commission supervises the funding of parties and of campaigns, publishes the accounts it receives, and may impose the penalties established by law.
3. The Commission shall not classify a party or a candidature by its programme or its ideology, and shall refuse a candidature only for a defect of form or of eligibility, by a reasoned decision subject to appeal to the Constitutional Court.

**Article 162: Composition**
1. The Commission consists of seven members elected for seven years.
2. Its administration is permanent, its officers are recruited by competition, and Article 128 applies to them.
3. Representatives of every candidature have the right to be present at the poll and at the count, and the count is public and takes place at the polling station.

## Chapter 4: The Commission on Information and Data

**Article 163: Function**
1. The Commission on Information and Data secures the right of access to public information under Article 20 and the protection of personal data under Article 32.
2. The Commission decides appeals against a refusal of access, and its decision binds the organ concerned, subject to appeal by that organ to the administrative court.
3. The Commission supervises registers of personal data, orders the erasure of data collected without a legal basis, and reports annually on the archives of the former State Protection Service and on access to them under Article 6(3).
4. The Commission is heard before the adoption of a law or a regulation establishing a register of personal data, and its opinion is published with the proposal.

**Article 164: Composition**
1. The Commission consists of five members elected for six years, of whom at least two are qualified in law and at least one in the processing of data.

# TITLE X: DEFENCE, PUBLIC ORDER AND EMERGENCY

## Chapter 1: Defence

**Article 165: The armed forces**
1. The armed forces defend the territory, the independence and the constitutional order of the Republic against armed attack.
2. The armed forces consist of professional members serving under contract and of a reserve trained under organic law.
3. Compulsory military service exists only during an armed attack or a state of war declared by the Assembly of the Republic, and Article 34(3) governs.
4. The armed forces shall not be employed against the population, and an order to that effect is unlawful and shall not be obeyed.

**Article 166: Command and control**
1. The Government directs defence policy and answers to the Assembly of the Republic for it.
2. The organisation, the establishment and the equipment of the armed forces are fixed by organic law, and the expenditure on them appears in the budget by category.
3. A committee of the Assembly supervises the armed forces and the intelligence service, receives classified information under conditions established by organic law, and reports annually in public in a form which protects sources.

**Article 167: Limits**
1. The Republic shall not station its forces on the territory of another state except within a mission of an international organisation of which it is a member, and only under a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly.
2. The Republic shall not permit a foreign force to enter or to be stationed on its territory except for exercise, rescue or transit authorised by the Assembly.
3. The Republic shall not export a weapon to a state engaged in an armed conflict or against which a finding under the instruments of the union under Article 18 is in force.

**Article 168: Intelligence**
1. The intelligence service collects information on threats to the territory and to the constitutional order from abroad.
2. It shall not collect information on a person by reason of their opinions, their party membership or their lawful association, and Article 6 applies to it.
3. A measure of the intelligence service restricting a right requires the authorisation of a judge of the Supreme Court designated for that purpose, who reports annually to the committee under Article 166(3) on the number and the type of authorisations granted and refused.

## Chapter 2: Public order

**Article 169: The police**
1. The police protect persons, prevent and investigate offences, and maintain public order, under the direction of the Government and the supervision of the courts.
2. The police act with the least force sufficient, and every use of a firearm is reported, investigated by a body independent of the police, and published in anonymised form.
3. Officers of the police are identifiable at all times by a number visible on their uniform, and the concealment of that number is a disciplinary offence.
4. A complaint against the police is investigated by a body independent of the police, whose conclusions are notified to the complainant and may be contested before a court.

## Chapter 3: Emergency

**Article 170: Declaration**
1. The Government may declare a state of emergency in the whole or a part of the territory in case of natural disaster, epidemic, grave breakdown of essential supply, or grave and imminent threat to public order.
2. The declaration states the reasons, the territory affected, the measures adopted and their duration, and takes effect on publication.
3. The declaration lapses unless the Assembly of the Republic confirms it within forty-eight hours by a majority of its members. Where the Assembly stands dissolved, its standing committee confirms it under Article 110(2).
4. A state of emergency is confirmed for no more than thirty days, and is extended only by a further decision of the Assembly for periods of no more than thirty days, each stating the reasons for the extension.

**Article 171: Measures**
1. Measures adopted during a state of emergency are limited to what the situation requires, are stated in the declaration, and cease on its expiry.
2. The following are not suspended in any circumstance: the right to life, the prohibition of torture, the prohibition of the death penalty, the right to a court and to counsel, the prohibition of retroactive criminal law, the freedom of conscience, the guarantees of Article 6, the right to a subsistence income and to shelter, and the supply of water and of care.
3. Measures shall not restrict the right to strike except in the services named in Article 46(2), and shall not dissolve a party, a trade union or an association.

**Article 172: Limits on the organs**
1. The Assembly of the Republic shall not be dissolved during a state of emergency, and elections due during it are held on the date fixed unless the poll is materially impossible in a part of the territory, in which case the Assembly postpones them for no more than ninety days by a decision stating the impossibility.
2. The Constitution shall not be revised during a state of emergency.
3. The courts continue to sit, and every measure of emergency may be contested before them without delay.
4. The Government reports to the Assembly every seven days on the measures in force and on their effects, and the report is published.

**Article 173: Armed attack**
1. Where the territory is subject to armed attack, the President of the Republic states the fact, and the Assembly of the Republic decides on the response within twenty-four hours.
2. Where the Assembly cannot meet, the Government orders the defence of the territory and reports to the Assembly at its first sitting.
3. A capitulation, an occupation or a cession of territory accepted by an organ of the Republic is void, and a duty of obedience does not arise from it.

# TITLE XI: REVISION OF THE CONSTITUTION

**Article 174: Initiative**
1. A proposal to revise this Constitution may be laid by one third of the members of the Assembly of the Republic, by the Government, or by a number of citizens fixed by organic law.
2. The proposal states the articles to be revised and the text proposed for them.

**Article 175: Procedure**
1. The proposal is voted by the Assembly of the Republic in two readings separated by at least ninety days, and is adopted in each reading by two thirds of the members.
2. The text adopted is published, and is submitted to a referendum where one third of the members of the Assembly, or a number of citizens fixed by organic law, so demand within sixty days of publication.
3. A referendum on a revision is decided by a majority of the votes cast, and no condition of turnout applies.
4. A revision enters into force on promulgation by the President of the Republic and publication, and takes the form of an Amendment numbered in sequence and appended to this Constitution.

**Article 176: Limits**
1. The following shall not be revised: the republican form of the State, the direct universal suffrage, the political pluralism of Article 5, the prohibition of the death penalty, the prohibition of torture, the equality of persons before the law, and this Article.
2. A revision shall not reduce the level of protection of a right declared in Title II, and a proposal to that effect is inadmissible.
3. The Constitution shall not be revised during a state of emergency, during an armed attack, or while the Assembly stands dissolved.

**Article 177: Review by the Constitutional Court**
1. Before the second reading, the Constitutional Court examines the proposal for conformity with Article 176 on the application of one fifth of the members of the Assembly, and decides within thirty days.
2. Where the Court finds the proposal inadmissible, the procedure ends.

**Article 178: Consolidation and publication**
1. The consolidated text of this Constitution, with the Amendments in force appended and the articles they affect marked, is published by the Assembly of the Republic within sixty days of the entry into force of an Amendment.
2. The consolidated text is available free of charge, in print and in electronic form, without registration.
3. Where an Amendment and an article of the original text conflict, the Amendment prevails.

---

# TITLE XII: FINAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

**Article 179: Entry into force**
1. This Constitution enters into force on 11 September 1994.
2. The Constitution of 1947 and the amendments to it are repealed, and are preserved and published as a historical text.

**Article 180: Existing law**
1. Laws and regulations in force on the date stated in Article 179 remain in force to the extent that they conform to this Constitution.
2. A provision contrary to this Constitution ceases to have effect on 11 September 1996, and the Assembly of the Republic shall bring the law into conformity before that date.
3. A court applies this Constitution directly where a law has not been brought into conformity.

**Article 181: First elections**
1. Elections to the Assembly of the Republic are held within one hundred and twenty days of the entry into force of this Constitution.
2. The election of the President of the Republic is held on the same day.
3. Elections to the councils of the communes and the districts are held within one year.

**Article 182: Constitution of the new organs**
1. The Constitutional Court is constituted within one hundred and eighty days of the first sitting of the Assembly of the Republic. On its first constitution, the terms of three of its judges are fixed at three years and of three at six years, by lot, so that Article 153(3) may operate.
2. The Judicial Council, the Court of Accounts, the Electoral Commission and the Commission on Information and Data are constituted within one year.
3. Judges in office on the entry into force of this Constitution continue in office, and the Judicial Council reviews their appointment within two years against the conditions of Article 148.

**Article 183: The former State Protection Service**
1. The Service is dissolved on the entry into force of this Constitution.
2. Its archives pass to the National Archive within ninety days, and their destruction, alteration or removal is an offence subject to no time limit.
3. A person who directed the Service, or who took part in the surveillance of persons for their opinions, shall not hold an office in the police, in the intelligence service, in the judiciary or in the administration of justice.
4. The Assembly of the Republic shall establish by organic law, within two years, the procedure by which a person may obtain their file and the identity of those who informed on them.

**Article 184: Property and enterprises**
1. Property of the whole people on the entry into force of this Constitution remains in social ownership.
2. The transfer of an enterprise in social ownership to private ownership requires a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic, stating the enterprise, the reasons and the conditions.
3. Claims for property taken before 1947 are decided by the courts under a law which the Assembly shall adopt within two years, and restitution shall not affect a dwelling occupied by a household as its home, nor land worked by a cooperative.

**Article 185: Continuity of obligations**
1. Treaties in force bind the Republic to the extent that they conform to this Constitution, and the Government shall submit to the Assembly within two years a list of those which do not.
2. The Republic assumes the obligations of the Republic under the Constitution of 1947 towards persons, and in particular pensions, savings deposits and rights acquired in employment.

**Article 186: Numbering of the Amendments**
1. Amendments adopted under Title XI are numbered in sequence from Amendment I, and each states the articles it amends, replaces or adds.
2. An Amendment which replaces an article states the full text of the article as replaced.

**Article 67 is replaced and Articles 190, 191 and 192 are added to Title III:**

**Article 67: Losses**
1. An enterprise in social ownership meets its losses out of its own reserves, and the founding organ shall not meet them out of the funds of the plan except under Articles 190 to 192.
2. An enterprise operates within a ceiling for each planning period, expressed in energy, materials, water, land, emissions and labour time, and in the funds allocated to it. The ceiling binds, and consumption beyond it stops the activity which depends on the exhausted allocation.
3. An enterprise keeps accounts of what it receives, what it produces, what it discards and what it emits, publishes them at the intervals the law fixes, and submits them to the Court of Accounts.
4. The concealment or falsification of these accounts is an offence, and the persons responsible answer for it personally.

**Article 190: Review of performance**
1. The branch association reviews each enterprise annually against its ceiling, its tasks in the plan, its accounts, its conditions of labour, and the needs it undertook to meet.
2. The review is public and states its reasons. The workers' council of the enterprise replies, and the reply is published with it.
3. Persistent failure to meet undertaken tasks, persistent consumption beyond ceiling, or persistent breach of the emissions budget under Article 85 starts the procedure of Article 191.
4. A shortfall arising from disaster, from the failure of another enterprise to deliver, or from a defect in the plan itself is recorded as a shortfall of that kind and starts no procedure against the enterprise.

**Article 191: Support**
1. An enterprise in difficulty may receive support from the solidarity fund of its branch association, to which every enterprise of the branch contributes, or from the reserve of the plan.
2. Support is granted only by a public decision stating the cause of the difficulty, the measures proposed, the quantities and funds allocated, the results required, and the date on which the support ends. The decision names the persons who took it.
3. Support runs for two years, and is renewed once for one further year only, on a fresh public decision founded on an independent evaluation showing that the measures are taking effect.
4. Support to one enterprise shall not exceed the proportion of the allocation of its branch which the organic law fixes. Support granted in each period is published in total and by recipient.
5. During support the enterprise reports quarterly, remains bound by its ceiling, which support shall not raise, and admits inspection by the Court of Accounts.
6. Support is granted to maintain a productive capacity which the plan requires, and shall not be granted to preserve an activity which the plan has ceased to require.
7. The expiry of support without the results required starts the procedure of Article 192, and further support to the same enterprise on the same grounds is prohibited for five years.
8. Support does not affect the income, the employment or the social security of the members of the enterprise.

**Article 192: Reorganisation and dissolution**
1. Reorganisation transfers the assets, the tasks and the members of an enterprise to another enterprise of the branch, to an enterprise of the commune or the district, or to a newly formed cooperative.
2. Reorganisation is decided by the founding organ after a public procedure in which the workers' council, the trade union, the users and the council of the commune are heard, and is subject to appeal to the administrative court.
3. Dissolution follows only where reorganisation cannot be effected, and follows the same procedure.
4. The members of a reorganised or dissolved enterprise keep their income during the period of transfer, receive a place in another enterprise or establishment, and receive training where the new task requires it.
5. Where the enterprise provided a service on the list under Article 195, that service continues without interruption, and its continuity is secured before the measure takes effect.

**Article 76(3) is replaced as follows:**

3. Credit unpaid at term is recovered. The Bank of the Republic shall not renew a credit to cover the non-payment of an earlier one, shall not grant credit to an enterprise under support except as part of the measures approved under Article 191(2), and reports annually to the Assembly of the Republic on the amounts unrecovered, by enterprise and by branch.

## AMENDMENT XIII
*Adopted 27 February 2025. Proposed by the Green Union, as the second of the three Amendments of the economic package.*

**Article 61 is replaced and Articles 193 and 194 are added to Title III:**

**Article 61: Accounts of the plan**
1. The plan is expressed in physical units for energy, materials, water, land, emissions, labour time and the useful life of goods, and in monetary values for the purposes of the budget and of prices.
2. Where a physical account and a monetary account point to different decisions, the physical account governs.
3. The plan includes balances for each resource named in paragraph 1, stated for the Republic and for each district, and states the stocks held at the opening and at the close of the period.
4. The data on which the balances rest are published in a form which permits the calculations to be repeated.

**Article 193: Ecological budgets**
1. The Assembly of the Republic adopts by law, for each planning period, ecological budgets for the Republic, expressed in physical units, covering emissions of greenhouse gases, the abstraction of water, the extraction of materials, the conversion of land, and the discharge of substances harmful to health.
2. An ecological budget is set at the level which the natural systems of the territory can sustain, established by the Ecological Budget Office on published evidence, and shall not be set above that level for reasons of cost.
3. The plan, the budget of the Republic, the plans of the districts and the ceilings of enterprises conform to the ecological budgets in force. A provision which exceeds a budget has no effect, and any person or association may apply to the administrative court to establish the excess.
4. An ecological budget shall not be raised except on a finding by the Office that the sustainable level has itself changed. A budget once lowered shall not be raised on the ground of a failure to observe it.
5. Where a period closes with a budget exceeded, the excess is deducted from the budget of the following period.

**Article 194: The Ecological Budget Office**
1. The Ecological Budget Office establishes the sustainable levels under Article 193(2), measures the use made of each budget, and publishes quarterly the position of each budget by branch and by district.
2. The Office consists of seven members elected by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds for seven years, chosen from persons qualified in the natural sciences, in engineering or in the accounting of resources, and Articles 157 and 158 apply.
3. The Office is heard before the adoption of the plan, of the budget of the Republic and of any law affecting the use of a resource under budget, and its opinion is published with the proposal.
4. The Office may require of an enterprise, an establishment, a district or a commune the physical accounts under Article 67(3), and may verify them on the premises.

**Article 84(1) is replaced as follows:**

1. The Government publishes annually the condition of the air, of the water, of the soil, of the forests and of the living species of the territory, together with the quantities of each material extracted, imported, used, recovered and discarded, stated in physical units and set against the ecological budgets in force.

*Adopted 27 February 2025. Proposed by the Communal Left, as the third of the three Amendments of the economic package.*

**Article 12(4) and 12(5) are replaced and Article 195 is added to Title III:**

4. The goods and services on the list of free provision are provided free of charge at the point of use, without any examination of the income of the person, without registration beyond that necessary to deliver them, and without charge of any kind, and are met from the plan.
5. The list of free provision is extended at each planning period and shall not be reduced. A good or service once placed on the list shall not be removed from it except by a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic on a finding, made by the Ecological Budget Office, that the resources to provide it are no longer available.

**Article 195: The list of free provision**

The Government states before the Assembly, with each plan, the goods and services which the productive capacity of the Republic would allow to be added, with the cost of each.
