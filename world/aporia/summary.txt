SUMMARY OF THE CONSTITUTION OF THE FREE REPUBLIC OF REFORMED LUCES APORIA

One line for each of the 195 articles. This summary has no legal force.
Where it differs from the Constitution, the Constitution governs (Article 178(3)).
Published by the Ministry of External Relations, Sluland.

TITLE I: FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES (articles 1 to 20)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1  The Republic
      A free, unitary, democratic socialist republic, with two official names of equal force.
   2  Sovereignty
      Power belongs to the people, and taking it outside these rules is a crime without a time limit.
   3  Supremacy of the Constitution
      The Constitution outranks every other rule, and Title II rights work in court on their own.
   4  Legality and publicity of the law
      Public power needs a legal basis, and an unpublished rule binds nobody.
   5  Political pluralism
      Parties are free and none may be given a leading role in the State or the economy.
   6  Prohibition of political policing
      No file on a person for their opinions, and the old political police cannot return under a new name.
   7  Human dignity
      Dignity cannot be violated, and no emergency or majority permits it.
   8  Territory
      The territory cannot be ceded or leased, the capital is Sluland, and the sea corridor stays open.
   9  Language, symbols and the calendar
      Aporean is the official language, minority languages are used with it, and the national day is 11 September.
  10  Citizenship
      Citizenship is set by organic law, and no citizen may be stripped of it or expelled.
  11  Economic order
      Social ownership in three forms, with private firms allowed under a ceiling on employees.
  12  Currency, prices and the direction of policy
      The marek is issued by the Bank alone, and the range of goods allocated by money is to shrink.
  13  Labour
      Labour is the ground of every claim on the product, and forced labour is prohibited.
  14  The natural environment
      The State protects the land, water, air and coast, and repairs the damage done to them.
  15  Secularity
      The Republic adopts no religion and religious communities govern themselves.
  16  Local self-government
      Communes and districts govern themselves, and local matters belong to the commune.
  17  Organs of the Republic and their separation
      The Assembly legislates, the Government executes, the courts judge, and four bodies stand outside all three.
  18  International relations
      Founding member of the Bloc, with treaties binding only after ratification and publication.
  19  Armed forces and public order
      The armed forces obey the civil power and may not be used against a lawful assembly.
  20  Access to public information
      Anyone may ask for information held by a public body, and a refusal must be reasoned and appealable.

TITLE II: RIGHTS, LIBERTIES AND DUTIES (articles 21 to 57)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  21  Equality
      Everyone is equal before the law, and hereditary titles are abolished.
  22  Scope and limitation of rights
      A right may be limited only by law, only where necessary, and never in its essential content.
  23  Foreigners, stateless persons and asylum
      Foreigners hold the same rights except those reserved to citizens, and asylum is granted for persecution.
  24  National minorities
      Minorities keep their language, culture and religion, and forced assimilation is prohibited.
  25  Remedy and reparation
      Every infringed right has a remedy, and the State pays for the unlawful acts of its agents.
  26  Life
      Life is inviolable and the death penalty cannot be brought back, including in war.
  27  Integrity of the person
      Torture is prohibited, and a statement obtained by it counts for nothing.
  28  Liberty and security of the person
      Arrest requires law, a judge within forty-eight hours, and counsel from the first hour.
  29  Fair trial and defence
      A public hearing before an independent court, with defence, interpretation and appeal.
  30  Legality of offences and penalties
      No offence and no penalty without a law in force at the time, and no penalty without review.
  31  Inviolability of the home
      The home is entered only on a judicial warrant, except in pursuit or danger to life.
  32  Correspondence and personal data
      Correspondence is secret, and data collected without a legal basis must be erased.
  33  Freedom of movement
      Movement is free, and the exit visa and the internal residence permit are abolished.
  34  Conscience and religion
      Conscience and religion are free, and objection to armed service carries a civil alternative.
  35  Expression and information
      Expression is free, censorship is prohibited, and insulting the State is no offence.
  36  Media
      No State monopoly over the press, and journalists may protect their sources.
  37  Assembly
      Assembly needs no prior permission, and force is used only once it stops being peaceful.
  38  Association
      Associations form freely and only a court may dissolve one.
  39  Petition and access to the organs
      Anyone may petition a public body and must receive a reasoned reply.
  40  Suffrage
      Universal suffrage from eighteen, with no turnout condition on any result.
  41  Access to public office
      Public office is open on equal terms, and office holders publish their interests and assets.
  42  Referendum and popular initiative
      Referendum and popular initiative, with rights placed outside their reach.
  43  Participation in local affairs
      Residents take part in local decisions, and a harmful installation needs a public hearing first.
  44  Work
      The right to work, protection against dismissal, and a floor and ceiling on income.
  45  Conditions of labour
      Forty hours, paid leave, safe conditions, and no employment of children in school.
  46  Strike
      The right to strike, including in the public service, with the lock-out prohibited.
  47  Trade unions
      Unions form freely and stay independent of the State, the parties and management.
  48  Self-management in the enterprise
      The working collective elects a council which appoints the director and decides the enterprise plan.
  49  Housing
      An adequate dwelling, allocated by need, with no eviction without a judicial decision.
  50  Health
      Health care free at the point of use, financed from the plan.
  51  Education
      Education free at every level, compulsory for at least nine years, with self-governing universities.
  52  Social security
      Social security in old age, sickness, disability and unemployment, adjusted to prices.
  53  Family and children
      The family is protected, children born outside marriage have equal rights, and children are heard.
  54  Culture, science and creation
      Cultural life is open to all, and knowledge made with public funds is published.
  55  Environment
      An environment which does not injure health, with information and an assessment before projects.
  56  Consumption and public services
      Users get safety, truthful information, repairable goods and a way to complain.
  57  Duties
      Obey the law, pay according to means, defend the territory, and preserve the environment.

TITLE III: THE ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL ORDER (articles 58 to 90)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  58  Objectives
      The economy serves needs, full employment, equality between districts, and the environment.
  59  Nature of the plan
      A five-year plan, revised annually, adopted as a law and binding on public bodies.
  60  Preparation of the plan
      The plan is negotiated between enterprises, communes, districts and the Office of the Plan.
  61  Content and accounts of the plan
      The plan is stated in money and in physical units, with balances for energy, water and labour.
  62  Effect of the plan on enterprises
      An enterprise which agreed a task performs it, and is released if its inputs fail to arrive.
  63  Districts and communes in the plan
      Districts and communes plan too, and transfers follow published criteria.
  64  The enterprise in social ownership
      The enterprise is a person in law whose assets belong to the whole people.
  65  Establishment and dissolution
      Enterprises are founded, merged and dissolved by the organ that created them, after consultation.
  66  Accounts and audit
      Accounts are published annually, audited, and open to questions from residents.
  67  Losses and support
      Losses fall on the enterprise, with support possible from the founding organ.
  68  Public establishments
      Health, education, water, post, rail and electricity stay in public hands and out of profit.
  69  Private enterprise
      Private firms in listed trades, under an employee ceiling and the same standards as everyone else.
  70  Cooperatives and agriculture
      Land is worked by cooperatives, public farms and family holdings under a size ceiling.
  71  Sole and principal suppliers
      A sole or principal supplier may not dictate terms, and price-fixing agreements are void.
  72  The budget
      An annual budget covering all revenue and expenditure, with nothing spent outside it.
  73  Taxation
      Taxes only by law, progressive, and never below the subsistence income.
  74  Public borrowing
      Borrowing only by law, only for investment, and foreign lenders only by a two-thirds vote.
  75  The Bank of the Republic
      The Bank issues the marek, answers to the Assembly, and may not fund a budget deficit.
  76  Credit to enterprises
      Enterprises borrow from the Bank on published conditions and in the order of the plan.
  77  Savings and household credit
      Household savings are guaranteed, and credit is for dwellings, study and small enterprise.
  78  Prices
      Some prices are fixed by the Government, and shortage is met by rationing for a limited period.
  79  Incomes
      Incomes are set by the workers' council within statutory limits and an annual national agreement.
  80  Employment
      A public employment service, advance notice of redundancy, and benefit without forced acceptance.
  81  Concessions over natural resources
      Natural resources are used under a public, revocable concession with restoration conditions.
  82  Spatial planning
      Land use follows the commune plan, and building on floodplains and unstable slopes is prohibited.
  83  Expropriation
      Property is taken only for a public purpose, on full compensation, never for a private firm.
  84  Protection of the environment and of resources
      Annual publication of the state of air, water, soil and forests, with polluters paying for repair.
  85  Energy and water
      Electricity, water and heating stay socially owned, and water is never cut off for non-payment.
  86  Foreign trade
      Bloc trade by agreement, other trade under a public licence.
  87  Trade agreements
      Trade agreements are ratified and published, and reserved activities stay closed to them.
  88  Foreign investment
      Foreign participation only under licence, with land, dwellings, press and banks excluded.
  89  Foreign exchange
      All foreign exchange passes through one external account, with quarterly publication.
  90  Assistance to other peoples
      Assistance to peoples in want, creating no debt and carrying no political condition.

TITLE IV: THE ASSEMBLY OF THE REPUBLIC (articles 91 to 110)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  91  Nature of the Assembly
      One chamber, representing the people, sitting in permanent session.
  92  Composition
      One hundred and twenty members for four years, half in constituencies and half from lists.
  93  Candidature
      Anyone entitled to vote may stand, campaign funds are published, and foreign money is prohibited.
  94  Incompatibility
      The seat is incompatible with judicial office, the armed forces and the direction of an enterprise.
  95  Mandate
      Members take no instruction, are not liable for their votes, and are protected against pretextual prosecution.
  96  Filling of vacancies
      List seats pass to the next candidate, constituency seats to a by-election.
  97  Sittings and publicity
      Sittings are public, and every vote is published by member within forty-eight hours.
  98  Officers and organisation
      The Assembly elects its officers, adopts its own rules, and gives budget scrutiny to the opposition.
  99  Committees
      Committees may summon ministers and directors, who must appear and answer.
 100  Committees of inquiry
      One third of the members can force an inquiry with the powers of a court.
 101  Quorum and majorities
      A majority present, a majority voting, and organic laws by a majority of all members.
 102  Laws and organic laws
      Organic laws cover the listed matters and go to the Constitutional Court before promulgation.
 103  Initiative
      Members, the Government, district councils and citizens may propose laws, with costs stated.
 104  Procedure
      Committee, then general vote, then article by article, with unconnected amendments inadmissible.
 105  Promulgation and publication
      The President promulgates within fifteen days, may return a law once, or may refer it to the Court.
 106  Delegated legislation
      The Assembly may delegate law-making narrowly, never over rights, taxes or offences.
 107  Powers of the Assembly
      The Assembly adopts the plan and budget, ratifies treaties, and elects the Prime Minister.
 108  Term
      Four years, with extension only during emergency or attack and only by two thirds.
 109  Dissolution
      The President may dissolve on the Government's proposal, and the Assembly may dissolve itself.
 110  Continuity
      A dissolved Assembly handles current business only, through a proportional standing committee.

TITLE V: THE PRESIDENT OF THE REPUBLIC (articles 111 to 117)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 111  Office
      The President represents the Republic and exercises no function of government.
 112  Election
      Directly elected for four years, two terms at most, by a majority of votes cast.
 113  Incompatibility and oath
      The office is incompatible with every other, and begins with an oath before the Assembly.
 114  Functions
      Promulgation, appointments, dissolution, treaties, command of the forces, and pardons.
 115  Countersignature
      Most presidential acts need the Prime Minister's countersignature; six do not.
 116  Vacancy and substitution
      In a vacancy the President of the Assembly acts, with a new election within sixty days.
 117  Responsibility
      The President answers for a grave breach of the Constitution before the Constitutional Court.

TITLE VI: THE GOVERNMENT (articles 118 to 130)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 118  Composition and function
      The Prime Minister and ministers conduct policy and answer to the Assembly together.
 119  Formation
      The Assembly elects the Prime Minister, who proposes the ministers for appointment.
 120  Censure
      Censure must name a successor, so a Government falls only when another can form.
 121  Resignation and continuation
      A defeated or resigned Government handles current affairs and appoints to nothing.
 122  Incompatibility and conduct of ministers
      Ministers hold no other office, publish their assets, and wait two years before related employment.
 123  Acts of the Government
      Regulations execute laws and may not add to them, and a court may annul one.
 124  Ministries and their number
      The number and business of ministries is fixed by law, and agencies answer to a committee.
 125  The Office of the Plan
      The Office of the Plan prepares the plan and publishes the data behind it.
 126  Duty to inform
      Written answers in thirty days, an oral question sitting each week, and contracts published.
 127  Principles of the administration
      The administration acts on law, gives reasons, and silence counts as refusal.
 128  Public service
      Recruitment by competition, no party test, and protection for refusing an unlawful order.
 129  Delegation and contracting
      Authority over persons is never contracted out, and awards are made by public procedure.
 130  Territorial administration
      State services in the districts execute national law and leave local decisions alone.

TITLE VII: LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT (articles 131 to 141)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 131  Units of local self-government
      Every place belongs to one commune and one district, and a merger needs a local referendum.
 132  Competence of the commune
      The commune decides what is local and receives the resources for what it is given.
 133  Competence of the district
      The district runs second-level schools, hospitals, roads and transport between communes.
 134  Organs of the commune
      An elected council and a directly elected mayor, who can be removed by referendum.
 135  Organs of the district
      The district council elects its president, who executes its decisions.
 136  Participation of residents
      An open commune assembly twice a year, a binding local referendum, and published accounts.
 137  Resources
      Local units have their own revenue and equalising transfers on published criteria.
 138  Property of local units
      Local property serves local use, and property in public use cannot be sold.
 139  Supervision
      The Government checks lawfulness only, and a suspension must go to court in thirty days.
 140  Cooperation
      Communes may cooperate without creating a further tier of government.
 141  Defence of local self-government
      A commune or district may go to the Constitutional Court to defend its competence.

TITLE VIII: THE COURTS (articles 142 to 156)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 142  Judicial power
      Courts are independent, no one instructs them, and special courts are prohibited.
 143  Access to justice
      A court is open to everyone, with fees remitted and legal assistance for those without means.
 144  Publicity
      Hearings are public and judgments are published.
 145  The courts
      Communal, district, appeal, supreme and administrative courts, set out by organic law.
 146  Lay participation
      Serious criminal cases are heard with lay judges drawn by lot from the voters' register.
 147  Enforcement
      Public bodies must execute judgments, and a court may penalise the officer who refuses.
 148  Status of judges
      Judges are appointed indefinitely and removed only by the Judicial Council on statutory grounds.
 149  The Judicial Council
      Fifteen members: eight judges, four elected by the Assembly, three residents drawn by lot.
 150  Selection and training
      Judges are chosen by public competition, and promotion criteria are published.
 151  The public prosecution
      Prosecutors follow the law and the evidence, and no instruction may be given in a single case.
 152  Investigation
      Warrants for intrusive measures, counsel from the start, and unconstitutional evidence excluded.
 153  Composition
      Nine judges for nine years: three from the Assembly, three from the President, three from the Supreme Court.
 154  Jurisdiction
      Review of laws and treaties, competence disputes, party dissolution, and individual applications.
 155  Individual application
      Anyone may apply after other remedies, free of charge and without a lawyer.
 156  Effects of decisions
      An unconstitutional provision ends on publication, and a conviction founded on it is reopened.

TITLE IX: INDEPENDENT BODIES (articles 157 to 164)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 157  Independence
      The independent bodies take no instruction and answer to the Assembly alone.
 158  Reports and access
      Each reports annually in public and may demand documents from any public body.
 159  Function
      The Court of Accounts audits the budget, the plan and every user of public funds.
 160  Composition and powers
      Nine members with the independence of judges, able to order repayment.
 161  Function
      The Electoral Commission runs elections and may not classify a party by its programme.
 162  Composition
      Seven members, a permanent staff, and a public count at the polling station.
 163  Function
      The Commission on Information and Data decides access appeals and supervises data registers.
 164  Composition
      Five members, with legal and technical qualifications required.

TITLE X: DEFENCE, PUBLIC ORDER AND EMERGENCY (articles 165 to 173)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 165  The armed forces
      A professional force with a reserve, never used against the population.
 166  Command and control
      The Government directs defence, and a committee supervises the forces and intelligence.
 167  Limits
      No forces abroad without a two-thirds law, and no foreign force stationed here.
 168  Intelligence
      Intelligence looks outward, and intrusive measures need a designated Supreme Court judge.
 169  The police
      Police act with least force, wear visible numbers, and are investigated by an outside body.
 170  Declaration
      The Government declares an emergency and the Assembly must confirm it within forty-eight hours.
 171  Measures
      Emergency measures are limited, and a list of rights is never suspended.
 172  Limits on the organs
      No dissolution, no constitutional revision, and courts keep sitting throughout an emergency.
 173  Armed attack
      On armed attack the Assembly decides within a day, and a capitulation is void.

TITLE XI: REVISION OF THE CONSTITUTION (articles 174 to 178)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 174  Initiative
      Revision may be proposed by a third of members, the Government, or citizens.
 175  Procedure
      Two readings by two thirds, then a referendum on demand, then an appended Amendment.
 176  Limits
      The republic, suffrage, pluralism and the ban on torture and the death penalty are unrevisable.
 177  Review by the Constitutional Court
      The Constitutional Court checks a revision against those limits before the second reading.
 178  Consolidation and publication
      A consolidated text is published within sixty days, free and without registration.

TITLE XII: FINAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS (articles 179 to 186)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 179  Entry into force
      In force from 11 September 1994, replacing the Constitution of 1947.
 180  Existing law
      Old law survives where it conforms, and expires in 1996 where it does not.
 181  First elections
      First elections within one hundred and twenty days, local elections within a year.
 182  Constitution of the new organs
      The new organs are constituted within a year, with staggered terms drawn by lot.
 183  The former State Protection Service
      The State Protection Service is dissolved, its archives preserved, its directors barred from office.
 184  Property and enterprises
      Social ownership continues, and privatisation needs a two-thirds law.
 185  Continuity of obligations
      Treaties survive where they conform, and pensions and savings are honoured.
 186  Numbering of the Amendments
      Amendments are numbered in sequence and state the articles they change.

ARTICLES ADDED BY AMENDMENT (articles 187 to 195)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
 187  The Defender of Rights
      The Defender of Rights examines complaints against any public body, free and without a lawyer.
 188  Powers of the Defender
      The Defender may enter places of detention unannounced and take cases to the Constitutional Court.
 189  Reports of the Defender
      The Defender reports annually and publishes every recommendation that was refused.
 190  Review of performance
      Each enterprise is reviewed annually in public against its ceiling, its tasks and its accounts.
 191  Support
      Support is public, capped, limited to three years, and names the people who granted it.
 192  Reorganisation and dissolution
      Where recovery fails, the enterprise is reorganised or dissolved without breaking provision.
 193  Ecological budgets
      Ecological budgets in physical units bind the plan, and an excess is deducted from the next period.
 194  The Ecological Budget Office
      The Ecological Budget Office sets the sustainable levels and verifies the accounts on the premises.
 195  The list of free provision
      A list of goods free at the point of use, extended each plan period and never reduced.
