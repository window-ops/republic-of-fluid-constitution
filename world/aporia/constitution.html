Constitution of the Free Republic of Reformed Luces Aporia

Adopted 11 September 1994 · 186 articles in twelve titles · nine articles added by Amendment · fifteen Amendments in force

This is the text in force. Each Amendment is printed after the body of the Constitution and states the articles it changes. Where an Amendment and an original article conflict, the Amendment governs under Article 178(3). References to other articles are linked, and each article lists the articles that refer to it.

Preamble

The people of Reformed Luces Aporia, having brought to an end the rule of a single party over the offices of the State, having endured the Stoppage of 1990 to 1993 and the exhaustion of the Renewal before it, and resolved that the wealth of the country shall be held in common and governed by consent, adopt this Constitution.

We preserve the social ownership won in 1946 and abandon the direction of it by decree. The land, the water and the air of this country belong to those who come after us as much as to us. We remain among the peoples who made the union at Lindoma, and we seek peace with every state that will have peace with us.

Title I · Fundamental principles

Articles 1 to 20

1

The Republic

A free, unitary, democratic socialist republic, with two official names of equal force.

  1. 1.Reformed Luces Aporia is a free, sovereign, unitary and indivisible republic.
  2. 2.Its official name shall be the Free Republic of Reformed Luces Aporia. The short name Reformed Luces Aporia shall have equal force in law.
  3. 3.The Republic is a democratic socialist republic, founded on political pluralism, on the social ownership of the principal means of production, and on the rule of law.
  4. 4.The republican form of the State shall not be revised.
2

Sovereignty

Power belongs to the people, and taking it outside these rules is a crime without a time limit.

  1. 1.Sovereignty belongs to the people and is exercised in the forms established by this Constitution.
  2. 2.The people exercise sovereignty through the Assembly of the Republic, through referendum and popular initiative, through the organs of local self-government, and through the bodies drawn by lot in the cases this Constitution provides.
  3. 3.No party, association, organ or person shall claim sovereignty or exercise public power outside the procedures of this Constitution. The assumption of power outside those procedures is usurpation, no obedience shall be owed to its author, and prosecution for it is subject to no time limit.
3

Supremacy of the Constitution

The Constitution outranks every other rule, and Title II rights work in court on their own.

  1. 1.This Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic and binds every organ of the State, every unit of local self-government and every holder of public function.
  2. 2.A law, decree, regulation or administrative act which conflicts with this Constitution has no effect, and only the conflicting part of it fails.
  3. 3.The rights declared in Title II may be invoked before any court without implementing legislation.
4

Legality and publicity of the law

Public power needs a legal basis, and an unpublished rule binds nobody.

  1. 1.Public power shall be exercised only on a legal basis and within its limits.
  2. 2.A law, decree or regulation shall enter into force only on publication in the Official Gazette, and no sooner than the fifteenth day after publication except where the law itself provides a shorter period for reasons stated in it.
  3. 3.Secret laws, secret decrees and secret regulations are prohibited. An act not published shall bind nobody.
  4. 4.The retroactive operation of a law is prohibited, except where it is favourable to the person concerned.

5

Political pluralism

Parties are free and none may be given a leading role in the State or the economy.

  1. 1.Parties, trade unions, cooperative federations and associations shall be formed freely, shall govern themselves democratically, and shall publish their statutes and their accounts.
  2. 2.No party shall be accorded a leading role in the State, in the economy, in the armed forces, in education or in any enterprise, and no such role shall be established by law.
  3. 3.Membership of a party shall not be a condition of appointment to public office, of admission to education, or of employment.
  4. 4.A party may be dissolved only by judgment of the Constitutional Court, on proof that it pursues by force the abolition of democratic government or of the equal dignity of persons, and the judgment shall state the acts proved.

6

Prohibition of political policing

No file on a person for their opinions, and the old political police cannot return under a new name.

  1. 1.No organ of the State shall collect, hold or process information on a person by reason of that person's opinions, party membership, trade union activity, religion or lawful association.
  2. 2.Bodies exercising the functions of the former State Protection Service are abolished and shall not be re-established under any name.
  3. 3.The archives of that Service pass to the National Archive. Every person shall have access to the file kept on them and to the identity of those who informed on them, subject only to the protection of persons still living from danger to life.
  4. 4.Surveillance of communications and of the person shall be conducted only by judicial warrant, for an offence defined by law, and for a stated period.

7

Human dignity

Dignity cannot be violated, and no emergency or majority permits it.

  1. 1.Human dignity is inviolable and is the foundation of the rights declared in this Constitution.
  2. 2.No emergency, no economic plan, no majority and no international obligation shall authorise its violation.
8

Territory

The territory cannot be ceded or leased, the capital is Sluland, and the sea corridor stays open.

  1. 1.The territory of the Republic comprises its land, inland waters, territorial sea, seabed, subsoil and airspace.
  2. 2.The territory is inalienable. No part of it shall be ceded, leased to the exclusive use of a foreign power, or made the site of a foreign military installation.
  3. 3.The capital of the Republic is Sluland. The seat of an organ of the State may be fixed elsewhere by law.
  4. 4.The Republic maintains the maritime corridor to the Republic of Fluid as a permanent way of exchange, and shall keep it open in peace and in war.
9

Language, symbols and the calendar

Aporean is the official language, minority languages are used with it, and the national day is 11 September.

  1. 1.The official language of the Republic is Aporean. The law shall provide for the use of the languages of national minorities before the organs of the State, in education and in the courts of the districts in which they are spoken.
  2. 2.The flag, the arms and the anthem of the Republic shall be established by organic law.
  3. 3.The eleventh of September, the day of the adoption of this Constitution, is the national day. The first of May and the eighteenth of March, the day of the liberation of 1946, are days of the Republic.
10

Citizenship

Citizenship is set by organic law, and no citizen may be stripped of it or expelled.

  1. 1.Citizenship shall be acquired and lost according to organic law.
  2. 2.No citizen shall be deprived of citizenship, expelled from the territory, or surrendered to a foreign state for an offence of a political character.
  3. 3.A citizen of a member state of the union to which Article 18 refers shall enter, reside, work and receive care in the Republic on the footing of a citizen of the Republic.
11

Economic order

Social ownership in three forms, with private firms allowed under a ceiling on employees.

  1. 1.The economy of the Republic rests on the social ownership of the principal means of production, on the enterprise of the working collective, and on planning by agreement between enterprises, districts and the organs of the State.
  2. 2.Social ownership takes three forms: property of the whole people, administered by public establishments, meaning bodies created by law to provide a service to the population; the enterprise of the working collective, which its members administer for as long as they work in it; and the property of the districts and communes.
  3. 3.Private enterprise is lawful in the trades, in agriculture, in repair, in catering, in transport of goods, in cultural production and in such other activities listed by law, subject to a ceiling of employed persons fixed by organic law and to the same taxes, standards and conditions of labour as apply to enterprises in social ownership.
  4. 4.Land, watercourses, aquifers, coastline, seabed, forests, mineral deposits and the radio spectrum are the property of the whole people. They shall not be alienated, and their use shall be granted only as a revocable and conditional concession.
  5. 5.The employment of persons for the appropriation of their surplus labour beyond the ceiling established under paragraph 3 is prohibited, and no market shall be established in the means of production.

12

Currency, prices and the direction of policy

The marek is issued by the Bank alone, and the range of goods allocated by money is to shrink.

  1. 1.The monetary unit of the Republic is the marek. It shall be issued by the Bank of the Republic alone.
  2. 2.The Bank of the Republic shall be accountable to the Assembly of the Republic, which shall appoint and may remove its Council. The Bank shall not lend to a foreign state, and shall not accept an instruction from a foreign body.
  3. 3.Prices in the socially owned sector shall be established by agreement between producing and using enterprises within the framework of the plan, and the law shall list the goods and services whose prices the Government fixes.
  4. 4.Housing, health care, education at every level, water, the care of children and of the aged, and public transport shall be provided free of charge at the point of use, and shall be met from the plan.
  5. 5.It is a standing objective of the Republic to reduce the range of goods and services allocated by money and to extend the range provided free of charge, at the pace that the productive capacity of the country allows. The Assembly of the Republic shall report on the progress of this objective every four years.

13

Labour

Labour is the ground of every claim on the product, and forced labour is prohibited.

  1. 1.Labour is the source of the wealth of the Republic and the ground of the claim of each person on its product.
  2. 2.Forced labour is prohibited. The direction of a person to a place of work against their will is prohibited.
  3. 3.Every working collective shall govern the enterprise in which it works, elect and remove its management, and decide on the disposal of the enterprise's revenues within the limits of the law and of the plan.
  4. 4.Income shall correspond to labour performed. Income derived from the bare holding of a productive asset is prohibited.
14

The natural environment

The State protects the land, water, air and coast, and repairs the damage done to them.

  1. 1.The Republic shall protect the land, the water, the air, the coast and the living species of the territory, and shall repair the damage done to them.
  2. 2.The State, the districts and the enterprises shall conduct their activity with regard to the capacity of the natural environment to sustain it.
  3. 3.Every person has the duty to preserve the natural environment and the right to information on its condition.
15

Secularity

The Republic adopts no religion and religious communities govern themselves.

  1. 1.The Republic adopts no religion. Religious communities are separate from the State, are equal before it, and shall govern themselves.
  2. 2.No public office shall carry a religious qualification, and no religious qualification shall be required for the exercise of a right.
  3. 3.Public education shall transmit verified knowledge and the methods by which it is tested.
16

Local self-government

Communes and districts govern themselves, and local matters belong to the commune.

  1. 1.The territory is divided into communes and districts, which govern themselves through elected councils.
  2. 2.Every matter of local concern falls to the commune, except where this Constitution or an organic law confers it on the district or on the State.
  3. 3.The resources of the communes and districts shall correspond to the functions assigned to them.
17

Organs of the Republic and their separation

The Assembly legislates, the Government executes, the courts judge, and four bodies stand outside all three.

  1. 1.Legislative power belongs to the Assembly of the Republic, executive power to the Government, and judicial power to the courts.
  2. 2.The President of the Republic represents the Republic and exercises the functions this Constitution confers.
  3. 3.The Constitutional Court, the Court of Accounts, the Electoral Commission and the Commission on Information and Data exercise their functions independently of the Government.
  4. 4.An organ shall not assume a function conferred on another, and a function shall not be delegated except on the authority of this Constitution.
18

International relations

Founding member of the Bloc, with treaties binding only after ratification and publication.

  1. 1.The Republic is a founding member of the Democratic Socialist Bloc, established at Lindoma in 1994, and shall remain a member so long as that union rests on social ownership, ecological limits, democratic self-government and the equality of its members.
  2. 2.A treaty shall bind the Republic only on ratification by the Assembly of the Republic and publication, and shall then have the force of law and prevail over a contrary provision of a law.
  3. 3.The Republic shall not conclude a treaty that submits it to a foreign or arbitral jurisdiction in a dispute with a company, that permits a foreign military installation, or that restricts the amendment of this Constitution.
  4. 4.The Republic renounces war as an instrument of policy, recognises no acquisition of territory obtained by force, and grants asylum to persons persecuted for their opinions, their origin or their activity in defence of rights.

Armed forces and public order

The armed forces obey the civil power and may not be used against a lawful assembly.

  1. 1.The armed forces are subordinate to the civil power, serve the defence of the territory, and shall not be employed in a political dispute or against a lawful assembly.
  2. 2.A member of the armed forces or of the police shall not belong to a party while in service.
  3. 3.The Republic shall neither possess nor accept on its territory a nuclear, chemical or biological weapon.
  4. 4.The armed forces shall act beyond the frontier only on a law adopted by two thirds of the members of the Assembly of the Republic.

Access to public information

Anyone may ask for information held by a public body, and a refusal must be reasoned and appealable.

  1. 1.Every person has the right of access to information held by an organ of the State, by a district, by a commune or by an enterprise in social ownership.
  2. 2.Refusal shall be reasoned, shall rest on a ground stated in an organic law, and shall be subject to appeal to a court.
  3. 3.The plan, the budget, the accounts of enterprises in social ownership, and the register of concessions granted under Article 11(4) shall be published.

Title II · Rights, liberties and duties

Articles 21 to 57

Chapter 1: General provisions

21

Equality

Everyone is equal before the law, and hereditary titles are abolished.

  1. 1.All persons are equal before the law and are entitled to its equal protection.
  2. 2.No person shall be favoured or prejudiced by reason of origin, nationality, language, sex, birth, age, disability, health, property, occupation, religion, opinion, party membership or family situation.
  3. 3.Measures adopted to remove an established inequality shall not be held contrary to this Article.
  4. 4.Privileges of birth, hereditary titles and hereditary offices are abolished.
22

Scope and limitation of rights

A right may be limited only by law, only where necessary, and never in its essential content.

  1. 1.The rights declared in this Title bind the Assembly of the Republic, the Government, the administration, the courts, the districts, the communes and the enterprises.
  2. 2.A right may be limited only by a law, only where the limitation is necessary for the protection of another right or of a public interest stated in this Constitution, and only to the extent required.
  3. 3.A limitation shall be general in its terms and shall not touch the essential content of the right.
  4. 4.Where a right is claimed to have been infringed, the organ concerned shall bear the burden of showing that the conditions of paragraph 2 are satisfied.
23

Foreigners, stateless persons and asylum

Foreigners hold the same rights except those reserved to citizens, and asylum is granted for persecution.

  1. 1.Foreigners and stateless persons lawfully in the territory enjoy the rights declared in this Title, except those which this Constitution reserves to citizens.
  2. 2.Asylum shall be granted to a person persecuted for their opinions, their origin, their belonging to a group, or their activity in defence of rights.
  3. 3.Extradition shall be refused where the offence is of a political character, where the person would face the death penalty, or where they would face torture or an inhuman punishment.
  4. 4.A person shall not be expelled to a territory in which their life or liberty would be in danger.
24

National minorities

Minorities keep their language, culture and religion, and forced assimilation is prohibited.

  1. 1.Persons belonging to a national minority have the right to preserve their language, their culture and their religion, to be taught in their language, and to use it before the organs of the State in the districts in which they reside in significant numbers.
  2. 2.The law shall provide for their representation in the councils of those districts.
  3. 3.Assimilation by measure of the State is prohibited.

25

Remedy and reparation

Every infringed right has a remedy, and the State pays for the unlawful acts of its agents.

  1. 1.Every person whose right has been infringed has the right to a remedy before a court, decided within a reasonable time.
  2. 2.The State shall make reparation for damage caused by the unlawful act of a public organ or of its agents, and shall recover from the agent at fault.
  3. 3.A person unlawfully deprived of liberty or convicted in error has the right to compensation and to the public rectification of the record.

Chapter 2: Life, integrity and liberty

26

Life

Life is inviolable and the death penalty cannot be brought back, including in war.

  1. 1.The life of the person is inviolable.
  2. 2.The death penalty is abolished and shall not be re-established, including in time of war.
27

Integrity of the person

Torture is prohibited, and a statement obtained by it counts for nothing.

  1. 1.The physical and mental integrity of the person is inviolable.
  2. 2.Torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment are prohibited.
  3. 3.A medical or scientific intervention on a person requires their free and informed consent, given in the forms laid down by law.
  4. 4.A statement obtained by torture, by threat or by deception shall be inadmissible in any proceeding.
Cited by152
28

Liberty and security of the person

Arrest requires law, a judge within forty-eight hours, and counsel from the first hour.

  1. 1.No person shall be deprived of liberty except in the cases and by the procedure established by law.
  2. 2.A person arrested shall be informed at once of the reasons and of their rights, and shall be brought before a judge within forty-eight hours, failing which they shall be released.
  3. 3.Detention before judgment shall be exceptional, shall rest on a reasoned judicial decision, and shall be subject to a maximum fixed by law.
  4. 4.Every person deprived of liberty has the right to inform a person of their choice, to counsel from the first hour, and to a medical examination.
  5. 5.Detention for debt is prohibited. Detention on the ground of illness, poverty, homelessness or want of employment is prohibited.
29

Fair trial and defence

A public hearing before an independent court, with defence, interpretation and appeal.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to a hearing in public before an independent and impartial court established by law.
  2. 2.The accused shall be presumed innocent until convicted by a final judgment.
  3. 3.The accused has the right to be informed of the charge, to time and facilities for defence, to counsel provided free of charge where they lack means, to interpretation, to examine witnesses, and to appeal.
  4. 4.No person shall be tried twice for the same act, nor compelled to testify against themselves or against a member of their family.
  5. 5.Extraordinary courts are prohibited. A civilian shall not be tried by a military court.

30

Legality of offences and penalties

No offence and no penalty without a law in force at the time, and no penalty without review.

  1. 1.An act shall be punished only where it constituted an offence defined by law at the time of its commission, and only with the penalty then prescribed.
  2. 2.A penalty shall be proportionate to the offence. Penalties shall serve the protection of society and the restoration of the person, and no penalty shall be perpetual and without review.
  3. 3.A penalty shall not extend to the family of the convicted person, and shall not entail the general forfeiture of goods.
  4. 4.Deprivation of liberty shall not entail the loss of the right to vote.
31

Inviolability of the home

The home is entered only on a judicial warrant, except in pursuit or danger to life.

  1. 1.The home is inviolable. Entry, search or seizure requires a reasoned judicial warrant, except in pursuit of a person taken in the act or in case of danger to life.
  2. 2.A search shall be conducted in the presence of the occupier or of witnesses, and a record shall be given to them.

32

Correspondence and personal data

Correspondence is secret, and data collected without a legal basis must be erased.

  1. 1.The secrecy of correspondence and of communication is guaranteed. Interception requires a reasoned judicial warrant, for a stated offence and a stated period.
  2. 2.Every person has the right to know what data on them is held by an organ of the State or by an enterprise, to obtain the correction of error, and to obtain the erasure of data collected without a legal basis.
  3. 3.Data collected for one purpose shall not be used for another, and the general numbering of persons across unconnected registers is prohibited.

33

Freedom of movement

Movement is free, and the exit visa and the internal residence permit are abolished.

  1. 1.Every person lawfully in the territory has the right to move within it and to choose their residence.
  2. 2.Every citizen has the right to leave the territory and to return to it. A passport shall be refused only by reasoned decision subject to appeal to a court.
  3. 3.The exit visa and the internal permit of residence are abolished and shall not be re-established.

Chapter 3: Conscience, expression and association

34

Conscience and religion

Conscience and religion are free, and objection to armed service carries a civil alternative.

  1. 1.Freedom of conscience, of religion and of belief is guaranteed, in private and in public, alone and in community.
  2. 2.No person shall be required to declare their religion or belief.
  3. 3.Conscientious objection to armed service is recognised, and the law shall provide a civil service of equal duration.

35

Expression and information

Expression is free, censorship is prohibited, and insulting the State is no offence.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to express and to publish their opinions, and to seek and receive information.
  2. 2.Censorship is prohibited. A publication shall not be seized or a broadcast suspended except by judicial decision.
  3. 3.Liability for expression shall arise only after publication and only in the cases the law defines. Insult to the State, to its symbols or to the holder of an office shall not be an offence.
  4. 4.Scientific research and artistic creation are free.
36

Media

No State monopoly over the press, and journalists may protect their sources.

  1. 1.A monopoly of the State over the press, broadcasting or telecommunication is prohibited.
  2. 2.The ownership and the funding of a medium shall be public, and the law shall prevent the concentration of media in few hands.
  3. 3.The public broadcasting service shall be independent of the Government, shall be governed by a council appointed by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds, and shall give access to the parties and associations on equal terms.
  4. 4.Journalists have the right to protect their sources and shall not be compelled to disclose them.
37

Assembly

Assembly needs no prior permission, and force is used only once it stops being peaceful.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to assemble peacefully and without arms, in public or in private, without prior authorisation.
  2. 2.Notice may be required for an assembly on a public way, and a failure of notice shall not of itself render the assembly unlawful.
  3. 3.An assembly shall be dispersed only where it has ceased to be peaceful, by decision of the responsible authority, and by the least force sufficient.
38

Association

Associations form freely and only a court may dissolve one.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to form associations and to join them or abstain from joining them.
  2. 2.An association becomes a person in law by registration, so that it may own property, make contracts and appear in court in its own name. Registration which shall be refused only by reasoned decision subject to appeal to a court.
  3. 3.An association shall be dissolved only by judicial decision.
  4. 4.Secret associations and associations of a military character are prohibited.
39

Petition and access to the organs

Anyone may petition a public body and must receive a reasoned reply.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to address a petition to any organ of the State and to receive a reasoned reply within the period laid down by law.
  2. 2.Every person has the right to be heard before a decision of the administration affecting them, and to receive the reasons for it in writing.

Chapter 4: Political rights

40

Suffrage

Universal suffrage from eighteen, with no turnout condition on any result.

  1. 1.Every citizen of eighteen years has the right to vote and to stand for election.
  2. 2.The vote is universal, equal, direct, secret and free. Voting is not compulsory.
  3. 3.No condition of turnout shall be attached to the validity of an election or of a referendum.
  4. 4.Residents who are not citizens shall vote and stand in elections to the councils of the commune after three years of lawful residence.
41

Access to public office

Public office is open on equal terms, and office holders publish their interests and assets.

  1. 1.Every citizen has the right of access to public office on conditions of equality, by competition or by election.
  2. 2.Appointment and promotion in the administration shall be made on merit established by public procedure.
  3. 3.The holder of a public office shall declare their interests and their assets, and the declaration shall be published.
42

Referendum and popular initiative

Referendum and popular initiative, with rights placed outside their reach.

  1. 1.A referendum shall be held on the demand of the Assembly of the Republic, of one third of its members, or of a number of citizens fixed by organic law.
  2. 2.A number of citizens fixed by organic law may lay a proposal of law before the Assembly of the Republic, which shall consider it and vote on it within six months.
  3. 3.A referendum shall not be held on a matter that would abolish a right declared in this Title.

43

Participation in local affairs

Residents take part in local decisions, and a harmful installation needs a public hearing first.

  1. 1.Residents have the right to participate in the decisions of their commune, through the assembly of the commune, through consultation on plans of land use, and through the local referendum.
  2. 2.A decision to site an installation harmful to health or to the environment shall be taken after a public hearing in the commune concerned, whose opinion shall be published together with the decision.

Chapter 5: Economic and social rights

44

Work

The right to work, protection against dismissal, and a floor and ceiling on income.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to work, to choose their occupation, and to be assisted by the State in obtaining employment corresponding to their qualification.
  2. 2.Dismissal shall be permitted only on a ground defined by law and shall be subject to appeal to a court.
  3. 3.Dismissal on the ground of trade union activity, of the exercise of a right, or of pregnancy is void.
  4. 4.Equal work is paid equally, and the law shall fix a minimum income and a maximum ratio between the highest and the lowest income in any enterprise.

45

Conditions of labour

Forty hours, paid leave, safe conditions, and no employment of children in school.

  1. 1.The working week shall not exceed forty hours. The law shall reduce it as the productivity of labour allows.
  2. 2.Every worker has the right to a daily and a weekly rest, to paid annual leave of no fewer than twenty-four working days, and to public holidays.
  3. 3.Every worker has the right to conditions of work that protect their health, to information on the hazards of their work, and to withdraw from work presenting a serious and immediate danger without loss of income.
  4. 4.The employment of children below the age at which compulsory education ends is prohibited.
46

Strike

The right to strike, including in the public service, with the lock-out prohibited.

  1. 1.The right to strike is guaranteed to every worker, including in enterprises in social ownership and in the public service.
  2. 2.The law shall provide for a minimum service in health, in water, in electricity and in emergency communication, and that minimum service shall not be set at a level which makes the strike ineffective.
  3. 3.The closure of a workplace by its management in answer to a strike, called a lock-out, is prohibited. A worker shall not be dismissed or penalised for participation in a lawful strike.

47

Trade unions

Unions form freely and stay independent of the State, the parties and management.

  1. 1.Workers have the right to form trade unions freely, to join the union of their choice, and to be represented by it.
  2. 2.Trade unions shall be independent of the State, of the parties and of the management of enterprises.
  3. 3.Collective agreements concluded between unions and enterprises or their associations have the force of law between the parties, and the law shall provide for their extension to a branch.
48

Self-management in the enterprise

The working collective elects a council which appoints the director and decides the enterprise plan.

  1. 1.The working collective of an enterprise in social ownership shall constitute a workers' council, elected by secret ballot from among its members.
  2. 2.The council shall adopt the statute of the enterprise, appoint and remove its director, decide on the plan of the enterprise, and dispose of its revenues within the limits of the law.
  3. 3.In an enterprise in private ownership employing more than the number of persons fixed by organic law, the workers shall elect a committee entitled to information on the accounts of the enterprise and to consultation before a change in the organisation of work.

49

Housing

An adequate dwelling, allocated by need, with no eviction without a judicial decision.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to a dwelling adequate to the size of their household, secure in tenure, and served with water, sanitation, heating and electricity.
  2. 2.Dwellings shall be allocated from the public stock according to need, on criteria published by the commune, in the order of a register open to inspection by the applicants.
  3. 3.A person shall not be evicted from their dwelling without a judicial decision and without the offer of alternative accommodation.
  4. 4.The purchase of dwellings for letting at a profit, and the holding of dwellings vacant, shall be limited by law, and the commune may take a vacant dwelling into the public stock on compensation.

50

Health

Health care free at the point of use, financed from the plan.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to the protection of their health and to care provided free of charge at the point of use by the public health service.
  2. 2.The public health service shall be general, shall serve the whole territory, and shall be financed from the plan.
  3. 3.Preventive medicine, the medicine of work and the health of mothers and children are duties of the State.
  4. 4.A charge shall not be imposed for treatment, for a medicine on the list established by law, or for an examination.
51

Education

Education free at every level, compulsory for at least nine years, with self-governing universities.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to education. Education is free of charge at every level, including higher education, and in the establishments of the State alone.
  2. 2.Education is compulsory to the age fixed by law and shall not be shorter than nine years.
  3. 3.Higher education shall be open on the basis of ability established by public examination.

Title VII · Local self-government

Articles 131 to 141

131

Units of local self-government

Every place belongs to one commune and one district, and a merger needs a local referendum.

  1. 1.The units of local self-government are the commune and the district. Every part of the territory belongs to one commune and one district.
  2. 2.A commune is created, merged or abolished by law, after a referendum in the population concerned, whose result the Assembly of the Republic shall follow.
  3. 3.The seat and the boundaries of a district are established by law.
132

Competence of the commune

The commune decides what is local and receives the resources for what it is given.

  1. 1.The commune decides every matter of local concern which this Constitution or an organic law has not assigned to the district or to the Republic.
  2. 2.The commune is responsible for the spatial plan, the local roads, water and sanitation, the collection of waste, the public stock of dwellings, the schools of the first level, the care of children and of the aged, the local cultural institutions and the public spaces.
  3. 3.A function may be assigned to the commune by law only together with the resources to perform it.
133

Competence of the district

The district runs second-level schools, hospitals, roads and transport between communes.

  1. 1.The district is responsible for the schools of the second level, the hospitals of the district, the roads of the district, public transport between communes, the plan of the district, and the coordination of the communes within it.
  2. 2.The district shall not review the merits of a decision taken by a commune within the commune's competence.
134

Organs of the commune

An elected council and a directly elected mayor, who can be removed by referendum.

  1. 1.The commune is governed by a council elected for four years by direct universal suffrage, and by a mayor elected in the same manner and for the same term.
  2. 2.The mayor executes the decisions of the council, directs the administration of the commune, and answers to the council, which may put questions and require documents.
  3. 3.The council adopts the budget, the spatial plan and the local regulations, and the mayor shall not adopt them in its place.
  4. 4.The mayor may be removed before the end of the term by a referendum held on the demand of the council by a majority of its members or on the demand of a number of residents fixed by organic law.
135

Organs of the district

The district council elects its president, who executes its decisions.

  1. 1.The district is governed by a council elected for four years by direct universal suffrage, which elects its president from among its members.
  2. 2.The president of the district executes the decisions of the council and answers to it.
136

Participation of residents

An open commune assembly twice a year, a binding local referendum, and published accounts.

  1. 1.The commune assembly, open to all residents, meets at least twice a year, receives the accounts of the mayor, and may put any matter within the competence of the commune to the council, which shall consider it and reply in public sitting.
  2. 2.A local referendum is held on the demand of a number of residents fixed by organic law, and its result binds the council in a matter within the competence of the commune.
  3. 3.The sittings of the council and of the district council are public, and their decisions, budgets and contracts are published.
137

Resources

Local units have their own revenue and equalising transfers on published criteria.

  1. 1.Communes and districts have their own revenue, consisting of local taxes and charges established within the limits of the law, of the revenue of their property and enterprises, and of transfers from the plan.
  2. 2.Transfers are distributed by published criteria which take account of population, of the cost of service in the territory, and of the difference in resources between units, so as to reduce that difference.
  3. 3.A commune or a district may borrow only for investment, within the limit fixed by law, and with the approval of the Government where the limit is exceeded.
138

Property of local units

Local property serves local use, and property in public use cannot be sold.

  1. 1.Communes and districts own property, which serves their functions and the use of their residents.
  2. 2.Property serving a public use shall not be sold or mortgaged.
  3. 3.A commune or a district may establish an enterprise for a service to its residents, and the enterprise answers to the council.
139

Supervision

The Government checks lawfulness only, and a suspension must go to court in thirty days.

  1. 1.The Government supervises the lawfulness of the acts of communes and districts, and shall not supervise their expediency.
  2. 2.Where the Government considers an act unlawful, it may suspend it by a reasoned decision and shall apply within thirty days to the administrative court, which decides within ninety days. The suspension lapses if the application is not made.
  3. 3.A council may be dissolved only by a law, on the ground of a grave and persisting breach of the Constitution or of the law established by a judgment, and elections are held within sixty days.

Communes may cooperate without creating a further tier of government.

  1. 1.Communes may cooperate by agreement for the joint performance of their functions, and may create joint bodies, which answer to the councils that created them.
  2. 2.Cooperation between communes shall not create a further tier of government, and a joint body shall not be given a general competence.
141

Defence of local self-government

A commune or district may go to the Constitutional Court to defend its competence.

  1. 1.A commune or a district may apply to the Constitutional Court against an act of the Republic which infringes its competence or removes the resources corresponding to its functions.

Title VIII · The courts

Articles 142 to 156

Chapter 1: Principles

142

Judicial power

Courts are independent, no one instructs them, and special courts are prohibited.

  1. 1.Justice is administered in the name of the people by the courts, which are independent and subject only to the Constitution and the law.
  2. 2.An organ of the State, a party or a person shall not give an instruction to a court, and shall not comment on a pending case in a manner intended to influence it.
  3. 3.A court decision is reasoned, is pronounced in public, and is binding on the organs of the State.
  4. 4.Extraordinary and special courts are prohibited, and Article 29(5) governs.
143

Access to justice

A court is open to everyone, with fees remitted and legal assistance for those without means.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to bring a case before a court, and a right shall not be denied for want of a procedure.
  2. 2.Court fees shall be proportionate and shall be remitted for a person without means.
  3. 3.Legal assistance is provided free of charge to a person without means, in criminal matters and in matters concerning dwelling, employment, social security and family.
  4. 4.Proceedings are conducted in Aporean, and in the language of a national minority in the districts under Article 24, with interpretation provided free of charge.
144

Publicity

Hearings are public and judgments are published.

  1. 1.Hearings are public. A court may exclude the public by a reasoned decision to protect a minor, the private life of a party, or a witness in danger, and shall pronounce the judgment in public.
  2. 2.Judgments are published, with the names of natural parties removed where the law so provides.

145

The courts

Communal, district, appeal, supreme and administrative courts, set out by organic law.

  1. 1.The courts are the communal courts, the district courts, the courts of appeal, the Supreme Court, and the administrative courts.
  2. 2.The Supreme Court secures the uniform application of the law, decides appeals in the cases the law states, and publishes the principles it establishes.
  3. 3.The administrative courts decide disputes between persons and the administration, including the annulment of a regulation under Article 123(3).
  4. 4.The organisation, the seats and the jurisdiction of the courts are established by organic law.
146

Serious criminal cases are heard with lay judges drawn by lot from the voters' register.

  1. 1.In criminal proceedings for an offence punishable by more than five years of deprivation of liberty, the court sits with lay judges drawn by lot from the register of voters of the district.
  2. 2.Service as a lay judge is a public duty, is compensated, and is a ground of excused absence from work.
  3. 3.Lay judges deliberate and vote on the facts and on the penalty together with the professional judges.

Public bodies must execute judgments, and a court may penalise the officer who refuses.

  1. 1.The organs of the State shall execute a judgment addressed to them within the period it states.
  2. 2.Failure to execute a judgment is an offence, and the court may impose a periodic penalty on the organ and name the officer responsible.

Chapter 3: Emergency

170

Declaration

The Government declares an emergency and the Assembly must confirm it within forty-eight hours.

  1. 1.The Government may declare a state of emergency in the whole or a part of the territory in case of natural disaster, epidemic, grave breakdown of essential supply, or grave and imminent threat to public order.
  2. 2.The declaration states the reasons, the territory affected, the measures adopted and their duration, and takes effect on publication.
  3. 3.The declaration lapses unless the Assembly of the Republic confirms it within forty-eight hours by a majority of its members. Where the Assembly stands dissolved, its standing committee confirms it under Article 110(2).
  4. 4.A state of emergency is confirmed for no more than thirty days, and is extended only by a further decision of the Assembly for periods of no more than thirty days, each stating the reasons for the extension.

Measures

Emergency measures are limited, and a list of rights is never suspended.

  1. 1.Measures adopted during a state of emergency are limited to what the situation requires, are stated in the declaration, and cease on its expiry.
  2. 2.The following are not suspended in any circumstance: the right to life, the prohibition of torture, the prohibition of the death penalty, the right to a court and to counsel, the prohibition of retroactive criminal law, the freedom of conscience, the guarantees of Article 6, the right to a subsistence income and to shelter, and the supply of water and of care.
  3. 3.Measures shall not restrict the right to strike except in the services named in Article 46(2), and shall not dissolve a party, a trade union or an association.

  2. 2.The Constitution shall not be revised during a state of emergency.
  3. 3.The courts continue to sit, and every measure of emergency may be contested before them without delay.
  4. 4.The Government reports to the Assembly every seven days on the measures in force and on their effects, and the report is published.

On armed attack the Assembly decides within a day, and a capitulation is void.

  1. 1.Where the territory is subject to armed attack, the President of the Republic states the fact, and the Assembly of the Republic decides on the response within twenty-four hours.
  2. 2.Where the Assembly cannot meet, the Government orders the defence of the territory and reports to the Assembly at its first sitting.
  3. 3.A capitulation, an occupation or a cession of territory accepted by an organ of the Republic is void, and a duty of obedience does not arise from it.

  2. 2.The text adopted is published, and is submitted to a referendum where one third of the members of the Assembly, or a number of citizens fixed by organic law, so demand within sixty days of publication.

  1. 1.The plan is expressed in physical units for energy, materials, water, land, emissions, labour time and the useful life of goods, and in monetary values for the purposes of the budget and of prices.
  2. 2.Where a physical account and a monetary account point to different decisions, the physical account governs.
  3. 3.The plan includes balances for each resource named in paragraph 1, stated for the Republic and for each district, and states the stocks held at the opening and at the close of the period.
  4. 4.The data on which the balances rest are published in a form which permits the calculations to be repeated.
193

Ecological budgets

  1. 1.The Assembly of the Republic adopts by law, for each planning period, ecological budgets for the Republic, expressed in physical units, covering emissions of greenhouse gases, the abstraction of water, the extraction of materials, the conversion of land, and the discharge of substances harmful to health.
  2. 2.An ecological budget is set at the level which the natural systems of the territory can sustain, established by the Ecological Budget Office on published evidence, and shall not be set above that level for reasons of cost.
  3. 3.The plan, the budget of the Republic, the plans of the districts and the ceilings of enterprises conform to the ecological budgets in force. A provision which exceeds a budget has no effect, and any person or association may apply to the administrative court to establish the excess.
  4. 4.An ecological budget shall not be raised except on a finding by the Office that the sustainable level has itself changed. A budget once lowered shall not be raised on the ground of a failure to observe it.
  5. 5.Where a period closes with a budget exceeded, the excess is deducted from the budget of the following period.

The Ecological Budget Office

  1. 1.The Ecological Budget Office establishes the sustainable levels under Article 193(2), measures the use made of each budget, and publishes quarterly the position of each budget by branch and by district.
  2. 2.The Office consists of seven members elected by the Assembly of the Republic by a majority of two thirds for seven years, chosen from persons qualified in the natural sciences, in engineering or in the accounting of resources, and Articles 157 and 158 apply.
