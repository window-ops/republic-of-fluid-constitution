Preamble

The peoples of the five states of Iosmein, having declined in 1949 to enforce an embargo imposed by others upon a neighbour, having built their common wealth upon the social ownership won in the years that followed, and having resolved to govern themselves by consent and in peace with every state, adopt this Constitution.

We joined the union established at Lindoma in 1994 and remain in it. We record that between 2008 and 2016 the offices of the State contracted debts abroad which the people had not authorised and from which the people obtained no benefit, that the Republic defaulted upon those debts in 2017, and that the peoples of the Bloc restored provision in this country with their own labour and goods. The provisions of this Constitution which restrict external borrowing, require the publication of public accounts and establish the Public Integrity Office were adopted in response to that period, and they bind every holder of public function.

Title I · Fundamental principles

Articles 1 to 22

1

The Republic

A federal, democratic socialist republic of five states, with two official names of equal force.

  1. 1.Iosmein is a federal, sovereign and indivisible republic, formed by the union of its states.
  2. 2.Its official name is the Allied States of Iosmein. The short name Iosmein has equal force in law.
  3. 3.The Republic is a democratic socialist republic, founded on the social ownership of the principal means of production, on political pluralism and on the rule of law.
  4. 4.The federal and republican form of the State shall not be revised.
2

Sovereignty

Power belongs to the people, and seizing it outside these procedures is a crime with no time limit.

  1. 1.Sovereignty belongs to the people of the Allied States and is exercised in the forms this Constitution establishes.
  2. 2.The people exercise sovereignty through the Federal Assembly, through the President of the Republic, through referendum and popular initiative, through the assemblies of the states, and through the organs of local self-government.
  3. 3.Public power is exercised only through the procedures of this Constitution. The assumption of power outside those procedures is usurpation, no obedience is owed to its author, and prosecution for it is subject to no time limit.
3

Supremacy of the Constitution

The Constitution outranks every other rule, and only the conflicting part of an act fails.

  1. 1.This Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic and binds every federal organ, every state, every unit of local self-government and every holder of public function.
  2. 2.A law, decree, regulation, statute of a state or administrative act which conflicts with this Constitution has no effect, and only the conflicting part of it fails.
  3. 3.The rights declared in Title II may be invoked before any court without implementing legislation.
4

Legality and publicity of the law

Public power needs a legal basis, and an act that has not been published imposes no obligation.

  1. 1.Public power is exercised only on a legal basis and within its limits.
  2. 2.A federal law, decree or regulation enters into force on publication in the Federal Gazette, and no sooner than the fifteenth day after publication, except where the act itself provides a shorter period for reasons stated in it.
  3. 3.Secret laws, secret decrees and secret regulations are prohibited. An act that has not been published imposes no obligation on any person.
  4. 4.The retroactive operation of a law is prohibited, except where it is favourable to the person concerned.

5

Political pluralism

Parties are free, and no party may hold a leading role in the State or in the economy.

  1. 1.Parties, trade unions, cooperative federations and associations are formed freely, govern themselves democratically, and publish their statutes, their membership figures and their accounts.
  2. 2.No party holds a leading role in the State, in the economy, in the armed forces, in education or in any enterprise, and no such role is established by law.
  3. 3.Membership of a party is no condition of appointment to public office, of admission to education, or of employment.
  4. 4.A party is dissolved only by judgment of the Constitutional Court, on proof that it pursues by force the abolition of democratic government or of the equal dignity of persons, and the judgment states the acts proved.
6

Social ownership

Land, water, forests, energy and the principal means of production are held in social ownership.

  1. 1.The land, the subsoil, the waters, the forests, the sources of energy, the railway and telecommunication networks and the principal means of production are held in social ownership.
  2. 2.Social ownership is administered by enterprises, cooperatives, states and units of local self-government, on the terms and for the purposes a federal law establishes.
  3. 3.Property in the categories named in paragraph 1 is inalienable. Other social property is transferred only under a law adopted by two thirds of the members of each chamber, by public procedure, and with the price and the counterparty published.
  4. 4.Personal property, the dwelling occupied by its holder, savings, and the tools of a trade are guaranteed.

Territory

The territory cannot be ceded or leased, the capital is Talmeru, and the Valmeza crossing stays open.

  1. 1.The territory of the Republic comprises its land, inland waters, territorial sea, seabed, subsoil and airspace.
  2. 2.The territory is inalienable. No part of it is ceded, leased to the exclusive use of a foreign power, or made the site of a foreign military installation.
  3. 3.The capital of the Republic is Talmeru. The seat of an organ of the State may be fixed elsewhere by law.
  4. 4.The eastern frontier crossing at Valmeza, which faces the Fluidian gateway at Corvaluri and has been open since 1989, is kept open for movement and exchange in peace and in war.

8

The states

Five states, each with an assembly, a government and courts, and boundaries changed only by referendum.

  1. 1.The Republic is formed of the states of Vetruna, Osreni, Kadrena, Velmar and Sarnovein.
  2. 2.Each state has an assembly elected by its residents, a government answerable to that assembly, and courts of first and second instance, constituted under its own statute and in conformity with this Constitution.
  3. 3.The statute of a state enters into force on approval by the Federal Assembly. Approval is refused only for conflict with this Constitution, and the refusal states the conflicting provision.
  4. 4.The boundaries of a state are altered only by a law adopted by two thirds of the members of each chamber and confirmed by referendum in each state concerned.
  5. 5.Talmeru and its district are administered under federal law. Their residents elect a council of the district and are represented in both chambers on the footing of the residents of a state.

9

Competences of the Republic

The competences the Republic holds, and the rule that unassigned competences belong to it.

  1. 1.The Republic exercises the competences of foreign relations, defence, citizenship, frontier control, currency and banking, the federal budget and federal taxation, the planning of the economy, external exchange and external borrowing, the criminal law and its procedure, the civil and commercial law, the register of enterprises, the energy and railway networks, weights, measures and technical standards, the census and the public statistics, and the supervision of public integrity.
  2. 2.A competence which this Constitution assigns to no organ belongs to the Republic.
  3. 3.A competence of the Republic is exercised by a state where a federal law delegates it, and the delegation states its purpose, its duration and the resources that accompany it.
10

Competences of the states

What the states decide for themselves, and the rule that a state borrows only from the Republic.

  1. 1.A state exercises the competences of local administration, of urban and rural planning within its territory, of the state courts, of primary and secondary schooling within the federal framework, of local transport, of water supply and of housing management, and those a federal law delegates to it.
  2. 2.A state legislates within its competences, and its law is void in so far as it conflicts with a federal law.
  3. 3.A state raises the charges a federal law authorises. It levies no other charge.
  4. 4.A state contracts a loan only from the Republic. Article 20 applies to an obligation of a state to a lender outside the Democratic Socialist Bloc.

Federal supervision

How the Republic acts when a state fails to apply federal law, and the limits on that action.

  1. 1.Where a state organ fails to apply this Constitution or a federal law, the Federal Government requires it to remedy the failure, stating the provision concerned and a period for compliance.
  2. 2.Where the failure persists, the Federal Assembly may authorise the Federal Government to exercise the competence concerned for a stated period of no more than two years, renewable once by a further authorisation. The authorisation names the officers who assume the competence and the acts that gave rise to it.
  3. 3.The state concerned may contest the measure before the Constitutional Court. The contest does not suspend the measure, and the Court decides within sixty days.
  4. 4.Supervision under this Article dissolves no state assembly, postpones no election in a state, and suspends no right declared in Title II.
  5. 5.A measure under this Article is published in full, with the reasons for it, on the day it is taken.

Equality of the states

The states are equal, and federal spending is distributed to equalise provision between them.

  1. 1.The states are equal before this Constitution and equal in the chamber of the states.
  2. 2.Federal expenditure is distributed so that provision in the categories named in Title III reaches an equivalent level in every state, and the accounts of that distribution are published by state each year.
  3. 3.No state receives a privilege in law, in the plan or in the budget by reason of its population, its output or the party governing it.

Language

Iosmen is official throughout, and a state language is official beside it within that state.

  1. 1.Iosmen is the official language of the Republic.
  2. 2.A language named in the statute of a state is official beside Iosmen within that state, in its assembly, its administration and its courts.
  3. 3.Every organ deals with a person in a language that person understands, and provides interpretation and translation without charge.
  4. 4.Federal laws are published in Iosmen and in each state language, and the versions have equal force.

Citizenship

One citizenship, no deprivation of it, and Bloc residents treated on the footing of residents.

  1. 1.There is a single citizenship of the Republic. The states confer no citizenship of their own.
  2. 2.No person is deprived of citizenship, and no act of an organ renders a person stateless.
  3. 3.Citizenship is acquired and renounced on the conditions a federal law establishes, applied without distinction on any of the grounds named in Title II.
  4. 4.A resident of another member state of the Democratic Socialist Bloc enters, remains, works, studies and receives care in the Republic on the footing of a resident of the Republic.

15

Belief

No established religion, freedom of belief, and public money for a religious building only as heritage.

  1. 1.The Republic establishes no religion, and its organs are separate from religious communities.
  2. 2.Freedom of belief, of practice and of change of belief is guaranteed, and no privilege or disadvantage follows in law from a belief held or refused.
  3. 3.Public funds are applied to a religious community only for the conservation of a building entered in the register of protected monuments, and the grant is published.
16

Public integrity

Public office is exercised for its purpose alone, declarations are published, and corruption carries no time limit.

  1. 1.A public function is exercised for the purpose for which it is conferred, and for no private advantage of the holder or of a person connected with the holder.
  2. 2.Every holder of public function declares, on taking office, each year in office and on leaving office, the assets, income, interests and obligations of the holder and of the members of the holder's household. The declarations are published.
  3. 3.Corruption in public function, and the diversion of public funds, are offences defined by federal law. No amnesty, pardon or commutation applies to them, and prosecution for them is subject to no time limit.
  4. 4.A holder of public function is removed on a finding of the Public Integrity Office under Title IX, subject to appeal to the courts.
  5. 5.A person who reports conduct falling under this Article is protected in employment, in tenure of office and in person, and the protection continues regardless of the outcome of the report.

Publicity

Sittings, contracts and accounts are published, and an unpublished contract binds no public body.

  1. 1.The sittings of the Federal Assembly, of a state assembly and of a local council are public, and their records are published.
  2. 2.A contract to which a public body is party is published in full, with the counterparty, the price, the procedure followed and the officers who approved it. A contract that has not been published creates no obligation on the public body.
  3. 3.Every person has access to the documents held by an organ. A refusal states its reasons, cites the provision relied on and is reviewable by the courts.
  4. 4.The accounts of the Republic, of each state and of each public enterprise are published in money and in physical units.

18

The Democratic Socialist Bloc

Founding member of the Bloc, on conditions, with Bloc acts taking effect only on enactment here.

  1. 1.The Republic is a founding member of the Democratic Socialist Bloc, established at Lindoma in 1994.
  2. 2.The Republic remains in the union for as long as the union rests on social ownership, ecological limits, democratic self-government and the equality of its members.
  3. 3.An act of an organ of the Bloc takes effect in the Republic on enactment by the Federal Assembly, except where Title XII provides otherwise for the duration of the rescue settlement.
  4. 4.Frontiers between member states are open, and the Republic concludes and maintains the agreements that keep them so.
  5. 5.Exchange within the Bloc is settled in goods, labour, technique and knowledge, recorded in physical units through a single external account.
19

Foreign relations

No military alliance, no unpublished treaty annex, and no submission to arbitration against a company.

  1. 1.The Republic is a member of no military alliance and permits no foreign military installation on its territory.
  2. 2.The Republic recognises no territorial acquisition obtained by force.
  3. 3.A treaty is ratified by a law of the Federal Assembly and published in full before ratification. An annex, protocol or understanding that has not been published imposes no obligation on the Republic.
  4. 4.No treaty submits the Republic to a foreign or arbitral jurisdiction in a dispute with a company, requires that a sector be opened to enterprises operating for profit, or restricts the revision of this Constitution. A commitment of that kind is void.
20

External borrowing

Borrowing outside the Bloc needs a two-thirds law and a referendum, and a breach creates no debt.

  1. 1.The Republic, a state, a unit of local self-government, a public enterprise and the Bank of the Republic contract no loan, credit, guarantee or other obligation from a lender outside the Democratic Socialist Bloc except under a law adopted by two thirds of the members of each chamber and confirmed by referendum.
  2. 2.The law submitted to referendum states the sum, the lender, the term, the charge, the purpose and the schedule of repayment. A loan differing from those particulars is void.
  3. 3.An obligation contracted in breach of this Article creates no debt of the Republic, of a state or of any public body, and the officers who contracted it are personally liable.
  4. 4.The Republic accepts no financial conditionality relating to its internal affairs, the form of its government, its ownership settlement or its provision under Title III.
  5. 5.Title XII applies to the obligations contracted between 2008 and 2016 and to the settlement made upon them.

Ecological limits

Resource use is capped in physical units, and exhausting a resource beyond recovery is prohibited.

  1. 1.The use of land, water, air, forest, fishery and mineral resources is subject to ceilings set in physical units by federal law and published each planning period.
  2. 2.An enterprise, a state and a unit of local self-government operate within the ceilings applying to them, and report their use in physical units.
  3. 3.Resources are used so that their availability to the generations to come is preserved, and an act that exhausts a resource beyond its recovery is prohibited.

Symbols

The flag, the arms, the seal and the anthem are described by an organic law.

  1. 1.The flag, the arms, the seal and the anthem of the Republic are described by an organic law.
  2. 2.A state uses its own flag and arms beside those of the Republic.

Title II · Rights, liberties and duties

Articles 23 to 74

Chapter I: General provisions

23

Persons protected

Who holds these rights, whom they bind, and what a person keeps while deprived of liberty.

  1. 1.The rights declared in this Title are held by every person in the territory of the Republic, except where a provision confines a right to citizens.
  2. 2.These rights bind every federal organ, every state, every unit of local self-government, every court and every public enterprise.
  3. 3.A right declared in this Title applies between private persons to the extent that its subject matter allows.
  4. 4.A person deprived of liberty keeps every right this Title declares, apart from those the deprivation itself removes.
24

Equality

Equality before the law, a list of prohibited grounds, and the conditions for advancement measures.

  1. 1.All persons are equal before the law and are entitled to its equal protection.
  2. 2.No person is favoured or disadvantaged by reason of ancestry, sex, colour, language, state of residence, place of birth, belief, political opinion, trade union activity, property, family circumstances, health, disability, age or sexual orientation.
  3. 3.A measure to advance a group which a published report shows to be disadvantaged is permitted where it serves equality, goes no further than that purpose requires and states the period for which it runs.
  4. 4.Women and men have equal rights in the family, in work, in remuneration, in education and in public office.

25

Enforcement of the rights

Civil rights work in court on their own, and social rights work to the extent of resources available.

  1. 1.The rights declared in Chapters II and III are enforceable before any court without implementing legislation.
  2. 2.The rights declared in Chapter IV are enforceable to the extent of the resources available to the Republic. Each of them has a content, established under Title III, which no law and no plan may reduce.
  3. 3.Where a court finds that the content described in paragraph 2 has been reduced, it says so, states what resources were available, and orders the organ concerned to restore the level within a stated period.
  4. 4.The Republic publishes each year the level attained for each right declared in Chapter IV, by state, in physical units and in money.
  5. 5.The organ which relies on a shortage of resources proves the shortage.
26

Limitation of rights

A right is limited only by law, only so far as the purpose requires, and its essential content stays intact.

  1. 1.A right is limited only by a law, only for a purpose this Constitution recognises, and only to the extent that the purpose requires.
  2. 2.A limitation leaves the essential content of the right intact.
  3. 3.A law which limits a right is general in its terms, names the right limited and states the purpose.
  4. 4.Articles 30, 31, 35 and 41 admit no limitation, and no emergency suspends them.

Remedy

Every infringed right has a remedy, free where means are short, and delay is itself compensable.

  1. 1.Every person whose right is infringed has a remedy before the courts.
  2. 2.Court fees and legal assistance are provided without charge to a person whose means are insufficient, and the insufficiency is established by declaration.
  3. 3.A person may apply to the Constitutional Court where the ordinary remedies have been exhausted, or where they would give no effective relief.
  4. 4.Proceedings are decided within a reasonable time, and delay attributable to the courts or to the administration is itself a ground of compensation.

Liability of public bodies

Public bodies answer for damage, recover from officers who acted with intent, and compensate wrongful conviction.

  1. 1.The Republic, a state, a unit of local self-government and a public enterprise answer for damage their organs cause in the exercise of public function.
  2. 2.The public body recovers the sum from the officer who acted with intent or with gross negligence, by proceedings which are public.
  3. 3.A person convicted in error or detained unlawfully is compensated, and the compensation is not conditional on proof of fault in any officer.

Other instruments

These rights are read with the ratified instruments, and the wider protection prevails.

  1. 1.The rights declared in this Title are read consistently with the international instruments the Republic has ratified.

Chapter II: Personal rights and liberties

30

Life

The right to life, the death penalty abolished, and no removal to a state that would impose it.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to life.
  2. 2.The death penalty is abolished and shall not be reintroduced.
  3. 3.No person is extradited, expelled or otherwise removed to a state where that person would face the death penalty, torture, or treatment contrary to Article 31.

31

Integrity of the person

Physical and mental integrity, an absolute bar on torture, consent to treatment, and no forced labour.

  1. 1.The physical and mental integrity of the person is inviolable.
  2. 2.Torture, and inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment, are prohibited. No emergency, no order of a superior and no purpose of investigation justifies them.
  3. 3.A medical or scientific intervention is carried out with the free and informed consent of the person, and a federal law states the conditions in which consent is given for a person unable to give it.
  4. 4.Forced labour is prohibited. Work required of a sentenced person, service required during a declared emergency and civic service under Article 73 fall outside this prohibition.
  5. 5.The human body and its parts are not sold, and no person takes a profit from their transfer.

32

Liberty and security

Deprivation of liberty only by law, reasons given at once, a judge within forty-eight hours.

  1. 1.A person is deprived of liberty only in the cases a federal law defines and by the procedure it establishes.
  2. 2.A person arrested is informed at the moment of arrest, in a language that person understands, of the reasons for the arrest and of the right to a lawyer, to a doctor and to inform a person of their choice.
  3. 3.A person arrested is brought before a judge within forty-eight hours, and the judge orders release or detention by a reasoned decision.
  4. 4.Detention before judgment is exceptional, is reviewed every thirty days, and lasts no longer than the maximum a federal law sets for the offence charged.
  5. 5.Every place of detention is entered in a public register, and detention in a place outside that register is an offence for which no time limit runs.
33

Review of detention

A detained person, or another acting for them, may seek review within seventy-two hours, without a fee.

  1. 1.A person deprived of liberty, and any other person acting for them, may apply to a court for the review of the lawfulness of the detention.
  2. 2.The court hears the application within seventy-two hours and orders release where the detention has no lawful basis.
  3. 3.The application is made in any form, and no fee is charged for it.
34

Fair hearing

A public hearing before an independent court, equal access to the file, and reasons in the judgment.

  1. 1.Every person is entitled to a public hearing, within a reasonable time, before a court established by law, independent and impartial.
  2. 2.A hearing is closed only by a reasoned decision, for the protection of a child, of a victim of a sexual offence or of a witness in danger, and the judgment is pronounced publicly in every case.
  3. 3.The parties have equal access to the file, to the evidence and to the means of presenting their case.
  4. 4.A judgment states the reasons on which it rests, and an appeal lies to a higher court.
  5. 5.Proceedings are conducted in a language the party understands, with interpretation and translation provided without charge.

35

Limits of the criminal law

No offence without a prior law, no double punishment, and evidence from a breach of rights is inadmissible.

  1. 1.An act is punished only where a law in force at the time of the act defined it as an offence and stated the penalty.
  2. 2.A criminal law does not operate retroactively. A law which removes an offence or reduces a penalty applies to acts committed before it.
  3. 3.No person is tried or punished twice for the same act.
  4. 4.A penalty is personal, and no penalty of a perpetual character is imposed.
  5. 5.Every person is presumed innocent until a final judgment establishes guilt. The burden of proof rests on the prosecution, and a doubt is resolved in favour of the accused.
  6. 6.Evidence obtained by torture, by unlawful intrusion into the home or the communications of a person, or by any other breach of this Title, is inadmissible in any proceedings.
36

Rights of the defence

Notice of the accusation, a lawyer, confidential communication, and equal treatment of witnesses.

  1. 1.A person charged is informed promptly and in detail of the accusation, and is given the time and the facilities to prepare a defence.
  2. 2.A person charged chooses a lawyer, and a lawyer is provided without charge where the means of the person are insufficient.
  3. 3.Communication between a person and their lawyer is confidential, and no organ intercepts it.
  4. 4.A person charged examines the witnesses against them and obtains the attendance of witnesses on their behalf on the same conditions.
  5. 5.No person is compelled to give evidence against themselves or against a member of their household.
37

Private and family life

Private and family life, equal partners, equal children, and separation only by a court.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to respect for their private and family life.
  2. 2.Marriage and partnership are entered into with the free consent of the persons concerned, who are equal in rights and duties.
  3. 3.Children born outside marriage have the same rights as children born within it, and no document of the State records the distinction.
  4. 4.A child is separated from their family only by a decision of a court, on grounds a federal law defines, and the poverty of the household is no such ground.
  5. 5.The identity of a person, including the recorded sex and the name, is corrected on the application of the person, on the conditions a federal law establishes.
38

The home

The home is inviolable, entry needs a warrant, and entry without one is reviewed within a day.

  1. 1.The home is inviolable.
  2. 2.Entry, search or seizure is carried out on a judicial warrant which names the place, the object of the search and the period of its validity.
  3. 3.Entry without a warrant is lawful only to assist a person in danger, to stop an offence in progress, or on the consent of the occupant recorded in writing. A judge reviews the entry within twenty-four hours.
  4. 4.No entry takes place at night, except in the cases named in paragraph 3.
39

Correspondence and communications

Communications are secret, interception needs a judge, and blanket collection is prohibited.

  1. 1.Correspondence, telephone communication and electronic communication are secret.
  2. 2.Interception is ordered by a judge, for an offence a federal law names, for a stated period of no more than sixty days, renewable by a further reasoned order.
  3. 3.General or indiscriminate collection of communications or of traffic data is prohibited.
  4. 4.A person whose communications were intercepted is informed once the investigation closes, and where the investigation produced no charge, within one year of the end of the interception.
40

Personal data

Data are collected for a stated purpose, seen and corrected on request, and decisions are taken by a person.

  1. 1.Every person has the right to the protection of data relating to them.
  2. 2.Data are collected for a stated purpose, used for that purpose alone, kept for a stated period and then destroyed.
  3. 3.Every person has access to the data an organ or an enterprise holds on them, learns the source of the data and obtains the correction or the deletion of data which are inaccurate or held without basis.
  4. 4.A decision producing a legal effect on a person is taken by a person, and an automated process serves only to prepare it.
  5. 5.Data revealing belief, political opinion, trade union membership, health, sexual life or ethnic origin are processed only with the consent of the person concerned, or for medical care, or for statistics in a form which identifies no individual.
41

Prohibition of political policing

No file on a person for their opinions, the Directorate abolished, and its archives opened.

  1. 1.No organ collects, holds or processes information on a person by reason of the opinions of that person, of party membership, of trade union activity, of belief or of lawful association.
  2. 2.Bodies exercising the functions of the former Directorate of State Security are abolished and are re-established under no name.
  3. 3.The archives of that Directorate pass to the National Archive. Every person has access to the file kept on them and to the identity of those who informed on them, subject only to the protection from danger to life of persons still living.
  4. 4.A person who held a position in that Directorate is named in a public register, and a federal law states the offices from which such a person is barred and the period of the bar.

42

Movement and residence

Free movement and residence in any state, free exit and return, and no expulsion of a citizen.

  1. 1.Every person lawfully in the territory moves and settles freely within it, in any state, and no permit is required for residence in a state.
  2. 2.Every person leaves the territory freely and returns to it.
  3. 3.No citizen is expelled from the territory, and no citizen is deprived of the right to enter it.
  4. 4.Travel documents are issued on application and are refused only by a reasoned decision of a court.
  5. 5.The Republic maintains open frontiers with the member states of the Democratic Socialist Bloc, and seeks agreements to the same effect with other states.
43

Conscience and belief

Belief is held, changed and practised freely, is recorded by no organ, and its sincerity is not examined.

  1. 1.Every person holds, changes and practises a belief, alone or with others, in public or in private, and is free to hold none.
  2. 2.No person is required to declare a belief, and no organ records the belief of a person.
  3. 3.A person whose belief prevents the bearing of arms performs the civic service Article 73 provides.
44

Expression

Expression is free, censorship is prohibited, and insulting an office is an offence in no law.

  1. 1.Every person expresses and disseminates opinion and information freely, in any form and by any means.
  2. 2.Censorship is prohibited. No prior authorisation, deposit or licence is required for publication.
  3. 3.Liability for expression arises after publication alone, before the courts, and on grounds a federal law defines.

  1. 1.In an enterprise entered on the list a federal law establishes, the responsible ministry appoints the director from among the candidates the council has ranked under Article 95(1).
  2. 2.Where the ministry appoints a candidate other than the one ranked first, it publishes its reasons within seven days.
  3. 3.The list is reviewed by the Federal Assembly at each planning period, and an enterprise is entered on it only where the interruption of its output would affect the supply of energy, of water, of transport, of medicines or of food throughout the Republic.

Accounts

Quarterly accounts in money and physical units, and no allocation until they are published.

  1. 1.A public enterprise publishes its accounts each quarter, in money and in the physical units of Article 88.

  3. 3.An enterprise which fails to publish receives no allocation from the plan until it has published.

Support

Support is published with amounts, terms and names, and a ceiling on it is left to a law not yet passed.

  1. 1.Support from public funds to an enterprise is granted by a reasoned decision of the responsible ministry, published within seven days with the amount, the term, the conditions and the names of the officers who granted it.
  2. 2.The decision states the losses which occasioned the support and the measures by which the enterprise is to end them.
  3. 3.The Federal Planning Office reports each year on the support granted, enterprise by enterprise, and states which enterprises have received support in each of the preceding three years.
  4. 4.A federal law may fix a ceiling on support and a maximum period for which it is granted.

Reorganisation and dissolution

An enterprise that cannot meet its obligations is reorganised or dissolved after its council is heard.

  1. 1.An enterprise which cannot meet its obligations is reorganised or dissolved by a decision of the organ that established it, taken after the council has been heard.

Investment

Investment comes from the plan, retained funds and credit, and stays within the physical ceilings.

  1. 1.Investment is made from the plan, from the funds an enterprise retains and from credit granted by the Bank of the Republic.

Prices

Some prices are fixed by the plan and published with their costs, the rest form freely.

Enterprises of foreign origin

A foreign enterprise operates only under a licence stating the technique transferred and the forum.

Chapter IV: Public finance and currency

103

The federal budget

The budget is adopted before the year begins, holds every receipt, and defaults to monthly twelfths.

  1. 1.The federal budget is adopted each year by a law, before the year begins, and follows the plan.
  2. 2.The budget states every receipt and every expenditure. No fund is held outside it, apart from the social funds Article 62(2) establishes.
  3. 3.Where the budget is not adopted before the year begins, expenditure continues at one twelfth of the preceding budget each month, and no new expenditure is undertaken.
104

Budgets of the states

State budgets fit within the federal allocations and are checked before adoption.

  1. 1.Each state adopts its budget, within the allocations the federal plan makes to it and the charges Article 10(3) authorises.
  2. 2.A state budget is submitted to the Federal Ministry of Finance before adoption, which verifies its conformity with the plan and publishes its opinion.
  3. 3.Title XII applies to the supervision of state budgets for as long as it remains in force.
105

Taxation

Taxes only by law, progressive on income, and an exemption for a named person is void.

  1. 1.A tax is established, modified and abolished only by a federal law, which states the base, the rate, the person liable and the exemptions.
  2. 2.Taxation of income is progressive. Taxation of goods and services exempts the items on the list under Article 83.
  3. 3.An exemption granted to a named person or enterprise is void.
106

Currency

The vela is issued by the Bank alone, and obligations in a foreign currency are void.

  1. 1.The currency of the Republic is the vela, issued by the Bank of the Republic, which alone issues it.
  2. 2.Wages, pensions, prices in retail supply and obligations between persons in the Republic are expressed in vela.
  3. 3.An obligation expressed in the currency of another state, or indexed to it, is void, except where a federal law permits it for exchange with a state outside the Democratic Socialist Bloc.
  4. 4.The Republic joins no monetary union and adopts the currency of no other state.
107

The Bank of the Republic

The Bank issues the currency, holds public accounts, and reports to the Assembly every quarter.

  1. 1.The Bank of the Republic issues the currency, holds the accounts of the Republic and of the states, and grants credit within the plan.
  2. 2.Its governor is elected by the Federal Assembly by a majority of two thirds, for six years, and reports to it every three months in public.
  3. 3.The Bank grants credit to no person outside the public sector and the cooperatives, except through the credit cooperatives a federal law establishes.
  4. 4.The Bank holds no obligation of a foreign state or of a foreign bank, except the working balances the external account requires.
108

Internal debt

Internal borrowing needs a law, is published monthly, and external borrowing stays under Article 20.

  1. 1.The Republic borrows within the Republic by an issue which a law of the Federal Assembly authorises, stating the sum, the term and the charge.
  2. 2.The total of such borrowing outstanding is published each month, with the schedule of repayment.
  3. 3.Article 20 governs every obligation to a lender outside the Democratic Socialist Bloc.
109

The Court of Accounts

The Court of Accounts audits every public body, publishes in full, and refers offences to prosecution.

  1. 1.The Court of Accounts audits the accounts of the Republic, of the states, of the units of local self-government, of the public enterprises and of the social funds.
  2. 2.Its members are elected by the Federal Assembly by a majority of two thirds, for nine years, and are not re-elected.
  3. 3.The Court publishes every report in full and refers to the prosecution every matter which discloses an offence under Article 16(3).
  4. 4.The Court receives no instruction and is refused access to no document.
110

Public contracts

Public contracts follow a published procedure, and one concluded outside it is void.

  1. 1.A contract of a public body is concluded by public procedure, on published criteria, and the award states the reasons for the choice.
  2. 2.A contract concluded without the procedure is void, and the officers who concluded it are personally liable.
  3. 3.Article 17(2) governs the publication of every public contract.
  4. 4.A person who has been convicted of an offence under Article 16(3), and an enterprise a person so convicted directs, is party to no public contract for the period a federal law states.

111

The external account

One external account, published monthly, and no foreign bank account without published authorisation.

  1. 1.Exchange with other states passes through a single external account, held by the Bank of the Republic and published each month.
  2. 2.The account records exchange in physical units and in money, by counterparty and by category of goods.
  3. 3.No organ, enterprise or cooperative holds an account with a foreign bank, except with the authorisation of the Bank of the Republic, published with its reasons.

Exchange within the Bloc

Bloc exchange is settled in goods and knowledge, and no unit of settlement circulates at home.

  1. 1.Exchange with a member state of the Democratic Socialist Bloc is settled in goods, labour, technique and knowledge, recorded in physical units.
  2. 2.A unit of settlement is used only where a counterparty requires it. It does not circulate in the Republic and is held by no resident.
  3. 3.The balance of each settlement period is carried forward as agreed deliveries, and the schedule of the deliveries is published.

Exchange with other states

Trade outside the Bloc runs through licensed public enterprises, and no weapon is exported.

  1. 1.Exchange with a state outside the Bloc is conducted by the public enterprises a federal law authorises.
  2. 2.An export is licensed, and the licence states the quantity, the counterparty and the price.
  3. 3.The export of a good on the list under Article 83 is licensed only where the supply within the Republic has been met.
  4. 4.The Republic exports no weapon and no component of a weapon.

Technique and knowledge

Incoming technique enters the knowledge commons, and no exclusive right over knowledge is accepted.

  1. 1.An agreement which brings technique into the Republic states the terms on which the technique enters the knowledge commons.

Solidarity

Solidarity is given without conditions and received on the same terms, with both published.

Sessions

An eleven-month ordinary session, extraordinary sittings on demand, and published rules of procedure.

Each chamber elects and may dismiss its President, who directs its proceedings.

  1. 1.Each chamber elects its President and its other officers at the first sitting, by secret ballot, for the term of the chamber.
  2. 2.The President of a chamber directs its proceedings, represents it and is dismissed by the vote of a majority of its members.

Committees sit in public, and the accounts and integrity committees are chaired from outside the Government.

  1. 1.Each chamber forms standing committees, composed in proportion to the strength of the lists represented in it.
  2. 2.A committee sits in public, publishes its minutes and the documents put before it, and hears the persons whose activity it examines.
  3. 3.The chairmanship of the committee on public accounts and of the committee on public integrity belongs to a member who supports no party forming the Federal Government.

A quarter of either chamber can open an inquiry that summons persons and compels documents.

  3. 3.An organ, an enterprise and a holder of public function comply with a requirement of the committee, and the refusal to comply is an offence.

A majority present, votes recorded by name and published, and a secret ballot only for elections.

  2. 2.Voting is by open ballot and each vote is recorded by name and published. A secret ballot is used only for an election this Constitution assigns to a chamber.
  3. 3.A vote cast by a person other than the member is void, and the fact is published.

  1. 1.The Director of the Public Integrity Office is elected by the Federal Assembly in joint sitting, by a majority of two thirds, for six years, and is not re-elected.
  2. 2.The Judicial Council presents the candidates, after a public competition on published criteria, and publishes the ranking with its reasons. The Federal Assembly elects from among the candidates presented and from no other person.
  3. 3.The Director appoints the staff of the Office by competition under Article 172(1), and no post in the Office is filled by political appointment.
  4. 4.The Director has one deputy. For as long as Title XII remains in force, one further deputy is designated by the joint committee that title establishes, under Article 211, and that deputy has the powers Article 195(5) confers and no power to remove a holder of public function.
  5. 5.The Director reports to the Federal Assembly every six months, in public, and answers questions from the committee on public integrity.

The register of assets

Procedure and safeguards

The person is heard before a finding, may appeal to the Supreme Court, and the reporter stays anonymous.

Title X · Defence, public order and emergency

Articles 199 to 205

199

The armed forces

A standing professional force and the state militias, subordinate to the civil power.

  1. 1.The armed forces defend the territory, the independence and the population of the Republic.
  2. 2.The forces consist of a standing professional force and of the territorial militias of the states.
  3. 3.The strength of the standing force, its equipment and its budget are fixed each year by a law, and the budget is published in detail.
  4. 4.The forces are subordinate to the civil power. The President commands them under Article 153, and the Federal Assembly decides their employment in the cases this Title states.
  5. 5.A member of the forces holds every right declared in Title II, apart from the limits an organic law places on political activity while in service, and votes in every election.
200

The territorial militia

Each state trains a militia, employed for defence and rescue and barred from acting against an assembly or a strike.

  1. 1.Each state maintains a territorial militia, composed of residents who serve voluntarily and are trained in the state in which they live.
  2. 2.The militia is employed within the state for defence against an armed attack, for rescue after a disaster and for the protection of works of water, energy and transport.
  3. 3.On the declaration of an armed attack the militias pass under the command of the President, and they return to the command of the state on the day the declaration ends.
  4. 4.A militia is employed against an assembly, a demonstration or a strike in no circumstances, and an order to that effect is unlawful and is executed by no member.
  5. 5.The equipment of a militia is held in armouries the Federal Assembly lists, and its stocks are audited each year by the Court of Accounts.
201

Limits

No alliance, no foreign base, no surveillance of the population, and no defence in a superior's order.

  1. 1.Article 7(2) governs foreign military installations, and Article 113(4) governs the export of weapons and their components.
  2. 2.The Republic joins no military alliance and enters no obligation to place its forces at the disposal of another state.
  3. 3.A member of the forces obeys no order to commit an act contrary to Article 31(2), and the order confers no defence upon the person who executes it.
  4. 4.The forces conduct no surveillance of the population, and Article 41 applies to every service of the forces.
  5. 5.A person leaves the forces on giving the notice an organic law states, and the Republic holds no person in service beyond a declared armed attack.
202

Action beyond the frontier

Rescue and relief by majority law, and any other action abroad by two-thirds law and referendum.

  1. 1.The forces act beyond the frontier only for the rescue of persons in distress, for relief after a disaster, and for participation in a mission of peace established by a body of which the Republic is a member.
  2. 2.Rescue and relief are authorised by a law adopted by a majority of the members of each chamber. The authorisation states the object, the territory and the duration, and carries no mandate to use force except in self-defence.
  3. 3.Every other action beyond the frontier requires a law adopted by two thirds of the members of each chamber and the approval of a referendum.
  4. 4.The question put to referendum states the object, the territory, the number of persons deployed, the duration, the rules governing the use of force and the date on which the mandate expires. Article 51(4) governs its clarity.
  5. 5.A mandate expires on the date stated. Its renewal follows the whole procedure again.
  6. 6.Members of the forces serving beyond the frontier remain subject to the criminal law of the Republic and to its ordinary courts, and the Republic accepts no immunity from the courts of the territory in which the mission takes place.
203

Public order

Civilian police, identifiable officers, recorded use of force, and independent investigation of complaints.

  1. 1.Public order is maintained by police services which are civilian, are established by law and answer to the organ the law names.
  2. 2.An officer on duty is identifiable at all times by a number visible on the uniform, and an officer who conceals it commits a disciplinary offence.
  3. 3.Force is used only where it is necessary and in the degree the necessity requires. Every use of force is recorded, reported within twenty-four hours and published in aggregate each quarter.
  4. 4.A complaint against an officer is investigated by a body independent of the service concerned, whose findings are published and whose refusal to prosecute is reviewable by a court.
  5. 5.The armed forces are employed in the maintenance of public order only after a disaster, and only for rescue, supply and the clearing of routes.
204

State of emergency

The President declares an emergency, both chambers confirm it in seven days, and core rights stand.

  1. 1.A state of emergency is declared only upon a natural disaster, an epidemic, the failure of a system on which the supply of water, energy, food or health care depends, or a comparable event which the ordinary powers cannot meet.
  2. 2.The President declares the state of emergency by a decree which states the territory covered, the danger met, the measures authorised and the date on which it expires. The decree is published at once with the evidence relied on.
  3. 3.The decree is transmitted immediately to both chambers, to the Constitutional Court, to the Ombudsman and to the assemblies of the states covered. It lapses after seven days unless each chamber confirms it by a majority of its members.
  4. 4.Before confirmation the Constitutional Court finds whether the grounds in paragraph 1 exist, and decides within seventy-two hours. The Court publishes the finding.
  5. 5.A confirmed state of emergency runs for thirty days and is renewed only by the same procedure.
  6. 6.During a state of emergency the rights Article 26(4) protects are neither limited nor suspended, no election is postponed except under Article 120(3), no chamber is dissolved, no revision of this Constitution is adopted, and the courts sit without interruption.
  7. 7.For as long as Title XII remains in force, the declaration is notified to the joint committee under Article 211 on the day it is published.

205

Armed attack

On an armed attack the chambers meet within a day and review the measures every thirty days.

  1. 1.Where the territory is under armed attack, the President states the fact, orders the measures the defence requires and convenes both chambers, which meet within twenty-four hours.
  2. 2.The Federal Assembly decides on the response by a law adopted by a majority of the members of each chamber, and reviews the measures taken every thirty days in public sitting.
  3. 3.Where a chamber cannot meet, the measures continue in force and are submitted to it on the first day it is able to sit.
  4. 4.Paragraph 6 of Article 204 applies during an armed attack, and Article 120(3) governs any extension of a term.
  5. 5.The Republic recognises no capitulation which cedes territory. An act of that kind is void and imposes no obligation on the Republic.

Title XI · Revision of the constitution

Articles 206 to 208

206

Initiative and procedure

Revision needs two thirds of each chamber and a referendum, checked by the Court for its limits.

  1. 1.A revision of this Constitution is proposed by one third of the members of either chamber, by the President of the Republic on the proposal of the Federal Government, by three state assemblies, or by one twentieth of the electors.
  2. 2.The proposal states the articles to be revised and the text proposed for each, and is published sixty days before the first vote.
  3. 3.The proposal is adopted by two thirds of the members of each chamber, voting on the same text.
  4. 4.The text adopted is submitted to referendum no earlier than thirty days and no later than ninety days after the second vote, and enters into force where a majority of the votes cast approves it.
  5. 5.Where a proposal fails at either stage, the same proposal is introduced again only in a later term of the Popular Chamber.
  6. 6.The Constitutional Court verifies, before the referendum, that the text adopted observes Article 207, and publishes its finding.

Limits of revision

The republican and federal form, the Title II rights and social ownership are beyond revision.

Title XIII · Final and transitional provisions

Articles 215 to 217

215

Entry into force and repeal

Entry into force, what was repealed, and the eighteen months allowed for outstanding organic laws.

  1. 1.This Constitution entered into force on 14 March 1961 and repealed the Fundamental Law of 1947 and every act inconsistent with it.
  2. 2.An Amendment enters into force on the day the result of the referendum approving it is published, unless the Amendment itself states a later day.
  3. 3.A law, a decree and a regulation in force on the day an Amendment enters into force continue in force to the extent that they conform to this Constitution, and the Constitutional Court decides any question of conformity on the application of the persons Article 192(2) names.
  4. 4.An organic law required by this Constitution and not yet adopted is adopted within eighteen months of the entry into force of the provision requiring it. The Court of Accounts reports each year on the organic laws outstanding.
216

Continuity of organs and of law

Organs carry on the work of those they replaced, and earlier decisions stay valid.

  1. 1.An organ established under this Constitution continues the functions of the organ it replaced, and takes over its property, its staff, its records and its pending business.
  2. 2.A holder of office elected or appointed before an Amendment enters into force completes the term for which that person was chosen, and the new conditions of eligibility apply at the next election or appointment.
  3. 3.A judgment, a licence, a registration and an administrative decision made under an earlier text remain valid, and are reviewed under this Constitution only where a person applies for the review.
  4. 4.A reference in a law to an organ that no longer exists is read as a reference to the organ which exercises its functions.
217

Transition from the institutions of 1961

How the 1961 institutions gave way to the present ones, and what became of their archives.

  1. 1.The Assembly of the Working People, constituted under the text of 1961, exercised the functions of the Federal Assembly until the first election of the two chambers under Amendment V, held on 9 December 1990.
  2. 2.The Presidium of the Assembly exercised the functions of the head of State until the first President of the Republic took office on 20 January 1991, and the Council of State exercised the functions of the Federal Government until the first Government took office under Amendment V.
  3. 3.The courts constituted under the text of 1961 continued to sit until the courts of the states were established under Amendment VI, and the judges then in office were confirmed or replaced by the Judicial Council within three years of its constitution.
  4. 4.The archives of the Assembly of the Working People, of the Council of State and of the single party pass to the National Archive. Access to them is governed by Article 41(3), and no record is destroyed, withdrawn or reclassified.
  5. 5.A person who held office under the text of 1961 loses no right by reason of that office alone, and Article 41(4) governs the offices from which a person is barred.
