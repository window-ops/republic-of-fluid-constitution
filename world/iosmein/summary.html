Summary of the Constitution

One line for each of the 217 articles · the summary has no legal force, and the Constitution governs where the two differ

Title I · Fundamental principles

Articles 1 to 22

  • 1The RepublicA federal, democratic socialist republic of five states, with two official names of equal force.
  • 2SovereigntyPower belongs to the people, and seizing it outside these procedures is a crime with no time limit.
  • 3Supremacy of the ConstitutionThe Constitution outranks every other rule, and only the conflicting part of an act fails.
  • 4Legality and publicity of the lawPublic power needs a legal basis, and an act that has not been published imposes no obligation.
  • 5Political pluralismParties are free, and no party may hold a leading role in the State or in the economy.
  • 6Social ownershipLand, water, forests, energy and the principal means of production are held in social ownership.
  • 7TerritoryThe territory cannot be ceded or leased, the capital is Talmeru, and the Valmeza crossing stays open.
  • 8The statesFive states, each with an assembly, a government and courts, and boundaries changed only by referendum.
  • 9Competences of the RepublicThe competences the Republic holds, and the rule that unassigned competences belong to it.
  • 10Competences of the statesWhat the states decide for themselves, and the rule that a state borrows only from the Republic.
  • 11Federal supervisionHow the Republic acts when a state fails to apply federal law, and the limits on that action.
  • 12Equality of the statesThe states are equal, and federal spending is distributed to equalise provision between them.
  • 13LanguageIosmen is official throughout, and a state language is official beside it within that state.
  • 14CitizenshipOne citizenship, no deprivation of it, and Bloc residents treated on the footing of residents.
  • 15BeliefNo established religion, freedom of belief, and public money for a religious building only as heritage.
  • 16Public integrityPublic office is exercised for its purpose alone, declarations are published, and corruption carries no time limit.
  • 17PublicitySittings, contracts and accounts are published, and an unpublished contract binds no public body.
  • 18The Democratic Socialist BlocFounding member of the Bloc, on conditions, with Bloc acts taking effect only on enactment here.
  • 19Foreign relationsNo military alliance, no unpublished treaty annex, and no submission to arbitration against a company.
  • 20External borrowingBorrowing outside the Bloc needs a two-thirds law and a referendum, and a breach creates no debt.
  • 21Ecological limitsResource use is capped in physical units, and exhausting a resource beyond recovery is prohibited.
  • 22SymbolsThe flag, the arms, the seal and the anthem are described by an organic law.

Title II · Rights, liberties and duties

Articles 23 to 74

  • 23Persons protectedWho holds these rights, whom they bind, and what a person keeps while deprived of liberty.
  • 24EqualityEquality before the law, a list of prohibited grounds, and the conditions for advancement measures.
  • 25Enforcement of the rightsCivil rights work in court on their own, and social rights work to the extent of resources available.
  • 26Limitation of rightsA right is limited only by law, only so far as the purpose requires, and its essential content stays intact.
  • 27RemedyEvery infringed right has a remedy, free where means are short, and delay is itself compensable.
  • 28Liability of public bodiesPublic bodies answer for damage, recover from officers who acted with intent, and compensate wrongful conviction.
  • 29Other instrumentsThese rights are read with the ratified instruments, and the wider protection prevails.
  • 30LifeThe right to life, the death penalty abolished, and no removal to a state that would impose it.
  • 31Integrity of the personPhysical and mental integrity, an absolute bar on torture, consent to treatment, and no forced labour.
  • 32Liberty and securityDeprivation of liberty only by law, reasons given at once, a judge within forty-eight hours.
  • 33Review of detentionA detained person, or another acting for them, may seek review within seventy-two hours, without a fee.
  • 34Fair hearingA public hearing before an independent court, equal access to the file, and reasons in the judgment.
  • 35Limits of the criminal lawNo offence without a prior law, no double punishment, and evidence from a breach of rights is inadmissible.
  • 36Rights of the defenceNotice of the accusation, a lawyer, confidential communication, and equal treatment of witnesses.
  • 37Private and family lifePrivate and family life, equal partners, equal children, and separation only by a court.
  • 38The homeThe home is inviolable, entry needs a warrant, and entry without one is reviewed within a day.
  • 39Correspondence and communicationsCommunications are secret, interception needs a judge, and blanket collection is prohibited.
  • 40Personal dataData are collected for a stated purpose, seen and corrected on request, and decisions are taken by a person.
  • 41Prohibition of political policingNo file on a person for their opinions, the Directorate abolished, and its archives opened.
  • 42Movement and residenceFree movement and residence in any state, free exit and return, and no expulsion of a citizen.
  • 43Conscience and beliefBelief is held, changed and practised freely, is recorded by no organ, and its sincerity is not examined.
  • 44ExpressionExpression is free, censorship is prohibited, and insulting an office is an offence in no law.
  • 45Information and the mediaThe media are free, ownership is public, and a journalist protects a source unless a court orders otherwise.
  • 46Research, teaching and creationResearch, teaching and creation are free, and publicly funded results enter the knowledge commons.
  • 47Assembly and demonstrationPeaceful assembly without authorisation, dispersal only for violence, and identifiable officers.
  • 48AssociationAssociations form freely, gain personality by registration, and are dissolved only by a court.
  • 49The voteUniversal, equal, direct and secret voting from eighteen, with local voting for settled residents.
  • 50Eligibility and public officeEvery citizen may stand, offices are filled by competition, and conviction for corruption bars a person.
  • 51Referendum and popular initiativeReferendums on demand, initiatives debated within ninety days, and no vote to reduce these rights.
  • 52Petition and complaintPetitions answered within thirty days, complaints kept confidential, and reprisal made an offence.
  • 53AsylumAsylum for the persecuted, no return to danger, and no funding of foreign bodies to stop movement.
  • 54Citizens abroadConsular protection abroad, the vote retained, and extradition of a citizen tightly conditioned.
  • 55Residents who are not citizensResidents who are not citizens hold these rights, and collective expulsion is prohibited.
  • 56WorkThe right to work, full employment pursued through the plan, and dismissal reviewable by a court.
  • 57Conditions of workSafe conditions, limited hours, paid leave, refusal of dangerous work, and unannounced inspection.
  • 58RemunerationPay according to the work done, equal pay for equal work, and a published ratio inside each enterprise.
  • 59Trade unions and the strikeUnions form freely, an agreement covers every worker in its scope, the strike is guaranteed, the lock-out is not.
  • 60Participation in the enterpriseThe workers elect a council that takes part in managing the enterprise and is heard before closure.
  • 61Work on one's own accountWork on one's own account is a right, registered on declaration, and past activity is regularised without penalty.
  • 62Social securityOne public social security system, its funds held apart, and benefits withdrawn only on appealable reasons.
  • 63HealthHealth care free at the point of use, equal between the states, and payment to staff made an offence.
  • 64HousingA dwelling of adequate standard, allocated from a public list, and eviction only where another is available.
  • 65EducationEducation free at every level, in the languages of Article 13, with maintenance grants for higher study.
  • 66Children and young personsThe interest of the child prevails, the child is heard, and violence against children is prohibited.
  • 67The family and maternityThe family is protected in whatever form, parental leave is paid, and dismissal during it is prohibited.
  • 68Older persons and persons with disabilityPensions, care and participation for older persons, and independence, equipment and access for disabled persons.
  • 69Culture and knowledgeCultural life is open to all, public knowledge is held in common, and state languages are supported.
  • 70The environmentAn environment that does not damage health, standing to sue without a personal interest, and the polluter pays.
  • 71Guaranteed provisionThe listed goods and services are free at the point of use, and the list may be cut only on published evidence.
  • 72Duties of the personObserve the Constitution, contribute according to means, and protect what is held in common.
  • 73Defence and civic serviceParticipation in defence, with civic service for those whose belief prevents bearing arms.
  • 74Public fundsWhoever administers public funds accounts for them and files the declaration, office or not.

Title III · The economic and social order

Articles 75 to 115

  • 75ObjectivesThe economy serves provision, employment, equal development between the states and the environment.
  • 76Forms of ownershipFour forms of property, no fifth created by law, and cooperative property kept undivided.
  • 77Administration of social ownershipSocial property is held in usufruct for a stated purpose, and cannot be sold or mortgaged.
  • 78LandLand is granted for use on long terms, and the sale, lease for gain or mortgage of a grant is void.
  • 79Natural resourcesThe subsoil, waters, forests and energy sources are worked by public enterprises, with no concessions.
  • 80CooperativesCooperatives govern themselves, one vote to each member, and their assets pass on to another cooperative.
  • 81Own-account activityOwn-account work is open outside the reserved sectors, with a uniform ceiling on employees.
  • 82Excluded marketsNo market in the means of production, land, dwellings, health care, schooling or public knowledge.
  • 83Guaranteed provisionThe law lists what is guaranteed free, and the plan allocates for that list before anything else.
  • 84The planA four-year plan, revised yearly, expressed in money and in physical units where ceilings apply.
  • 85PreparationThe draft plan is published six months ahead with the proposals it accepted and refused.
  • 86AdoptionThe Assembly adopts the plan, and neither chamber may adopt one that underfunds the guaranteed list.
  • 87ContentThe plan states resources, allocations, investments, responsible organs, deadlines and a reserve.
  • 88Ceilings in physical unitsPhysical ceilings for energy, water, timber, fish, listed minerals and emissions, published quarterly.
  • 89Categories planned in moneyWhat is planned in money, and the standing duty to examine extending physical planning further.
  • 90The Federal Planning OfficeThe Planning Office prepares and monitors the plan, publishes contested findings, and takes no instruction.
  • 91RevisionThe plan is revised by law, and no revision may cut the guaranteed list.
  • 92Publication of the accountsQuarterly accounts of the plan, an annual audit, and the naming of misapplied allocations.
  • 93The public enterpriseA public enterprise is created by law, keeps its own accounts and is entered in a public register.
  • 94Self-managementThe workers elect a council which runs the enterprise, meets in public and can be recalled.
  • 95The directorThe council selects the director by competition, directs the director and may dismiss the director.
  • 96Enterprises of federal importanceFor listed enterprises the ministry appoints from the council's ranking and must publish its reasons.
  • 97AccountsQuarterly accounts in money and physical units, and no allocation until they are published.
  • 98SupportSupport is published with amounts, terms and names, and a ceiling on it is left to a law not yet passed.
  • 99Reorganisation and dissolutionAn enterprise that cannot meet its obligations is reorganised or dissolved after its council is heard.
  • 100InvestmentInvestment comes from the plan, retained funds and credit, and stays within the physical ceilings.
  • 101PricesSome prices are fixed by the plan and published with their costs, the rest form freely.
  • 102Enterprises of foreign originA foreign enterprise operates only under a licence stating the technique transferred and the forum.
  • 103The federal budgetThe budget is adopted before the year begins, holds every receipt, and defaults to monthly twelfths.
  • 104Budgets of the statesState budgets fit within the federal allocations and are checked before adoption.
  • 105TaxationTaxes only by law, progressive on income, and an exemption for a named person is void.
  • 106CurrencyThe vela is issued by the Bank alone, and obligations in a foreign currency are void.
  • 107The Bank of the RepublicThe Bank issues the currency, holds public accounts, and reports to the Assembly every quarter.
  • 108Internal debtInternal borrowing needs a law, is published monthly, and external borrowing stays under Article 20.
  • 109The Court of AccountsThe Court of Accounts audits every public body, publishes in full, and refers offences to prosecution.
  • 110Public contractsPublic contracts follow a published procedure, and one concluded outside it is void.
  • 111The external accountOne external account, published monthly, and no foreign bank account without published authorisation.
  • 112Exchange within the BlocBloc exchange is settled in goods and knowledge, and no unit of settlement circulates at home.
  • 113Exchange with other statesTrade outside the Bloc runs through licensed public enterprises, and no weapon is exported.
  • 114Technique and knowledgeIncoming technique enters the knowledge commons, and no exclusive right over knowledge is accepted.
  • 115SolidaritySolidarity is given without conditions and received on the same terms, with both published.

Title IV · The federal assembly

Articles 116 to 145

  • 116The Federal AssemblyTwo chambers, seated at Talmeru, which legislate, adopt the plan and supervise the Government.
  • 117The Popular ChamberOne hundred and fifty members, four years, proportional representation with no threshold.
  • 118The Chamber of the StatesThirty members, six from each state, elected on the same day and bound by no instruction.
  • 119Conduct of electionsAn elected Electoral Commission runs the poll, and party finance is capped, published and audited.
  • 120TermFour-year terms, elections within sixty days, and extension only during an emergency or attack.
  • 121Eligibility and incompatibilityWho may stand, which offices are incompatible, and the two-year bar on contracts after the mandate.
  • 122The mandateA member votes according to their own judgment and cannot be recalled by a party.
  • 123ImmunityNo liability for votes or opinions, and otherwise a member is prosecuted like any other person.
  • 124Interests of membersMembers declare their assets and any interest in a vote, and non-declaration costs the seat.
  • 125Loss of the mandateThe grounds on which a seat is lost and how it passes to the next candidate on the list.
  • 126SessionsAn eleven-month ordinary session, extraordinary sittings on demand, and published rules of procedure.
  • 127The presidency of a chamberEach chamber elects and may dismiss its President, who directs its proceedings.
  • 128CommitteesCommittees sit in public, and the accounts and integrity committees are chaired from outside the Government.
  • 129Committees of inquiryA quarter of either chamber can open an inquiry that summons persons and compels documents.
  • 130Quorum and majoritiesA majority present, votes recorded by name and published, and a secret ballot only for elections.
  • 131InitiativeWho may introduce a bill, and the statement of cost and effect that must accompany it.
  • 132Ordinary procedureThree readings in the Popular Chamber, then thirty days in the Chamber of the States.
  • 133Matters requiring both chambersThe matters on which both chambers must agree, including the plan, treaties and revision.
  • 134ConciliationA conciliation committee of eight proposes a common text, and failure makes the bill lapse.
  • 135Organic lawsWhich matters need an organic law, and the review of such a law before promulgation.
  • 136Promulgation and publicationThe President promulgates within fifteen days, may return a law once, and silence passes the pen on.
  • 137Review before promulgationReferral to the Constitutional Court before promulgation, decided in thirty days.
  • 138Limits of delegationNo delegation over rights, the plan, taxation or the electoral system, and none beyond twelve months.
  • 139The plan and the budgetThe plan and budget arrive by October, are examined in public, and cannot be cut below the guaranteed list.
  • 140TreatiesTreaties are published thirty days before ratification, and a conflicting one waits for revision.
  • 141Elections by the AssemblyThe offices the Assembly elects, the public hearings before the vote, and the fallback after three ballots.
  • 142Questions and interpellationWritten questions answered in thirty days, weekly oral questions, and the President twice a year.
  • 143Supervision of the administrationEither chamber can compel documents and attendance, and the oversight reports are debated in public.
  • 144Initiative of the electorsAn electors' proposal reaches the agenda in thirty days and may go to referendum if it is rejected.
  • 145Publicity of proceedingsSittings are public and transcribed, and a register records who lobbies whom and about what.

Title V · The president of the republic

Articles 146 to 161

  • 146The officeThe head of State, exercising the powers this Constitution confers and holding no party office.
  • 147ElectionDirectly elected in two rounds, on the same rules of finance and publication as other elections.
  • 148TermSix years, one term only, and no candidacy after two years in the office.
  • 149Eligibility and incompatibilityThirty-five years of age, ten years resident, and no other office or paid activity.
  • 150Entry into officeThe oath before both chambers, and the assets declaration verified within ninety days.
  • 151The Federal GovernmentThe President appoints the Prime Minister and ministers, presides over the Government and signs its decisions.
  • 152Foreign relationsThe President directs foreign relations, concludes treaties and reports to the Assembly twice a year.
  • 153DefenceCommand of the armed forces, movement within the territory, and no action abroad outside Title X.
  • 154Acts of the PresidentDecrees cite their legal basis, some need countersignature, and none has the force of law.
  • 155Promulgation, referral and referendumPromulgation and referral, and a referendum only if both chambers approve the question.
  • 156PardonPardons are reasoned and published, and none is granted for corruption or for torture.
  • 157Publicity of the officeThe President's official diary, budget and staff appointments are published.
  • 158Suspension and removal by referendumBoth chambers may suspend the President, and a referendum within thirty days decides removal.
  • 159Removal for an offence of integrityA finding of corruption confirmed by the Constitutional Court removes the President without any vote.
  • 160LiabilityNo liability for opinions expressed in office, and no deferral of prosecution for anything else.
  • 161VacancyHow the office falls vacant, who acts in the interim, and what that person may not do.

Title VI · The federal government

Articles 162 to 174

  • 162The Federal GovernmentThe Government administers the Republic and answers to the Popular Chamber, with ministries fixed by law.
  • 163FormationThe programme is presented within fifteen days, and three refusals of confidence bring dissolution.
  • 164The Prime MinisterThe Prime Minister directs the Government, proposes ministers with published reasons, and answers weekly.
  • 165The ministersA minister runs one ministry, holds no other paid activity, and gives up any parliamentary seat.
  • 166Sittings of the GovernmentThe Government meets weekly, records each vote, publishes within seven days, and excludes interested members.
  • 167Acts of the GovernmentRegulations cite the law they execute, and one contrary to a law or the plan is void.
  • 168CensureA motion of censure must name the successor, and carrying it appoints that person.
  • 169Resignation and continuityWhen a Government resigns, and what a resigned Government may no longer do.
  • 170Liability of members of the GovernmentMinisters are prosecuted like any other person, and an instruction called unlawful must be confirmed in writing.
  • 171The public administrationThe administration gives reasons, answers within thirty days, and silence counts as a refusal.
  • 172The civil serviceAppointment by open competition, dismissal only on defined grounds, and no clear-out on a change of Government.
  • 173The states and the administrationFederal law is executed through federal or state services, with no instruction in an individual case.
  • 174The OmbudsmanThe Ombudsman examines complaints free of charge and publishes both recommendation and reply.

Title VII · Local self-government

Articles 175 to 182

  • 175Principle and unitsLocal affairs are run by elected communes and districts, each with its own property and budget.
  • 176CompetenceGeneral competence in local matters, and a task transferred without resources imposes no obligation.
  • 177The commune council and the mayorThe commune council is elected proportionally and elects the mayor, who can be replaced by a named successor.
  • 178The district councilThe district council is elected the same way, and the mayors of its communes are heard.
  • 179Publicity and participationLocal sittings and papers are published, a tenth of residents can force a referendum, and residents vote on investment.
  • 180Local finance and propertyLocal finance comes by published formula, cannot be withheld, and local property cannot be sold.
  • 181SupervisionThe state supervises lawfulness alone, through the courts, and only a court may dissolve a council.
  • 182Cooperation and boundariesUnits may act jointly, including with a unit across the frontier in another Bloc state.

Title VIII · The courts

Articles 183 to 193

  • 183Judicial powerThe courts judge in the name of the people, take no instruction, and extraordinary courts are prohibited.
  • 184Status of judgesJudges serve to retirement, are moved or removed only by the Judicial Council, and declare their assets.
  • 185The Judicial CouncilFifteen members, nine of them judges elected by judges, sitting in public and publishing reasons.
  • 186Appointment, promotion and disciplineAppointment and promotion by published competition, and discipline in public with the judge named.
  • 187Organisation of the courtsState courts in first and second instance, with the Supreme Court above them on federal law.
  • 188Access and conduct of proceedingsCases assigned by lot, judgments published, and each court publishing its delays and reversals.
  • 189The prosecutionThe prosecution is independent, its head elected by two thirds, and discontinuance is reasoned and reviewable.
  • 190The Anti-Corruption ProsecutionA separate corruption prosecution with its own budget, assignment by lot, and no immunity in its way.
  • 191The Constitutional CourtNine judges from three sources, nine years, renewed by thirds, and removable only by the Court itself.
  • 192Jurisdiction of the Constitutional CourtWhat the Court decides, who may bring a case, and when a provision found unconstitutional falls.
  • 193Procedure of the Constitutional CourtThe Court sits in public, hears a person without a lawyer or fee, and binds every organ and every court.

Title IX · Independent bodies

Articles 194 to 198

  • 194Common provisionsWhat the independent bodies have in common: their own budgets, no instructions, and full publication.
  • 195The Public Integrity OfficeThe Office verifies declarations, removes office holders for conflicts, and states unexplained wealth.
  • 196The Director of the OfficeThe Director is elected from candidates the Judicial Council presents, with a Bloc deputy during the settlement.
  • 197The register of assetsA public register of assets, searchable without registration, kept for ten years after office.
  • 198Procedure and safeguardsThe person is heard before a finding, may appeal to the Supreme Court, and the reporter stays anonymous.

Title X · Defence, public order and emergency

Articles 199 to 205

  • 199The armed forcesA standing professional force and the state militias, subordinate to the civil power.
  • 200The territorial militiaEach state trains a militia, employed for defence and rescue and barred from acting against an assembly or a strike.
  • 201LimitsNo alliance, no foreign base, no surveillance of the population, and no defence in a superior's order.
  • 202Action beyond the frontierRescue and relief by majority law, and any other action abroad by two-thirds law and referendum.
  • 203Public orderCivilian police, identifiable officers, recorded use of force, and independent investigation of complaints.
  • 204State of emergencyThe President declares an emergency, both chambers confirm it in seven days, and core rights stand.
  • 205Armed attackOn an armed attack the chambers meet within a day and review the measures every thirty days.

Title XI · Revision of the constitution

Articles 206 to 208

  • 206Initiative and procedureRevision needs two thirds of each chamber and a referendum, checked by the Court for its limits.
  • 207Limits of revisionThe republican and federal form, the Title II rights and social ownership are beyond revision.
  • 208Publication of the revised textA revision is consolidated and published, and the wording it displaced is kept in the Annex.

Title XII · The rescue settlement

Articles 209 to 214

  • 209Purpose and durationWhy this Title exists, when it began, and how it ends when the obligations are discharged.
  • 210The obligations of 2008 to 2016The 2008 to 2016 debts are scheduled and published, and unauthorised ones create no debt.
  • 211The joint committeeThe joint committee supervises and publishes, and may not legislate, annul, remove or instruct a court.
  • 212Supervision of budgets and borrowingState budgets are checked and public borrowing is authorised centrally while the settlement runs.
  • 213Planning and integrity during the settlementThe federal plan prevails over state plans, and the Integrity Office reaches state and local office holders.
  • 214Effect on the statesThe Title limits state competences temporarily, and they return on the day it is declared spent.

Title XIII · Final and transitional provisions

Articles 215 to 217

  • 215Entry into force and repealEntry into force, what was repealed, and the eighteen months allowed for outstanding organic laws.
  • 216Continuity of organs and of lawOrgans carry on the work of those they replaced, and earlier decisions stay valid.
  • 217Transition from the institutions of 1961How the 1961 institutions gave way to the present ones, and what became of their archives.
