The economy

Prices, wages, poverty, the two currencies, the debt and the aid balance, as they stood in June 2026

Iosmein is the poorest of the three member states of the Democratic Socialist Bloc. Median income has risen in each year since 2019 and one household in four remains below the national poverty line. This page sets out the figures the Federal Office of Statistics publishes, and it is written from the position of the households at the bottom of the distribution, because that is where the difference between the published averages and the lived arithmetic is widest.

Two currencies and a euro reference

The vela is the currency of the Republic under Article 106. It replaced the sarn in 2011 under Amendment X. Exchange within the Bloc is settled in goods, labour, technique and knowledge, recorded in physical units, so the rate below governs no Bloc settlement. It governs what a visitor pays, what an imported good costs and what a wage buys when it is compared with a wage earned elsewhere.

The euro and marek (the currency of Reformed Luces Aporia) appear here as a reference and for no other reason. No Iosmen wage, price or obligation has ever been expressed in it, and Article 106(4) forbids joining a monetary union or adopting the currency of another state.

The vela lost about three fifths of its external value between 2016 and 2018. The fall stopped in 2021, the year Amendment XII entered into force, and the rate has recovered by about 8 per cent since. A household holding vela and buying imported goods carries that movement directly, which is why the imported items in the price table below cost several weeks of work at the minimum remuneration.

Prices

Consumer prices rose by 13.0 per cent in the twelve months to June 2026. The rate has fallen in each year since 2021 and remains the highest in the Bloc by a wide margin. Aporean prices rose by 2.4 per cent over the same period.

A part of consumption is free. Article 71 and Article 83 place health care, medicines on the federal list, schooling with its materials, meals and transport, water for domestic use and public transport within a state outside the reach of money. Income, poverty and the distribution

Median net monthly income was 11,000 vela in June 2026. Measured in euro at the rate of each year, it fell from about 295 euro in 2011 to about 230 euro in 2017 and has since risen to about 458 euro. The rise since 2019 is the longest continuous one the Office has recorded.

The national poverty line is 60 per cent of median equivalised income. In June 2026 it stands at 6,600 vela a month for a single adult and 13,860 vela for two adults and one child. One household in four falls below it, against one in three in 2017.

The Gini of 0.28 places the distribution among the more equal in Europe, and it coexists with a poverty rate of nearly 25 per cent, because the distribution is compressed around a low median. A country can be equal and poor at the same time.

What costs nothing, and where the queues are

The guaranteed provision is the reason a household on 10,200 vela a month is housed, treated and schooled. Article 83(3) requires the plan to allocate for the guaranteed list before it allocates for any other purpose, and Article 71(2) allows the list to be cut only by a two-thirds law published with the evidence of exhausted resources. No such law has been adopted.

The list covers health care and the medicines on it, schooling at every level with materials, meals and transport, water for domestic use, public transport within a state, and a nursery place from the end of parental leave. A dwelling from the public stock, despite having no rent, needs to be maintained.

For the bottom decile the constraint appears as waiting.

  • 19 monthsDwelling from the public stock

    National median wait for an allocation from the list kept under Article 64(2). The wait is 31 months at Ilmesca and 24 months in the Talmeru district, and 9 months in Velmar. Allocations made are published, and the order of the list is public.

  • 26 daysSpecialist appointment

    National median wait. The wait reaches 41 days in Sarnovein, where two of the four district hospitals were operating below their established staffing in the returns for 2025.

  • Full priceMedicines outside the federal list

    A medicine that is not on the list is paid for in full. The list is extended at each planning period under Article 63(3), and the extension of 2025 added twenty-one preparations.

  • Free, and distantTransport across a state boundary

    Public transport within a state is free at the point of use. A journey crossing a state boundary is paid, at 180 vela for Talmeru to Vetruna, which is 5.1 hours at the minimum remuneration and is a reason internal migration for work is lower here than in Fluid.

The debt

External obligations outstanding stood at 146 per cent of annual output at the end of 2025. They peaked at 178 per cent in 2017. The fall of 19 points between 2018 and 2019 is the effect of Article 210(2), which provides that an obligation contracted without the authorisation the law then required, or applied to a purpose other than the one declared, creates no debt of the Republic. The remainder is discharged on the schedule of the settlement of 2017, and each payment is published.

The service of that debt is the reason the plan allocates less to investment than the Federal Planning Office proposes in each of its drafts. Article 210(5) forbids contracting a new obligation for the purpose of discharging one on the schedule, so the payments come from current output and the schedule runs to 2041 on present terms.

Assistance received and given

The Republic received about six times the assistance it gave in 2025. What is received is furnished in goods, labour, technique and training, recorded in physical units through the external account, and published each year by state and by form under Article 115(3). Iosmein is the largest recipient of the solidarity transfers required by Article 303 of the Constitution of the Republic of Fluid.

What is given is small and it is rising, and it is furnished on the terms Article 115(2) states: it creates no debt on the recipient and has no condition touching the internal affairs, the form of government or the alignment of the recipient. Those are also the terms on which the Republic received assistance between 2017 and 2021.

A eurocrisis without a Troika

The period of 2016 to 2019 is called the eurocrisis here. The euro had no part in it, and the Republic has at no time used that currency. That name was borrowed from the sovereign debt crises of the euro area because the position was recognizable: that of a state that had borrowed abroad, could no longer pay, and had lost the value of its currency in two years.

However, in Iosmein, no external body set conditions for the assistance that arrived. No creditor committee sat in Talmeru, no ministry was placed under the supervision of a foreign administration, no sale of public property was required, and no reduction of pensions or of the guaranteed list was demanded. Article 20(4) now states the rule in the text, and it states what was already the practice of the Bloc.

Adjustment came by three routes. Obligations contracted without authorisation were declared to create no debt under Article 210(2), and the officers who contracted them are personally liable under Article 20(3). Supply of medicines, fuel, spare parts and grain arrived from the Bloc in physical units and was recorded in the external account. The constitutional provisions that had allowed the borrowing were replaced by Amendment XII in 2021.

The cost of the adjustment was tolerated domestically, and the charts on this page show where it fell. Prices rose by 38.5 per cent in 2017 and the rate has taken nine years to return toward 13 per cent. Median income measured in euro fell by a fifth between 2015 and 2017 and took until 2021 to pass its 2011 level. The poverty rate rose by twelve points in two years and has taken nine years to recover eight of them.

In the Republic of Fluid the phrase is used differently. Article 117(5) of the Fluidian Constitution forbids the Republic to borrow outside the Bloc at all, so the sequence that occurred here has no route into that country. The fear expressed there concerns a situation its own text has closed. Here the same fear describes something that happened, and the arithmetic of it is in the tables above.
