SUMMARY OF THE CONSTITUTION OF THE ALLIED STATES OF IOSMEIN

One line for each of the 217 articles, grouped by title.
The summary has no legal force. The Constitution governs where the two differ.

TITLE I: FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES  (articles 1 to 22)

   1  The Republic
      A federal, democratic socialist republic of five states, with two official names of equal force.
   2  Sovereignty
      Power belongs to the people, and seizing it outside these procedures is a crime with no time limit.
   3  Supremacy of the Constitution
      The Constitution outranks every other rule, and only the conflicting part of an act fails.
   4  Legality and publicity of the law
      Public power needs a legal basis, and an act that has not been published imposes no obligation.
   5  Political pluralism
      Parties are free, and no party may hold a leading role in the State or in the economy.
   6  Social ownership
      Land, water, forests, energy and the principal means of production are held in social ownership.
   7  Territory
      The territory cannot be ceded or leased, the capital is Talmeru, and the Valmeza crossing stays open.
   8  The states
      Five states, each with an assembly, a government and courts, and boundaries changed only by referendum.
   9  Competences of the Republic
      The competences the Republic holds, and the rule that unassigned competences belong to it.
  10  Competences of the states
      What the states decide for themselves, and the rule that a state borrows only from the Republic.
  11  Federal supervision
      How the Republic acts when a state fails to apply federal law, and the limits on that action.
  12  Equality of the states
      The states are equal, and federal spending is distributed to equalise provision between them.
  13  Language
      Iosmen is official throughout, and a state language is official beside it within that state.
  14  Citizenship
      One citizenship, no deprivation of it, and Bloc residents treated on the footing of residents.
  15  Belief
      No established religion, freedom of belief, and public money for a religious building only as heritage.
  16  Public integrity
      Public office is exercised for its purpose alone, declarations are published, and corruption carries no time limit.
  17  Publicity
      Sittings, contracts and accounts are published, and an unpublished contract binds no public body.
  18  The Democratic Socialist Bloc
      Founding member of the Bloc, on conditions, with Bloc acts taking effect only on enactment here.
  19  Foreign relations
      No military alliance, no unpublished treaty annex, and no submission to arbitration against a company.
  20  External borrowing
      Borrowing outside the Bloc needs a two-thirds law and a referendum, and a breach creates no debt.
  21  Ecological limits
      Resource use is capped in physical units, and exhausting a resource beyond recovery is prohibited.
  22  Symbols
      The flag, the arms, the seal and the anthem are described by an organic law.

TITLE II: RIGHTS, LIBERTIES AND DUTIES  (articles 23 to 74)

  23  Persons protected
      Who holds these rights, whom they bind, and what a person keeps while deprived of liberty.
  24  Equality
      Equality before the law, a list of prohibited grounds, and the conditions for advancement measures.
  25  Enforcement of the rights
      Civil rights work in court on their own, and social rights work to the extent of resources available.
  26  Limitation of rights
      A right is limited only by law, only so far as the purpose requires, and its essential content stays intact.
  27  Remedy
      Every infringed right has a remedy, free where means are short, and delay is itself compensable.
  28  Liability of public bodies
      Public bodies answer for damage, recover from officers who acted with intent, and compensate wrongful conviction.
  29  Other instruments
      These rights are read with the ratified instruments, and the wider protection prevails.
  30  Life
      The right to life, the death penalty abolished, and no removal to a state that would impose it.
  31  Integrity of the person
      Physical and mental integrity, an absolute bar on torture, consent to treatment, and no forced labour.
  32  Liberty and security
      Deprivation of liberty only by law, reasons given at once, a judge within forty-eight hours.
  33  Review of detention
      A detained person, or another acting for them, may seek review within seventy-two hours, without a fee.
  34  Fair hearing
      A public hearing before an independent court, equal access to the file, and reasons in the judgment.
  35  Limits of the criminal law
      No offence without a prior law, no double punishment, and evidence from a breach of rights is inadmissible.
  36  Rights of the defence
      Notice of the accusation, a lawyer, confidential communication, and equal treatment of witnesses.
  37  Private and family life
      Private and family life, equal partners, equal children, and separation only by a court.
  38  The home
      The home is inviolable, entry needs a warrant, and entry without one is reviewed within a day.
  39  Correspondence and communications
      Communications are secret, interception needs a judge, and blanket collection is prohibited.
  40  Personal data
      Data are collected for a stated purpose, seen and corrected on request, and decisions are taken by a person.
  41  Prohibition of political policing
      No file on a person for their opinions, the Directorate abolished, and its archives opened.
  42  Movement and residence
      Free movement and residence in any state, free exit and return, and no expulsion of a citizen.
  43  Conscience and belief
      Belief is held, changed and practised freely, is recorded by no organ, and its sincerity is not examined.
  44  Expression
      Expression is free, censorship is prohibited, and insulting an office is an offence in no law.
  45  Information and the media
      The media are free, ownership is public, and a journalist protects a source unless a court orders otherwise.
  46  Research, teaching and creation
      Research, teaching and creation are free, and publicly funded results enter the knowledge commons.
  47  Assembly and demonstration
      Peaceful assembly without authorisation, dispersal only for violence, and identifiable officers.
  48  Association
      Associations form freely, gain personality by registration, and are dissolved only by a court.
  49  The vote
      Universal, equal, direct and secret voting from eighteen, with local voting for settled residents.
  50  Eligibility and public office
      Every citizen may stand, offices are filled by competition, and conviction for corruption bars a person.
  51  Referendum and popular initiative
      Referendums on demand, initiatives debated within ninety days, and no vote to reduce these rights.
  52  Petition and complaint
      Petitions answered within thirty days, complaints kept confidential, and reprisal made an offence.
  53  Asylum
      Asylum for the persecuted, no return to danger, and no funding of foreign bodies to stop movement.
  54  Citizens abroad
      Consular protection abroad, the vote retained, and extradition of a citizen tightly conditioned.
  55  Residents who are not citizens
      Residents who are not citizens hold these rights, and collective expulsion is prohibited.
  56  Work
      The right to work, full employment pursued through the plan, and dismissal reviewable by a court.
  57  Conditions of work
      Safe conditions, limited hours, paid leave, refusal of dangerous work, and unannounced inspection.
  58  Remuneration
      Pay according to the work done, equal pay for equal work, and a published ratio inside each enterprise.
  59  Trade unions and the strike
      Unions form freely, an agreement covers every worker in its scope, the strike is guaranteed, the lock-out is not.
  60  Participation in the enterprise
      The workers elect a council that takes part in managing the enterprise and is heard before closure.
  61  Work on one's own account
      Work on one's own account is a right, registered on declaration, and past activity is regularised without penalty.
  62  Social security
      One public social security system, its funds held apart, and benefits withdrawn only on appealable reasons.
  63  Health
      Health care free at the point of use, equal between the states, and payment to staff made an offence.
  64  Housing
      A dwelling of adequate standard, allocated from a public list, and eviction only where another is available.
  65  Education
      Education free at every level, in the languages of Article 13, with maintenance grants for higher study.
  66  Children and young persons
      The interest of the child prevails, the child is heard, and violence against children is prohibited.
  67  The family and maternity
      The family is protected in whatever form, parental leave is paid, and dismissal during it is prohibited.
  68  Older persons and persons with disability
      Pensions, care and participation for older persons, and independence, equipment and access for disabled persons.
  69  Culture and knowledge
      Cultural life is open to all, public knowledge is held in common, and state languages are supported.
  70  The environment
      An environment that does not damage health, standing to sue without a personal interest, and the polluter pays.
  71  Guaranteed provision
      The listed goods and services are free at the point of use, and the list may be cut only on published evidence.
  72  Duties of the person
      Observe the Constitution, contribute according to means, and protect what is held in common.
  73  Defence and civic service
      Participation in defence, with civic service for those whose belief prevents bearing arms.
  74  Public funds
      Whoever administers public funds accounts for them and files the declaration, office or not.

TITLE III: THE ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL ORDER  (articles 75 to 115)

  75  Objectives
      The economy serves provision, employment, equal development between the states and the environment.
  76  Forms of ownership
      Four forms of property, no fifth created by law, and cooperative property kept undivided.
  77  Administration of social ownership
      Social property is held in usufruct for a stated purpose, and cannot be sold or mortgaged.
  78  Land
      Land is granted for use on long terms, and the sale, lease for gain or mortgage of a grant is void.
  79  Natural resources
      The subsoil, waters, forests and energy sources are worked by public enterprises, with no concessions.
  80  Cooperatives
      Cooperatives govern themselves, one vote to each member, and their assets pass on to another cooperative.
  81  Own-account activity
      Own-account work is open outside the reserved sectors, with a uniform ceiling on employees.
  82  Excluded markets
      No market in the means of production, land, dwellings, health care, schooling or public knowledge.
  83  Guaranteed provision
      The law lists what is guaranteed free, and the plan allocates for that list before anything else.
  84  The plan
      A four-year plan, revised yearly, expressed in money and in physical units where ceilings apply.
  85  Preparation
      The draft plan is published six months ahead with the proposals it accepted and refused.
  86  Adoption
      The Assembly adopts the plan, and neither chamber may adopt one that underfunds the guaranteed list.
  87  Content
      The plan states resources, allocations, investments, responsible organs, deadlines and a reserve.
  88  Ceilings in physical units
      Physical ceilings for energy, water, timber, fish, listed minerals and emissions, published quarterly.
  89  Categories planned in money
      What is planned in money, and the standing duty to examine extending physical planning further.
  90  The Federal Planning Office
      The Planning Office prepares and monitors the plan, publishes contested findings, and takes no instruction.
  91  Revision
      The plan is revised by law, and no revision may cut the guaranteed list.
  92  Publication of the accounts
      Quarterly accounts of the plan, an annual audit, and the naming of misapplied allocations.
  93  The public enterprise
      A public enterprise is created by law, keeps its own accounts and is entered in a public register.
  94  Self-management
      The workers elect a council which runs the enterprise, meets in public and can be recalled.
  95  The director
      The council selects the director by competition, directs the director and may dismiss the director.
  96  Enterprises of federal importance
      For listed enterprises the ministry appoints from the council's ranking and must publish its reasons.
  97  Accounts
      Quarterly accounts in money and physical units, and no allocation until they are published.
  98  Support
      Support is published with amounts, terms and names, and a ceiling on it is left to a law not yet passed.
  99  Reorganisation and dissolution
      An enterprise that cannot meet its obligations is reorganised or dissolved after its council is heard.
 100  Investment
      Investment comes from the plan, retained funds and credit, and stays within the physical ceilings.
 101  Prices
      Some prices are fixed by the plan and published with their costs, the rest form freely.
 102  Enterprises of foreign origin
      A foreign enterprise operates only under a licence stating the technique transferred and the forum.
 103  The federal budget
      The budget is adopted before the year begins, holds every receipt, and defaults to monthly twelfths.
 104  Budgets of the states
      State budgets fit within the federal allocations and are checked before adoption.
 105  Taxation
      Taxes only by law, progressive on income, and an exemption for a named person is void.
 106  Currency
      The vela is issued by the Bank alone, and obligations in a foreign currency are void.
 107  The Bank of the Republic
      The Bank issues the currency, holds public accounts, and reports to the Assembly every quarter.
 108  Internal debt
      Internal borrowing needs a law, is published monthly, and external borrowing stays under Article 20.
 109  The Court of Accounts
      The Court of Accounts audits every public body, publishes in full, and refers offences to prosecution.
 110  Public contracts
      Public contracts follow a published procedure, and one concluded outside it is void.
 111  The external account
      One external account, published monthly, and no foreign bank account without published authorisation.
 112  Exchange within the Bloc
      Bloc exchange is settled in goods and knowledge, and no unit of settlement circulates at home.
 113  Exchange with other states
      Trade outside the Bloc runs through licensed public enterprises, and no weapon is exported.
 114  Technique and knowledge
      Incoming technique enters the knowledge commons, and no exclusive right over knowledge is accepted.
 115  Solidarity
      Solidarity is given without conditions and received on the same terms, with both published.

TITLE IV: THE FEDERAL ASSEMBLY  (articles 116 to 145)

 116  The Federal Assembly
      Two chambers, seated at Talmeru, which legislate, adopt the plan and supervise the Government.
 117  The Popular Chamber
      One hundred and fifty members, four years, proportional representation with no threshold.
 118  The Chamber of the States
      Thirty members, six from each state, elected on the same day and bound by no instruction.
 119  Conduct of elections
      An elected Electoral Commission runs the poll, and party finance is capped, published and audited.
 120  Term
      Four-year terms, elections within sixty days, and extension only during an emergency or attack.
 121  Eligibility and incompatibility
      Who may stand, which offices are incompatible, and the two-year bar on contracts after the mandate.
 122  The mandate
      A member votes according to their own judgment and cannot be recalled by a party.
 123  Immunity
      No liability for votes or opinions, and otherwise a member is prosecuted like any other person.
 124  Interests of members
      Members declare their assets and any interest in a vote, and non-declaration costs the seat.
 125  Loss of the mandate
      The grounds on which a seat is lost and how it passes to the next candidate on the list.
 126  Sessions
      An eleven-month ordinary session, extraordinary sittings on demand, and published rules of procedure.
 127  The presidency of a chamber
      Each chamber elects and may dismiss its President, who directs its proceedings.
 128  Committees
      Committees sit in public, and the accounts and integrity committees are chaired from outside the Government.
 129  Committees of inquiry
      A quarter of either chamber can open an inquiry that summons persons and compels documents.
 130  Quorum and majorities
      A majority present, votes recorded by name and published, and a secret ballot only for elections.
 131  Initiative
      Who may introduce a bill, and the statement of cost and effect that must accompany it.
 132  Ordinary procedure
      Three readings in the Popular Chamber, then thirty days in the Chamber of the States.
 133  Matters requiring both chambers
      The matters on which both chambers must agree, including the plan, treaties and revision.
 134  Conciliation
      A conciliation committee of eight proposes a common text, and failure makes the bill lapse.
 135  Organic laws
      Which matters need an organic law, and the review of such a law before promulgation.
 136  Promulgation and publication
      The President promulgates within fifteen days, may return a law once, and silence passes the pen on.
 137  Review before promulgation
      Referral to the Constitutional Court before promulgation, decided in thirty days.
 138  Limits of delegation
      No delegation over rights, the plan, taxation or the electoral system, and none beyond twelve months.
 139  The plan and the budget
      The plan and budget arrive by October, are examined in public, and cannot be cut below the guaranteed list.
 140  Treaties
      Treaties are published thirty days before ratification, and a conflicting one waits for revision.
 141  Elections by the Assembly
      The offices the Assembly elects, the public hearings before the vote, and the fallback after three ballots.
 142  Questions and interpellation
      Written questions answered in thirty days, weekly oral questions, and the President twice a year.
 143  Supervision of the administration
      Either chamber can compel documents and attendance, and the oversight reports are debated in public.
 144  Initiative of the electors
      An electors' proposal reaches the agenda in thirty days and may go to referendum if it is rejected.
 145  Publicity of proceedings
      Sittings are public and transcribed, and a register records who lobbies whom and about what.

TITLE V: THE PRESIDENT OF THE REPUBLIC  (articles 146 to 161)

 146  The office
      The head of State, exercising the powers this Constitution confers and holding no party office.
 147  Election
      Directly elected in two rounds, on the same rules of finance and publication as other elections.
 148  Term
      Six years, one term only, and no candidacy after two years in the office.
 149  Eligibility and incompatibility
      Thirty-five years of age, ten years resident, and no other office or paid activity.
 150  Entry into office
      The oath before both chambers, and the assets declaration verified within ninety days.
 151  The Federal Government
      The President appoints the Prime Minister and ministers, presides over the Government and signs its decisions.
 152  Foreign relations
      The President directs foreign relations, concludes treaties and reports to the Assembly twice a year.
 153  Defence
      Command of the armed forces, movement within the territory, and no action abroad outside Title X.
 154  Acts of the President
      Decrees cite their legal basis, some need countersignature, and none has the force of law.
 155  Promulgation, referral and referendum
      Promulgation and referral, and a referendum only if both chambers approve the question.
 156  Pardon
      Pardons are reasoned and published, and none is granted for corruption or for torture.
 157  Publicity of the office
      The President's official diary, budget and staff appointments are published.
 158  Suspension and removal by referendum
      Both chambers may suspend the President, and a referendum within thirty days decides removal.
 159  Removal for an offence of integrity
      A finding of corruption confirmed by the Constitutional Court removes the President without any vote.
 160  Liability
      No liability for opinions expressed in office, and no deferral of prosecution for anything else.
 161  Vacancy
      How the office falls vacant, who acts in the interim, and what that person may not do.

TITLE VI: THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT  (articles 162 to 174)

 162  The Federal Government
      The Government administers the Republic and answers to the Popular Chamber, with ministries fixed by law.
 163  Formation
      The programme is presented within fifteen days, and three refusals of confidence bring dissolution.
 164  The Prime Minister
      The Prime Minister directs the Government, proposes ministers with published reasons, and answers weekly.
 165  The ministers
      A minister runs one ministry, holds no other paid activity, and gives up any parliamentary seat.
 166  Sittings of the Government
      The Government meets weekly, records each vote, publishes within seven days, and excludes interested members.
 167  Acts of the Government
      Regulations cite the law they execute, and one contrary to a law or the plan is void.
 168  Censure
      A motion of censure must name the successor, and carrying it appoints that person.
 169  Resignation and continuity
      When a Government resigns, and what a resigned Government may no longer do.
 170  Liability of members of the Government
      Ministers are prosecuted like any other person, and an instruction called unlawful must be confirmed in writing.
 171  The public administration
      The administration gives reasons, answers within thirty days, and silence counts as a refusal.
 172  The civil service
      Appointment by open competition, dismissal only on defined grounds, and no clear-out on a change of Government.
 173  The states and the administration
      Federal law is executed through federal or state services, with no instruction in an individual case.
 174  The Ombudsman
      The Ombudsman examines complaints free of charge and publishes both recommendation and reply.

TITLE VII: LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT  (articles 175 to 182)

 175  Principle and units
      Local affairs are run by elected communes and districts, each with its own property and budget.
 176  Competence
      General competence in local matters, and a task transferred without resources imposes no obligation.
 177  The commune council and the mayor
      The commune council is elected proportionally and elects the mayor, who can be replaced by a named successor.
 178  The district council
      The district council is elected the same way, and the mayors of its communes are heard.
 179  Publicity and participation
      Local sittings and papers are published, a tenth of residents can force a referendum, and residents vote on investment.
 180  Local finance and property
      Local finance comes by published formula, cannot be withheld, and local property cannot be sold.
 181  Supervision
      The state supervises lawfulness alone, through the courts, and only a court may dissolve a council.
 182  Cooperation and boundaries
      Units may act jointly, including with a unit across the frontier in another Bloc state.

TITLE VIII: THE COURTS  (articles 183 to 193)

 183  Judicial power
      The courts judge in the name of the people, take no instruction, and extraordinary courts are prohibited.
 184  Status of judges
      Judges serve to retirement, are moved or removed only by the Judicial Council, and declare their assets.
 185  The Judicial Council
      Fifteen members, nine of them judges elected by judges, sitting in public and publishing reasons.
 186  Appointment, promotion and discipline
      Appointment and promotion by published competition, and discipline in public with the judge named.
 187  Organisation of the courts
      State courts in first and second instance, with the Supreme Court above them on federal law.
 188  Access and conduct of proceedings
      Cases assigned by lot, judgments published, and each court publishing its delays and reversals.
 189  The prosecution
      The prosecution is independent, its head elected by two thirds, and discontinuance is reasoned and reviewable.
 190  The Anti-Corruption Prosecution
      A separate corruption prosecution with its own budget, assignment by lot, and no immunity in its way.
 191  The Constitutional Court
      Nine judges from three sources, nine years, renewed by thirds, and removable only by the Court itself.
 192  Jurisdiction of the Constitutional Court
      What the Court decides, who may bring a case, and when a provision found unconstitutional falls.
 193  Procedure of the Constitutional Court
      The Court sits in public, hears a person without a lawyer or fee, and binds every organ and every court.

TITLE IX: INDEPENDENT BODIES  (articles 194 to 198)

 194  Common provisions
      What the independent bodies have in common: their own budgets, no instructions, and full publication.
 195  The Public Integrity Office
      The Office verifies declarations, removes office holders for conflicts, and states unexplained wealth.
 196  The Director of the Office
      The Director is elected from candidates the Judicial Council presents, with a Bloc deputy during the settlement.
 197  The register of assets
      A public register of assets, searchable without registration, kept for ten years after office.
 198  Procedure and safeguards
      The person is heard before a finding, may appeal to the Supreme Court, and the reporter stays anonymous.

TITLE X: DEFENCE, PUBLIC ORDER AND EMERGENCY  (articles 199 to 205)

 199  The armed forces
      A standing professional force and the state militias, subordinate to the civil power.
 200  The territorial militia
      Each state trains a militia, employed for defence and rescue and barred from acting against an assembly or a strike.
 201  Limits
      No alliance, no foreign base, no surveillance of the population, and no defence in a superior's order.
 202  Action beyond the frontier
      Rescue and relief by majority law, and any other action abroad by two-thirds law and referendum.
 203  Public order
      Civilian police, identifiable officers, recorded use of force, and independent investigation of complaints.
 204  State of emergency
      The President declares an emergency, both chambers confirm it in seven days, and core rights stand.
 205  Armed attack
      On an armed attack the chambers meet within a day and review the measures every thirty days.

TITLE XI: REVISION OF THE CONSTITUTION  (articles 206 to 208)

 206  Initiative and procedure
      Revision needs two thirds of each chamber and a referendum, checked by the Court for its limits.
 207  Limits of revision
      The republican and federal form, the Title II rights and social ownership are beyond revision.
 208  Publication of the revised text
      A revision is consolidated and published, and the wording it displaced is kept in the Annex.

TITLE XII: THE RESCUE SETTLEMENT  (articles 209 to 214)

 209  Purpose and duration
      Why this Title exists, when it began, and how it ends when the obligations are discharged.
 210  The obligations of 2008 to 2016
      The 2008 to 2016 debts are scheduled and published, and unauthorised ones create no debt.
 211  The joint committee
      The joint committee supervises and publishes, and may not legislate, annul, remove or instruct a court.
 212  Supervision of budgets and borrowing
      State budgets are checked and public borrowing is authorised centrally while the settlement runs.
 213  Planning and integrity during the settlement
      The federal plan prevails over state plans, and the Integrity Office reaches state and local office holders.
 214  Effect on the states
      The Title limits state competences temporarily, and they return on the day it is declared spent.

TITLE XIII: FINAL AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS  (articles 215 to 217)

 215  Entry into force and repeal
      Entry into force, what was repealed, and the eighteen months allowed for outstanding organic laws.
 216  Continuity of organs and of law
      Organs carry on the work of those they replaced, and earlier decisions stay valid.
 217  Transition from the institutions of 1961
      How the 1961 institutions gave way to the present ones, and what became of their archives.
