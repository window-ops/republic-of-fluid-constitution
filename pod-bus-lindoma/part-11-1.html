Telling her mother§

Nine days before the departure, on a Sunday evening in Japan. Akima has the address written out, the photograph of the house that her mother sent years ago, and the name in kanji on a fresh sheet where she copied it more carefully the second time. She rings at eight, the hour she settled on.

Akima

Okaasan. Kimatta kara, denwa shita. (Mother. It is decided.)

Mother

Kimatta tte, Kochi no koto? (Decided, meaning Kochi?)

Akima

Un. Hito ga iku. Watashi ja nakute. (Yes. Somebody is going, not me.)

Mother

Hito tte, dare. (Somebody. Who?)

Akima

Mae ni hanashita deshou. Onaji danchi no, tatemono ga kowasareta hou no hito. Vlad tte iu no. Ruumania kara kiteru. (I told you about him before. The one from my estate, from the building that was pulled down. His name is Vlad. He is from Romania.)

Mother

Ruumania. (Romania.) Nihongo wa. (And Japanese?)

Akima

Tango ga ikutsu ka. Tokyo made Nozaki-san ga mukae ni itte, Kochi made issho ni iku. Nozaki-san wa mae no kaisha no hito. (A few words. Nozaki is meeting him in Tokyo and going with him as far as Kochi. Nozaki is from my old company.)

Mother

a longer pause Sore de, anta wa. (And you.)

Akima

Watashi wa ikenai. Ikitakunai n ja nakute, ikenai no. Koseki nuita mama de, juuminhyou mo nai. Modoreba mata noserarete, kaeru toki ni wa mou kaerenaku naru. Kocchi de mo yakusoku ga aru. Nanajuunanajikan no waku to, kumiai no touban to, sore o oite ikenai. (I cannot. It is not that I do not want to, I cannot. My koseki is withdrawn and I have no juminhyo. If I go back I am put on the register again, and then leaving becomes the thing I cannot do. And I have obligations here. My hours, my turns at the cooperative, and I cannot walk out on them.)

Mother

Wakatta wa. Kami no koto de kaerenai nante, anta no sei ja nai wa. (All right. Being unable to come home over paperwork is not your fault.)

Ojiichan ni wa atashi ga iu kara anta kara iwanakute ii wa. Ruumania kara no hito ga kuru tte itte mo, ano hito wa wakaranai deshou kara, Akima no kawari ni tomodachi ga kuru, sou iu tsutae kata ni suru wa. Nannichi ni tsuku no ka, dono jikan ni ie ni kuru no ka, sore o oshiete. Ueda-san ni mo denwa shite oku wa, shiranai hito ga michi kara agatte kitara odoroku kara. (I will tell your grandfather so you do not have to. Telling him a man from Romania is coming will mean nothing to him, so I will put it as a friend coming in Akima's place. Tell me the day he arrives and what time he will come to the house. I will ring Ueda as well, since a stranger coming up from the road would startle her.)

Akima

Rainshuu ni deru. Watashi ga kita toki to onaji michi o, gyaku ni tadoru dake. Ressha de Lutoliluri made ichinichi, soko kara feri de umi o watatte Sluland, Sluland no soba no Zecina no kuukou kara hikouki. (He leaves next week. He is simply retracing the way I came, in reverse. A day by rail to Lutoliluri, then the ferry across to Sluland, and the flight from Zecina airport beside it.)

Watashi ga kita toki mo sou datta yo. Kuukou de okutte kureta deshou. Ano hi kara, hikouki to feri to ressha de Lindoma made yonjuuhachijikan kakatta. (That is how I came as well. You saw me off at the airport. From that day it was forty-eight hours by plane, ferry and rail to Lindoma.)

Mother

Anta ga tsuita tte renraku kureta toki, kocchi wa mou yoru datta wa. Atashi wa terebi tsuketa mama daidokoro ni suwatte, nando mo denwa mite ite, futsuka me no yoru da to kizuita no. (When you sent word that you had arrived, it was already night here. I was sitting in the kitchen with the television on, looking at the phone over and over and I realised it was the second night.)

Akima

Ima wa amari iu koto ga nai. Hitsuyou na koto wa zenbu itta to omou. Nokori wa hi to jikan dake de, sore wa kimattara okuru. (There is not much to say now. I think I have said everything that needed saying. What is left is the day and the time, and I will send those once they are fixed.)

Mother

Ii wa yo, sore de. Hi to jikan ga kimattara okutte. Sore made atashi wa nanimo shinai wa. Ojiichan ni mo, hakkiri shite kara iu hou ga ii mono ne, sakini itte shimau to mainichi genkan no hou o miru kara. (That is fine. Send me the day and the time when they are settled. Until then I will do nothing. It is better to tell your grandfather once it is definite as well, since if I say it too early he will watch the front door every day.)

Akima

Wakatta. Kimattara sugu okuru. Ja, mata ne, oyasumi. (Understood. I will send it as soon as it is fixed. Well then, good night.)

Fixing the dates§

The line goes and the kitchen is quiet. She waits a minute, then picks the phone up again and calls the third floor at the far end of Marginal.

Akima

Vlad. My mother knows. She is telling my grandfather herself. What she wants now is the day and the hour you will be at the door, and I told her I would send it once it is fixed.

So let us fix it. What went on the leave form, and what did the cooperative write down for your first day back?

Vlad

They wrote than in nine days from now I will be leaving and I can return on the eight day if I can't return on the seventh day.

Akima

Then departure is the twenty-second and you are back on the twenty-ninth or the thirtieth. Two days out, two days home, three days in Japan.

Vlad

Yeah, it would take two days to go and return. The card is ready, the cash too. I have pulled out my papers, so they are ready too. I am ready.

Akima

Then the day of arrival is the twenty-fifth. the sound of her writing it down I will send that to my mother.

Vlad

Alright. I didn't expect this to be so difficult.

Akima

The easy version was the one where I said I could not go and you said fine. a pause, and then flatly Come up tomorrow evening and I will go through the house with you. There is more to it than an address.

Vlad

I will.

Akima

Tomorrow, then.

The plan on the table§

The next day, Vlad works through the robot requests that came in over the weekend and spends the afternoon on the two crates he has been walking around for a fortnight, so that by four the room has a shelf in it and the flattened cardboard is stacked by the door. The euro notes and the card sit in the drawer under the window with the passport on top of them. Toward six the light goes long across the park and the swings fill with the after-school crowd, and at seven he walks the length of Marginal to the diagonal block, up three flights, and knocks.

Akima

opening the door with a pencil still in her hand and standing back to let him past Come in. Shoes off there, and the kitchen is straight through. the table has the photograph of the house on it, the sheet with the name, and a sheet of paper she has already ruled into a plan I started without you, because it was easier to draw than to say it.

Vlad

So, what's the plan?

Akima

turning the ruled sheet round so it faces him The house is in Kami, up the Monobe valley, past Tosayamada where the yuzu terraces start. From Ryoma airport you take the bus in to Kochi station, then the Dosan line two stops to Tosayamada, and from there it is the village bus up the river, which runs four times a day and stops running at four in the afternoon. Nozaki has the timetable. The house is the third one above the road on the upstream side, wooden, grey tile, a persimmon tree at the corner, and the door you want is the one at the side.

Vlad

Alright, and how Nozaki will get there from Tokyo?

Akima

You land at Haneda, not Ryoma. Nozaki meets you there and the two of you take the same domestic flight down together, Haneda to Ryoma, an hour and a half, and everything after that is one journey with her beside you. She is coming from Koganei, so she is on the Chuo line to the monorail and she will be at the terminal before you exit the arrivals hall.

Vlad

I initially thought about the Shinkansen even though I previously heard there was a domestic flight option.

Akima

The Shinkansen stops at Okayama and goes no further, since there is no bullet line anywhere on Shikoku. You would ride to Okayama, change to the Nanpu across the bridge, six hours end to end and about twenty-one thousand yen. The flight is ninety minutes and costs less than that, and you have a day of ferry and rail behind you before you even reach Haneda. Take the plane and let Nozaki book both seats together.

Vlad

I will. I have never been in foreign excursions. I have only been in local excursions.

Akima

setting the pencil down You crossed a sea and two borders to get here, so you have done the hard version of it already, with less help than you will have this time.

Vlad

Yeah, and how is Nozaki?

Akima

thinking about it before answering Direct, in a way that surprised me. She was the quiet one and I thought of her as one who agreed with everybody, and then the moment we were writing to each other she started saying things I never heard her say at a desk. She will look after you without making a performance of it, and she will tell you when you are doing something wrong.

Vlad

And I noticed you are hedging and direct at the same time.

Akima

I hedge on the things I have decided already, because saying them flat sounds like I am asking you to agree, and I am direct about everything else, which is mostly other people's arrangements.

Vlad

Saying them out flat doesn't sound like making me to agree.

Akima

looking up from the plan Then I will try it your way. I want you to go, I think you are the right person to send, and I am glad it is you standing in that room instead of a stranger from an agency.

What her mother is afraid of§

Vlad

It's much better sending a person you know. And then there was kodokushi, where you as an elder die alone, covered in cardboard and then because the smell is absorbed, you remain undiscovered.

Akima

She keeps saying he is well, he is well, and then she tells me Ueda goes in once a week and the ward office was turned away at the door, and I think the thing she is actually afraid of is that the once a week becomes two weeks and then nothing. She would not say the word to me. I am not certain that is what is underneath it, but it is what I would be afraid of in her place.

Vlad

And another aspect to consider is that while the republic's population is stable and it is relatively inexpensive to have children here, it is expensive to have children there in the vast majority of towns.

Akima

My sister had two in Tokyo on one and a half wages and it has taken everything since, the flat, the hours, the distance from my mother that she gets blamed for. She chose it knowing the arithmetic but in her place I would have chosen the other way. Here the arithmetic is different and I still do not want one now. Later, maybe, in this country, with somebody I have not met yet.

Crowds and the young§

Vlad

Another aspect to consider is that here in Lindoma, crowding and very high densities are unacceptable on the grounds of discomfort. And then I have seen that despite being fewer youth than elders, they are often still concentrated and abundant, and I don't feel comfortable being surrounded by many people and especially of the same age as me, because in my experience, the youth tends to be more aggressive than the elderly.

Akima

Mine went the other way. In Tokyo the crowd is dense and silent, nobody looks at anybody, and the danger is that you could stand there breaking and no one would turn around. What I got here in the first month was a whole tram talking, strangers asking me things across the aisle, and it took me a long time to stop reading that as a threat. I do know that the same age group at home is quiet on the train and different outside a station at midnight, so I understand what you are describing, and it is not the version I met.

Vlad

In general, when I was a teenager, I had a lot of animosity towards others primarily because others also had that animosity. And despite being grown-up, I still look younger than I am and I think myself as younger than I am. And then on the bus, I hated seeing people from the same school I used to be in that I did not get along with, and I still have this problem even today.

Akima

I had a version of that, later than you and much weaker. Not at school, not at university, at the company. There were people I would see waiting on the same platform every morning and I never wanted to speak to any of them. It was closer to nothing than to hatred, and I would move down the platform to stand somewhere else without even deciding to.

Vlad

I genuinely hate people who have nothing substantive to talk about. There was no other person in class other than me that thought maybe it's not acceptable to practice this stereotype of girls talking all the time and boys running around and doing fuck-ups. I hated the prospect of being alone in my opinion.

The few subjects§

Akima

Being the only one who thinks a thing is worse than the thing itself. Every lunch was the same few subjects and I decided the fault was in me for wanting something else, so I stopped talking at lunch and read instead, and after a while people arranged themselves around that. What I had wrong was the assumption that no one else in the room was bored, and I never once tested it. Nozaki was two desks away the whole time.

Vlad

If you are lonely and surrounded by young people who only like superficial things, then you cannot say you feel lonely no matter how many times you feel lonely or how intense that feelings is.

Akima

You can say it, and the sentence goes nowhere, which amounts to the same. Both times when I tried at the company I got sympathy for about a minute, and then it turned into advice about joining things, and after the second one I decided it was cheaper to say nothing.

Vlad

And then you are questioned, why do you decide all that stuff all by yourself, you do you pull out conclusions all by yourself, why do you think of yourself as superior as all the others, when none of that is true. The accusations appear when you are just one step above all the others in terms of maturity.

Akima

I got a different accusation, so I know the shape of it without knowing that exact one. Mine was that I was stiff, that I made things heavy, that I could not let a conversation be light. They said I was serious, as a fact about me that explained why the room went quieter.

Vlad

It happens when you are smart, socially, or when you are different.

Akima

Or when you notice things out loud. The people who thought the same as me were around, they had learned to keep it behind their teeth, and I had not, and it read as difference to a room even when the thought was ordinary.

Vlad

When I was driving the train in the simulator, I didn't want to see the crowd, because some people were either interesting or simply, or simply watching everyone on their phones is uncomfortable for me.

Akima

You come from a place with fewer faces in it, so a platform of two hundred people is not a background for you, it is two hundred separate things to read. And you read people harder than I do. I looked at that crowd for many years and saw a mass, and you looked at it through a screen for a few months and started noticing individuals in it.

Vlad

When you notice individuals in it, for some reason, the social desire activates, and you know cannot meet that desire, so you want to run as fast as possible. Our minds evolved for small groups.

Akima

Then we are built differently, because that switch does not come on in me. A full platform is scenery, and I can stand inside it thinking about something else for twenty minutes and come out having noticed the weather and one broken tile. I was closed off long before Fluid and it cost me almost nothing at the time, which is its own problem.

Vlad

I know. And there is this theory of holistic and analytic thinking, where the west tends to be analytic and the east holistic and the middle both. And I have been both holistic and analytic, but I think this has to deal more with personality. When I wanted to be left alone, I wanted to be physically left alone, not just emotionally.

Akima

I never needed the room empty. Crowds tire me and I can sit inside one for an hour with my mind elsewhere, so what I wanted was for no one to speak to me, and the walls could stay where they were.

Beautiful outside§

Vlad

Sometimes the desire to have friends and talk is pointless. With people who are not like you, with people who are unable to care about you or keep promises, like the vast majority of any crowd, you can't get that sense of fulfillment. Many people style themselves to look beautiful while having an ugly inside.

Akima

Most of that holds and I would have said it in the same words a year ago. What stops me now is that I put Nozaki in that crowd. She was one of the faces on the platform, one of the people at the lunch table with the few subjects, and I had written her off as somebody who had nothing to say to me. She was the one who wrote when the building came down. So I was right about almost everybody in the crowd and wrong about the one person in it who cared, and I would not have found that out if she had not moved first.

Vlad

Why don't we all start to be ugly or just decent from the outside and beautiful from the inside?

Akima

Nozaki spends more time on the outside than I ever have. The caramel hair is kept that way on purpose, her hands are soft in the way that takes work, and none of it made her worse. I had a rule that said effort on the surface meant nothing underneath, and she is a counterexample, so I threw the rule out. Some people are beautiful outside and hollow and some are plain and hollow, so you cannot read one off the other.

Vlad

We had different experiences, but since we are here, we are starting to converge.

Akima

pushing the ruled sheet an inch toward him We are. looking at the plan again You should go before it gets late. Take this, and read it so that you have questions for me tomorrow.

Vlad

Or just understand stuff, without questions. Anyways, it's been a good conversation.

Akima

seeing him to the door It was. I wanted to have this deep conversation. Go on, before you fall asleep on the stairs.

Vlad

taking the plan and sitting up Okay, see you.

Akima

holding the door See you tomorrow, Vlad.

Lindoma, Marginal and the diagonal block, the housing and currency cooperatives, Lutoliluri, Sluland and Zecina airport are invented, and the text is written for the story. Nerima, Chiba, Koganei, Haneda and the monorail, Kochi Ryoma airport, Kochi station, the Dosan line, Tosayamada and the Monobe valley in Kami, the Shinkansen as far as Okayama and the Nanpu across the bridge, and kodokushi are real, and no procedure set out here comes from the office that runs it.

Akima and her mother speak about ageing alone, crowds and the cost of a child out of one family in Nerima and one house in Kochi. Vlad sets the Fluidian arithmetic beside theirs in the same scene, and what either side generalises stays with the person who said it.

Akima's mother speaks as a woman of seventy-one whose knee has gone and whose children live at a distance, and Akima answers as the daughter who cannot go back. Families who manage the same things without trouble are outside this call, and neither woman is placed to weigh them.
