The bag and the stair§

It is a little before six. The flat at the far end of Marginal has been in order for days now, shelf up, kettle on the counter, nothing standing against the wall where the crates were. He has been preparing the bag since five, passport and euro notes and the card out of the drawer, two shirts, the charger, his papers in the side pocket where he can reach them without opening the whole thing. Bread and cheese for the train go in last. The phone lights up with Akima's name on it.

Vlad

picking up the phone Yes, I am leaving.

Akima

I know. a door closing behind her I am at the bottom of your stair.

Vlad

closing his bag I am coming.

Akima

Take your time.

Vlad

going down the stairs, and then after reaching the ground floor landing So, what you were about to say?

Akima

standing by the bicycle racks with her hands in her jacket pockets That I am coming to the station with you. I swapped the first two hours of my shift on Friday, so they will not be waiting for me at eight.

Vlad

Okay, so we need to go to the main station. But you remain in Lindoma, right?

Akima

Yes, I stay here. I put you on the train and then I go to the docks.

Vlad

pointing to the tram at the circle It's leaving in ten minutes.

Akima

taking the strap of his bag off his shoulder and putting it over her own Then we walk.

Three things at Haneda§

They go out onto Marginal, past the drying frames and the fence line where the park begins. At the circle the 28 stands with its doors open and three people already on it.

Vlad

entering the tram and sitting on a seat by the middle

Akima

sitting down next to him with the bag on her knees Tell me the first three things you do at Haneda.

Vlad

I get off the plane and go through the checks, I change money and I follow Nozaki.

Akima

Change money later, Nozaki will do it with you. Immigration, your bag off the belt, customs, and then out into the hall where she will be reading the arrivals screen.

Vlad

Okay.

Akima

the harbour still in the window behind them And at the house, do not stand in the doorway explaining who you are. Say my name first.

Vlad

I will. Does your grandfather know English?

Akima

No, none. Nozaki will interpret, so everything you say goes through her, and everything he says comes back the same way.

The ninth of November§

Vlad

looking out the window Now I am not sure what else to say. Like, after having talked about logistics, to make sure the conversation is not boring, I start talking casually.

Akima

Then talk casually, I have no more logistics in me either.

Vlad

Was there ever a time when you had zero friends?

Akima

Yes, most of the years at home. I was much more closed than I am now, I said nothing at work beyond what the work needed. On my birthday I sat in my flat with a cake I had bought myself.

Vlad

When it's your birthday by the way?

Akima

The ninth of November.

Vlad

The ninth of November. I am on the fourteenth of July.

Akima

July is easier than November, you get nice weather for it. Mine falls when it is already dark by five at home.

Vlad

Many people wish me happy birthday, and I don't like many of the generic wishes, especially from people I don't care about that much or am not close with.

Akima

I would take them, all of them, even from people who read the date off a screen.

Vlad

Why would you take all of them?

Akima

Because somebody had to open the message and type it. It costs almost nothing, and almost nothing is more than the silence I had.

Vlad

You are a person I care about, so I would wish you happy birthday on November.

Akima

the bag shifting on her knees as she turns to him Then I will be waiting for it.

Two lines left in Tokyo§

Vlad

noticing the tram starting to move It's starting to move.

Akima

On time as well. the doors closing and the four notes for the next stop playing You have the whole line to think about what you will say at that door.

Vlad

Are there trams in Tokyo?

Akima

There is the Toden line in the north, one car at a time, thirty stops from Minowabashi to Waseda, and the Setagaya line in the west, which is private. Everything else was pulled up in the sixties for the roads and the subway.

Vlad

The previous day you were saying a lot and now you don't.

Akima

We had two weeks of it and I said most of what I had. What is left now is the door in Kami, and I have gone through that with you three times already.

What you do after work§

Vlad

Nozaki was pretty nice and sentimental, and I liked that.

Akima

She never did that at the company. What you do there is go out after work and drink until the thing comes out sideways, and the next morning it counts as nothing said, and neither of us drank, so we simply went home with it.

Vlad

How did you manage to be alone and not say what you are feeling?

Akima

You get good at it, that is the whole trick. I would come home, put the light on, eat standing up, and by the time I sat down there was nothing left that wanted saying.

Vlad

I would not last that way. The geography teacher once said in high school, when I commented that I could not resist being alone on a floating house in the sea, that I could not resist because like the vast majority of teenagers, I needed stimulation from other people. And I said that's true, I can't last friends or being surrounded by meaningful conversation.

Akima

I was the other kind of child. My mother would send me out to play with the girls in the building and I would go, and by four I had come back up on my own to read on the floor of the corridor where it was cool, and she thought something was wrong with me for a while.

Vlad

My social life was inherently risky because I was social but not necessarily adaptive or considerate. I got into a lot of situations and I was doing things that were not the norm at the time, and still do. And when my mother warned me of something, I was like, I don't need to be warned, I can do it, It's not a problem, I can manage by myself, I won't be rejected.

Akima

My mother warned me about nothing, because I never gave her anything to warn me about. the tram slowing for a stop There is a cost to that as well, and I paid it later than you would have.

Vlad

What cost?

Akima

I arrived here at twenty-five with almost no practice at any of it. A woman on the tram asked me something in my second week and I gave her an answer you would give a supervisor, and I could hear myself doing it and could not stop.

Vlad

What answer?

Akima

She asked if I was new and where I had come from, and I told her the month I arrived, the name of the cooperative, and what my hours were. She only wanted to know whether I liked it here.

Being impulsive§

Vlad

People here don't usually ask about work. And my primary problem was that I was really impulsive.

Akima

the tram running past the yard with the tool library in it Impulsive how?

Vlad

I am both worried about the signals that I get when I am doing wrong and I am ignoring them at the same time. I can be easily frustrated and lose control. I usually don't like talking about my own frustration problems.

Akima

Then leave it there, I am not going to open that on a tram half an hour before you get on a train.

Vlad

Good, and by the way, have you noticed the contrast between us where I am lively and gesturing a lot and you are staying like a stone?

Akima

I noticed it on the first day, in the empty car. You were doing the impression with your whole arm and I was sitting there with my hands down.

Vlad

I already noticed you are quiet, so it's the same thing.

Akima

There is a difference, though. A quiet person can still move, and my grandmother used to say I sat like furniture.

Vlad

Right. So, do you have any more questions?

Akima

If the door does not open, or if it opens and he sends you away, what do you do then?

Vlad

Go soak in the sun in Tokyo with Nozaki.

Akima

a short laugh Soak in it and see how long you last. It is still close to thirty there at the end of September, and wet.

The platform and the van§

Vlad

murmuring Mamonaku, ichiban seni... means, shortly, at platform 1...

Akima

Mamonaku, ichibansen ni. (Shortly, at platform 1.) turning to look at him Where did you get that?

Vlad

There was a recorded train announcement that said "Shortly, at platform 1, a train headed for Shibuya and Shinjuku will arrive. It is dangerous, so, please stand behind the yellow line".

Akima

Mamonaku, ichibansen ni, Shibuya, Shinjuku houmen yuki ga mairimasu. Abunai desu kara, kiiroi sen no uchigawa made osagari kudasai. (Shortly, at platform 1, the service bound for Shibuya and Shinjuku will arrive. It is dangerous, so please stand back behind the yellow line.) facing forward again That is the whole of it.

Vlad

It's pretty hard for me to remember because in the video breakdown, the subject was at the end and you had to read the sentence in reverse, so it was like "Shortly, at platform 1, headed for Shibuya and Shinjuku, a train will arrive. It dangerous, so on the yellow line you stand behind, please".

Akima

That is how it works, the verb waits until the end. You will hear it many times before Kochi and stop noticing the order.

Vlad

Almost every week in the summer at home I heard a variation of "fiare vechi, fier vechi, baterii, televizoare, mașini de spălat, frigidere, aragaze, fier vechi, cumpăram". (Old scrap, old scrap, batteries, televisions, washing machines, fridges, ovens, old scrap, we are buying.)

Akima

I have no idea what any of that was. Say it in something I can follow.

Vlad

"Old scraps, old scrap, batteries, televisions, washing machines, fridges, ovens, old scrap, we are purchasing!"

Akima

We had nothing like that in Chiba. Who is saying it, and where from?

Vlad

Usually a man in a van drives through the village and shouts a catalog of products that could be old scrap. People that have old scrap are expected to exit the house and stop the van to sell their old scrap. After all the old scrap has been collected, it is being sold at a disassembler that sorts the old scrap. It occurs in poorer villages and never in the city, because it is both informal and illegal.

Akima

We have the same van, but slightly different. Kochira wa haihin kaishuusha desu, gokatei de fuyou ni narimashita, terebi, reizouko, sentakuki, jitensha, kowarete ite mo kamaimasen. This is a scrap collection vehicle, televisions, fridges, washing machines, bicycles, broken is fine. The difference is that ours does not pay you.

Statiun Centrala§

Vlad

I thought only my country had scrap vans. Anyways, we are approaching the semi-center, so we must be close to the main station.

Akima

Four more stops. checking the front of the car, where the strip shows the next name

Vlad

Is riding in a tram or train better?

Akima

The tram, and I mean this line specifically. On a train you sit for hours with the same view, and here you get the harbour, then the yards, then the middle of the town in twenty minutes.

Vlad

I would say the same, and we always get priority.

Akima

At every circle, yes. In Chiba I waited for cars at crossings on foot, and here the cars wait for us.

The tram goes through the first stop without slowing, since the platform is empty. At the next two it opens and closes inside half a minute each, one person off, two on, the strip at the front turning over each time. Then the line straightens and the last name comes up on the strip, Statiun Centrala, and the car begins to slow.

Vlad

sitting up This is where we need to get off.

Akima

standing with the bag already on her shoulder

Vlad

going through the same door they entered the tram

Akima

stepping down onto the platform after him and stopping to look at the departure board over the hall doors You have fifty minutes.

Vlad

I know. walking toward the station and loading the necessary tickets to Lutoliluri on his Dubrazec card and passing through the gates in the concourse So, why did you decide to follow me here when I knew the architecture of the city?

Akima

Because you would have got on that train without saying goodbye to you.

Vlad

Oh, so you want me to say goodbye at the train and not at the flat.

Akima

Yes, at the train. A door in a stairwell is nothing.

Vlad

The train will arrive at platform 3, so how do I say in Japanese that in fifty minutes, the 35 train headed to Torvena will arrive at platform 3?

Akima

Gojuppun go ni, sanbansen ni, Torvena yuki no sanjuugo-gou ga mairimasu. In fifty minutes, at platform three, number 35 bound for Torvena will arrive. On a real board they would never say the fifty minutes, they only announce when it is coming in.

Vlad

walking toward the third platform There is not much to do besides just waiting.

Akima

Then we wait. walking beside him with the bag still on her shoulder There is a bench at the far end where the roof stops.

Vlad

going toward the bench and sitting on it

Akima

setting the bag down between her feet and sitting beside him
