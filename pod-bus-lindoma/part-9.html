The call from Nerima§

The block came down in five days in the end, the ball working the standing half from the road and the breakers taking the ground floor and the basement walls apart in courses, and the pile was picked out by lorry in the week after that. Marginal opened at both ends, the 28 came back onto its own line, and the drying frames went back on their posts. The area is filled and rolled, fenced only along the road side, and the commune placed swings and the climbing frame at the old end, forty-five metres of open ground at the new, two benches and a line of young limes along the fence. The plan for the site goes to the assembly in October with nothing built over this ground. It is Sunday, early in the afternoon, and the neighbourhood has been quiet for days. Akima is in her kitchen in the diagonal block with the window open and the children audible below when her phone lights up on the table with the name that appears on it most evenings.

Akima

picking up on the second ring Moshi moshi. (Hello.)

Mother

Akima. Ima, hanaseru? (Akima. Can you talk now?)

Akima

setting her cup down Hanaseru yo. Kocchi wa nichiyou no gogo. Dou shita no. (I can. It is Sunday afternoon here. What is it?)

Mother

Ojiichan no koto na no. Byouki ja nai wa yo. Genki. Tada ne, mou hitori de wa muri na no. (It is about your grandfather. He is not ill. He is well. It is only that he can no longer manage on his own.)

Akima

Ikutsu ni natta no. (How old is he now?)

Mother

Kotoshi no natsu de kyuujuu. Tonari no Ueda-san ga shuu ni ichido nozoite kureru kedo, ano hito mo nanajuu-hachi yo. Yakusho kara mo hito ga kita kedo, ojiichan wa uchi ni irenakatta no. (Ninety this summer. Ueda next door looks in once a week and she is seventy-eight herself. The ward office sent somebody and he would not let them in.)

Akima

Okaasan wa ikenai no. (Can you not go?)

Nerima kara Kochi made ichinichi kakaru no yo, hikouki to basu de. Hiza ga kono toshi de dame ni natte, sannen ittenai wa. (Nerima to Kochi is a whole day, plane and bus. My knee has gone and I have not been in three years.)

She stays at the window after the call ends, then writes two words on the corner of a newspaper, puts her shoes on and goes down. Vlad is across the new ground on the far bench, by the fence where the drying frames used to stand.

What her mother asked§

Akima walks down Marginal with the newspaper corner folded in her hand and turns in at the new block. The courtyard behind it is smaller than the old one and still raw, gravel where the grass has yet to take, the multi-capsules gone from the far end and their footings showing.

Akima

stopping a couple of metres short, one hand up Hi. sitting down on the wall, leaving a gap between them Your gravel is worse than our gravel.

after a moment, looking at the racks rather than at him My mother rang. Not the usual call. She wants me to go to my grandfather in Kochi, around the equinox, look through the house and see him. turning the folded paper over once in her fingers And I cannot go.

Vlad

And what can I do?

Akima

looking at him now You can go instead of me. seeing his face and putting a hand out flat, slowing it down Wait. I will tell you the whole of it before you say anything, because it sounds like a favour and it is not a small one.

He is ninety. He lives outside Kochi, on Shikoku, in a house my grandmother died in, and he will not let the ward office through the door. My mother cannot make the journey and my sister cannot either. Somebody has to walk through the rooms, see what is broken, tidy the grave and sit with him for an afternoon so that he has seen a face from the family this year. None of that needs Japanese. It needs a person standing in the room.

a short pause, the paper folded smaller in her hand And you have the two things I do not. You still hold euro at the cooperative from before you came, so the fare is yours to spend. And you can leave.

Vlad

You also hold euro if you saved yen from the outside. And I am not sure I will be able to go to Japan all by myself.

Akima

stopping, and her face changes I do. I paid all of it in when I arrived, the whole transfer, with the papers showing where it came from. I have never once had a reason to touch it. So the fare is mine.

folding the paper again And you would not go alone. Nozaki lives in Tokyo and she has read about this place every day for a week. I will ask her. She has her mother three days out of seven, so she may well say no, and then we think of something else.

The name on the paper§

Vlad

What is on the paper? There are two characters.

Akima

turning the corner of newspaper over and looking at it herself for a moment My grandfather's name.

里見

handing it across Satomi. I wrote it while she was talking. The first is village, and the second is see. This is the first thing I have written by hand in Japanese since I came.

Vlad

So... you want to send a turkish messenger to your grandfather's door.

Akima

a short laugh Turkish. looking at him sideways It's a joke I do not have. I am sending a man from Romania, and to him you will be a foreigner at the door either way.

Vlad

It wasn't a good joke, I am sorry. So, what are we going to do?

Akima

waving it off It was fine. I laughed. sitting forward on the wall Tonight I write to Nozaki and ask her, and I ring my mother back tomorrow with whatever answer I have. Before that I want two things from you. Go to the cooperative this week and find out what a person actually does to leave the country. And tell me whether you want to go at all.

Vlad

We'll decide after you talk to Nozaki.

Akima

nodding once Fair. standing up off the wall and looking down the length of the new ground toward her own block She works early tomorrow, so I will write tonight and there may be an answer by the morning. pausing before she goes Thank you for sitting through that.

Vlad

I'll wait for the answer.

Akima

already turning, half over her shoulder You will have it, one way or the other.

The teller at Calmura§

The 28 runs it inbound along the jingle line, Marquena, Dolvena, Serpina, four notes at each. Vlad gets down at Calmura, where the communal office stands on the square side, a low building with the constitution on a shelf inside the door and the currency cooperative's teller window at the back of the same room, under a printed card listing the days a teller is present.

Vlad

approaching the teller Sali, so, I need to go to Japan very soon and I need to convert some euros into yen for personal use. How do I extract and use them?

Teller

Yen is a hard currency. Your euro sits in Ireland and we hold it there for you, so a personal direct withdrawal for travel is straightforward. We hold no yen at all, and there is no counter here that will hand you yen notes. You can draw euro notes here, on notice, and change them at a counter after you land, or take a card.

The card is a Tuby's PowerCard. It is a CARD-ROM with a processor in it, and the software on it presents itself to a terminal as a card issued by a bank. The charge lands on your euro balance and the terminal does its own conversion. It works on older terminals. Some of the newer ones read the format, decide they do not like it, and refuse it, and there is no arguing with them at the counter. So you carry notes as well and you treat the card as the convenience.

We can issue one. It goes on the request and it takes days, and we issue very few of them, so bring the dates of your travel in writing when you come back.

Vlad

What is Tuby's PowerCard exactly?

Teller

It is an emulator. Inside there is a small computer with its own battery, and the software on it plays the part of a bank card at the terminal: it answers the terminal's questions in the order a bank card answers them, it produces the codes for each transaction as it goes, and it drives the stripe as a coil, so an older reader takes it as a swipe. Behind all that it is talking to us in Dublin, and what you spend comes off your euro.

The software is written by Tuby, a cooperative here. They write for PowerPC, so their card runs a PowerPC part, and the whole emulator is one piece of software they build and sign themselves. They have an ARM build as well. The people who repair these tell me the PowerPC one is the one that comes back least often, so that is what we hand out.

It fails at the newer terminals where they look at the format, decide it has no scheme they support, and stop.

Vlad

Now I know. I think in a week or so I will be leaving.

Teller

reaching under the counter and putting a printed form on the top of it, turning it to face him Then fill this now and the days start counting from today. Name, member number, the dates you travel, where you are going, and down there the amount in euro you want in notes and whether you want the card as well.

putting a pen beside it I will take it as it stands. Come back when you have the exact dates and I will write them in myself.

The request to Tokyo§

Akima is at her kitchen table with the window shut, the phone in front of her, writing to Nozaki Rie in long gaps while the other woman is on her way to work.

    situationism.fl

    Situationist design · What it is

    Situationism holds that a settlement is judged by the time its people spend in it while going nowhere. Commuting is time surrendered to distance and work is time surrendered to an employer. What remains is the measure: sitting on a wall, walking a street with no destination, standing at a corner talking, taking the long way home. A place that produces none of that has been built as a corridor, no matter how well it moves people.

    The doctrine came out of the drifting experiments of the 1950s and stayed marginal for thirty years. It entered practice through municipal design offices in western Europe from the late 1980s, first as pedestrianisation and traffic calming, later as the standard method for laying out a district. Most of what is now ordinary in a Dutch or Danish street plan is situationist in origin, applied without the name.

    The Republic of Fluid took the doctrine further than the countries it learned it from. Where a European municipality applies situationist measures inside a land market and a road network it does not control, the Republic holds the ground in common, books cars instead of owning them, and can therefore narrow a road, close a through route, or hand a courtyard to its residents against no owner's objection. Housing, transport and the plan answer to the same body, so a district scoring badly can be redesigned rather than ignored.

    https://situationism.fl/maps/annex/method

    situationism.fl

    Annex: how a city map is measured

    Three quantities are recorded on the ground and combined:

    • ppm, persons per minute: A counter stands at a fixed point and counts people crossing a line, in both directions, for a measured period. This gives the flow and produces the arrows. Flow alone says nothing about the quality of a place, and a motorway footbridge can score highly.
    • pt, person-time: The time a person spends in a place while neither commuting nor working. It is taken by observation over a fixed interval: a person who crosses in forty seconds contributes forty seconds, and a person who sits for an hour contributes an hour. pt is the situationist quantity proper, since it counts only unbought time.
    • pt·d, cumulative person-time by density: pt summed over an area and weighted by the density of people in it. This separates a wide empty square where a few people linger for a long while from a narrow lane where many linger briefly. The shading on the maps is pt·d.

    A district is marked as having dérive where pt·d passes the threshold set for its density band, and as utilitarian where it does not. The true centre of a settlement is the district where the heaviest ppm arrow terminates in the highest pt·d, which is frequently not the administrative centre and is frequently not the largest interchange.

    On rail corridors

    A wide rail corridor moves people at an efficiency no stroad and no eight-lane boulevard approaches. The situationist objection stands anyway: a corridor is a wall.

    What the surveys record on either side of a wide corridor is consistent. ppm along the corridor is high and ppm across it collapses to the few points where a bridge or an underpass exists. pt·d in the strip either side falls well below the band for its density. People cross the wall and they do not linger against it. The frontages facing the tracks are back walls, storage, car parking and hoardings. Buildings turn away from the line, and a district cut by one is measured as two districts with a gap between them.

    The corridor is also ugly in a way that is difficult to design out. The structure carries fencing, gantries, signal equipment and overhead line, and it stays visible along its whole length.
