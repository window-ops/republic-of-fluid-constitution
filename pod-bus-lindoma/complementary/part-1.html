01The start of another story§

The carriage is one of the refurbished units on Linka C, white panelling, orange grab poles, red striped seats set in facing pairs with fold-down seats along the far wall. It is nearly empty between Muzeum and I. P. Pavlova. Vlad has the window seat of a facing pair. Mathilda sits opposite him, knees angled to the side so they do not touch his, a canvas bag on her lap and a paper cup of coffee she has stopped drinking. She has been looking on her phone since Florenc, and she has looked up at him twice.

Vlad

You know...

Mathilda

You know what?

Vlad

I am not sure how to say this, but it's once again the start of another story.

Mathilda

The start of another what?

Vlad

Another meeting with a stranger on the train, which will end up in friendship, when in real life, on the STB, it's not really like that.

Mathilda

shifts the cup to her other hand So you've decided in advance that this ends in friendship. That's a lot of confidence for a man who hasn't asked me my name. And what's the STB?

Vlad

The bus to and from Bucharest I take.

Mathilda

Right. And on that bus no one talks to you.

02Acquaintances that do not stick§

Vlad

Actually, on occasions they do and I had acquaintances, and there was a woman from dental who had a 3D printer whom I talked to when I seen her, and I have seen her like two or three times this year, and when I was studying, because I don't go out, so I don't get to meet strangers. And then there were people from the local university who were nice and I've even got contact with one of them after she recommended me a film but then when I said "hi, how are you doing", a month later I got ghosted, blocked. But there was one person I met on the bus whom I became friends with and that happened to be the friend of my ex-friend, so not new.

Mathilda

So it does happen, it just doesn't stick. That's a different complaint from the one you started with.

Vlad

Yeah, and it has been boring for me to have only like one religious close friend who is busy most of the time. I did get out with her and I was more mature this time than with the ex-friend, but I am looking for people who are different, more spontaneous maybe, who are more open. I don't trust people who are flowery because some people I met were flowery in the sense that they were interesting and had this conversationalist, creative side, but they were not particularly committed. At the same, I want one who is committed and interesting at the same time.

Mathilda

The interesting ones aren't committed and the committed ones bore you. looks out the window as the tunnel lights go past I've heard that from people who mostly want to be interesting themselves and are checking whether the other person can keep up. Which one are you doing?

Vlad

Right now, neither. I don't think I am interesting person, but that friend I have now wants to make me feel like I'm interesting. She sometimes says "Vlad, the cute one, Vlad, the cute one" and I am not sure what to say, so I say that she is cute too. I am trying to be interesting, but I mostly try being myself.

Mathilda

And when she says it, do you believe her, or do you just hand it back so the sentence has somewhere to go?

Vlad

I try to believe her, but sometimes I don't, so I say something so the sentence has somewhere to go.

Mathilda

turns the cup around once in her hand, says nothing for a while Yeah. I don't have anything for that.

03Rigging the outcome§

Vlad

So, because I am bored and I want another close friend, I create characters, on and on, using the same scenarios I have gone through but every time I altered it towards a favorable outcome. And you might say, that's serious, like rigging an election. But that's what I did in my stories for so long, rigging outcomes so they are favorable to me and I get a friend and then realize... they were all just multiplications of my mind.

Mathilda

Then you've been talking to yourself in different coats. sets the cup on the ledge by the window What does the favorable outcome actually look like, in the ones you write?

Vlad

They become friends, I enjoy the friendship, there is always something important to talk about, and maybe I experience love later on, but not always. In fact, most of the time, I create like four characters in the same universe, and then based on how future events would go, I choose who will get even closer.

Mathilda

Four at once, and then you pick. That's an audition, not a friendship. scratches at the seam of the canvas bag And the three who don't get picked, what happens to them, do they just stop existing mid-sentence?

Vlad

They continue to exist, but frequency reduces because it's just so hard to balance everyone, and then I say, this is enough, and I stop writing at all.

Mathilda

So they don't end, they just get less of you until there's nothing.

04Where the material comes from§

Vlad

Yeah, and then I decided to extend my sources of inspiration by watching other people relate things. There was a woman who described her life in Japan, which eventually got to be the source of inspiration for Nozaki in the "Pod Bus, Lindoma" section of the Republic of Fluid universe. There were also street photographers that embedded stories and then there were also researchers who documented longer stories, and I liked the researchers more.

Mathilda

Why the researchers more?

Vlad

Because the researchers run significantly longer interviewers, which reveal more personality, but there is one disadvantage where the people getting more coverage are either intellectuals, people fighting for a cause or just really mature people who know what they are talking about, and the ordinary people talk less.

Mathilda

Well, that's me disqualified from your ordinary people, then.

Vlad

Many people don't have a lot to say, or something worth being friends for. That is what I encountered in real life. I must select people, and it is a very difficult process. You can't just approach anyone.

Mathilda

You keep saying select. You said audition was too harsh and then you went straight back to selecting.

Vlad

Because that is what happens often when I want to talk to strangers.

Mathilda

And where am I in that, out of curiosity. Have you already run me through it, or is that happening while we talk?

Vlad

I have already ran through it, you were bored, now I let feelings guide me.

Mathilda

I was bored. takes the cup back off the ledge That's an odd thing to say to someone on a train.

Vlad

That's how it really is.

Mathilda

Fine. At least the second half is honest, letting feelings guide you.

That if you write people who agree with you, of course they'll sound like you.

06Speech at five§

Vlad

Do you know how can I stop this?

Mathilda

No. How are you actually doing, though, underneath all the theory about it?

Vlad

I am feeling somewhat lonely. I feel that many connections I could have are inaccessible. And that is in spite of knowing the systemic conditions or the circumstances I was born in, not having been able to have a social life at five because I could speak until five.

Mathilda

You couldn't speak until five?

Vlad

Yes. I was born with late speech. I thought it was a simple condition, but then I realized just how much it altered my way of thinking.

Mathilda

In what way? I mean the thinking, not the speech.

Vlad

It is very difficult for me to predict what the other will gesture, how the other will feel without much information, to tolerate superficiality, and to tolerate not talking about myself at all.

Mathilda

So the characters are easier because you write both halves and there's nothing to predict.

Vlad

Yes, in some way, but it also serves as temporary replacements for future friends I will have should conditions change.

Mathilda

What conditions, specifically?

Vlad

Finally moving away from my peers, out of twelfth grade, and finding places with more mature or intelligent people I can actually relate to.

You ruled it out before trying it, or after?

I have ruled it out after trying.

How many, and how long did you stay in each?

Vlad

Two courses, weekly, and wait, there was a third and fourth...

Mathilda

Take your time.

Vlad

Wait another few years...

Mathilda

I meant take a second to remember, not that. sets the cup down again You're going to sit out several years?

08Being early§

Vlad

Yeah, unfortunately. Since I got in high school I thought to try making friends early as much as I can, knowing I will be hitting into some very hard walls, and hope I will find love or at least another significant friend in four years.

Mathilda

Four years is a long time to be on a schedule you set yourself at fourteen.

Vlad

I set at fifteen. And that's the unfortunate reality of being above your years in some things. You have to try and wait.

Mathilda

Or you're just early to some things and behind on others, like everyone.

Vlad

Yes, but early on so many things that I've even got accusations of seeming superior without concrete proof.

Mathilda

Accusations from who, your classmates?

Vlad

From older teachers.

Mathilda

What were you doing when they said it?

Vlad

It's actually only my IT teacher, and a few others I don't remember where the accusations came from. I was doing all tasks, then not doing some of them because I already did them. I eventually got recognized as a power user after I've got third place in an olympiad where I presented an older program I made.

Mathilda

So they were right that you weren't doing the work, and wrong about why. small shrug That's most teachers, honestly. What was the program?

09The questionnaire§

Vlad

It's a questionnaire my mother told me to make as a demo to demonstrate to the National Health Insurance House, where she works, that digitization is possible. It sat an year unused, even after it got promoted, and even if the source code was public and the my mother decided when there were complaints about lack of digitization once again to ask me for the direct link to the source code, which I pasted to her, and she forwarded it, and then finally the House took a copy of my program and integrated into its own system.

Mathilda

Wait, they actually took it? The whole thing, running in a state institution, and you were what, seventeen?

Vlad

My mother says that yes, and I wrote the whole logic in simple javascript, what you would expect of the simplest programs out there.

Mathilda

Did they pay you, or thank you, or anything?

Vlad

Of course not, my mother only told me the program was integrated. And wait, I made the program two years ago, it only got integrated now.

Mathilda

So sixteen, then. And it took them two years and a complaint to copy a link your mother had the whole time.

Vlad

Of course. The procurement is chaotic. I was even offering the program under a free license where the only restriction was that a copy of it may link back to me, and they didn't know that the source was available for the public.

Mathilda

Did they credit you in the end?

Vlad

My mother says that has been passed off to the programmers. They must have credited me and read the licenses, but of course since it's inside the healthcare system, which is private, I cannot verify that they credited me.

Mathilda

Then you'll never know.

10Sifonator§

Vlad

It doesn't really matter now. It finally got into the system. And I've got other new projects to work on by the way.

Mathilda

Like what?

Vlad

Whatever ideas come into my head, like Fluid, Sifonator, Legacy IDE, other stories... by the way, Sifonator and Legacy IDE are technically complete and I have not tested them since. I move on fast.

Mathilda

Complete and untested is a strange definition of complete.

Vlad

They are tested while in the development cycle. Now they are out, and I come back when I plan to revive them. They are personal projects after all.

Mathilda

Fair. What's a Sifonator?

Vlad

Sifonator, or SifonatorPRO, is a fictional subscription-based service that aids corrupt politicians in Romania to siphon off public money by exploiting loopholes in the procurement and justice system. Of course it's satire, and there were three fictional APIs, like ANAF Sync, PSD Direct Connect and Mântuire Proiecte. ANAF Sync is a government API that connects to E-Factura and provides a detailed overview of purchases. starting to laugh for a bit PSD Direct Connect is a service that allows you to pay for all the fees of SifonatorPRO and everything small you would desire by using public money, essentially using taxpayer money as if it were yours, under certain conditions. And "Mântuire Proiecte" allows you to salvage shoddy projects, and it is the fastest way to gain reputation points in the game, which can be used for press and escaping DNA investigations and drop convictions like Vanghelie.

Mathilda

laughs Of course it is subscription-based. Is there a free tier for siphoning at a reduced rate?

Vlad

Of course not. There is a feature called the magic wand, and if you are not planning to subscribe, you can buy hours. The magic wand uses accounting tricks to multiply the money you cash in from public projects, therefore draining even more taxpayer money.

Mathilda

Paying per hour of wand sounds worse but funnier at the same time. Did you build the whole interface or just the idea?

Vlad

I built the interface. It's playable, but it's in Romanian.

Mathilda

I don't have Romanian, so I'd be clicking buttons and hoping. Do people play it, or is it another one that went out and stayed where it landed?

Vlad

People don't play it, I kept as a personal project. All personal projects are public.

Mathilda

You build the thing, put it somewhere public, and then don't tell anyone it's there, same as the questionnaire, right?

Vlad

Yeah, there is no way to announce.

Mathilda

There's no way, or you haven't looked for one?

Vlad

I'll look for a way later, right now I am not interested.

Mathilda

Alright.

Vlad

Do you have to get down somewhere?

Mathilda

Kačerov. Three more, I think. You?

Vlad

The same. I am not heading anywhere in particular.

Mathilda

You got on a train to go nowhere in particular?

Vlad

What else to say?

Mathilda

I don't know. Where you were actually going, maybe.

Vlad

I don't know Prague, that's the thing. I come from Bucharest.

Mathilda

Then why Kačerov? There's nothing at Kačerov, it's a bus interchange and a road.

Vlad

Because I've been looking at generic housing estates. In fact, many of my stories happen in the most generic places possible.

Mathilda

Why generic?

Vlad

Because I go through them every day. And I get bored in usual tourist destinations. At camps, I soaked up in clothing, standing on my tiring feet, looking at castles and other overflowing buildings.

12Sídliště§

Mathilda

I grew up in one of those estates, so I can tell you the inside view is less interesting than the outside one. You look at it and see a setting but I look at it and see the lift being broken.

Vlad

In one of my stories, I also featured a generic inside. And when I thought about Leriduson, a port city in that story, I thought that it's architecture would be like, center, sídliště, sídliště, sídliště, sídliště, sídliště and then sídliště.

Mathilda

laughs That's most of the country, honestly. Does anything happen in Leriduson, or is it just blocks?

Vlad

Leriduson is where Marliene, a fluidian-born woman, lives. She moved from Prevessa to there. In later parts, after Vlad returning from Japan and stopping at Leriduson before going to Lindoma, she hangs out with him in a restaurant and then she invites him to sleep on an extended couch in her apartment block, which is located inside a "silies", the fluidian term for "housing estate".

Mathilda

Vlad. So you're in the story yourself, under your own name.

Vlad

In some way, yes. And Vlad lives in a silies at the Marginal neighborhood in Lindoma, with Akima and Nozaki eventually. There has not been a single character that didn't live in a generic housing estate.

Mathilda

Three of them in the flat with you. Sorry, with him.

Vlad

In different buildings. I have used the word "sídliště" many times in my recent stories, and not just in Fluid.

Mathilda

You use the Czech word, in stories that aren't set in Czechia?

Vlad

Yes, because I learned from the archives what a sídliště is.

Mathilda

What archives?

Vlad

There is an archive of news from CT24 called "Paneláková kultura". In the 1950 recording, the reporter says via a ball experiment that they are made of expanded clay and then the rest documents their growth until they eventually start decaying, and it ends with a note that most people would agree more on rehabilitating than demolishing them.

Mathilda

The demolition argument lost, yes. What happened instead was that they wrapped nearly all of them in polystyrene, put in new windows, painted them in colours somebody thought were cheerful, and now the panels underneath still crack and the joints still let water in.

14Relational work§

Vlad

Yeah, because if the setting is an urban place, what other place to think about than either the most boring city centers like Geneva or the most boring peripheries like everywhere in Romania, Poland, Czechia, and so on.

Mathilda

Most people pick a place with something in it. You go looking for the flattest option available and then build there. Why?

Vlad

Because the place will no longer be doing the relational work for them, and it's where most people live.

Mathilda

Relational work meaning what, exactly?

Vlad

In romantic films like Before Sunrise, the place is doing a lot of work in place of the characters, who mastered teleportation for some reason and the church is non-euclidean because the inside scene has been filmed in a different church than the one being shown on the outside.

Vienna is a city where it's pretty easy to add events that seem spontaneous, and since the architecture is so coherent, you can film in different places without ever attracting attention. And many of those activities are designed to help them get closer in a seamless way.

Mathilda

So the city hands them excuses. They never have to invent a reason to keep walking together, the street just supplies the next thing.

Vlad

Yes, that's how romance works in this film. I wrote a story where both characters, Celeste and still Vlad but slightly different, know about the Before Trilogy, get off and get to know each other at Geneva, visit Lyon, her provincial city, and then visit Bucharest, his city.

Mathilda

They know about the film in the story?

Vlad

Yes, the story is meant to a critique of this romance film.

Mathilda

What's the critique?

Vlad

That's it's just not really feasible in real life despite sounding very realistic.

Mathilda

And what happens to them in your version? nods towards the window as the train slows Two more.

Vlad

They still get to meet each other, but they've got to realize just how banal they are.

Mathilda

Banal how? They talk about nothing, or they talk about a lot and it turns out to be the usual things?

Vlad

They talk about a lot of things, and it turns out to be none of the interesting philosophical things in the film.

Mathilda

Which is what we've been doing, more or less.

Vlad

Of course, I wanted to say, that's how it really goes with most people, don't go chase after those interesting conversations. Not every one of us has the capacity to wrap up their theories or life experience in beautiful words.

Mathilda

I don't have much to say either. Most days I go to work, I come back, I watch something. Most people are like that and it's fine.

15What was on the syllabus§

Vlad

Films and stories are often designed to be out of the ordinary. Romanian literature has many instances where relationships go wrong in extreme ways because at the time the text was written, it gathered the most attention.

Mathilda

Wrong in what way? Someone dies, or someone leaves?

Vlad

Usually both. In Ion, Ana dies. In Ultima noapte de dragoste, Gheorghidiu is a person full of misogyny, so much so that he decides to revenge on Ela for just talking to someone named G. And then because Gheorghidiu is deep in his revenge, she leaves him, and Gheorghidiu is at war, so he left too and just didn't think about her anymore.

Mathilda

I don't know either of those, I've never read Romanian literature. What year is the second one?

Vlad

It's 1930.

Mathilda

Then he's a man of his time being awful in a way his readers recognised. Was that the point of the book?

Vlad

Many of those books do not have a point, they are meant to be exploratory.

Mathilda

Did you have to study them, or did you read them yourself?

Vlad

I had to study them.

Mathilda

For the exam at the end. Do you actually like any of them, or are they just in your head because you had no choice?

Vlad

They were somewhat good, but they are both way too dramatic.

Mathilda

And your own stories are the opposite, you said. Is that reaction, or is that just what you find true?

Vlad

It's both reaction and the fact that I find them true.

Mathilda

The train's slowing. Kačerov next but one, I think, or is this it?

Vlad

The next one is.

Mathilda

Right. So this is Pankrác, then.

16Writing alone§

Vlad

You have be challenging me in a really good way.

Mathilda

I've been asking normal questions.

Vlad

Yeah, but you know what is like when I am writing all these alone.

Mathilda

Tell me, because I don't. What's it like?

Vlad

I am planning everything, I am sitting alone with my mind, I am directing what characters should be saying, I am making portraits for many of the characters except for me using a simple sampling procedure, I get bored, I create another character, another scenario, different story, but everything converges. And it's tiring, but there is nothing else to do to keep company.

Mathilda

What's the sampling procedure?

Vlad

The sampling procedure is where I pick some images and I derive faces, because I am more like a programmer than an artist.

Mathilda

Do you sample faces from real people?

Vlad

I only pick stock images and then make sure they are not identifiable, so as to make sure the characters are always unique.

Mathilda

So you build a face for everyone in the story except yourself. Why not yourself?

Vlad

I tried to generate portraits of fictionalized versions of myself, but it is a lot harder for men, and there is a lot less variety.

Mathilda

Less variety in the images, or less variety in what you'd accept as looking like you?

Vlad

Less variety in images, there is the over-representation problem, where some features are a lot more represented that others on the internet.

Mathilda

So they all come out looking like the same man.

Vlad

Of course they do.
