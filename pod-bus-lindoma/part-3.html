Lagoon Fishery and Shellfish Cooperatives of Marendo, in cooperation with the coastal monitoring station
The lagoon weed of Ravelluri: a note for visitors

1. Name and classification

Division Chlorophyta, class Ulvophyceae, order Ulvales, family Ravelluriaceae. The species is Ravellura marendensis, described in 1974 from the inner lagoon. It is the only member of the family. Local usage is lagoon weed, and the older fishing term is rope weed, which refers to the handling of it rather than to its form.

2. Description

Thalli are strap shaped, 40 to 120 centimetres long and 8 to 20 millimetres wide, olive green above and blue green in the lower third. Attachment is by a discoid holdfast fixed to shell fragments and to buried stone. Growth is continuous through the year, with the greatest extension between March and July. The species occurs at depths of 0.4 to 3.1 metres and has been recorded nowhere outside the inner lagoon and the two connected channels.

3. Mechanical strength and the condition of the water

The wall of the thallus carries a matrix of sulfated polysaccharide cross-linked by calcium bridges and by disulfide bonds between the wall proteins. In the oxygenated water of the lagoon these bonds remain in the oxidised state and the cross-linking is at its densest. Rupture of a fragment 10 millimetres wide requires a load of about 50 kilograms, which is a force near 490 newtons.

On removal from the water the matrix begins to lose bound water and part of the calcium bridging fails. The disulfide bonds are unaffected, so a load near 30 kilograms is still required. Drying to the point of brittleness takes some days, and the fragment is by then of no biological interest.

In a reductive environment, meaning water or sediment of low oxygen content and high dissolved sulfide, the disulfide bonds are reduced to free thiol groups and the cross-linking is lost. The tissue then tears under light finger pressure. This condition is met in the anoxic mud of the lagoon floor, where the fallen thalli are broken down within a season.

4. The conducting strands

Four to nine strands of elongated cells run the length of the thallus and are visible against light as pale lines. They carry water, sugars and dissolved sulfur compounds between the holdfast and the growing tip. The arrangement is convergent with the vascular tissue of land plants and is not homologous with it, and the term vascular is used in this note for convenience only.

5. The odour

Sulfur taken up from the sediment is voided from the tips as a mixture of volatile sulfides and methylated esters. The mixture is perceived as a strong sweet odour and is detectable across the whole of the dune line in warm weather. It is harmless. Visitors should expect it to remain in clothing for two to three washes.

6. Status and protection

IUCN Red List: Endangered, EN B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii). Extent of occurrence is 31 square kilometres and the whole population stands in one location. The decline recorded is in the quality of habitat, through coastal retreat and the narrowing of the channels.

The lagoon and its seagrass and weed beds are protected against conversion under Article 30 (1). Collection, removal and possession of any part of the species are prohibited, and the prohibition extends to detached material found on the shore. Enquiries go to the coastal monitoring station.

Coastal monitoring station of Marendo, with the hydrobiology group of the University of Sederna
Downstream establishment of Ravellura marendensis in the Anselura outflow and on the eastern shelf: seven years of survey

Abstract. Ravellura marendensis has been treated since its description as confined to the inner lagoon of Ravelluri and its two connected channels, and the current listing of Endangered rests on that assumption. Surveys of the Anselura outflow and of the shelf between 2019 and 2026 record establishment at 31 of 44 stations, with continuous cover from kilometre 4 of the outflow to the bar, and holdfasts on hard substrate at four shelf stations at depths of 2 to 6 metres. Extent of occurrence calculated on the 2026 stations is 407 square kilometres, against the 31 square kilometres on which the present listing was assessed. Mean annual advance along the outflow over the last four years is 1.9 kilometres. On criterion B the species no longer meets the thresholds for Endangered, and a listing of Vulnerable is the defensible reading of the current data.

Establishment downstream differs from the lagoon population in three respects. Thalli reach 180 centimetres against a lagoon maximum of 120. Cover at the four densest outflow stations exceeded 80 per cent of available substrate, with Zostera and two native red algae absent from quadrats where cover exceeded 60 per cent. Fragments detached by current retain the holdfast tissue and re-attach, which is a route of spread not observed inside the lagoon, where the anoxic floor breaks down fallen material within a season. The species is native to the catchment and is behaving on the outflow and the shelf as an invasive one.

We recommend that any revision of the listing be taken together with a decision on the downstream populations, since the arguments for downlisting and the arguments for control rest on the same observations.

Corcina mine and reactor works, sub-region of Belvacius, with the mycology group of the University of Sederna
The tracer fungus of the Corcina galleries: a note for visitors

1. Name and classification

Division Ascomycota, class Eurotiomycetes, order Chaetothyriales. The species is Radiotropha corcinensis, described in 1969 from the abandoned upper galleries. Miners called it the string, and the older survey records call it the finding thread.

2. Description

A filamentous fungus forming flat cords of 1 to 4 millimetres across on bare rock, brown to near black, growing in unbranched lines that may run twelve metres or more. Where a cord reaches an ore body it stops extending and forms a dense mat around it. Growth is slow, at 20 to 60 centimetres a year, and the cords persist for decades once formed.

3. Orientation toward the deposit

The cords do not run at random. Extension is faster on the side of the tip receiving the greater ionising dose, so a cord curves toward any source and arrives at it. In the galleries the sources are pitchblende and the secondary uranium minerals of the fracture walls, and the fungus also tracks the daughter products, so a cord may end at a radium enrichment with no uranium behind it.

Shallow deposits under intact rock are found the same way, at depths to about four metres, by cords which stop and mat over ground with nothing visible in it. The mine survey still records these as an indication and drills them.

4. Tolerance of the dose

The pigment of the cord wall is a melanin which absorbs a large part of the incident energy before it reaches the cell. Within the cell, the repair of double-strand breaks runs continuously rather than on damage, and the genome is held in several copies, so a break is repaired against an intact template. Cultures have been held at doses some hundreds of times the natural background without loss of growth.

5. Alteration of the rock

The mat secretes oxalic and citric acids and a siderophore which binds uranium in preference to iron. Over years this dissolves the carbonate and part of the silicate around the ore body and leaves the uranium fixed in the mat and in a covering of oxalate crystals.

6. Status and protection

IUCN Red List: Near Threatened, NT. The species is common through the karst of the sub-region, occurs in nine other cave systems, and grows readily on artificial substrate. It sits one step above the threatened categories because its whole range lies in one karst system, and a change in the drainage of the galleries would reach every population at once. No collection restriction applies. Visitors are asked to leave the cords in place, since a broken cord is a survey record destroyed.

1. On the incident

3. On the vehicles

The eastern branch: a working history of the seed and breed registry at Prevessa
Doru Vestali, keeper of the eastern branch, 1968 to 1998

Chapter one: Why it is here at all

The registry stands in Prevessa because of the blockade of 1914. The commune councils were rationing bread by procedure and discovered within a winter that nobody could say with confidence what had been sown in the country, nor in what quantity, nor whether the seed held back for the spring would come up true. A clerk of this town was instructed to write it down. He began with the fields he could walk to.

His first book covers, in several communes, the name of the holdings, the varieties sown, the weight of the crops, and in a fourth column the name of the person who told him. That fourth column was the method of authentication of the office and it has never been altered. A registry which cannot say who told it a thing is a rumour with a stamp on it.

Prevessa was never industrialised, was never struck, and was never audited. The Directorate looked at the town twice and found nothing here it could set a tonnage against, which is the kindest thing that period did for us. We were left alone with the books through twenty years in which almost every other institution in the Republic was told what to count and how. When the strike wave came and the plan was opened again, the eastern branch was one of the few offices that had nothing to correct.

I have been asked what a registry is for and I have never found a shorter answer than this. Everything a farmer here plants was kept alive by somebody who chose to keep it rather than plant something easier, and the record of that choice is the only reason the variety still exists.

Marliene

stepping down onto the platform and turning to wait for him, the whole train emptying around them because the line ends here Yours goes from platform two, the western line. nodding across the tracks You have about ten minutes, so do not stand here being polite with me.

Vlad

I won't.

Marliene

setting her bag down at her feet and pulling him in, both arms around him, holding it far past the length anyone here would call polite Go on, then. not letting go yet, her face against his shoulder Tonight, when you are home.

Vlad

holding her in the hug until the platform announcement and then mockingly The train will be zero point nine seconds early. I have to go.

Marliene

laughing into his shoulder and letting go Then run, and forgive the nine tenths. picking up her bag and stepping back Go, Vlad.

Vlad

taping her shoulder Go, Marliene.

Marliene

catching his hand on her shoulder for a second before it goes See you. turning toward the exit, then stopping and looking back across the platform until he reaches the stairs for platform two
