The courtyard§

Marliene got down at Leriduson and the train ran the six hours on to Lindoma through the night. Vlad and Akima walked back from Statiun Centrala on the first tram of the morning and slept in their own buildings for the first time in a week.

The commune answered his balcony report several days later. His block was already on the list, and the finding on the two slabs moved it to the front of it. The residents' assembly met on Thursday the thirteenth and set the date.

The people of his building started emptying it over the weekend. Most of them are going into the new block at the far end of Marginal street, four hundred metres and no further, and the commune sent the portable belts for the move. Two of them are standing in the courtyard now on their folding legs, running from the stairwell doors out to the loading bay, with crates going along them.

The rest of the neighbourhood came for the ceremony. They are standing in the courtyard between the belts and the drying frames, the older residents at the front where the chairs are. Akima is at the side by the bin shelter, out of the way of the loading, listening.

Ilvena

My name is Ilvena and I am with the housing cooperative for this end of Marginal. I have no paper with me, so I will say it the way I would say it to any of you at the door.

Marginal 20 is a P-4 block of the 1966 series, eight floors, four stairwells. Its extended useful life ended in March. We told the assembly then and we are telling you now in the open: the building has to come down, and it has to come down this autumn.

You have watched it happen from your own windows. The fifth floor balcony went first, two years ago in the rain, and after it the fourth, the third, the second and the first. The slabs above them are cracked in the same places and the reinforcement behind them is thin and wet, so they will go the same way. The inspection puts the risk of a structural collapse at one year or less. The tremor on the third of August was step one here and almost nobody felt it, and the instruments on the north wall recorded movement at four of the balcony anchorages that has not closed since.

So the block is emptied and nobody stays in it, and that part is settled. We are standing here for the other part.

It was a standard building. There are four hundred like it in this sub-region and the drawings are the same in every one. It was also where the courtyard cinema was put up in the spring, where the assembly met for thirty years before the school hall was built, and where a number of you were born.

The courtyard stays as it is. It is being kept as a playground, and the plan for the site behind it goes to the assembly in October with nothing built over this ground. The belts run to the new block at the far end, four hundred metres, and the cooperative moves whatever will not go on a belt.

I am sorry about the building. Take your time this morning, and come and find me if anything about your move is unclear.

Why it cannot be repaired§

Vlad

raising his hand Why couldn't the building be rehabilitated or reconstructed, like the panelaks, plattenbauses, and blocuri?

Ilvena

Because renovation works on a sound frame, and this one has lost it. Insulation, windows, a new roof, new lifts, all of that we have done on twenty blocks along this street and it adds forty years. It adds nothing where the reinforcement in the slabs is corroded through and the anchorages are moving. looking up at the north wall We costed the reconstruction in February. Taking out and recasting the floor edges on eight storeys came to more than the new block, and everybody would have had to leave for two years anyway.

Resident

And the dust? gesturing along the street That is eight floors of reinforced concrete coming down. Everything from here to the water tower will be under it, and the windows are open in this weather.

Ilvena

It is not going down with charges. pointing at the roofline A machine works from the top with a jaw and takes it apart floor by floor, and the water cannon runs on the arm the whole time, so the dust is wetted where it is made.

The rest is on us. We give you the dates and you close the windows on those days, and there are screens on the two sides facing the street and a wash for the road every evening. a pause You will still get dust on the sills.

Resident

Then the noise. raising her voice a little from the back A jaw taking apart a concrete floor is worse than somebody drilling a wall, and it carries across the whole courtyard. How long are we listening to that?

Ilvena

Twelve weeks for the structure, and the basement after it. The hours are eight to six with nothing on Sunday, and the loudest part is the first three weeks, when the floor edges come off.

For the nights, anybody who cannot sleep through it puts their name down with me. There are temporary rooms at the school annexe and the cooperative is bringing in multi-capsules for the courtyard behind the new block, and they stay for as long as the work runs. We are also putting dampening on the two faces of the new building that look this way. It comes off when we finish here.

The metal and the cooker§

Resident

older man near the front, in a work jacket And the metal? Where I came from a van went down the street twice a week with a loudspeaker on the roof calling for old iron, batteries, washing machines, televisions, fridges, cookers. a short shrug There is no money here and I still see people carrying doors and pipes out of a yard at the end of a job. Eight floors of rebar sitting in this courtyard is a lot of iron.

Ilvena

You see it because it happens. Somebody takes a radiator off a wall, hands it to a cousin, and the cousin puts a set of shelves on his balcony in return. On paper that transaction does not exist. In practice it exists all over this street, and the reason it stays small is that the same radiator can be booked out of the reuse yard for nothing by anybody who asks.

The site is fenced and the sorting is done inside it. Cable, pipe, radiators, doors, frames, sanitary ware, the good panels, all of it goes to the yard at Sederna gate and comes back into the catalogue, and the rebar goes to Ferrista for melting. The record is published, tonne by tonne, and it stays open.

to the whole courtyard What I ask of you is this. Take from your own flat whatever you want to keep and take it now, before Friday. Anything left in the building on Friday goes into the sort, and once it is inside the fence I have to account for it.

Resident

a woman on the chairs at the front And our things? The cooker in my kitchen is nine years old and it works. Does it come with me, and if the new flat has one already, who takes the old one out? And if the new one breaks in a month, who answers for it?

Ilvena

Your cooker is yours to keep and it goes on the belt with the rest, and the fitters connect it at the other end. The new flats have the connections and nothing standing in them.

Whatever you leave behind goes to the yard, gets tested, and goes back into the catalogue for somebody else. Anything past repair is stripped for parts.

On the guarantee, it is the same as anywhere. Every appliance carries a declared service life, parts are furnished free for the whole of it, and the workshop is on Iupin. If a heater or a lift or a tap fails in the new building, that is on the cooperative and it is repaired without any of you writing to anybody, since we hold the maintenance. a small movement of her hand Water, heat and power go on the day you move in.

The bus and the new block§

Resident

younger man standing by the belts And the transport. The 28 comes right up to this corner and I get on it downstairs. The bus goes nowhere near Silist street, so from the new block I am walking back here to catch anything.

Ilvena

The 28 stays where it is, the depot is not moving a tram line for us, and from the new block it is a four minute walk to the same stop you use now. turning to point down Marginal The bus route is being extended past the junction to the end of Silist with a new stop at the top, and the stop is called Silist after the street.

Resident

middle-aged woman, arms folded And what are we moving into? I have seen what goes up elsewhere. Ten years and the cladding is coming off, and we don't know who built it or what it is made of.

Ilvena

Reinforced concrete frame, cast on site, with the floors as flat slabs and the walls non-loadbearing. The insulation is on the outside in mineral wool, the cladding is fibre cement on rails and every panel comes off with a key, and the balconies stand on their own columns with a thermal break, so a balcony there can be replaced without touching a floor. Five floors, a lift in each of the three stairwells, and the risers run in a shaft you can open from the landing.

to the whole courtyard The design life is a hundred years, with a full inspection every five and the record published each time. Marginal 20 was given sixty and got fifty-nine of them, and the reason it is coming down is the same design fault everywhere in that series, the reinforcement too near the surface.

You can read the whole file. Materials, where each of them came from, who cast what and on what day, the inspection dates, all of it is in the building's record and the copy is at the cooperative office on Iupin, open to anybody.

What it is built to hold§

Resident

older woman, near the chairs And has it been checked for the shaking? a small movement of her hand In Belvacius they lost buildings that everybody said were sound, and those were newer than this one.

Ilvena

It has, twice. The frame was designed to the current seismic code and it was checked again by a second engineer who had nothing to do with the design, which is required above four floors, and both reports are in the file with the calculations in them.

a pause Belvacius is the reason the code was rewritten in 2019. What came down there was mostly infill and stair cores in buildings from the eighties, where the frame held and the stairs failed and people could not get out. The new block has the stairs cast with the frame in one piece, and the partitions are tied back so they stay standing.

I will not tell you a building cannot fall. I can tell you what it is built to hold, and this one is built for step 7 with people inside it and no collapse.

Resident

the same man in the work jacket It is still a plain concrete frame. There are no dampers in it, nothing under the foundation, none of what the Japanese put in their towers.

Ilvena

There are none, and there is nothing here to put them in. Isolators and dampers earn their cost in a tower, where the building is tall enough to sway and the mass at the top does the damage. Five floors do not sway, they take the shaking straight through the frame, so the answer at this height is the reinforcement in the joints and the stairs cast in one piece.

a small movement of her shoulder Nothing in this sub-region is above eight floors, and the code is written for what we actually build.

Akima

from the side, near the bin shelter, her voice carrying just far enough Excuse me. I live in the square block on the diagonal, so none of this is my move. How large are the flats?

Ilvena

finding her at the edge of the crowd Two rooms is fifty-two square metres, three is sixty-eight, four is eighty-six, and every one of them has a balcony of six and a store off the hall. The standard is by household size and it is the same standard in every commune.

Akima

quietly, more to herself than to the courtyard Mine is fifty-four. looking up at the block coming down My flat in Chiba was twenty-eight for two rooms, and I thought I was doing well to have it.

Ilvena

Resident

And the thing is floating. You cannot see it and you cannot feel it standing here. The door on my stairwell binds in the mornings and swings clear by six, the crack over the second landing opens and closes, and last week the frame on my kitchen window sat three millimetres out of where it was in spring. Nothing in that building is holding still.

Ilvena

nodding slowly, and looking at him rather than at the crowd You are describing the readings. There are twelve targets on the north face and they have been moving through the day and back, and the daily range has been getting wider since June, and the anchorages stopped returning to where they started.

to the courtyard This is the whole of it, in ordinary words. The building moves and nothing you can see tells you when.

The wind comes down Marginal§

A gust comes down the length of Marginal and crosses the courtyard, and the north face of the block moves with it. The movement is small and everybody sees it. Something goes in the basement under the second stairwell, a low sound with grinding in it that carries up through the ground and into the soles of everyone standing on the concrete, and the plaster comes off the parapet in a long sheet and breaks on the loading bay.

Ilvena

already moving, her voice up and flat It is going. Everybody back to the far wall, past the frames, now. turning to the belts Get those crates off and clear the doorways, and then leave the belts where they stand.

Ilvena

to the crew at the stairwell doors Out. Anybody inside comes out with their hands empty. Nobody carries anything, nobody goes up a flight for one more box.

Resident

the man with the tape, backing away along the painted line, watching the second stairwell The sound was under the stair. That is the wall in the basement.

The wind comes again, harder and without a break in it this time, and it keeps on coming down the street between the two blocks. The north face goes over with it and stays over, and the parapet line is no longer straight against the sky. Dust starts to come out from under the eaves along the whole length of the building, in a thin continuous run rather than in falls.

Ilvena

counting the crew off the stairwell doors as they come through, one hand up to hold the courtyard back Four out, five, that is six. Who is on the third stairwell?

Crew

last man out, coming at a run with nothing in his hands All out. The doors are open and there is nobody past the ground floor.

Ilvena

pulling the radio off her belt without taking her eyes off the wall Marginal 20 to the depot. Cut the power and the gas to the block now, and get the road closed at both ends of Marginal.

Everybody to the far end§

The wind holds and the north face keeps going with it. The building starts to lose height at the corner, slowly, in a way that is easier to read against the drying frames than against the sky. The floor edges above the fallen balconies crush down onto the ones below them, a few centimetres at each level, and the whole corner takes on a sag that runs from the roof to the second floor. Cracks open along the line of every landing window at once. The frame stops there, standing on the four inner columns and the stair core, which are the only parts of it still sound.

Crew

at the meter cupboard by the first stairwell, pulling the last of the leads clear Power is off, gas is off, water is capped at the main. We are out.

Ilvena

walking the front of the crowd, turning people by the shoulder toward the far end of the street Everybody to the new block, all the way to the end of Marginal, and stay on the far pavement. Leave the crates and leave the belts.

The neighbours go in a loose column past the drying frames and out through the gate, the older residents first with the chairs left where they were standing. Vlad goes near the back with his bag over one shoulder, looking behind him twice.

Akima crosses her own courtyard and goes up. She closes both windows in the kitchen and stands at them. From there the sagging corner is straight in front of her at eye level, three floors down and forty metres away, with dust running out of the joints in the sunlight and the parapet line bent where it used to be flat.

Props, nets and sheeting§

The street empties inside a quarter of an hour. After the megaphone goes round twice the courtyard, the only movement on Marginal is the closure barriers going up at both ends and a road crew wetting the surface ahead of the machines.

The reinforcement goes in the way a pit stop goes: eight people to a stairwell, everything cut to length on the pavement and pushed in. In the diagonal block they start at the ground floor on the courtyard face, props under the landing slabs at every stairwell, adjustable steel to the underside and hardwood plates top and bottom, then hooks driven into the joints and strapped across the two openings that face the sagging corner. The low buildings get the same treatment, and the substation gets a frame around it and boarding over the vents.

Outside, another crew works on the windows. Netting goes over every opening on the courtyard face of both blocks, hung off hooks and pulled tight, fine mesh on the lower three floors and coarser above. The frames on the first two levels are taped where the seals are old. Every entrance door on this side gets a sheet across it, weighted at the bottom and taped to the frame, with a slit down the middle for the crews to pass through.

Crew chief

at the diagonal block door, counting his people back out Props are in and loaded on three stairwells. Nets are on the courtyard face to the fifth. Doors sheeted, both entrances.

Duty engineer

from the courtyard, without turning round Then get everybody behind the barrier and give me the machines.

The corner that fell§

The first machine comes through the gate on its tracks with the jaw folded and the water line already running along the arm. It goes up the far end of the roof, away from the sagging corner, and starts taking the parapet down in bites. The concrete comes off in pieces the size of a table and drops inside the footprint, and the water cannon runs the whole time, so what rises off the pile is a grey mist that stays low.

The work goes slowly and it goes well. The machine clears the eighth floor along the south half, sheet by sheet, moving north a few metres at a time, and the second machine works the pile at the base and keeps the ground clear ahead of it.

Then the north-east corner goes.

It goes with no more sound than the machine is already making, and it takes four floors with it in about two seconds. The corner column buckles at the second landing, the floor slabs above it come down flat onto each other, and the whole quarter of the building folds inward and drops into its own footprint. A wall of dust comes up out of it and rolls across the courtyard as far as the drying frames, over the belts, and up the face of the diagonal block, and the netting on the lower floors goes grey in one movement. The machine on the roof is thirty metres away at the far end and its operator has it backing before the dust reaches him.

DUTY ENGINEER

on the radio, already walking to the barrier All stop. Everybody off the pile and off the roof. Nobody moves until I can see the building.

The dust takes several minutes to drop. What comes out of it is the south half of the block still standing to the eighth floor with the machine on it, and a slope of broken slab where the north-east quarter used to be, running back into the footprint at about forty degrees.

Duty engineer

at the barrier with the survey tablet, taking readings across the face before he says anything It came from underneath. The brace we put across the basement wall in June has taken the load off that corner column and moved it onto the footing, and the footing has gone, so the column lost its base and the four floors above it followed. The sequence started below ground and everything visible up there was the consequence.

turning the tablet toward the crew chief Nothing above the sixth moved on the south half, which is what I would expect if the frame is intact there. What I cannot tell you from here is whether the other three basement braces are doing the same thing more slowly.

into the radio Machines stay off the pile and off the structure until the basement is surveyed. Get me the ground radar and a second engineer, and hold the props in the diagonal block where they are. Nobody works this side of the courtyard.

The first robot gets forty metres along the main corridor before the picture changes. Water is coming across the floor toward it, moving, and by the time it reaches the second cross wall the wheels are throwing a wake.

Engineer

Engineer

Duty engineer

Duty engineer

The crane on the road§

The ball follows on a second trailer with the rigging.

The crew starts on the buildings again while the crane is still being set up. Marginal is narrow here and the standing half of the block is close to both sides, so the props go in a second time and deeper: the diagonal block gets its basement propped along the whole courtyard face, the low buildings get raking props off the pavement into the ground floor openings, and the substation frame is braced back onto its own slab. Duty engineer

Duty engineer

into the radio, watching the standing half Water is capped and the basement is pumped. Nets are on, curtain is up, props are loaded. Take the top course on the south end.

What the ball cannot reach§

The ball works the block down through the morning and into the afternoon, a swing at a time, from the south end toward the corner that fell on its own. The pile in the footprint rises as the building comes off it, and the second machine keeps the ground clear along the road side so the crane always has a hard stand in front of it.

By four there is no building above the first floor. What stands is the ground floor slab on its four inner columns, the stair core to the first landing, and the basement walls under all of it, low enough that the ball cannot swing at them without dropping onto the courtyard.

Duty engineer

walking the edge of the pile with the crew chief The ball has done what it can. Everything left is below the height it works at, and I want the basement walls taken apart, not knocked over, because the ground under the north-east corner is still soft.

Crew chief

Duty engineer
