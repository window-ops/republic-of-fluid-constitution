Thirty minutes early§

After a few hours, the lamp is still off, and the window has gone from black to a flat grey along the bottom, with the coast running past on the left and the spit showing as a dark line across the water. The train has been slowing for some minutes. The panel by the door has stopped counting running time and now shows a station name, with an arrival thirty minutes ahead of the one printed on the record. She leans over the edge of the upper bunk, one hand on the rail, and puts the other lightly on his shoulder.

Akima

keeping her voice very low, close to his shoulder Vlad. waiting until he moves before she says any more We are almost at Leriduson. The panel says thirty minutes before it should, and I think either the panel is wrong or this train arrives early.

Vlad

sitting up the bed It is common for some trains to arrive early.

Akima

drawing her hand back and settling on the rail as he sits up At home a train that runs ahead holds at the next station until the timetable catches it, because every connection after it is built on that minute.

Hours above the average§

Vlad

That's true, and I thought what you said. You have been working more hours than the sixteen thousand average in your country and you have been more conservative in norms than the average of people who both stay in Japan and arrive here, and then Gaman is not really a sufficient explanation for national norms.

Akima

sitting up against the rail I did not know that figure. I only ever counted mine, and it was twenty five hundred, so whatever the country averages, I was never inside it. Gaman was the word I used on myself when I needed a reason to keep going, and it worked well enough that I stopped looking for another one. Two people in my section told me plainly that I was making my life heavier than it had to be. a small pause You know Romania from the inside, which is very far west of us, and north of here.

Vlad

In fact, in Romania we make a lot of generalizations about how Romanians and national identity is a disputed part considering we are a post-socialist country.

Akima

nodding slowly, watching the coast go past We generalise about ourselves constantly. There is a whole shelf of books explaining what the Japanese are, and programmes where foreigners are invited to confirm it. The weight comes when the same sentence arrives from outside, because then it lands as a verdict, and the room goes quiet and polite around it. a small pause We also never argued about who we were. Yours sounds disputed, so people can pull at it. Ours was settled, so the description held, and when I failed to match it the fault was mine.

Vlad

Akima

Emergency earthquake warning§

The lamp is still off. Somewhere above the door a relay clicks, and the running noise of the train drops as the brakes are being applied.

TRAIN VOICE

two rising tones, twice, then a flat recorded voice in Fluidian and again in English Emergency earthquake warning. An earthquake has occurred on the southern margin, beneath the Belvacius hills. Estimated intensity here, 3 of 10. Slight shaking expected in about forty seconds. Move to the floor, get under the fixed furniture or beside a fixed seat frame, hold on, and keep clear of the doors and the windows. This train is braking. No coastal warning is in force.

Akima

already down off the upper bunk and on the floor with her back against the bunk frame before the message finishes, one hand out for his arm Come down here. quietly, once he is beside her My body did that before I understood the words. The two tones are almost the ones from home.

TRAIN VOICE

the two tones again, then the flat voice continuing in Fluidian and in English Earthquake detail. Origin time 04:12. Epicentre on the southern margin beneath the Belvacius hills, depth 24 kilometres. Magnitude 5.4 on the moment scale, given here as Richter for comparison. Estimated intensity at this location, step 3 of 10 on the Republic scale, equal to shindo 3 on the Japanese scale from which the Republic scale is derived, and to IV on the Mercalli, European macroseismic and MSK scales. Step 3 means shaking felt by most people aboard, hanging objects swinging, glass and crockery moving, no damage to structures. Highest estimated intensity anywhere is step 5 in the Belvacius sub-region. Shaking here begins in about twenty seconds.

Vlad

holding a bar under the bunk bed So detailed... and it's the first early earthquake warning, or rather, kinkyu jishin sokuho, I experienced.

TRAIN VOICE

the flat voice again, under the sound of the brakes Estimated intensity by sub-region. Step 5, Belvacius. Step 4, Ravelluri, Cortius, Quintorum, Sornorum. Step 3, Anselaqui, Marnaqui, Thurioma, Esteveduson, and Leriduson. Step 2, Kelvaqui, Tessilius, Durorilaqui, Halvorum, Aspreduson. Step 1 or below in the remaining western sub-regions, including Lindoma. No coastal warning is in force. Shaking now.

The floor lifts once, softly, and then a second time, and the lamp cord swings against the frame of the upper bunk for a few seconds after the movement has gone.

Akima

keeping her hand on the bar until the cord stops That is all of it. Kinkyu jishin sokuho, you said it correctly. It is my first one here as well. a small breath At home you get the tone and four words, and the rest reaches you on television an hour later when you are already at your desk. This one read out the entire country to us while the floor was still moving.

Vlad

This is because the meteorological agency does not differentiate broadcasts. The NHK model is applied.

Akima

letting go of the bar and sitting back against the bunk frame I heard it. That is the NHK voice exactly, the same flat delivery, the same order of items. At home it comes to you in pieces. First the tone and the region, then the intensity a minute later, then the map with the sub-prefectures filled in once somebody has drawn it. This one gave us the depth, the magnitude, the scale equivalents and every sub-region in the country before the shaking arrived.

Vlad

It is because many people around here do not understand what an early earthquake warning is, as a recent invention by the national meteorological agency. Earthquakes are rare events and the warning is not well-integrated.

Akima

Then the length of it makes sense. You explain everything when nobody has heard it before. At home the explaining was done for you in school, so the broadcast could be short. Here the broadcast has to carry the school as well. a small pause I do not know how well it works. I suppose you find that out on the day something bigger comes.

Densification§

Vlad

They have not considered the fact that both Fluidian and English are both inefficient are transmitting lots of information at rapid pace.

Akima

thinking back through the sound of it Sometimes. The Fluidian ran first and it finished before the English did. counting off the two she is sure of Abaxa and tena. Abaxa is get down, and it is the word a driver uses for slowing. Tena is hold, and it is also hold a post, and keep going a while longer. a small shrug English wanted a clause for each of those. My Fluidian is poor, so I may have the third sense of tena wrong.

Vlad

The language academy named this aspect densification rather than polymorphism.

Akima

Densification. That is a fair name, and we do more of it than you do here. counting it out on her fingers Sumimasen is an apology, a thank you and an excuse me. Daijoubu covers am I hurt, do you need help, and no thank you. a small pause We also take things out. The warning at home has no subject at all and nobody is confused by that. Fluidian keeps every word in its place. It gives one word more work to do and it never shortens the sentence around it.

Vlad

And then some words are suffixed while others are not. For example, "jizde" stands for "ride" and "riding" at the same time. I say "me no jizde" when saying that I do not ride and "me no se jizde" when saying that I am not taking the ride, where "se" is an object article.

Akima

repeating both of them under her breath to hear the join Me no jizde. Me no se jizde. looking at him I have heard se hundreds of times and I took it for a small sound people put in for rhythm. a pause We hang that off the particles. Norimasen with wa in front of it says I am not someone who rides. The same verb with a different particle says this ride, tonight, the one you are asking me about. The verb itself does not move.

Vlad

And then some words change meaning by suffix, so Huclavy is a real place name in Lutoliluri far south, huclav means hungry, huclava means the hungry one and huclav is the base form. People argued the word derived from the name Hulvaky from Ostrava, since we had contact with Ostrava where they gave us trams produced in Prague, but the academy said it doesn't come from there.

Akima

the shape of it settling Huclav, huclava, Huclavy. So the place is the hungry ones. a pause I never once looked up what Baraki meant. Nakayama is middle mountain and there is no mountain anywhere near it. The name was simply the sound the train made before the doors opened.

The irregulars§

Vlad

Suffixes are sometimes pretty weird, because -y sometimes means place name and other times means property of object, so it depends on whether the word it came from was an adjective, in which case it converts to a noun, or a noun, in which case it converts to an adjective.

Akima

working it through slowly Then the suffix decides nothing on its own, and you have to know what the word was before it can tell you anything. Huclav is an adjective, so huclavy makes it a place. Pod is a noun, so pody would come out meaning of the bridge. checking his face Have I got that the right way round?

Vlad

In general yes, but pody is not a valid form since it's an irregular word. The adjective form of pod is podak.

Akima

Podak. saying it twice, the stress landing where he put it So I had the rule and the wrong word to try it on. a small pause Is there a list of the irregular ones somewhere, or do people simply learn them one at a time from being corrected?

Face time and output§

Vlad

Did the warehouse where you worked at prioritized face time, output or both?

Akima

Output, on paper. The shift had a target and the target was the whole of it, and when the last pallet went out the work was finished. Then there was the other layer when the section head sat until eight and nobody stood up before him, so you found things to do with your hands for an hour after the job was done.

Marliene

leaning forward with her elbows on her knees And what did they pay you for all that?

Akima

Not much. Enough for the flat and the fare and something left over that I did not spend. looking out as a level crossing goes past I looked at other work. There were jobs paying higher for the same hours, some of them for fewer. a small pause I never applied to one.

Akima

The docks warehouse. I went there because it was the work I already knew how to do well, and they were generous about taking me with the language I had. Case 41§

Vlad

It is twenty hours by organic law, and then of course there are lots of constitutional arguments for whether self-imposed overtime should be less sanctionable than cooperative overtime. At the same time, in the tech sector, it is difficult to separate between volunteering and work.

Marliene

turning her phone sideways and holding it out between the seats It is all filmed, so you can watch the whole thing. This is the one from the spring, the Marendo software cooperative case.

Constitutional Court, plenary sitting
Case 41 of this year. Challenge under Article 43 to section 9 of the organic law on working time

Justice Tarvel: Counsel, your members worked outside the registered hours. On what article do you place that work?

Counsel for the cooperative: On Article 70, paragraph 6. Every person chooses the form, the place and the duration of their contribution. The work in question was a defect in a public transport system. Correcting it is contribution within paragraph 2.

Justice Tarvel: Article 72, paragraph 1, admits of no exception in its terms. Weekly working time in no case exceeds twenty-four hours. Does your reading leave that paragraph with any content?

Counsel for the cooperative: It leaves it with its content in full. Paragraph 1 binds the enterprise. The enterprise may schedule no more than twenty-four hours and it may require no more. The question before the Court is whether the same paragraph binds the person against their own registered wish.

Justice Semiran: Then the ceiling would hold only where somebody is compelled, and the enterprise obtains the additional hours by welcoming them. Article 72, paragraph 3, secures disconnection outside working time. A right that the holder may sign away in advance is a right the employer will arrange to have signed away.

Counsel for the cooperative: The right was exercised. Nine of the eleven persons concerned disconnected and the systems recorded it. Two continued.

Justice Semiran: And of the two, one is the person who wrote the component that failed.

Counsel for the cooperative: That is so.

Justice Tarvel: The Office of the Defender of Rights submits that the distinction between voluntary and directed labour is unascertainable in this sector, since the work is performed alone, on a network, without observation. Counsel, how does the Court verify a wish that leaves no trace apart from the work itself?

Counsel for the cooperative: By the register. The hours appear, they are counted, and the ceiling of twenty hours in the year is applied to them. Nothing was hidden from the Court or from the cooperative.

Justice Odena: Article 70, paragraph 4, provides that the duty of contribution gives rise to no sanction. The section under challenge sanctions the person. Counsel for the Assembly, address that.

Counsel for the Assembly: Paragraph 4 governs a failure to contribute. It says nothing of an excess, and the excess is what Article 72 addresses. The sanction falls upon the enterprise in the first place and upon the person only where a course of conduct is established over three years.

Justice Odena: Then the person who fixes one defect on one night is outside the section entirely.

Counsel for the Assembly: Outside it entirely.

Vlad

In the case of this software company, the constitutional court banned overtime.

Marliene

taking the phone back and scrolling to the end of the record They upheld the section and read Article 72 as binding on the person as well. The cooperative had to close the network outside hours, so the desks lock now.

Akima

I agree with them. On my first week here I saw a crew stopped on the road outside Sederna for collecting scrap past their hours, and the officer counted them and sent them home. I remember thinking it was excessive. Then the organic law allows twenty hours a year and the Court says the ceiling binds the person, and those two do not sit together.

How many panel buildings there are

There are about 41,200 panel buildings in the Republic, built between 1958 and 1989. They house 6.2 million people, one third of the population. Most stand in the estates of Lindoma, Durorilaqui, Leriduson and Cortius. The commonest series is the P-4 type, of eight floors, with four stairwells and lifts in two of them.
