The register underneath§

Vlad

So, if you are from the Netherlands and you acquire a citizenship in the Republic, you lose your citizenship. But if you are from Japan and you got here, how did you lose your citizenship exactly?

Akima

I have not lost it. My passport is Japanese and it stays Japanese for another two months, and the five years here are three and a half years away. What went is the registration underneath it, which is a different thing.

The koseki is the family register and the juminhyo is the residence record built on top of it, and when you move abroad the ward office takes the juminhyo off. My name is still on my father's koseki, and its registered address is in Chiba. Kochi is my mother's side, so my grandfather's house has no register of mine in it at all. On paper I am a national with no address in the country, so nothing that runs off an address works for me there: the health insurance, the pension, the seal registration, a bank counter, a phone contract.

Japan does the same thing the Netherlands does, though. If I reach five years here and the registrar writes it down, that is another nationality, and they expect you to choose within two years.

Vlad

So, the conditions are different from that of a visitor or tourist.

Akima

They are worse. A tourist walks in on a foreign passport, nothing is expected from them, and they leave in a week with the stamp closing it.

I walk in on a Japanese passport, so I am a national coming home, and a national is expected to have an address. Fluid gives me none they will read, since I am short of the five years and hold no citizenship here, and there is no consulate of theirs to explain me to them.

So I am a Japanese woman with a passport running out and nothing underneath it, and the counter has one procedure for that, which is to put me back on the register and spend time.

Vlad

And when they put you back into the registrar, you are expected to stay at home even though Fluid allows you to go back?

Akima

It is the other way. A short visit is treated as a stay abroad, so the ward office will refuse a re-registration for anyone home under a year, and I would be in Kochi with no address, no insurance card and a passport about to expire.

To be registered I have to be back to live, which means declaring an address and filing within fourteen days of arriving, and from that moment I am a resident of Japan again with the tax and the pension and the insurance running on me.

Fluid would let me fly home the same week, and the paper in Japan would say I live in Chiba while I sleep in Lindoma and work at the docks. So one side has me missing and the other side has me lying.

What the papers are worth§

A few days later, Vlad searches for what a resident actually has to do to leave the country and lands on cvria.fl, an explainer site kept by a group of law students at Lutoliluri.

cvria.fl · Travel and frontiers
Leaving the Republic: what your papers are worth abroad

1. The starting point

Article 57(3) says every person may leave the territory and return to it. Nothing in the Constitution permits an exit condition, an exit visa, a tax clearance, an employer's release or a police check, and Article 296(5) forbids the Republic from agreeing with any state to prevent a departure. Your own side of the journey is therefore settled before you begin.

The difficulty is on the other side. A frontier is opened by agreement, and the Republic has fewer of those than a state of its size normally has, because Article 291 removes what large partners would ask in exchange. Article 290(3) then requires a referendum for any agreement opening a frontier to the free movement of persons. Frontier agreements are consequently slow to make and few in number.

2. Where a resident of the Republic may go freely

  • Allied States of Iosmein: open land frontier at the Corvaluri crossing since 1989, and open in the Bloc sense since 1994. You enter, remain, work, study and receive care on the footing of a resident there, under Article 296(2).
  • Free Republic of Reformed Luces Aporia: same footing, reached by the maritime corridor from Lutoliluri to Sluland. This is the route to Zecina airport, which since its opening is the only airport in the Bloc.

The traffic runs both ways on identical terms. An Iosmenian or an Aporean arriving here enters, works and receives care as a resident of the Republic does.

3. Where a resident of the Republic needs a visa

Everywhere else, in practice. The Republic issues a passport recognised as a travel document, and the recognition ends there. Consular representation abroad is thin: a small number of missions, several of them shared with Iosmein, and none at all in most of Asia, Africa and the Americas. Article 58(3) states that citizenship confers no right a resident lacks and exists to represent the person before other states. What that representation is worth at a foreign desk depends entirely on the state at the desk.

Consequences a traveller should plan around:

  • Visas are applied for in person, at a mission that is often in another country: budget the journey to the mission as part of the journey.
  • Appointments are the constraint, not the fee: nothing charged here covers a foreign fee, which is met from your brought-in balance at the currency cooperative.
  • A residence certificate from a communal office is the document foreign desks understand least: carry it, expect it to be read twice, and carry the passport as the operative document.
  • Bloc membership carries no weight outside the Bloc: no third state grants anything on account of it.

4. Foreign nationals resident here have an easier journey

Residence in the Republic attaches to presence and intention under Article 57(2), and it does not touch the nationality you arrived with. Article 58(1) grants citizenship automatically at five years, with no oath and no renunciation, and Article 58(4) prohibits deprivation.

A foreign national resident here therefore holds two things at once. Inside the Republic and inside the Bloc, they move as a resident. Outside it, they travel on the passport of their state of origin, with whatever visa waivers that passport carries. An Austrian resident in the Republic uses the Austrian passport for a journey to Eswatini and reaches the desk on the same footing as any other Austrian.

Two cautions:

  • Your state of origin may read the five-year certificate as a voluntary acquisition of another nationality: several jurisdictions withdraw their own nationality on that ground, and the automatic character of the Fluid procedure has not persuaded them. Establish your own country's rule before the five years elapse.
  • Consular protection abroad follows the passport you travel on: the mission of your state of origin assists you. The Republic can rarely do so and in most countries it cannot establish missions.

5. The European Union

The Union is the Republic's largest trading partner and there is no free movement agreement, so a resident travelling on a Fluid passport applies for a visa in the ordinary way. Nationals of Union member states who reside here keep their own passports and their own rights of entry, so the journey is trivial for them and troublesome for everybody else. The Republic's standing objection to the externalisation of the Union frontier, which Articles 296(5) and 296(6) require it to make, has no bearing on an individual application.

6. Kingdom of Bor Brashavenko

The southern land frontier has been closed since 1939 and no crossing exists. There is no mission of the Republic in the Kingdom, no consular assistance of any kind, and no route home if something goes wrong. Travel there is strongly discouraged. Anyone who goes despite this should understand that the Republic can do nothing for them beyond what a third state agrees to do on its behalf.

7. Before you go

  • Check the expiry of the passport you will actually travel on, and count months or years rather than weeks.
  • Establish the visa rule for that passport, and not for your residence.
  • Arrange foreign currency at the currency cooperative on notice, since currency here does not convert at the frontier.
  • Register nothing on departure. No exit declaration exists, and your dwelling, your provision and your registration are unaffected by an absence of ordinary length.
