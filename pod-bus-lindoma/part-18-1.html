An hour out from Lindoma§

The call§

Vlad

dialing Nozaki Alright, this is it.

Nozaki

picking up on the second ring, her voice low and close to the phone I did not think you would ring straight away.

Vlad

I want to give you a virtual hug.

Nozaki

the sound of her shifting under the blanket, then her breath going uneven again I have been fine all week. I got up, I went in, I did the work, I came back, and then you wrote and it all came apart in about four minutes. a long unsteady breath You held onto me by the water and I felt taken care of, and then I put you on a plane and came back to a flat where no one speaks to me until the morning. I go through a whole day now and every part of it is a person handing me something and not looking at me, and I did not notice how much of that there was until three days when it was not like that.

Vlad

I looked up on the interned to find opinions against Tokyo's urban sprawl, and there was virtually no one that said, well, this is desolate and full of concrete. Everyone said it was efficient.

Nozaki

sniffing, then something closer to a laugh Of course they said that. The people writing about it come for a week and they ride the trains, and the trains are the good part. Nobody writes the rest down because the rest is not a photograph. Forty minutes standing, a room the size of the bed, the shops all the same six shops. quiet for a second I have said it is efficient myself, to people at work. It is easier than saying I have lived here my whole life and it has never once felt like it was mine.

The timetable and the driver§

Vlad

And now I think that train simulators are a symbol of just how strict the conditions are. If you are late, the timetable does not shift for you. In one game you have to try catch up with the timetable, but of course, you can't because of speed restrictions and accumulated lateness, while in another game you have to wait until the next lot if you are a few minutes late or early.

Nozaki

the blanket moving again, her voice steadier now The one everybody knows here is the crash at Amagasaki, near Osaka, the year I was still at school. The driver had overrun a platform and was running about a minute and a half behind, and he went into the curve far too fast and the front of the train went into a block of flats. What came out afterwards was that the company used to send drivers off the trains for being late, to pull weeds and clean the toilets and write out apologies where everyone could see them, and he had been through it before. a pause on the line So your game has the shape of it right. The timetable holds and the person bends.

Vlad

And you have to bend too.

Nozaki

I bend every day and nobody dies of it. the blanket again, then the sound of her sitting up I have to sleep. I am on the early shift and I take the first Chuo, so I am up in five hours.

Vlad

Then why don't you sleep now?

Nozaki

Because I want to cry for a while longer first, and I would rather do it with you on the line than after you hang up.

Crying on the line§

Vlad

letting her cry and staying silent

Nozaki

crying quietly for a long time, the phone against the pillow, then her voice coming back thin I hate that anyone sees this. At my mother's house I do it in the bathroom with the tap running, and at work I go out to the stairwell. You are the second person who has heard it and the first one was you as well.

Vlad

You can cry for as long as you want.

Nozaki

a long breath that ends halfway I am tired of all of it. I know I have said that to you already and I am saying it again because it is still true in the middle of the night. I do not want to be a woman who breaks. I get up, I go, I hold my mother's arm on the stairs, I do the shift, and none of that stops because I am lying here doing this. If I let myself be the kind of person who comes apart, there is no one behind me to take the week.

Vlad

It's okay to feel like that, I am here.

Nozaki

the crying easing off, her breathing slower against the phone I know you are. It is the whole reason it came out.

Weak and strong§

Nozaki

starting again, quieter this time, the phone slipping down onto the pillow Naijaku da ne, watashi. Kore, ichiban shitakunakatta koto.

wiping her face with the back of her hand Nannen mo hitori de yatte kite, ima ni natte kore. Denwa no mukou ni hito ga iru dake de kore.

How weak I am. This is the thing I least wanted to do.

Years of doing it on my own, and now this. One person on the other end of a phone and this is what happens.

Vlad

What is that?

Nozaki

a wet, small laugh Sorry. I forgot who I was talking to for a second. I said I am weak, and that this is the thing I wanted least, and that years on my own did nothing and one person on a phone did this.

Vlad

You're not weak. In fact, you're strong for this.

Nozaki

quiet for a while Everyone says that to a woman who is still standing. It is easier to say than to ask what it costs her.

Vlad

Then I did a great offense.

Nozaki

No. You are the one who asked.

Why she moved the flights§

Vlad

Nozaki

the phone shifting as she lies back down You came for Akima's grandfather. That was the whole of it when I stood at the barrier at arrivals, a favour for a woman I write to, three days and then home. Somewhere on the way to Kochi it turned into me wanting the days for myself, and I was moving flights so I would have the evening.

Vlad

There was this divide, compassion versus empathy, where some believed that empathy is not enough, and that we need compassion, we need action.

Nozaki

You did the action part before you knew me. You got on two planes for a woman you have never seen in a room, sat two days on trains, and went up a valley to an old man who talked at you in a dialect you could not follow. And here you can do nothing at all, you are on a train on the other side of the world, and I still feel better than I did four hours ago. So it is not that I need you to fix anything.

The announcement§

Vlad

Nozaki

Kiken desu kara, kiiroi sen no uchigawa ni sagatte omachi kudasai. Ressha ga mairimasu made, ima shibaraku omachi kudasai. Nanbansen ka wa shiranai kedo, mou sanbansen de ii ya. Otsukaresama deshita. (Shortly, at platform three, the train from Torvena bound for Lindoma will arrive. It is dangerous, so please stand back behind the yellow line. Please wait a short while until the train arrives. Vlad

Shortly, at platform three, the train to Lindoma from Torvena will arrive... and then the usual stuff...

Nozaki

The rest of it is the yellow line and being told to wait, which is most of what any of them say. The part I put in the middle was me admitting I do not know your platforms, and the last thing was otsukaresama deshita.

Vlad

Which is...

Nozaki

You have worked hard, near enough. It is what you say to someone who has got to the end of something.

Vlad

in mock annoyance Stimați călători, vă rugăm să stați în spatele liniei galbene în morții lu' măta. (Passengers, please stand back behind the yellow line, and a curse on your dead.)

laughing now, the first time all call I understood the first half of that and I am fairly sure the second half was not on the recording.

Nozaki

Vlad

Nozaki

You saw it at the good hour. Try it at eight in the morning, when the man behind you decides where your feet go.

Amagasaki and the derailment on the line there, the retraining that drivers were sent to for lateness, Osaka, Kochi, Haneda, Tokyo, Koganei, the Chuo line, the National Health Insurance House in Romania and the Romanian sentence printed here are real, and no announcement, procedure or account printed here comes from the body it is attributed to. Nozaki gives the crash as a woman who was at school when it happened and has heard it discussed since, and the wording is hers.

The offshore platform, the case filed against it, the drafting pages under the DSB section and the line from Torvena into Lindoma belong to the Republic. Nozaki answers out of shift work, a long commute and three days a week at her mother's house, and Vlad out of five years in the Republic and a household in Romania that has never lost anything. Neither of them is describing a country, and the households and workplaces for which none of this holds are outside the call.

Japanese is printed in Hepburn as spoken, with the English following in brackets. The station announcement is Nozaki's own, put together out of the ones she hears every day, and the platform number in it is invented.
