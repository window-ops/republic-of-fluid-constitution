The side door§

The airport bus takes them in to Kochi station and they sleep the night there. In the morning it is the local out of Kochi, seven small stations and about twenty-five minutes to Tosayamada, then the bus up the Monobe from the front of the station, the river on one side and the terraces climbing on the other, and it sets them down on the road below the houses. The third one up on the upstream side is wooden with grey tile and a persimmon tree at the corner, and they come around it to the side door.

Nozaki

putting her bag down on the concrete and pushing her hair back behind her ear Third house, persimmon, side door, so this is it. I go first and say who we are, and you stand beside me.

Vlad

standing beside her Alright.

Nozaki knocks twice on the frame and steps back a little. There is a long nothing, then a sound of movement inside, and the door slides back about a third of the way. Satomi is standing in it in a work shirt and a cardigan, shorter than either of them, holding the edge of the door with one hand.

Nozaki

bowing, her bag still on her shoulder Ohayou gozaimasu. Satomi-san de irasshaimasu ka. Nozaki to moushimasu. Akima-san no kawari ni, kochira no kata to futari de ukagaimashita. (Good morning. Are you Satomi? My name is Nozaki. We have come in Akima's place, the two of us.)

Satomi

looking at her, then past her at Vlad, then back Kiichuu, kiichuu. Musume kara denwa ga atta ki. Akima no kawari no hito yaro. (I have heard, I have heard. My daughter telephoned. You are the ones coming in Akima's place.)

Nozaki

half turning her head toward him without stepping out of the doorway He says he has heard about it, that his daughter telephoned him, and that we are the ones coming in Akima's place.

Satomi

sliding the door the rest of the way open and moving back off the step Sonna toko ni tatteneede, hairiya. Chanoma wa soko ja ki. Ocha demo dasu ki, agatte. (Do not stand out there, come in. The sitting room is right through there. I will put some tea on, come up.)

Nozaki

He is telling us to come in and stop standing at the door. The sitting room is straight ahead and he is going to make tea. Shoes off on the step, and put them together facing out.

Vlad

removing his shoes and placing them facing outside I do not understand Japanese, so Nozaki will be my interpreter.

Nozaki

stepping up after him and setting her bag against the wall Kochira no kata wa nihongo ga wakarimasen node, watashi ga tsuuyaku itashimasu. Ruumania kara irasshaimashita. (This gentleman does not understand Japanese, so I will interpret. He has come from Romania.)

Satomi

stopping in the passage with one hand on the frame and turning round Ruumania. looking at Vlad for a moment Toui toko kara yoo kite kureta. Watashi wa eigo mo nanchaa dekihan ki, sonata ni zenbu makasu. (Romania. You have come a long way. I cannot manage any English at all, so I leave the whole of it to you.)

Vlad

putting his bag alongside hers and walking toward the sitting room So, this is the sitting room.

Nozaki

following him in and looking around the room once This is it. Six mats, the low table in the middle, the television against that wall and the altar in the corner with his wife's photograph on it, which is Akima's grandmother. Sit on the side away from the door and leave the cushion nearest the altar empty.

Icons, temples and what is on paper§

Vlad

sitting on the mat away from the door The alter is significant, like the alters of Fluidian churches for those that still go to church.

Nozaki

kneeling down on the far side of the table It is a butsudan, the household one, so every house that keeps a dead person has a small version of the same thing. The photograph is his wife, the incense in front of her is lit in the mornings, and the bell is struck once before anyone speaks to her. When he comes back in you can kneel there a moment with your hands together, and he will take that as you having greeted her.

Vlad

hearing the kettle in the far side of the house Alright, I'll wait.

Nozaki

sitting back on her heels and listening toward the kitchen He is slow with it. I heard him fill the kettle and then nothing for a while.

Vlad

My grandmother had a lot of icons and intricate patterns in her flat because she practises Orthodox Christianity.

Nozaki

I have never been inside a house with icons in it. The only ones I have seen are photographs, gold faces on a dark wall.

Vlad

Many icons are carved into wood and they often represent saints. Today, it is common practice to pray in front of them, but back in 726-842, during the Iconoclasm, there was a camp that thought that icons were carved faces, not true representations of saints, so they were destroyed, although the political cause is not exactly clear, common theories being the Islamic raise and weakening of Byzantine power.

Nozaki

I did not know any of that. hearing something set down in the kitchen I have Buddhism at funerals and a shrine at New Year and nothing that anybody argued about, so a country tearing its own images down is not a thing I can picture.

Vlad

Do you practice Buddhism?

Nozaki

No. I go to a shrine at New Year with everyone else, buy the paper and write a wish on it and tie it to the frame, and that is the whole of it.

Vlad

On paper, I am technically an Orthodox Christian, except that in practice I am non-religious and my religious friend told me that you are not meant to baptize at birth, you are meant to choose when to baptize, when you have the judgement to decide whether you should be baptized or not, and I have not decided to baptize.

Nozaki

There is nothing written down for me anywhere. My mother's house belongs to a temple, which is how the funerals get done, and I understand it goes by the house and not by the person in it, so I was not asked whether I agree to any of it.

Vlad

It is often thought, in my country of origin, that if I get baptized, I am permanently tied to that religion, even though the rules of the religion itself say that you can self-baptize.

Nozaki

Here a house can leave its temple, and I know that only because my mother said once that it costs money to do and that she would rather not deal with the priest, so it sits there undecided. hearing the tray knock against the doorframe He is coming.

Vlad

sitting quiet, waiting for him to come

The tea and the empty days§

Satomi

coming in sideways with a lacquer tray held in both hands, setting it down on the low table and lowering himself onto the cushion by the altar Machashite kannin ne. Yuu kappu ga mitsukaranankute. (Sorry to have kept you waiting. I could not find the good cups.)

Nozaki

taking the cup he pushes toward her with both hands Osoreirimasu. Watashitachi wa kamainai desu kara, douzo omotase shita nante osshaeranaide kudasai. (Not at all. We are fine either way, so please do not apologise to us for that.)

Satomi

pouring the second cup and putting it in front of Vlad, then sitting back with his hands on his knees Kamawan, kamawan. Ichinichi juu nanchaa suru koto nai ki ne. Asa okite, hatake mite, terebi tsukete, sore de owari ja. Machiyou ni mo, hito ga kuru koto nanka mettani nai ki, kyou wa yokatta. (Never mind about that. There is nothing at all to fill a day with here. I get up, I look at the vegetable patch, I put the television on, and that is the whole of it. Few people come to this house, so today is a good day.)

Nozaki

setting her cup down and turning to Vlad He says never mind about it. There is nothing to fill a day with up here, he gets up, looks at the vegetable patch, puts the television on, and that is the whole of it. Few people come to this house, so today is a good day for him.

Vlad

What happened exactly, why few people come here?

Nozaki

turning back to him Kochira no kata ga, naze koko ni wa hito ga amari konaku natta no ka, kikitagatte imasu. (He is asking why it is that people stopped coming up here.)

Satomi

lifting his head toward the window and the road below it Minna outa n ja. Waka mon wa Osaka e itte, kaette kon. Kono ue no ie mo, sono mata ue mo, akiya yo. Nokottoru no wa toshiyori bakari de, sono toshiyori mo hitori zutsu inou naru. (They all left. The young ones went to Osaka and did not come back. The house above this one is empty, and the one above that as well. Only old people are left, and the old people go one at a time.)

Nozaki

They all left. The young ones went to Osaka and never came back, the house above this one is empty and the one above that as well, and what is left is old people who go one at a time.

Vlad

So, everyone is leaving.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi with her cup still in her hand Minna dete iku n desu ne, to iutte imasu. (He says that means everybody is leaving.)

Satomi

nodding slowly, looking at the table Sou yo. Washi ga wakakatta koro wa, kono tani ni gakkou ga atte, kodomo ga sanjuunin bakari otta. Ima wa basu mo hi ni yon hon ja. Deteiku no wa shikata nai. Koko ni nani mo nai ki ne. (That is right. When I was young there was a school in this valley with about thirty children in it. Now the bus runs four times a day. Leaving is the sensible thing, since there is nothing here.)

Nozaki

When he was young there was a school in this valley with about thirty children in it. The bus is down to four a day now. He says leaving is the sensible thing to do, because there is nothing here.

How many people are left§

Vlad

A demographic collapse is occurring at the Monobe, near Kochi.

Nozaki

turning back to Satomi Kono hen wa mou jinkou ga kuzurete iru, tte iutte imasu. Monobe no tani zentai no hanashi da to. (He says the population up here has already collapsed, and that he means the Monobe valley as a whole.)

Satomi

turning his cup once on the table without lifting it Sou ja. Washi ga kodomo no koro wa, kono tani ni ichiman nin bakari otta to iwarete ita. Mura ga shi ni narou koro ni wa, mou san zen nin gurai ja. Ima wa nannin oru ka, kazoeta koto mo nai. (That is so. When I was a boy they used to say there were about ten thousand people in this valley. By the time the village was folded into the city it was down to around three thousand. How many are here now I have not counted.)

Nozaki

When he was a boy they used to say there were about ten thousand people in this valley. By the time the village was folded into the city it was down to around three thousand, and how many are here now he has not counted.

Vlad

In Romania, a massive emigration occurred due to poor economic and political conditions, and now the population is down to nineteen million, and Fluid has eighteen million people and is stable.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Ruumania de mo, keizai to seiji ga waruku natte, hito ga ooku kuni o dete itta sou desu. Ima wa sen kyuuhyaku man nin gurai de, kono kata ga ima sunde iru kuni wa sen happyaku man nin, soko wa hette inai sou desu. (In Romania many people left the country because the economy and the politics went bad, and it is down to about nineteen million now. The country he lives in has eighteen million and is holding steady.)

Satomi

looking up at Vlad for the first time in a while Sonnara, koko to onaji koto ga, kuni no ookisa de okitoru wake ja ne. Deteitta mon wa, kaette kuru gaka. (So the same thing that happened here is going on at the size of a country. Do the ones who left come back?)

Nozaki

He says the same thing that happened here is happening at the size of a country. He is asking whether the ones who left come back.

Vlad

The ones who left usually don't come back.

Nozaki

turning back to him Deteitta hito wa, taitei kaette konai sou desu. (He says the ones who left mostly do not come back.)

Satomi

nodding once, both hands back on his knees Onaji ja. Bon ni kaette kite, haka ni te o awasete, sanjuunichi ni wa mou densha ni notoru. (The same here. They come back at Bon, put their hands together at the grave, and by the thirtieth they are on the train again.)

Nozaki

The same here. They come back at Bon, put their hands together at the grave, and by the thirtieth they are on the train again. Bon is in the middle of August, three days when the dead are said to come back to the house, so the whole country moves at once and everyone goes to the family grave.

What is done with the dead§

Vlad

We have parastas, a year celebration of the dead, when you eat a lot of food, and the food you eat is said to feed the soul of the dead. More food than you can eat is cooked.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Ruumania ni mo, nakunatta hito no tame ni toshi ni ichido atsumaru gyoushiki ga aru sou desu. Takusan ryouri o tsukutte, tabekirenai hodo dashite, sore o tabete kuyou suru to. (He says in Romania there is a rite once a year for the dead as well. They cook a lot, put out more than anyone can eat, and eating it is how the dead are looked after.)

Satomi

reaching over to the altar and tapping the small dish in front of the photograph Kotchi wa gyaku ja. Asa, meshi to mizu o chiisai wan de sonaeru dake yo. Yuge ga tomattara sagete, ie ni oru mon ga sore o kuu. Sonaeru no wa nioi to yuge ja ki, ryou wa kankei nai. (Ours is the other way around. In the morning rice and water go up in a little bowl and that is all of it. Once the steam stops it comes down again and whoever is in the house eats it. What is offered is the smell and the steam, so the quantity has nothing to do with it.)

Nozaki

Theirs is the other way around. Rice and water go up in a little bowl in the morning and that is all of it, and it comes down again once the steam has stopped, and whoever is in the house eats it. What is offered is the steam and the smell, so the amount never matters.

Vlad

And in Fluid, there is no such practice. Church exists, but almost no one goes to it.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Ano kuni ni wa kou iu narawashi wa nai sou desu. Kyoukai wa aru keredo, iku hito wa hotondo inai to. (He says there is no custom like this in his country. There are churches, but almost nobody goes to them.)

Satomi

glancing at the altar and then back Sore de mo ie no naka ni nanika oiteru n ja rou. Nanchaa nakattara, shinda mon wa doko ni oru gaka. (There must still be something kept in the house, surely. If there is nothing at all, where do the dead stay?)

Nozaki

He says there must still be something kept inside the house. If there is nothing at all, he wants to know where the dead are supposed to stay.

Vlad

There is not much consensus on where the dead are supposed to stay. Right now, the current law offers choices between burial, cremation at the power plant, storage for legal medicine or organ donation, but there are proposals still debated on making organ donation or legal medicine default with burial and cremation as signed opt-ins while the person is still alive.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Ano kuni de wa, shinda ato no koto wa mada kimatte inai sou desu. Ima wa dosou ka, hatsudensho de yaku ka, houigaku ni azukeru ka, zouki teikyou ka, sono yottsu kara erabu. Sore o gyaku ni shite, houigaku to zouki teikyou o hajime kara no atarimae ni shite, dosou to kasou wa ikite iru uchi ni jibun de shomei suru, to iu an mo giron sarete iru to. (He says that there it has not been settled what happens after death. At present a person chooses between burial, being burned at the power station, being kept for forensic medicine, or organ donation. There is a proposal under debate to turn that around, so forensics and donation become the default and burial or cremation have to be signed for while the person is still alive.)

Satomi

frowning, one hand lifting off his knee Hatsudensho de yaku no ka. Sore wa, atatakasa o toru tame ka. (Burned at the power station. Is that so the warmth gets used?)

Nozaki

He is asking about the power station. Whether the burning is done that way so the heat gets used.

Vlad

Cremation entails burning at the power station, for both heat and energy.

Nozaki

turning back to Satomi Sou desu. Kasou wa hatsudensho de suru koto ni natte ite, netsu mo denki mo sono mama tsukau sou desu. (That is right. Cremation is done at the power station, and both the heat and the electricity are used.)

Satomi

sitting with it for a moment Kotchi no yakiba wa, yaku dake yaite, kemuri wa sora ni ageru dake ja. Mottainai to omoeba mottainai ga, sonna fuu ni kangaeta koto mo nakatta. (The crematorium here burns and sends the smoke up into the sky and that is the end of it. It is a waste if you look at it that way, and I have never once thought about it before.)

Nozaki

The crematorium here burns and the smoke goes up into the sky and that is the end of it. He says it is a waste if you look at it that way, and that he has never once thought about it before.

Vlad

In Fluid, the reduce emissions, or CO2e in official terms, it has been decided that remains may be burned for energy generation if the person chooses to be cremated.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Haishutsu o herasu tame ni, kasou o erabu hito no itai wa moyashite denryoku ni mawashite mo yoi, to kimatta sou desu. (He says it was decided that, in order to cut emissions, the body of a person who chooses cremation may be burned to make electricity.)

Satomi

Sonnara, hone wa dou naru gaka. Kotchi ja yaita ato ni, mijika na mon ga hashi de hone o hirote, tsubo ni ireru. Sore ga nakattara, haka ni ireru mono ga nai. (Then what becomes of the bones? Here, after the burning, the close family pick the bones up with chopsticks and put them in a jar. Without that there is nothing to put in the grave.)

Nozaki

He wants to know what happens to the bones. Here the family pick them out with chopsticks after the burning and put them in a jar, and without that there is nothing to put in the grave.

Vlad

The bones are turned into ash, which may be placed in a globe.

Nozaki

turning back to Satomi Hone wa hai ni shite, marui utsuwa ni ireru sou desu. (He says the bones are reduced to ash and put into a globe.)

Satomi

looking at the photograph on the altar, then back at Vlad Sono tama wa, ie ni oku no ka, haka ni ireru no ka. (Does that globe stay in the house, or does it go into a grave?)

Nozaki

He is asking whether the globe stays in the house or goes into a grave.

Vlad

If the person doesn't choose, then the family may be informally asked when the person is at old age where the ash goes once it is contained. If the person chooses, that choice overrides the family's choice. It is usually up to the individual to decide at old age whether the globe stays in the house or grave. When no decision is made, the ash is burned or buried, depending on whether the local soil is capable of preserving it or not.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi, one hand flat on the table Honnin ga kimeteireba, sono kimeta koto ga kazoku no iken yori tsuyoi sou desu. Kimete inai baai wa, toshi o totta koro ni kazoku ga hikoushiki ni kikarete, hai o ie ni oku ka haka ni ireru ka o kimeru. Dare mo nanimo kimenai mama datta toki wa, tochi no tsuchi ga hai o mamoreru ka dou ka de, umeru ka moyasu ka ga kimaru to. (He says a person's own decision outweighs the family's. Where no decision was made, the family are asked informally once the person is old whether the ash stays in the house or goes into a grave. When nothing at all was decided, whether it is buried or burned depends on whether the soil there can preserve it.)

Satomi

shaking his head slowly Kikareru dake ka. Kotchi wa, kiku mo nanimo nai. Ie no haka ga atte, soko ni haitte, ato wa nokotta mon ga mendou o miru. Erabu to iu kangae ga nai gayo. (Only asked, is it. Here there is no asking at all. The house has its grave, you go into it, and whoever is left looks after it. The idea of choosing does not exist.)

Nozaki

He says here there is no asking at all. The house has its grave, you go into it, and whoever is left looks after it. Choosing is not something that comes up.

Cooperatives and assemblies§

Vlad

In Fluid, it is usually a mix of person chooses, if not, cooperative.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Muko de wa, mazu honnin ga kimete, kimete inakereba kumiai ga hikitoru sou desu. (Over there the person decides first, and if they have not decided the cooperative takes it on.)

Satomi

looking at her, then at Vlad Sore wa, mura no yoriai mitai na mon ka. Sonna mon ga hito no hone made mendou miru gaka. (Is that something like the village assembly? Does a thing like that see to a person's bones as well?)

Nozaki

He is asking whether a cooperative is something like the village assembly, and whether a thing like that sees to a person's bones as well.

Vlad

A cooperative does not see to the person's bones as it's a public enterprise. The cooperative is like the village assembly to a point, because then there also also federations of cooperatives and cooperatives that run multiple factories in multiple cities.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Kumiai wa hone no sewa wa shinai sou desu. Are wa koukyou no jigyoutai da kara da to. Mura no yoriai ni nite iru no wa aru tokoro made de, kumiai no rengoutai mo areba, ikutsu mo no machi de ikutsu mo koujou o ugokashite iru kumiai mo aru sou desu. (He says a cooperative does not see to anyone's bones, because it is a public enterprise. The resemblance to a village assembly only goes so far, since there are federations of cooperatives, and cooperatives running several factories across several towns.)

Satomi

hands going back to his knees Kotchi de kumiai to ieba noukyou yo. Shukka mo kane mo zenbu soko o tooshita ga, are wa mura no yoriai to wa betsu mono ja. (Around here a cooperative means the farm co-op. Everything I sent out and everything I was paid went through it. It is a different thing altogether from the village assembly.)

Nozaki

Around here a cooperative means the farm co-op. Everything he sent out and everything he was paid went through it, and he says it is a different thing altogether from the village assembly.

Vlad

The vast majority of villages in Fluid are governed by direct democracies, larger cities by assemblies.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Muko de wa, mura no hotondo wa juumin ga chokusetsu kimete ite, ookii machi ni naru to gikai o oku sou desu. (He says that over there most villages decide things directly among the residents, and the larger towns have assemblies.)

Satomi

one hand going out toward the road Mukashi wa koko ni mo yoriai ga atta. Michi no koto mo, mizu no koto mo, soko de kimeoutta. Ima wa shiyakusho ga Tosayamada ni atte, koko ni nokotoru no wa shisho dake ja. Hito ga oran ki, yoriai ni naran. (There was an assembly here once too. The road and the water were all settled in it. Now the city hall is down in Tosayamada and what is left up here is a branch office, and there are too few people for an assembly to form.)

Vlad

The villages of Fluid are extremely small and not many people see opportunity there, despite the people there being provisioned basic needs as much as people in urban areas.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Muko no mura wa dore mo hijou ni chiisakute, taberu mono mo sumu tokoro mo machi to onaji dake ateraretoru no ni, soko ni kikai ga aru to omou hito wa sukunai sou desu. (He says their villages are all very small, and although the basics are provided there the same as in the towns, few people see an opportunity in them.)

Satomi

nodding once at the window Onaji ja. Nenkin mo aru shi, tsukuru mono mo aru. Nai no wa shigoto to, hito to, machi no akari yo. Wakai mon wa sore o mi ni iku ki, kaette kon. (It is the same here. There is the pension and there are things to grow. What is missing is work, people and the lights of a town. The young go to look at those, and they do not come back.)

Nozaki

It is the same here. There is the pension and there are things to grow, and what is missing is work, people and the lights of a town. The young go to look at those, and they do not come back.

Vlad

Marliene has gone from Prevessa to Leriduson, and she didn't come back. She comes back only to visit her grandmother. Prevessa is a small town with a fully-functional transit system.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Shiriai no josei mo, Prevessa to iu machi kara Leriduson ni dete, sono mama modotte inai sou desu. Kaeru no wa obaasan ni au toki dake de, sono machi wa chiisai keredo, kotsuu wa chanto sorotte iru to. (He says a woman he knows left a town called Prevessa for Leriduson and has not moved back. She goes only to see her grandmother, and the town is small although it has a full transit system.)

Satomi

shifting his weight on the cushion Basu ga atte mo onaji koto ja. Deteiku riyuu wa noriai ja nai ki ne. (Having the buses makes no difference. What sends them away is not the transport.)

Nozaki

Having the buses makes no difference to it. What sends people away is something other than the transport.

Vlad

I know.

Nozaki

picking her cup up again and finding it has gone cold Wakatte iru sou desu. (He says he knows.)

Where each of them has lived§

Satomi

looking at Vlad, both hands on his knees Anta wa, kurashi no ii machi no soba ni shika sunda koto no nai hito ni mieru. Musume ga iiyotta ga, Rindoma mo Reriduson mo umi no soba yaro. Sonnara Niigata mitai na toko yo. Umi ga atte, shigoto ga atte, densha ga toru machi ja. (You look to me like a man who has only ever lived near towns that are doing well. My daughter was saying that Lindoma and Leriduson are both on the sea. Then they are places like Niigata. Sea, work, and trains running through.)

Nozaki

You look to him like a man who has only ever lived near towns that are doing well. His daughter told him Lindoma and Leriduson are both on the sea, so he takes them for places like Niigata, with the sea, work, and trains running through.

Vlad

picking up his cup and finding out it has gone cold too I have been near Bucharest for nearly all my life, and now I live in Lindoma.

Nozaki

turning to Satomi Kono kata wa, jinsei no hotondo o Bukaresuto no chikaku de sugoshite, ima wa Rindoma ni sunde iru sou desu. (He says he spent nearly the whole of his life near Bucharest, and that he lives in Lindoma now.)

Satomi

reaching over to move the teapot toward her without getting up Yappari na. Miyako no soba to, kuni no soto no machi ka. (As I thought. Near the capital, and then a town outside the country.)

Nozaki

He says that is what he thought. Near the capital, then a town in another country.

Vlad

Of course.

Nozaki

lifting the teapot and filling Satomi's cup before her own Sou desu ne, to. (He says yes, that is right.)

Satomi

reaching to straighten the tray on the table Washi wa Toukyou ni wa ichido mo itta koto ga nai. Musume mo, ue no mago no Mayumi mo, sono ko-tachi mo minna asoko ni oru no ni, gojuu nen zutto koko ja. Akima mo Toukyou ni otta ga, are wa dete itta. (I have never been to Tokyo once. My daughter is there, the elder granddaughter Mayumi is there with her children, and I have been up here for fifty years. Akima was in Tokyo too, and she left it.)

Nozaki

setting the pot back down Watashi mo Toukyou desu. Koganei to iu, nishi no hou desu kedo. Akima-san no iru kuni no koto mo, shashin ya kaite aru mono o zuibun yomimashita. (I am from Tokyo myself. Koganei, out on the western side. I have also read a fair amount about the country Akima is in, photographs and whatever was written.)

Nihon wa densha mo michi mo mukou yori zutto ii noni, kuchite iku you ni mieru n desu. (I am sorry, I am close to crying. Everyone around me has a proper life and I am the only one who does not. Akima is settled over there, and she can rest knowing there is no more work she has to do. What I still have to work through goes on and on. That country is stable. It is not decaying. It is imperfect, there is no coffee at times and the unusual fruit is out of reach, from what I have read, and there is always something else to eat. Japan has better trains and better roads than they do, and it looks as though it is rotting.)

Satomi

hands going flat on his knees again Washi mo onaji you ni miete oru. Ii toki ni hataraita n ja. Shigoto wa ikura demo atte, kane mo ugoiyotta. Sono koro tateta michi mo hashi mo, ima wa dare mo tooran. Tani wa kara ni natte, ie wa akiya bakari ja. Musume mo, mago mo, dare hitori raku ni shite oru mon wa oran. Kuni ga yoku natta hazu no toshi ni hataraite, sono saki ga kore yo. (It looks the same way to me. I worked in the good years. There was work without end and money moving. The roads and the bridges from those years have nobody driving on them now. The valley is emptied out and the houses stand vacant. My daughter, the grandchildren, not one of them has it easy. I worked through the years the country was supposed to have got better, and this is what came after.)

Nozaki

pressing the side of her finger under one eye Sore ni, mukou wa iroiro to chigau n desu. Omotta koto o sono mama itte ii shi, tooshi ni tooshi o kasaneru hitsuyou mo nai. Hito mo konde inai. Zenbu ga sukunai n desu. (And things over there are different in a lot of ways. You can say what you think outright, and there is no need to pile hedge upon hedge. It is not crowded either. There is less of everything.)

Satomi

lifting a hand toward the window and letting it drop Hito ga fueru dake fuetara, dou shiyou mo nai yo. Densha mo michi mo ie mo, sono kazu ni wa oitsukan. Sore ga zenbu machi ni katamattoru. Toukyou wa afurete, koko wa kara ja. (When the numbers climb the way they did, there is nothing to be done about it. The trains, the roads, the housing, none of it keeps up with that many people. And all of it is packed into the cities. Tokyo overflows and this place is empty.)

Nozaki

setting her palm flat on the table and marking off a small square beside it with two fingers, then drawing a much larger rectangle around and past it Mukou wa kuni zentai de sen happyaku man nin desu. Rindoma wa hyaku yonjuu man nin de, hirosa mo Toukyou to sono shuuhen ni kurabetara zutto chiisakute, kou hirogatte mo inai n desu. Toukyou dake de, ano kuni no jinkou zentai yori ooi n desu yo. (The whole country over there is eighteen million. Lindoma is one million four hundred thousand, and the area is far smaller than Tokyo and everything around it, and it does not sprawl like this. Tokyo on its own holds more people than that entire country.)

Satomi

following her hands on the table Sonnara, mukou no inaka wa dou natte oru gaka. Ichiban ookii machi ga hyaku yonjuu man shika oran nara, nokori no hito wa doko ni oru gayo. Kotchi mitai ni kara ni wa naatoran hazu ja. (Then what is the countryside like over there? If the largest town holds only one million four hundred thousand, where are the rest of them living? It cannot be emptied out the way it is here.)

Nozaki

turning her hand over on the table Shashin de mita kagiri desu kedo, inaka wa kara ni wa miemasen deshita. Ie ni hito ga sunde ite, hatake mo tsukatte arimasu. Sore ni, Rindoma no shita ni juu man nin gurai no machi ga ikutsu mo aru n desu. Hito ga hitotsu no tokoro ni katamatte inai n da to omoimasu. (This is from photographs only, but the countryside did not look empty. There are people living in the houses and the fields are in use. And below Lindoma there are a number of towns of around a hundred thousand. I think the people are simply not piled into one place.)

Satomi

sitting back against the wall Sono kurai no ookisa no kuni nara, sonna koto mo dekiru no ka mo shiran. Ichi oku ni sen man de wa muri ja. Nihon o sono katachi ni motte iku no wa, mou dekan yo. (A country of that size may be able to do such a thing. With a hundred and twenty million it is out of the question. Japan cannot be brought into that shape any more.)

Nozaki

Watashi mo sou omoimasu. Dakara koso, deteikitai n desu. Sono tabi ni jibun de, muri da, ikanai hou ga ii, tte iikikaseru n desu kedo. (I think the same, which is exactly why I want to get out. And every time, I talk myself to not do that, it cannot be done, better not to go.)

Satomi

Wakai mon ga soko e ikitai to iu nara, ikeba ee. Sono bun kono kuni ga motto suitara sore made ja. Yasui chingin de kaisha no tame ni hataraite, nenkin no tame dake ni toshi o tsukatte, ookii tokoro ni hairetara mouke mono da to omoute matte oru yori wa, deteiku hou ga mashi na koto mo aru. Tada, hontou ni kane no nai mon wa deteikenai. Deteikeru no wa, sono ki no aru mon dake yo. (If the young want to go there, let them go. If this country empties out further for it, so be it. Working for a company on poor wages, spending years for the sake of a pension, waiting on the chance of getting into somewhere big, leaving is sometimes better than that. Only, the ones with no money at all cannot leave. The ones who can are the ones with the drive for it.)

Nozaki

Sono ki no mae ni, mazu okane ga nai n desu. Hikouki dai mo, nimotsu o hakobu bun mo, mukou de saisho no nanikagetsu o motaseru bun mo. Ki ga aru dake de deteikeru nara, mou minna deteitte imasu. (Before the drive comes into it at all, there is no money. Not for the flight, not for shipping what I own, not for the first several months on the other side. If drive alone got a person out, everyone would have gone by now.)

Satomi

Kane no aru mon wa kotchi ni ottemo komaran ki, deteiku hanashi ni mo naran. Komatte oru no wa, mono no mikata made bimbou de kimatte shimau koto yo. Washi mo zutto sou jatta. Musume mo sou ja. Ima wa Mayumi mo Akima mo onaji tokoro ni oru. (People with money have no trouble staying, so leaving never comes up for them. The trouble is that being poor decides even how you see things. It was that way with me the whole time. It is that way with my daughter. Now Mayumi and Akima are in the same place.)

Nozaki

Uchi mo sou desu. Haha mo zutto sono mama de, watashi mo sono mama ni narimashita. (It is the same in my family. My mother has been in that position the whole time, and I ended up in it as well.)
