PHONE SCREEN

Notice printed by the Lidoma Transport Cooperative, 27 April

The cooperative reprints the following notice on each anniversary of the dissolution of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia. Copies are posted in tram cars on the 3, 7 and 28, in the trolleybus fleet, and at the shelters on Statiun Centrala.

Rules of conduct for passengers

  1. Do not talk loudly.
  2. Do not spread Yugoslavian propaganda.
  3. Titoist ideas may be discussed only in measured debate.
  4. Fluid is not Yugoslavia.

The cooperative states that rules 2 to 4 have no force and that no passenger has ever been removed from a vehicle under them. PHONE SCREEN

Correction and note on the third rule

The third rule was printed in the 2019 run without the qualification carried by the second and fourth, and passengers wrote to ask whether a debate on the tram could be conducted incorrectly. It cannot. All four rules are without force. A passenger who shouts is covered by the first rule, which is the only one the cooperative applies.

On the comparison

Josip Broz Tito led the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia from the end of the second war until his death in 1980. From 1950 the federation ran workers' self-management: enterprise councils, firms directed by the people employed in them, and property held neither by a ministry nor by an owner. It stood outside both blocs and was the closest thing to this Republic that has existed.

Its failures are specific. Labour-managed firms preferred existing members to new hires, which held employment down. They underinvested, because a member who left could take nothing with him and had little reason to pay for an asset lasting twenty years. The gap between the richer and poorer republics widened. Through the 1980s the federation borrowed abroad on a large scale and inflation consumed what was left.

Each of those has a provision written against it. Membership is automatic after a probation of at most six months, so there is no class of employed non-member. Usufruct carries no right of disposal, so no member holds a share that can be sold. Surpluses from technical improvement pass into the following period's ceiling. New enterprises are founded from the reserve and incumbents pay nothing toward them. Equalisation between territories is owed as of right. Foreign borrowing is prohibited outright.

The cooperative prints this because the comparison abroad is usually made in the broad sense, as though the objection were self-management itself. The objection that matters is narrower and is answered above.
