# Changelog

## Scale of the change

The 2024 text had seven articles: a preamble, general principles, values, the political system, the economy, rights, foreign policy and final provisions. It stated aims at length and procedure hardly at all.

The current text has 340 articles. Most of its length covers what happens when things fail: when an enterprise cannot meet its ceiling, when a commune defaults, when an election cannot be held, when the plan doesn't work, when a majority wants something fundamental to the Republic to change.

The drafting principle behind the expansion is recorded in Article 319 and in `economy/unresolved.html`. Every guarantee names the organ that owes it, the procedure that delivers it, and the court that hears the complaints when it fails.

## 1. Classification of parties by ideology

Article 2 of the 2024 text required the Electoral Commission to check party platforms against a published list of policy combinations, and to label parties neoliberal, conservative or authoritarian left. Reaching 30 per cent collectively triggered a State of Democratic Safeguard, which suspended ordinary legislation and made every law conditional on a national referendum.

The power fired once, in September 2024, against a four per cent monarchist party and a housing campaign polling at nineteen. It was withdrawn in November after mass demonstrations, and the housing campaign became a party with 18 seats.

The current text prohibits the classification outright. Article 12(4) forbids the registration or classification of associations by opinion or ideology. Article 246 forbids the Electoral Commission to judge a list's programme, Article 263 denies the Office of Democratic Integrity any such power, and Article 265(1)(m) makes an act of that kind void whoever adopts it.

Restriction of a party now requires a court judgment on acts proved in evidence under Article 12. Democratic self-defence works through substantive limits on what may be done, in Articles 265 and 266, together with a monitoring office that publishes, refers, and sanctions nothing.

## 2. Turnout thresholds as conditions of validity

The 2024 text set a 60 per cent turnout condition on national elections, a 60 per cent quorum on local initiatives, a 60 per cent participation requirement on national economic proposals, and a 60 per cent population requirement on amendment by referendum.

On 10 June 2025 turnout came in at 57.3 per cent. The election was void, and the text said nothing about what followed. The Republic went fourteen weeks with no parliament and no lawful route to one.

Every threshold of that kind is gone. Article 140 makes a referendum decide by a majority of votes cast with no turnout condition, and Article 309 applies the same rule to amendments.

Article 123 fixes the term at five years and forbids the Assembly to extend it. Postponement requires a prior finding of the Constitutional Court, runs six months at a time and twelve months at the outside, and expires into an election held in the districts where it is possible, with the remaining seats vacant.

## 3. An executive branch declared and never built

Article 0 of the 2024 text proclaimed a separation of powers into three branches. The text then constituted a legislature and a court, and left execution unaddressed.

Title VI constitutes it. The Republic has no head of state under Article 145, and execution belongs to committees of the Assembly composed in the Assembly's own proportions under Article 148, so every list is represented in every committee.

Article 149 gives the chairs a Coordinating Bureau that coordinates without commanding. Article 154 bars the committees from planning, allocating resources, legislating, or restricting a right by regulation.

Articles 158 to 162 govern administrative procedure, the career service, the duty to refuse an unlawful order, and personal liability for serious fault.

## 4. Currency abolished with no allocation rule

Article 3 of the 2024 text abolished currency, financial markets and speculative capital. It specified nothing about how goods would reach people when need exceeded supply.

Through the winter of 2024 insulation, timber, antibiotics and spectacle lenses ran short in particular places. Allocation proceeded through acquaintance and position, because the text supplied no lawful way to rank claims, ration or queue.

Chapter 4 of Title IV supplies the rule:

| Provision | Effect |
| - | - |
| Article 62 | Food, water, housing, energy, healthcare, education, care and transport are free and unconditional |
| Article 63 | Every resident registers what they need, and the planners are bound by the register |
| Article 111 | A residual unit of account, personal, equal for everyone, expiring at the end of the period, void on transfer |
| Article 112 | Resource-intensive goods held in common and booked, beyond the reach of the unit |
| Article 113 | Shortage governed by published rules applying urgency, duration of wait and the drawing of lots |
| Article 113(2) | Waiting lists public in anonymised form, stating each applicant's position, open to challenge |
| Article 113(3) | Preference by office, function, contribution or acquaintance void, with the grantor personally liable |
| Article 114 | Sale, resale, letting and pledge of goods received in provision void, trade in queue positions an offence |

## 5. Economic planning by plebiscite

The 2024 text ran national economic decisions through a participation threshold and a majority of participants, with a technology-assisted forum producing provisional plans in the gaps.

Planning now runs as a negotiated cycle in Chapter 3 of Title IV. Councils propose, and each level reconciles what it can and passes upward only what it cannot, across at least three rounds under Article 97.

Article 90(4) publishes the plan with its variants and its rejected alternatives, and Article 96(4) leaves the Planning Secretariat computing and deciding nothing. Every delegate is mandated and recallable at any time under Article 94(3).

Participation is justiciable. Under Article 143 any resident, cooperative or commune may sue on the ground that the consultation the Constitution requires did not occur, and the court may order the planning stage to be repeated.

## 6. Ecology stated as an aspiration

The 2024 text made environmental stewardship a guiding value in Article 1 and a responsibility of cooperatives in Article 3. No organ set a limit, no limit bound anything, and no remedy existed.

Title II supplies the apparatus:

| Provision | Effect |
| - | - |
| Articles 18 to 19 | Natural entities hold rights to exist, function and be restored, with standing for any person, no fee, no need to show personal harm |
| Article 20 | Whoever harms nature restores it from their own resources, with personal liability |
| Article 22 | Total energy and material throughput capped at a safe level, efficiency gains applied to sufficiency |
| Articles 23 to 24 | An independent Authority sets ceilings for energy, materials, emissions, water, land, nutrients, toxins and harvests, protected from defunding mid-term |
| Article 26 | The budgets bind the plan, and anything exceeding them is void |
| Articles 27 and 221 | An Ecological Chamber of the Constitutional Court enforces them, sitting with scientific assessors |
| Article 35 | Ecological data measured continuously and published, with falsification a crime |

## 7. Rights declared with no enforcement

The preamble of the current text states the failure: the earlier texts declared rights and named nobody to enforce them. Article 4 of the 2024 text listed sixteen rights drawn from the Universal Declaration, with no direct effect, no limitation test, no non-derogable list and no remedy.

Title III carries the machinery. Article 37 extends every right to every person present, documented or otherwise, and Article 39 gives the rights direct effect without implementing legislation, binding employers and services.

Article 40 sets one limitation test: clear law, listed purpose, necessity. Article 41 makes the minimum core of each social right enforceable today and subjects the rest to reasonableness review, and Article 42 lists the rights from which no emergency permits derogation.

Article 43 guarantees a fast, free remedy with reparation. A Defender of Rights takes complaints against anyone about any right in the title under Articles 252 to 255, with divisions for automated systems and for unannounced inspection of places of detention.

## 8. Court appointed by the chamber it reviews

Article 6 of the 2024 text had justices elected exclusively by the National Council.

Appointment now runs by lot:

| Provision | Effect |
| - | - |
| Article 217 | Justices drawn by lot from a public pool, the Judicial Council refusing twice per vacancy at most |
| Article 218 | The pool open to any qualified resident with fifteen years of legal experience |
| Article 219 | A single twelve-year term, non-renewable, five of fifteen replaced every four years |
| Article 201 | Cases assigned by lot, with no power to pick a judge or move a case afterwards |
| Article 223 | Size, terms, jurisdiction and appointment entrenched against changes affecting anyone in office |
| Article 209 | A Judicial Council of twenty-one, thirteen of them residents drawn by lot |
| Article 214 | Discipline in an Inspectorate independent of the Council |
| Article 206 | Discipline confined to conduct, heard in public, never touching the content of a ruling |

## 9. Amendment by 55 per cent of the chamber

The 2024 text permitted amendment either by 55 per cent of representatives or by referendum, and entrenched nothing.

Article 309 requires a referendum in every case. Article 308 requires a check against the core, six months of publication, and examination by a body drawn by lot before the vote.

Article 310 places eleven limbs beyond the amending power: human dignity, the republican form and popular sovereignty, the ecological obligation and the binding force of the budgets, social ownership and the abolition of income from bare holding, participatory democracy and universal suffrage, the non-derogable rights, essential provision, equality and the extension of rights to every resident, judicial independence and the Court's jurisdiction, the void acts of Article 265, and the article itself.

Replacement of the whole text is confined to an elected Convention ratified by referendum under Article 312, and Article 313 carries the core through the replacement.

## 10. Emergency powers with an open end

The 2024 text extended representatives' terms automatically during mobilisation, war, siege or emergency, until those states ceased. The precedent it inherited was the Presidium governing for forty-seven months under a power drafted for floods, upheld in a one-page judgment holding that the court had no jurisdiction to look.

Title X closes the route. A declaration lapses within seven days without a two-thirds vote of the Assembly and a finding of the Constitutional Court under Article 279, and Article 280 caps renewals at thirty days each and six months in any twenty-four.

Article 281 confines emergency powers to listed measures, and places elections, courts, provision and the oversight offices beyond their reach. Article 282 opens every measure to challenge within seven days, free of charge, and Article 265(1)(j) makes an emergency without a terminal date void.

## 11. Territory left to convention

Local direct democracy in the 2024 text amounted to an initiative threshold and a quorum. Nothing described how communes were governed, what they were competent to do, or how resources reached them.

During the fourteen-week vacancy of 2025, communal councils and branch federations kept water, power, food, clinics and schools running on their own authority, with no constitutional footing for doing so.

Title VII writes the footing. Article 166 establishes four tiers, and Article 167 gives the commune general competence over anything not expressly assigned upward, with doubt resolved in the commune's favour.

Articles 179 and 180 abolish tax and allocate resources to each territory directly by a published formula computed in the open, with named people obliged to justify any departure from it. Article 181 gives poorer or harder territories extra as of right, and Article 182 lets a territory that saves resources keep them.

A defaulting territory keeps its council under Article 190, and a court-appointed commissioner is confined to named tasks. Article 191 forbids the abolition or suspension of any territory's elected bodies.

## 12. Foreign policy as a list of objectives

Article 5 of the 2024 text set out non-alignment and eighteen development goals, with no procedure, no test and no floor.

Title XI replaces the list with instruments:

| Provision | Effect |
| - | - |
| Article 288 | A life-limiting regime is defined by acts established in evidence, and never by what a state calls itself |
| Article 290 | Treaties require two thirds of the Assembly and a court check, unions and borders a referendum |
| Article 291 | Treaties creating external debt, arbitration, privatisation, patents or foreign bases prohibited |
| Article 294 | Union acts denied direct effect, binding nobody until the Assembly enacts them |
| Article 298 | Needs met at home first, domestic capacity developed before importing |
| Article 303 | A solidarity floor of one hundredth of all allocated resources, beyond the reach of any majority |
| Article 304 | Solidarity routed through peoples' own organisations |
| Article 305 | Ecological debt accounted, and what was taken from other peoples returned |

## 13. Cooperatives as the only permitted form

Article 3 of the 2024 text barred the State, and every non-cooperative body, from holding means of production. Communal utilities, hospitals, universities and the grid had no lawful owner.

Article 6(2) establishes four social forms, and Article 82 forbids organic law to invent a fifth:

| Form | Governed by |
| - | - |
| The commons | The whole people, through the organs of Chapter 2 of Title IV |
| Cooperative usufruct | Those who work in the enterprise |
| Communal and regional enterprise | Inhabitants, through their councils |
| Public establishments | Users, workers and the Republic jointly |

Article 84 holds cooperative assets in usufruct for as long as the cooperative works them, with ownership resting nowhere private. Article 88 keeps personal craft free of authorisation while the work stays within the household, and prohibits profit enterprises, tradable claims, interest, letting for gain, and the appropriation of another person's product.

## 14. Defence and policing left to a clause

The 2024 text mentioned an armed emergency defence apparatus for repelling aggression and stopped there.

Defence is now a voluntary territorial militia with no standing army and no conscription under Article 271, and objectors lose nothing by refusing under Article 272. Article 269 places armed bodies under civilian direction and forbids their use against assemblies or strikes.

Article 270 prohibits weapons of mass effect, autonomous killing systems, and the export of any weapon or component. Policing belongs to the communes under Article 274, with officers unarmed by default and firearms released on judicial authorisation.

Any action beyond the frontier requires two thirds of the Assembly and a referendum under Article 277. Article 285 forbids a domestic intelligence service and prohibits mass collection in every circumstance.

## 15. Publication dependent on foreign suppliers

The 2024 programme loaded Bootstrap and a Google-hosted typeface from outside the Republic, which Articles 109(5) and 301 now forbid to public information systems.

The register loads no external font, script or image. It ships the text as Markdown and JSON with a cross-reference table under Article 314, renders every reference between articles as a link in both directions, and shows for each article which articles cite it.

Article 315 requires an official summary carrying no legal force, corrected within ninety days where it diverges from the text. Article 316 requires a free printed copy for every dwelling.
