The economic order

Why the Bloc shares it

What the Republic requires of a union, what it refuses to require of anyone, and why it opposes embargoes even against governments it dislikes.

A structural test, not a doctrinal one

Article 293 opens membership of the Democratic Socialist Bloc to any state founded on social ownership of the means of production, ecological limits, democratic self-government and the equality of its members. It requires no doctrine, no party, no declaration and no alignment. It says nothing about what a member calls itself.

What it does forbid is joining any union whose founding instrument requires a market in the means of production, in labour, in land, in dwellings, in health, in education or in knowledge. That is the whole reason the Republic is not in the European Union: it meets almost every condition of membership and cannot meet that one.

The union cannot make law here

Article 294 refuses direct effect entirely. A common standard agreed in the Bloc reaches nobody in the Republic until the Assembly enacts it as a law of the Republic. No act of a Bloc organ applies of its own force, binds a court or creates a right, and no competence of the Assembly, the planners, the courts, the oversight offices or the territories may be transferred to it.

This is a real cost and the Republic accepts it knowingly. It can be outvoted in the Bloc and still decline, and the instrument may attach consequences to declining. The alternative was a union that could quietly amend the Constitution, and Article 291 forbids that in any case.

Two allies, arranged differently

Iosmein is poorer than Fluid by every measure of provision and is the largest recipient of the solidarity required by Article 303. Reformed Luces Aporia runs a democratic socialist republic on a considerably shorter constitution, a point its representatives make in Bloc sittings and which Article 319 concedes in advance. Neither is asked to copy anything.

Non-intervention is not politeness

Article 286(1) binds the Republic to non-intervention in the internal affairs of others, and Article 302(3) forbids any transfer that carries a condition touching a recipient's internal affairs, form of government, economy or alignment. The Republic therefore makes no claim that its arrangement suits anyone else, and has no mechanism for pressing it on anyone if it did.

This is not modesty. It follows from the same principle that produced the Republic: an economic order that is imposed, and never decided, is not self-government, whatever its content.

The embargo question, and Cuba

Article 286(4) prohibits the Republic from participating in a blockade that deprives a population of food, water, medicine or energy, and from applying any measure whose effect falls upon a population. Article 288(4) states that a finding about a state attaches to its government and never to its people. Article 300(6) keeps humanitarian assistance, rescue and medical supply flowing to every state without exception, including one the Assembly has found life-limiting.

The Republic's opposition to the United States embargo on Cuba follows from those three provisions and from nothing else. It is not an endorsement of the Cuban government, which Article 288 would examine on its acts like any other, and which would be examined on the suppression of assembly and the imprisonment of people for opinion as readily as on anything else. The objection is to the instrument. A measure that reaches a population through its medicine supply is the same instrument whoever aims it, and the Republic has been on the receiving end of two blockades and one embargo of its own.

The United Nations General Assembly has adopted a resolution calling for the embargo's end every year since 1992, in recent years by margins of well over a hundred and eighty states to a handful. The states maintaining it answer that a sovereign decision about whom to trade with is not a blockade, and that pressure on a government is a legitimate instrument of policy. The Republic's answer is that Article 286(4) asks where the effect lands, not what the intention was, and that it would apply the same rule to an identical measure aimed at a government it disliked. Whether that is right is a matter on which reasonable states disagree, and the Republic's position binds nobody but the Republic.
