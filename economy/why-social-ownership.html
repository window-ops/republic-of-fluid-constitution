The economic order

Why social ownership

The case the Republic makes for its own economic order, and the record it makes it from.

The claim in one sentence

Article 6 abolishes income drawn from the bare holding of a productive asset. Everything else in the economic order follows from that single move: if owning a thing that produces cannot itself pay you, then there is nothing to buy, nothing to accumulate toward, and no class of people whose living depends on other people's work.

What replaces ownership

Four forms, set out in Article 6(2). The commons, held by everyone and inalienable: land, water, the atmosphere, the spectrum, the energy grid, the knowledge commons. Cooperative usufruct, where the people who work in an enterprise hold its assets for as long as they work them. Communal and regional enterprise, run by inhabitants through their councils. And public establishments for health, education, care and research, governed by their workers and their users together.

None of these is state ownership. The Republic as such owns very little, and Article 163 forbids it to contract out the few things it does hold. This distinction is the one most often lost, and the page on socialism and communism deals with it directly.

Why the Republic thinks this works

The argument is not that people are naturally generous. It is that the information and the incentives sit in the right place. The people who know what a workshop can do are the people in it, and Article 92 requires them to say so publicly. The people who know whether a service is adequate are the people using it, and Article 93 gives their assemblies equal standing. Article 97 then makes the plan out of those statements through rounds of negotiation, with no target sent down a hierarchy.

The design partly draws on Elinor Ostrom's work on how commons are actually governed without either privatisation or a central authority, which won the Nobel prize in economics in 2009 and whose design principles appear in Article 83(5). It draws on Pat Devine's account of negotiated coordination, on the Yugoslav experiment in self-management, and on the Mondragon federation in the Basque Country, whose inter-cooperative solidarity fund is the ancestor of Article 89(5).

The Republic's own evidence, for

The strongest case is the Assembly Reform of 1974. After the strike wave, planning was rebuilt as published rounds of negotiation between workplace, communal and regional councils. Output per hour rose through the following decade while hours fell, repair capacity recovered, and the ecological accounts of 1978 were the first anywhere in the region to be published in tonnes and hectares.

The second is that for fourteen weeks in 2025, between the void election and the interim Council, the Republic had no parliament and no lawful means of getting one. Water, power, food, clinics and schools kept running, administered by communal councils and branch federations on their own authority. An economy in which provision depends on continuous central direction would not have survived that. This is why general competence now sits with the commune under Article 167.

The Republic's own evidence, against

The Directorate years, 1954 to 1974, are the strongest case the critics have. A planning body created for an embargo outlived it, closed its workings on security grounds, and set targets in tonnage. Goods were built heavy because heavy hit the target. Repair shops closed because repair produced no tonnage. The Marnaqui river was dead below the chemical works by 1966 and the audit that said so was suppressed in 1968. Three dockers died at Lutoliluri in 1971 working a crane past its limit to meet a figure set six hundred kilometres away.

Nothing about socialism prevented that. What ended it was a strike wave, and what prevents its return is written procedure: Article 90(4) publishes the plan, its models, its assumptions and its rejected variants; Article 143(9)(b) lets any resident sue when the participation the text requires has not actually happened; Article 116 requires arduous tasks to be registered publicly and engineered away.

The second piece of evidence against is two years old. The 2024 constitution abolished currency and wrote no allocation rule to replace it, and through the winter of 2024 the Republic discovered what happens next: insulation, antibiotics and lenses short in particular places, no lawful way to rank claims, and allocation happening anyway through acquaintance and position. Abolishing money does not abolish scarcity. It only decides who gets to be quiet about it.

What the present text does about that

Articles 106 to 114 exist because of the Long Winter. Provision is graded: essentials free and unconditional, general provision free, minor discretionary goods on a personal expiring token that cannot be saved or traded, and resource-heavy goods held in common and booked. Where supply falls short, Article 113 fixes the order in public: urgency, then waiting time, then lot, with waiting lists published and preference by office or acquaintance void and personally actionable.

Guidance published by the Office of Constitutional Revision. It is not law, and where it differs from the Constitution the Constitution governs. Article 53(4) protects criticism of the plan, of officeholders and of this Constitution in full.
