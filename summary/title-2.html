Register › Official summary › Title II

Ecological Order

Articles 18 to 36 · 19 articles · 3 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Rights of Nature

18

Rights of natural entities

Nature holds rights to exist, to function and to be restored, whether or not a statute names it.

19

Standing

Anyone at all may sue on nature's behalf, with no fee and no need to show personal harm.

20

Guardianship and remedies

Whoever harms nature must restore it from their own resources, and can be held personally liable.

21

Foreign economic entities

Foreign firms operate only on a revocable licence, under stricter duties, and hold no rights from Title III.

Chapter 2 · The Ecological Budgets

22

Steady state

Total energy and material use is capped at a safe level, and efficiency gains buy sufficiency and reduce throughput.

23

Content of the budgets

The budgets set ceilings for energy, materials, emissions, water, land, nutrients, toxins and harvests.

24

The Ecological Budget Authority

An independent scientific Authority sets the budgets, publishes everything, and cannot be defunded mid-term.

25

Procedure

Budgets are built from workplace reports, assembly objections, local studies and local knowledge.

26

Binding force

The budgets bind the plan, anything exceeding them is void, and essentials keep priority.

27

Judicial enforcement

A chamber of the Constitutional Court enforces the budgets and can order restoration.

Chapter 3 · Substantive Regimes

28

Water

Water is common, managed by watershed, and a dwelling can never be cut off.

29

Land, soil and food

Land is held in trust and allocated for use, and hoarding it or ruining the soil is prohibited.

30

Forests, wetlands, coasts and sea

Old forests, peat, wetlands, dunes, reefs and seagrass cannot be converted, and seabed mining is banned.

31

Biodiversity and animals

Species and habitats are protected, and animals kept for food live close to natural conditions.

32

Energy

The energy system is socially owned, fossil extraction is banned, and nuclear is transitional only.

33

Materials, durability and circulation

Goods must last, be repairable by their user, and return their materials to use.

34

Pollution, toxic substances and remediation

Pollution needs a permit within the ceilings, and dumping burdens on poor areas is prohibited.

35

Ecological information

Ecological data is measured continuously and published, and falsifying it is a crime.

36

Ecological emergency

The Authority may declare an ecological emergency, which lapses unless the Assembly confirms it.
