Title V · summary

The Assembly And Popular Decision

Articles 119 to 144 · 26 articles · 5 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Composition and Election

119

The Assembly of the Republic

A single Assembly of two hundred members makes the laws and executes them.

120

The electoral system

Members are elected by open list in the sub-regions, with seats shared out nationally in proportion.

121

Districts and apportionment

Sub-regions are the districts, each returning at least five members, drawn by an independent commission.

122

Candidacy

Any resident of sixteen may stand, campaigns are publicly resourced, and donations are prohibited.

123

Term and date of election

The Assembly serves five years, and can never extend its own term.

124

The mandate

Members vote by their own judgement, and keep their seat if they leave their list.

125

Incompatibilities

A seat cannot be held together with judicial, planning, oversight or enterprise office.

126

Provision and conditions of members

Members get the same provision as anyone else, plus what the job actually requires.

127

Immunity and responsibility

Members can never be prosecuted for a vote cast or an opinion expressed in office.

128

Recall of a member

Voters in a district may recall the member they elected.

129

Vacancy and dissolution

The Assembly may dissolve itself, but never during an emergency or an attack.

Chapter 2 · Functioning

130

Sittings, publicity and record

Sittings are public, and the record with every named vote is published within a day.

131

Quorum and majorities

Ordinary laws pass by a majority of votes cast, and organic laws need three fifths.

132

Committees and inquiry

Committees may summon anyone, and a quarter of the members may open an inquiry.

Chapter 3 · Legislation

133

Initiative

Bills may come from members, committees, regions, communes, planners, producers or residents.

134

Assessment and consultation

Every bill has a published assessment of its effect on rights, ecology and resources.

135

Procedure

Bills get three readings, with amendments and the final text published in advance.

136

Organic laws and reserved matters

Organic laws are always checked by the Constitutional Chamber before they take effect.

137

Preventive constitutional review

Texts can be reviewed for constitutionality before promulgation, which suspends them meanwhile.

138

Promulgation, publication and entry into force

Adopted texts are promulgated within fifteen days and published free in open formats.

Chapter 4 · Popular Decision

139

Popular initiative

Fifty thousand residents can introduce a bill, and force a referendum if it is rejected.

140

Referendum

Referendums decide bindingly by a majority of votes cast, with no turnout threshold.

141

Local decision

Localities decide in person where they can, and by elected council where they cannot.

142

Deliberative bodies drawn by lot

Bodies drawn by lot may be convened to examine questions, and must be answered in writing.

Chapter 5 · The Assembly and the Organs of Planning

143

Accountability of the planning organs

The Planning Congress answers to those who elect it and to the Assembly, and it can be sued.

144

Control of execution

The Assembly questions and controls the executive committees.
