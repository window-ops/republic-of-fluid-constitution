Title VI · summary

Execution And The Administration

Articles 145 to 165 · 21 articles · 4 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Representation of the Republic

145

Absence of a head of state

The Republic has no head of state, and nobody personifies it.

146

The Presiding Officer of the Assembly

The Assembly's Presiding Officer chairs it, signs texts and performs protocol, deciding nothing.

Chapter 2 · The Executive Council

147

Nature

Execution belongs to committees of the Assembly, and there are no ministers and no cabinets.

148

Constitution and composition of the committees

Committees are composed in the Assembly's own proportions, so every list is represented.

149

Chairs and the Coordinating Bureau

Each committee elects a chair, and the chairs form a Bureau that coordinates without commanding.

150

Recall, vacancy and continuity

The Assembly may recall a member from a committee, or reconstitute a committee entirely.

151

Responsibility

Committees publish their acts, minutes and named votes, and answer to the Assembly.

152

Conditions and incompatibilities

Committee members keep their seat, hold the same provision as anyone, and declare their interests.

153

Functions of the Executive Council

Committees execute the laws and the plan, and run the administration in their field.

154

Limits of execution

Committees cannot plan, allocate resources, legislate, or restrict a right by regulation.

Chapter 3 · Initiative of Producers and Users

155

Right of economic initiative

Federations, unions, user assemblies and communal councils can introduce bills and demand answers.

156

Duty of consultation

Measures affecting production, work, provision or ecology are void without prior consultation.

157

Subsidiarity in execution

Execution belongs to the closest level, and local knowledge prevails on local matters.

Chapter 4 · The Administration

158

Principles

The administration must give reasons and decide on time, or the applicant wins by default.

159

The career service

Officials are recruited by open competition, hold tenure, and must refuse unlawful orders.

160

(Vacant. Political cabinets are abolished by Article 147(3). The number is reserved for renumbering at the drafting stage)

Vacant. Political cabinets are abolished by Article 147.

161

Administrative procedure

You are heard before an adverse decision, and can appeal to an independent body and then a court.

162

Liability

The Republic pays for unlawful administration, and officials answer personally for serious fault.

163

Public functions inalienable

Custody, inspection, registers, planning systems and essential provision can never be contracted out.

164

Public information systems

Public systems run on free and open software, with open formats and no single supplier.

165

Account and audit

The Bureau publishes an annual account of the administration in physical units.
