Title XII · summary

Constitutional Change, Accessibility And Revision

Articles 306 to 319 · 14 articles · 5 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · The Amending Power

306

Nature of the amending power

The Constitution changes only by the routes in this Title, and never during an emergency.

307

Initiative

Amendments may be proposed by members, residents, communes, or both regional councils.

308

Procedure

Proposals are checked against the core, published for six months, examined by a lot-drawn body, then voted.

309

Referendum and entry into force

Every amendment needs a referendum, with no turnout threshold.

Chapter 2 · The Core

310

Beyond the amending power

Eleven things can never be amended, including dignity, ecology, social ownership and equal rights.

311

Guardianship of the core

The Constitutional Court guards the core, and can void an amendment that breached it.

Chapter 3 · Replacement of the Constitution

312

The Constitutional Convention

The whole Constitution may be replaced only by an elected Convention and a referendum.

313

Limits and continuity

The core survives replacement, and nothing is suspended while a Convention sits.

Chapter 4 · Accessibility

314

Consolidation and publication

The consolidated text is published free, in open formats, with a full cross-reference table.

315

The official summary

An official plain-language summary exists, has no legal force, and must be corrected if it diverges.

316

Distribution

A free printed copy goes to every dwelling, in every language and accessible format.

Chapter 5 · Revision and Review

317

The Office of Constitutional Revision

An Office of Constitutional Revision reports contradictions and proposes simplifications.

318

Periodic review

Every ten years a body drawn by lot asks whether this text still serves the people bound by it.

319

Duty of intelligibility

Complexity is never a reason to deny anyone a right or a remedy.
