Register › Official summary › Title XI

External Relations And Solidarity

Articles 286 to 305 · 20 articles · 6 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Principles

286

Conduct of external relations

Foreign relations rest on the equality of peoples, non-intervention, and public conduct.

287

Competence and conduct

The competent committee conducts them, with mandates and negotiating texts published.

288

Life-limiting regimes

A state is life-limiting because of what it does, never because of what it calls itself.

289

Defectors and persons in flight

Anyone fleeing such a state becomes a citizen on recognition, without the five-year wait.

Chapter 2 · Treaties

290

Negotiation, signature and ratification

Treaties need two thirds of the Assembly and a court check, and unions and borders need a referendum.

291

Prohibited commitments

Treaties may never accept arbitration, privatisation, patents, debt or foreign bases.

292

Withdrawal

Withdrawal follows the same route, and is immediate if a treaty would require a void act.

Chapter 3 · The Union

293

Membership

The Republic belongs to the Democratic Socialist Bloc, and joins no union requiring a free market.

294

Effect of union acts

Union standards take effect only when the Assembly enacts them, and nothing applies of its own force.

295

Union settlement and currency

Exchange is settled in kind, and a common currency can never be accepted.

Chapter 4 · Frontiers and Movement

296

Open frontiers

Open frontiers are the goal, and no agreement may make the Republic police anyone's departure.

297

Rescue and assistance

Rescue is owed to anyone in distress, and rescuers are never prosecuted.

Chapter 5 · Trade, Imports and Technological Sovereignty

298

Order of supply

Needs are met at home first, and unused domestic capacity is developed before importing.

299

Costly imports and project licences

Costly imports need a public licence, and the Ecological Authority can refuse it outright.

300

Exchange with life-limiting regimes

Trade with a life-limiting state is banned, except for materials available nowhere else.

301

Technological sovereignty

Public systems must run without any foreign supplier's consent, licence or continued existence.

Chapter 6 · Solidarity

302

Obligation of solidarity

Solidarity is owed to peoples in want, without conditions and without debt.

303

The floor

At least one hundredth of all allocated resources goes to solidarity, and can never be cut.

304

Direction and refusal

Solidarity reaches peoples through their own organisations, never through an apparatus of coercion.

305

Ecological and colonial reparation

The Republic accounts its ecological debt, and returns what was taken from other peoples.
