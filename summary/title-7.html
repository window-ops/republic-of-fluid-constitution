Communes, Sub-Regions And Regions

Articles 166 to 191 · 26 articles · 10 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Principles of Territorial Organisation

166

The tiers

The Republic governs at four levels: commune, sub-region, region and centre.

167

General competence and subsidiarity

Communes may act on anything not expressly given to a higher level, and doubt favours the commune.

168

Equality between territories

Every resident gets the same standard of provision wherever they live.

Chapter 2 · The Commune

169

Government of the commune

Communes are run by an assembly of residents, or by an elected council where that is impractical.

170

The communal council

Communal councils are elected proportionally for four years, and are recallable.

171

Competences of the commune

Communes run housing, water, waste, local roads, schools, clinics, libraries and local ecology.

172

Neighbourhoods

Neighbourhoods hold assemblies which can put anything on the council's agenda.

Chapter 3 · The Sub-Region

173

The sub-region and its council

Sub-regional councils are made of delegates from the communes, mandated and recallable.

174

Competences of the sub-region

Sub-regions run watersheds, school and clinic networks, local transport, water works and rescue.

Chapter 4 · The Region

175

The region and its council

Regional councils are made of delegates from the sub-regions.

176

Competences of the region

Regions run cross-boundary infrastructure, hospitals, universities, protected areas and regional balance.

Chapter 5 · The Republic

177

Competences of the Republic

The centre keeps the Constitution, rights, ecology, criminal law, courts, treaties and national systems.

178

Standards of general application

The centre sets the standards that must be identical everywhere, including vehicle dimensions and loads.

Chapter 6 · Resources

179

Principle of direct allocation

There is no tax, and territories receive resources straight from the plan.

180

The formula

The formula is public and computed openly, and named people must justify any departure from it.

181

Equalisation

Poorer or harder territories get extra as of right, without asking anyone's permission.

182

Local surplus and local initiative

A territory that saves resources keeps them, and can never be penalised for saving.

Chapter 7 · Economic Councils and Political Control

183

Relation of the economic councils to the elected councils

Economic councils answer to the elected council of their own level.

Chapter 8 · Spatial Planning, Land and Housing

184

Spatial planning

Communes adopt spatial plans after public inquiry, and habitat connectivity is a condition of validity.

185

Allocation and reallocation of housing

If a household shrinks, exchange is offered for three years before reallocation, with protections.

Chapter 9 · Cooperation, Boundaries and Communities

186

Cooperation and joint bodies

Territories may contract and form joint bodies, but never transfer a competence permanently.

187

Boundaries, merger and division

Boundaries change only with the agreement of the residents affected, by referendum.

188

Communities, languages and heritage

Communities may use their language with any authority, and govern their own heritage.

Chapter 10 · Conflict, Default and Protection

189

Conflicts of competence

The Constitutional Court settles disputes over which level is competent.

190

Default and the commissioner

A defaulting territory keeps its council, and a court appoints a commissioner for named tasks only.

191

Protection of self-government

No territory may be abolished, suspended, or stripped of its elected bodies.
