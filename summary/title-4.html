Title IV · summary

Social Ownership And Economic Coordination

Articles 82 to 118 · 37 articles · 6 chapters · read the full text

Chapter 1 · Forms of Social Ownership

82

The forms and their relation

Productive property takes four social forms, and no fifth may be invented.

83

The commons

The commons cannot be sold or pledged, and the people who use them help govern them.

84

Cooperative usufruct

A cooperative holds its assets for as long as it works them, never as owner.

85

Internal constitution of the cooperative

Every worker is a member with one vote, and officers are elected and recallable at any time.

86

Communal and regional enterprise

Communes and regions may run enterprises, governed together with workers and users.

87

Public establishments

Health, education, care, research and the commons run as public establishments, never for gain.

88

Personal craft and prohibited forms

You may work alone or with your household, and employing anyone outside it is prohibited.

89

Federations

Enterprises in a branch federate, sharing knowledge, reserves and representation.

Chapter 2 · Organs and Procedure of Planning

90

Principles of the plan

The plan meets registered needs in physical units, decided by people and never by computation.

91

Priority within the plan

Essentials come first, then restoration, then upkeep, then everything else.

92

Workplace assemblies

Each workplace states publicly what it needs and what it can make.

93

User and neighbourhood assemblies

Neighbourhoods and users hold assemblies whose statements count equally with the workplaces'.

94

Communal economic councils

Each commune has an economic council answering to its elected council.

95

Regional economic councils

Each region has an economic council reconciling what its sub-regions could not.

96

The National Planning Congress and the Planning Secretariat

A National Planning Congress adopts the plan, and its Secretariat computes but decides nothing.

97

The planning cycle

Planning runs a five-year cycle of statements, computation, at least three rounds of negotiation, and public accounts.

98

Reconciliation and deadlock

Disagreements rise one level at a time, and delay never interrupts essential provision.

99

Adjustment during the period

The plan is adjusted in-year for disaster, shortfall or changed need, with reasons published.

Chapter 3 · Allocation, Accounts and Enterprise Discipline

100

Resource ceilings and accounts

Every enterprise holds a binding resource ceiling and keeps public accounts in physical units.

101

Review of performance

Federations review each enterprise yearly in public, against needs met and ceilings kept.

102

Support

A failing enterprise may be supported for at most three years, publicly, and is then reorganised.

103

Cause of difficulty

Before support, the federation must say whether the fault lay with the plan or with the management.

104

Reorganisation and dissolution

Reorganisation moves assets and people, and nobody loses provision, training or a place.

105

Reserve, formation and experiment

The plan holds a reserve for disaster, for founding new cooperatives, and for experiment.

Chapter 4 · Provision and Distribution

106

Categories of provision

Provision comes in four grades: essential, general, minor discretionary, and shared heavy goods.

107

General provision

General provision covers clothing, furniture, tools, bicycles, books, devices, transport and much more, free.

108

Personal computing and communication devices

Everyone who needs one gets a repairable phone and computer, with free parts for ten years.

109

Freedom of software

Device software is free and open, the user holds full control, and locked devices are prohibited.

110

Durability and the standard of design

Goods must meet minimum service lives, guaranteed parts, and a route back into use.

111

The residual unit of account

A personal, equal, expiring token allocates small luxuries, and it cannot be saved, lent or traded.

112

Resource-intensive goods and common use

Resource-heavy goods are held in common and booked, never allocated to one person.

113

Fair allocation and waiting

When supply falls short, allocation follows urgency, waiting time and lot, never favour.

114

Prohibition of parallel markets

Reselling what you were given, hoarding for exchange, or trading queue places is prohibited.

Chapter 5 · Labour in the Plan

115

Allocation of labour

Labour reaches tasks by choice, and unpopular work gets shorter hours, with no compulsion available.

116

Arduous tasks, rotation and training

Dangerous and degrading tasks are registered publicly, rotated, and engineered away.

Chapter 6 · External Economic Relations

117

Planned external exchange

Foreign trade runs through one account, counts against the budgets, and creates no debt.

118

Solidarity transfers

Solidarity is sent in goods and knowledge, never as a loan and never with conditions.
