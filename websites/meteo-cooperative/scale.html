The REP intensity scale

Intensity describes what an earthquake does at a place. The Republic uses a ten-step scale derived from the Japanese shindo scale, which is itself descended from Mercalli. The two shindo steps that admit a minus and a plus, 5 and 6, are expanded into four whole steps here, and shindo 7 is divided into two. Magnitude, by contrast with intensity, describes the event itself and takes one value everywhere.

RangeREP 1 to REP 10, whole numbers only
Derived fromThe Japanese shindo scale, itself derived from Mercalli
ExpansionShindo 5-, 5+, 6- and 6+ become REP 5, 6, 7 and 8; shindo 7 becomes REP 9 and REP 10
Computed fromInstrumental intensity, filtered three-component acceleration, rounded down to the step
Warning thresholdA warning is issued wherever the estimated step exceeds REP 2
Also statedShindo, MSK-64, EMS-98 and Modified Mercalli, in every broadcast

Why the scale was expanded

The shindo scale distinguishes 5-minus from 5-plus and 6-minus from 6-plus, and the distinction matters, since the damage at 6-plus differs from the damage at 6-minus by more than the damage at 4 differs from that at 5. Suffixed steps travel poorly. They defeat numeric comparison in firmware, they require a convention for the suffix in every text encoding, and they oblige every device manufacturer to handle a value that sorts as a string.

The Republic therefore promoted each suffixed step to a whole number. The correspondence is exact and reversible, so a REP value converts to shindo and back without loss. Shindo 7 is open-ended, and the cooperative divided it at 6.0 m/s² into REP 9 and REP 10, so that the top of the scale distinguishes severe destruction from total destruction. No event in the record of the Republic has reached either.

Acceleration bands are indicative. The step is computed from instrumental intensity and not from peak acceleration alone, so a value near a boundary may fall on either side according to the frequency content and duration of the motion.

How the value is computed

The station computes an instrumental intensity from the three components of ground acceleration. The three components are filtered to the frequency band that shakes buildings and combined into a single motion. The size that motion reaches, for a total of 0.3 s during the event, is taken as the amplitude. That amplitude enters a logarithmic expression producing a continuous number, which is rounded down to the whole step.

I_R = 2 * log10(a) + 0.94         a in m/s^2, vector amplitude
REP = floor(I_R)                  clipped to the range 1 to 10

The continuous value is retained everywhere it is used. A broadcast states the whole step, and the archive, the shake maps and the conformance vectors hold the continuous number to two decimal places.

Intensity and magnitude

Two different quantities

Magnitude describes the event and takes a single value. Intensity describes a place and takes a different value at every place. The event of 3 August 2026 was magnitude 5.4 everywhere; its intensity was REP 5 in the Belvacius sub-region, REP 4 at Cortius, REP 3 at Leriduson and REP 1 at Lindoma.

The value stated first in a broadcast is the intensity estimated for the receiving device, since that is the number governing what a person should do. Magnitude follows, together with the highest intensity estimated anywhere in the territory.

Correspondence with the other scales

MSK-64, EMS-98 and Modified Mercalli are macroseismic scales assessed from observed effect, and shindo and REP are computed from instrument records. The correspondence between the two families is approximate at every step and is stated as a range wherever a REP step spans two macroseismic degrees. It rests on the pairs observed in the Republic since 1962, supplemented by the published Japanese correspondence for shindo.

Every REP-KJS broadcast states all four correspondences, so that residents who learned an intensity scale in another country, and visitors present in the Republic, can place the number without consulting a table.
