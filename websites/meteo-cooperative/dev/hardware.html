Sensor and station hardware

Every station the cooperative operates is documented well enough to be built again. The repository holds circuit boards, firmware, enclosure drawings, mounting details and a bill of materials for each class, together with the calibration procedure. The designs use parts obtainable inside the Republic and tolerate substitution where a part becomes unavailable.

The seismic station in detail

Detection time governs everything REP-KJS achieves, so a fork will spend most of its hardware effort here. Three parts determine the performance: the sensor, the timing and the link.

The strong motion station uses a three-axis MEMS accelerometer sampled at 200 Hz through a 24-bit converter. Noise floor is about 30 micro-g per root hertz, which is adequate for intensity and inadequate for locating small events. The broadband station handles the second task, and the two are deployed together at 21 sites on the margin.

Timing comes from satellite time with a temperature-compensated oscillator disciplined to it. Arrival times are the basis of every location, so a station clock 400 ms out corrupts the estimate for every event it contributes to. The firmware reports its own time uncertainty in each packet, and the issuing node discounts any station reporting above 20 ms.

Triggering happens at the station. Sending continuous data from 296 seismic stations would exceed the links available in the upland, so each station computes its own trigger and sends a short packet on firing, followed by the first seconds of the record. Continuous data flows from the 21 margin sites only, where the links support it.

Cost of the seismic network in material

A strong motion station is roughly 340 items on the bill of materials, of which the accelerometer, the converter and the timing receiver dominate. The remainder is enclosure, mounting and power.

The design was constrained to parts with at least two sources and a stated substitute for each, because the whole network was built during a period when the external catalogue was narrowing. Four substitutions have been made since 2021, and each is recorded in the repository with the calibration difference it introduced.

Siting

The drawings state where a station may stand, and the constraint differs by class. A strong motion station must sit on ground representative of the settlement it reports for, since an instrument bolted to rock beside a town built on sediment will understate what the town experiences. A rainfall gauge must stand clear of obstacles by at least twice their height. A river gauge must be clear of the debris line and above the highest recorded stage.

Departures from the siting rule are recorded in the station metadata and published, so a user can judge a series against the conditions it was measured in. Eleven stations in the network sit in positions the rules would exclude, mostly for want of any alternative in the sub-region.

Building one

The repository holds enough to build a station without asking anybody. Clone src.meteo.rof/rep-station. Board files open in KiCad, the firmware compiles with an ordinary toolchain, the mechanical drawings are dimensioned, and the bill of materials names each part with its substitutes. The calibration procedures state the equipment required and the tolerance to be met.

What the repository cannot supply is the local information. A fork must establish its own ground-motion model, its own soil map, its own station spacing and its own thresholds. Those are the subject of the porting guide.
