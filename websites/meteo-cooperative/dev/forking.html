Porting to another country

Any country may take these systems and operate them. This page states what transfers unchanged, what requires work in the receiving country, and how long each part occupied the cooperative. The software transfers in weeks. The local calibration occupies years, and it is where every fork has spent its effort.

What transfers unchanged

The software makes no major assumption about the Republic beyond a table of area codes. Replace that table and the whole warning path runs over any territory.

Components requiring no adaptation

RQUAK client Receives and presents on every platform Translation of the fixed wording

The order to do it in

The cooperative built REP-KJS first. The earthquake system delivers value earliest, needs the fewest local measurements, and produces the delivery paths, the certification scheme and the public familiarity the other two depend on. A country starting with flood warning would spend four years before anybody received a message.

Tests should begin immediately regardless of the order chosen, since a catchment model needs years of record before it can be calibrated, and that record accumulates only in real time.

A working shortcut

The soil amplification map is the largest single obstacle and the one most easily reduced. Any country holding a geological survey at 1:50,000 already possesses most of the input. Two of the three known forks began with a map derived from the geological survey alone, refined it against observed intensities afterwards, and reached a usable system some eighteen months earlier than the Republic did.

Six steps

  1. Take the trees

    Clone src.meteo.rof/rep-wire, src.meteo.rof/rep-kjs, src.meteo.rof/rquak and src.meteo.rof/rep-vectors.

  2. Replace the area table

    The area codes of the Republic sit in rep-wire/areas.tsv. Substitute your own administrative units. This is the only change the warning path needs before it will run over your territory.

  3. Mint a signing key

    Use Ed25519. Publish the public half where any device in your territory can fetch it, and set a rotation of a year with a ninety-day overlap. Devices hold the current key and the previous one.

  4. Begin testing on the first day

    A catchment model calibrates against years of record, and that record accumulates only in real time. Install river gauges and soil moisture probes before you write a line of flood code. The cooperative began testing in 2018 and issued its first flood warning in 2025.

  5. Build the local layers

    Station siting, the ground-motion model, the soil amplification map, the slope units and the thresholds. Derive the first soil map from your geological survey and refine it against observed intensities afterwards. This is the part that takes years.

  6. Run the vectors, then test the wording

    Run src.meteo.rof/rep-vectors against your build; the encoding and presentation families are territory-independent and must pass unchanged. Then test your translated wording on the population that will hear it, before any of it is broadcast.

Operator Since Systems Note

Notice of a fork is requested and never required. Two further implementations are known to the cooperative through fault reports and appear here without further detail, since neither operator has asked to be listed.

What the cooperative offers a fork

Questions receive answers. Calibration data, the correspondence work between REP and the macroseismic scales, and the failure record of every threshold set are supplied on request. A fork may send a threshold set for review, and the cooperative will state where it appears too tight or too loose against the Republic's own experience.

The cooperative operates nothing outside its territory, guarantees nothing about a fork, and reviews no operational decision. A country operating a warning system answers for it to its own population.

Faults found in a fork

Corrections have come back three times since 2022, twice concerning the message decoder and once concerning a rounding error in the intensity computation near a step boundary. Each was applied here and republished, and one of the three is still open as W-042. Send faults through the public information system, or to faults@meteo.rof.

Limits of a port

What a fork will find

The blind zone travels with the method. A country whose earthquakes occur beneath its cities gains far less from an earthquake warning system than one whose earthquakes occur at a distance from them, and no amount of engineering changes that.

Thresholds cannot be borrowed. A rainfall threshold fitted to the Republic applied to different soils will produce warnings on days nothing happens and silence on days something does. Fitting requires a local record of failures, which takes years to accumulate.

Fixed wording must be tested on the population that will hear it. Translating the Fluidian or English text and broadcasting it untested is the failure mode the cooperative would most expect to see in a hurried port.
