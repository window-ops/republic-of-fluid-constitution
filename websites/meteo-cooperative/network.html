The observing network

Every figure the cooperative publishes originates at a station. The network holds 2,410 reporting instruments across twelve classes, from the surface synoptic stations that have reported since 1901 to the slope extensometers installed for REP-LEW in 2024. The principal stations are listed below with what each measures and how often it reports.

Gaps in coverage

Where coverage is thin

Four radar sites leave three areas with coarse rainfall detail: the interior of Halvorum, western Aspreduson, and the upper Vandorum valleys. Flash flood warning in those places depends on rain gauges reporting rain that has already fallen, which shortens the notice available.

Roughly 3,400 km of watercourse is ungauged, mostly small upland streams. Stage there is inferred from the catchment model with no observation to correct it.

Seismic station spacing is 12 km on the Belvacius margin and 60 km in the western sub-regions. Detection of a western event is therefore slower, which matters little, since the western sub-regions produce almost no seismicity.

Two upper-air stations were closed in the 1990s, at Maravel and Cortius, and the record from both ends at that point. Proposals to reopen the Cortius station appear in each of the last three plan rounds.

The archive

Every observation is retained, including observations rejected by quality control, since a rejection is itself a datum about the station. Metadata for every station records each move, screen change and instrument change, so a user may reconstruct the correction applied to any series and work from the raw values where the correction is disputed.

The whole archive is published without charge and without registration. It is the same feed the cooperative uses internally, in the same format, with no reduced public version.
