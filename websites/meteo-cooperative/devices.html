Certified devices and RQUAK

A warning reaches a person through a device that knows how to receive it. The cooperative certifies devices that meet the conformance requirements and publishes a mark for them. Certification is voluntary, and no rule obliges any device distributed in the Republic to have it. For equipment with no native support, the cooperative publishes RQUAK, a client for every platform, compiled primarily for Linux.

CertificationVoluntary. A device may be distributed, used and connected in the Republic without it
Scope of a certificateAll three systems together. No device is certified for one alone
MarkThe three-band mark with the certificate number beneath it
TermFive years, renewable on retest against the format in force
ChargeNone. Testing is done by the cooperative within its own allocation
Client for uncertified equipmentRQUAK, published for every platform the cooperative can build for

What certification requires

A device holding the mark satisfies every requirement below. The full text of each is in the conformance specification, and the vectors that test them are published.

Conformance requirements
Requirement Limit Reason
Presentation latency Within 400 ms of receipt Every 400 ms is 1.4 km of S wave travel
Audible at a fixed level Overrides mute and volume settings A muted device warns nobody
Wakes a sleeping device From any low-power state within 400 ms Most events occur when most people are asleep
States every field received All fields of the segment The public learns the meaning of a warning from the broadcast itself
Handles cancellation Within 400 ms of receipt A false alert left standing damages the credibility of the whole system
Works with no account No registration, login or subscription Article 14 (2); a warning is a public act
Retains the last state offline Displays the age of the information A device out of contact should say so
Answers the monthly test First Wednesday, 11:00 An untested receiver is an unknown receiver
Reports no personal data Receive-only by default Article 14; the cooperative learns nothing about who received a warning

Certification records the model and the firmware version together. A firmware change affecting the warning path voids the certificate until retest.

What certification is not

A voluntary scheme

No rule requires certification. Telephones, computers, televisions and peripherals lacking the mark are distributed and used freely in the Republic, and a large share of the equipment in service has no native support of any kind.

The mark states that a device was tested and passed. It states nothing about the quality of the device in any other respect, and the cooperative recommends no model over another.

Anyone may test a device against the published vectors and reach their own conclusion. The vectors, the reference implementation and the test procedure are public, and the cooperative holds no monopoly on the assessment.

RQUAK

RQUAK is the client the cooperative publishes for equipment with no native support. It receives all three systems, presents the message in Fluidian or English, sounds the tones, and satisfies the same conformance requirements as a certified device where the host platform permits it.

It is written in C with no dependency beyond the standard library and the platform sound interface. The daemon occupies about 340 kB in memory and works on hardware some decades old. That was a stated design requirement, since a household with an old machine is a household that would otherwise receive nothing.

Platform Form Conformance Note

Partial conformance means the platform prevents a requirement from being met. The specific requirement is named in the release notes for that build.

Delivery paths

A warning travels by every path at once, and a device uses whichever reaches it first. Duplicate arrivals are discarded on the message identifier.

  • Data network: The published feed over the network of the telephony federation, which is the fastest path for most devices.
  • Broadcast radio: A low-bit-rate channel alongside the ordinary programme, which reaches receivers with no network connection.
  • Television: Inserted into the broadcast stream and rendered by the decoder.
  • Cell broadcast: To handsets in the coverage area, including handsets with no subscription.
  • Wired installations: Public address, rail signalling and industrial controllers over their own circuits.

Limits

What may prevent a warning arriving

A device out of coverage receives nothing. Mobile coverage in the upper Vandorum valleys and in parts of Halvorum is incomplete, and broadcast radio is the only path that reaches some of those places.

A device that is switched off receives nothing. A device asleep receives the warning where its platform allows a wake, and the builds marked partial above may present the message late.

Within the blind zone described under detection and limits, no delivery path is fast enough, since the shaking arrives before the message does.

The cooperative publishes the conformance vectors so any person may establish what their own equipment does. Testing a device against them takes a few minutes and is the only reliable way to know.
