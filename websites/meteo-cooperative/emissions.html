Emissions position of the Republic

The Republic emits more greenhouse gas than its territory absorbs. It is therefore a net polluter, and it has been one in every year of the record. The series has fallen in each of the last eleven years, and on the present trend the gross figure meets the territorial sink between 2049 and 2058.

What the cooperative measures and what it declines to measure

The cooperative compiles the physical inventory: quantities of gas released, by source, by sub-region and by year, together with the quantity absorbed by soil, woodland, peat and coastal sediment. Those quantities go to the Ecological Budget Authority, which fixes the ceilings, and to the Audit Office, which examines consumption against them. The cooperative fixes no ceiling and audits no enterprise.

Measurement is direct where direct measurement is possible. Six flux towers, continuous concentration monitoring at four background sites, and aircraft transects twice a year over the industrial sub-regions establish the totals independently of what the sources report. Where the two disagree, both figures are published with the discrepancy stated.

Where the remaining emissions come from

Electricity ceased to be the largest source in 1970, once fission passed half of supply, and the wind installations above Corcina have reduced the residual further since 1998. The sources that remain are the ones with no settled substitute.

Territorial and consumption accounts

The territorial account counts what is released inside the frontiers. The consumption account counts what is released anywhere in producing what the Republic uses, and it is the larger of the two, since the Republic imports concentrates, machinery and finished goods from beyond its borders.

The gap between the accounts stood at 31 per cent in 1990 and stands at 14 per cent now. It narrows as imports fall, which is the direct consequence of approaching resource independence. That narrowing is the subject examined on the harm to the periphery page, since emissions displaced abroad are borne by the countries where the production occurs.
