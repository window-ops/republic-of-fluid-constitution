How the company came to be here

Concentrate has left this coast since 1985. What changed on 7 July 2026 is the instrument under which a foreign company may handle any of it. The Constitution that came into force that day put every foreign entity operating in the Republic onto a revocable licence, on terms that had no precedent anywhere the founders of this company had traded before.

Maasdelta was incorporated in Rotterdam five months earlier, in anticipation of exactly that. Its four founders had read the draft, understood that the old ways of operating in the Republic were ending, and formed a company whose sole business would be a trade conducted on the new terms.

Why the company took this licence

The terms are the worst this company has ever traded on. It owns nothing in the Republic and can own nothing there. It cannot go to arbitration and must answer in the courts of a state whose law it had no part in making. In any question about the ecological budgets it carries the burden of proving itself right. A protective measure can be challenged on procedure alone. The licence can be taken away at any hour, and the technique the company brings passes into the knowledge commons whether the company likes it or not.

Against that stands the deposit. It is shallow, extensive and of unusually good grade, it is the only mineral wealth this territory has ever held in quantity, and the Republic is now retiring the reactors that consumed the greater part of it. Domestic demand falls on a published schedule while the surplus available for export rises.

The founders judged that a term of good tonnage on bad terms was worth more than the alternatives available to a house of this size, and that the licence conditions, however heavy, are at least written down and published.

The position after 2038

The Republic classes fission as transitional. Each reactor authorisation lapses on a date already certified, its own fuel demand falls on a published schedule, and the mining authorisations carry dates of their own. The company holds no view on whether those dates will move and plans on the basis that they will not.

A term contract running past 2038 is written with a clause on the position then obtaining. Maasdelta gives no assurance of supply beyond 31 December 2029, which is the date its licence expires. Whether the licence is renewed is decided on the terms then in force, and holding one now creates no expectation of holding one afterwards.
