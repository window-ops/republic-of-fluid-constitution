What the company does

Maasdelta buys the concentrate outright and resells it. A broker would introduce both ends of a sale and take a fee for doing so, leaving the goods the seller's until they became the buyer's. This company stands between the two ends instead: it becomes the owner of the concentrate, and it becomes the seller to the converter.

Ownership passes to Maasdelta as the drums are loaded aboard at Lindoma. From that moment the company holds the goods, pays for them whether or not it has yet found a buyer, and carries every risk attaching to them. If the price of concentrate falls between loading and delivery, the loss is the company's. If a cargo is lost or damaged at sea, the loss is the company's and its insurers'. If a buyer refuses a lot, the company is left holding it. The export cooperative of the Republic has been paid and is out of the matter.

Maasdelta operates no mine, no mill and no vessel, and it holds no property in the Republic at any point: what it owns is the concentrate, from the moment of loading until the moment of delivery.

Price is settled against the published long-term reference for U3O8, adjusted for the uranium content shown on the certificate and reduced by the deductions set out on the specification page.

What the licence permits, and what it withholds

The licence covers trade in concentrate and the brokerage attaching to it. It confers no ownership of property in the Republic, no right to hold a public function, and no right to arbitration outside the courts of the Republic. It is granted for a term and may be revoked before that term ends.

The technique the company brings to the trade passes into the knowledge commons of the Republic as a condition of holding the licence. The company publishes the register of its consignments for the same reason.

The term of this trade

The Republic classes fission as transitional and lapses each reactor authorisation on a certified date. Its own demand for fuel therefore falls on a published schedule, and the surplus available for export rises for a period before the mines themselves reach the end of their authorised life.

The company gives no assurance about supply beyond the term of its licence, which ends on 31 December 2029.
