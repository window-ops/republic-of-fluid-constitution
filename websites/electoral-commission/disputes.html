Electoral disputes

Any resident may contest the list, the conduct of the ballot, the count, the computation or the proclamation. It costs nothing, and the reasons come back in writing.

Article 248(1): disputes are decided by the Commission in the first instance, upon reasoned and published decision, and are appealable to the Constitutional Court.

Article 248(2): any resident may bring a dispute, and Article 231(4) governs costs.

Article 248(3): an irregularity annuls an election only where it was capable of affecting the result, and the Court states the finding on which the annulment rests.

Bringing a dispute

A dispute states what happened, where and when, and names the operation contested. A dispute may be delivered to any communal office, sent through the public information system, or spoken to a clerk who writes it down and reads it back. It may be brought in any language of record.

Residence is required and nothing further. No personal interest, no membership of a list and no loss need be shown.

The threshold for annulment

Two questions are put. The first is whether an irregularity occurred. The second is whether it was capable of affecting the result, and only the second supports an annulment. The discrepancy of fourteen papers at station 4-118 was established and was incapable of affecting a margin running to thousands. The finding of irregularity stands published, and the ballot stands.

The late opening at Lindoma quay denied the vote to an unknown number of residents. The margin at that station likewise put the result beyond doubt, so the remedy was directed at the conduct of the next ballot.
