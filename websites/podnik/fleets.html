For communes and sub-regions

Choosing a fleet

A council with lines to run brings its gradients, its counts and its depot. The federation returns two costed variants in physical units with the ecological account attached. The council decides, and the federation has no say in that.

Before the survey

Six things to bring

  • The lines

    Length, the slopes, the tightest curve, the narrowest street and the height of anything the vehicle passes under. The tightest corner on a route decides more of the answer than the length of it does.

  • The passenger numbers

    Counts by hour across a full week, including the school weeks and the market days. Where no count exists, the federation runs one, which takes four weeks.

  • The depot

    Bay lengths, door heights, pits, roof height, the yard turning circle, and what the electricity supply at the site can carry today.

  • The limit

    The materials and emissions the commune holds for the period. A fleet proposal that breaches it is a proposal the commune cannot lawfully adopt, so the ceiling is part of the question from the start.

  • What is already there

    The existing fleet with its ages and its remaining parts support. Most advice ends with keeping a good part of it.

  • The neighbours

    Where a network crosses a boundary, the councils on both sides run it jointly, so both are heard from the start.

The procedure

From request to traffic

About eighteen months, of which the federation holds four.

  1. The request

    Any communal council, sub-regional council or user assembly may ask. It costs the commune nothing: the federation is funded through the plan and its accounts are public.

  2. Survey, four to six weeks

    Two engineers walk every line, measure the constraints and check the depot. Counts run if none exist. The survey is published when it is finished, whatever it says.

  3. Two variants, eight weeks

    Always two, never one. Each names its models, its numbers, the building work at its depot, its parts schedule and its ecological account, and each states plainly what it gives up.

  4. The hearing

    The assembly of the operating cooperative, the users of the service and the communal council are heard together, in public, with the variants already sent out.

  5. The commune decides

    Podnik has no power of decision here. The council adopts a variant, adopts neither, or sends both back with reasons.

  6. Build and review

    Delivery runs to the plan period. Two years after the last vehicle enters service the federation reviews the advice against what actually happened and publishes the comparison.

Limits

What the federation cannot do

The federation advises and the commune decides. Podnik exercises no public authority. It cannot allocate a vehicle to a commune, refuse one, or make its advice a condition of anything. Where a member enterprise of the federation is reviewed, that happens under the procedure organic law lays down, with the assembly of the enterprise, its users and the communal council heard.

Advice can be objected to. Any person may put a question about a survey, a variant or an account, and receive a reasoned answer within the period fixed by law. A decision taken on the advice can be challenged in an ordinary court.

Surveys are published whole. A survey that finds the existing fleet good enough is published in the same form as one that finds it lacking. The federation publishes the count of each in its annual report.
