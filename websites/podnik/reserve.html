The size of the fleet

Holding 18,592 to run 12,400

Having many vehicles allows any city or village to cover failures within eight minutes, absorb the inspection cycle and meet the peaks. They also exist because nobody here has a car to fall back on.

The arithmetic

One vehicle for every 990 people

18,592 vehicles serve 18,399,000 residents. That is one public vehicle for every 990 people. A country of the same size with private cars in it would hold seven or eight million of them, and would still need a public fleet on top.

Cars here sit above the resource intensity threshold. Communes, federations and public establishments hold them, and residents book them by rotation or by lot. Nobody is allocated a car of their own. Lindoma holds fewer cars than any other town in the country.

A missed vehicle has no substitute. In many places where private cars are common, a bus that fails puts out the people who could have driven and strands the people who could not. However, in the Republic, everyone gets stranded.

Seven reasons

Why the margin exists

  1. Waiting time is a social duty

    Residents ride to parks, parties, restaurants, gatherings and one another's homes, and a service that keeps them waiting shrinks the distance anyone will travel for company. A gap of ten minutes costs nobody an arrangement, and an hour with nothing running takes the evening from everyone who planned around it. Two strangers from the same commune will often stay aboard for a second loop to finish a conversation, and the social commitments allow for that.

  2. A failure has to be covered in minutes

    A vehicle reporting a fault is taken off the route and a relief vehicle takes the next departure. The target is eight minutes in the towns and twenty on the sub-regional routes.

    Holding to that target requires vehicles standing ready with a driver booked on.

  3. Repair happens at leisure

    Once the swap is done, the faulty vehicle goes to the workshop and stays there until it becomes fully functional. Nothing about the timetable presses on the mechanic, and no vehicle returns to service after an incomplete repair.

    This is why the expected lives in the catalogue are as long as they are. A tram gets thirty-five years because it never runs damaged.

  4. The inspection cycle takes vehicles out constantly

    Every vehicle in the register is off the road on some schedule: four hours every 10,000 km, two days every 60,000, five days a year, and nine to sixteen weeks at the half-life rebuild. Nationwide, at any hour of any day, about 1,730 vehicles are being inspected and another 1,240 are at a factory.

  5. The peaks are short and sharp

    School weeks, market days, the harvest movements out of the grain basin and the Lindoma port shift require more vehicles to be suddenly released for a few hours. The alternative to holding them is refusing the journeys.

  6. Special duties draw on the same pool

    Escort and support vehicles for Exceptional Transports, engineering trains, the evacuation reserve held against flood on the eastern marshes, and the coaches held for the annual assembly movements contribute to the count.

  7. Fleets are distributed evenly, so the total rises

    A rural commune keeps more vehicles for each resident than a city does, because a single coach covering four villages must still be replaced within twenty minutes when it fails. Spreading the fleet evenly across twenty sub-regions costs more vehicles than keeping it in one place would.

Materials

Why the factories can build at this rate

The Bloc exchange settles in goods, labour, technique and knowledge, recorded in physical units through a single external account. Steel sections, aluminium, copper for motor windings and battery cells arrive on that account, and they always arrive in large quantities.

The binding limit is the ecological limit. Materials being available does nothing to raise the emissions and materials limits a commune adopts for any plan period. A fleet proposal breaching any of the limits is a proposal the commune cannot lawfully adopt. Every vehicle in the catalogue contains its build account for that reason.

Having plenty shows up as long life. A factory with enough steel can build more units that last longer and have a heavier floor frame, a thicker floor panel and a bolted roof panel that can be replaced on its own.

From 2027

Opening new-build export

The federation assembly decided in March 2026 to open new-build export. Until now a city outside the Republic could buy vehicles the Republic had finished with, which are listed on the surplus pages and depend on what happens to be coming out of service. The factories have the materials and the capacity to build new vehicles to order, and the assembly has decided they should.

The intended buyer is a city that cannot build its own fleet. Modern low-floor vehicles are sold at prices that put them out of reach of most city operators in the world, and the cheap alternative is usually a vehicle nobody outside the original factory can repair. A city in that position buys twice: once for the vehicle and again for every part, on terms the seller sets.

Podnik quotes the cost of the work and the materials and adds nothing to it. Patents are banned here, so no licence fee is charged. The drawings, the parts list and the list of moulds and tools go with the vehicle in every case.

Terms

What an export buyer gets

  • Repairable by the buyer

    Every model in the programme was designed to be worked on in a city depot with ordinary tools. Bolted roof panels, door modules common across the range, and a traction package that comes out on four bolts.

  • Build it yourself if you prefer

    A buyer taking the drawings may build the next batch in their own factory and owes the federation nothing for doing so.

  • Parts at the cost of making them

    Ten years guaranteed from delivery. After that the drawings remain and any workshop can make parts from them.

  • The two conditions

    The buyer must run the vehicles in public service and not resell them to a state the Republic has found life-limiting.

A note on timing

Export comes after the Republic's own renewal. The programme uses the capacity left after the domestic orders have been placed, which is why the dates above run from 2027 to 2030 and why quantities in the first years will be modest.
