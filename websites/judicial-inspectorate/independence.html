Independence

This body is independent of the Judicial Council whose proceedings it opens. The Council may not appoint it, instruct it, pay it, discipline it or remove it.

Article 214(2): the Inspectorate is independent of the Judicial Council. Its head is appointed by the Constitutional Court in plenary for a single term of seven years, non-renewable, from among candidates presented by open competition.

Article 214(3): the Judicial Council may in no case appoint, instruct, remunerate, discipline or remove the Inspectorate or its members.

Appointment of the head

Step By whom
Open competition upon published criteria Candidates present themselves
Appointment from among the candidates presented The Constitutional Court, sitting in plenary
Term Seven years, single, never renewed
Instruction during the term Nobody. The Judicial Council is named expressly.

The arrangement of the three bodies

This body opens disciplinary proceedings and decides none. The Judicial Council hears them through a panel composed for each case by lot, in which the members drawn by lot hold the majority. The Constitutional Court decides an appeal finally.

The division is plain: the Inspectorate opens proceedings under Article 206(2), and decides them in no case.

Who examines this body

Its reports are public, and an annual report goes to the Assembly, to the Judicial Council and to the public. Article 215(1) has the Audit Office audit the resources of the judiciary annually, and Article 215(2) publishes the audit in full.

Every person is allowed to address an oversight office and to receive a reasoned answer. A criticism of the conduct of this body may be addressed to it directly, and the answer is published.
