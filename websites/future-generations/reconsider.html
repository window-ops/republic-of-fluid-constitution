Going to court instead

Where the matter is an ecological harm within Title II, this office holds standing before the Ecological Chamber under Article 257(3), Article 19(2) and Article 27(1).

Article 27(2): the Chamber may annul the offending act, enjoin the activity in the interim, order restoration on a fixed timetable, order the sequestration of resources sufficient to accomplish it, and declare the personal liability of those responsible.

Article 27(3): interim relief is granted where a credible risk of irreversible harm is shown, and the applicant furnishes no security.

Article 19(3): court fees, security for costs and adverse costs orders are excluded, except where the court finds the action vexatious.

Why the office rarely uses it

Standing before the Ecological Chamber extends to actions under Title II. Much of what this office examines falls outside that Title: deferred maintenance, obligations binding future bodies, and risks transferred without ecological harm. For those matters the powers available are the published opinion and the required reconsideration.

Any person has standing to bring an action in the name of a natural entity, with no showing of personal injury, residence, nationality or economic interest. Communes, cooperatives, unions, scientific institutions and associations hold the same standing. The computations of this office are published, and actions are frequently brought upon them by other applicants.
