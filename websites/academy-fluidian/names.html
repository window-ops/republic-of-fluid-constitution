Names and public terms

What places are called in Fluidian, and the vocabulary of government, office and wrongdoing.

How a place name is treated

A foreign name is written as it is heard and then made to obey the stress rule, which means the ending usually moves. Four patterns account for nearly all of it.

  • A final vowel is dropped where the name would otherwise be stressed in the wrong place: Warsaw is Varsov, Marseille is Marsil, Ljubljana is Lubian.
  • A capital or a large city keeps a final -i where the source has one: Bucharest is Bucaresti, Budapest is Budapesti.
  • A smaller town takes -y, the same ending that makes a place name from an adjective: Thessaloniki is Salony, Katowice is Katovicy, Cluj is Clujy.
  • A cluster the language will not say is broken by an inserted vowel: Brno is Birno.

Names taken whole are left whole. Prague is Praha. Ostrava has two forms, Ostrov and Ostrava, and both are current; the shorter form is older and is the one used in the depots.

Demonyms

A demonym is made from the name of the state. A name ending in a vowel drops it and takes -an; a name ending in a consonant takes -ian. Italia gives italian, Moldova gives moldovan, Egipt gives egiptian. The demonym is an adjective and follows its noun in the ordinary way: un persone italian.

Forty names do not follow the rule. They are set out below.
