Members

Members are elected for five years by open list, with proportionality computed across the whole territory.

Article 120(1): members are elected by universal, equal, free, secret and direct suffrage under Article 60, by open list, in the sub-regional districts established under Article 121.

Article 120(2): proportionality is computed at the level of the Republic, and the seats are apportioned among the lists in proportion to the votes each has obtained throughout the territory.

Article 120(3): the seats obtained by a list are assigned to its district lists in the order of the votes each district list has obtained.

End of term Upon the convening of the succeeding Assembly. Extension by decision of the Assembly itself is in no case available. Article 123(3)
Postponement Only where an emergency or armed attack makes an election impossible in part of the territory, by a majority of two thirds, upon the prior finding of the Constitutional Court Article 123(4)

The first Assembly under this Constitution is to be elected within a year of commencement, and the Electoral Commission has given notice for 4 July 2027.

Incompatibilities

Article 125(1): the seat is incompatible with delegation to the National Planning Congress, with judicial office, with the offices of Title IX, with membership of the Ecological Budget Authority, with command in the forces, and with a management organ of an enterprise, a federation or a public establishment. Membership of an executive committee is compatible with the seat and is reserved to members of the Assembly.

Article 125(2): a member elected to an incompatible office chooses between them within fifteen days, and silence is taken as resignation of the seat.

A public declaration is required on taking the seat, verified and published by the Office of Democratic Integrity. Declarations for the current membership are complete.

Immunity

Article 127(1): a member may in no case be prosecuted, detained or held liable at any time, including after the end of the mandate, for an opinion expressed or a vote cast in the exercise of the office. This immunity admits no exception.

Article 127(2): prosecution for any other act proceeds under the ordinary law. Detention before judgment requires the authorisation of the Assembly, which is refused only upon a finding that the prosecution is directed at the exercise of the mandate, and which is required in no case for an offence committed in flagrancy or for an offence of corruption.

The first paragraph protects speech and votes without limit of time. The second leaves every other act to the ordinary law, and removes the authorisation requirement altogether for flagrancy and for corruption.

Investigations touching a member are reported in aggregate to the public at annual intervals, and the prosecution service publishes that report.
