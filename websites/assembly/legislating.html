Organic law

An organic law requires three fifths of the members of the Assembly and compulsory referral to the Constitutional Chamber. The effect of an organic law altering an entrenched matter is delayed until after the first ordinary election following promulgation, and it reaches no justice, judge or member of the Judicial Council in office.

The delay separates the majority that adopts such a law from the composition of the bodies it alters.

Commenting on a bill

Say what the bill would do and what should change. A comment may be lodged at any communal office, by letter, or through the public information system, free of charge, in any language of record, and may be spoken to a clerk who writes it down.

The committee publishes the comments received together with its answer to each substantiated objection. The organic law on the drawing of lots received 1,412 comments, each substantiated objection answered.

Public sittings

Sittings of the Assembly are public and transmitted, and votes are recorded. Committees sit in public, and a committee may require the attendance of any member of an executive committee, any officer of the administration, of a planning body, of a federation or of a public establishment, and any person holding a delegated public function.
