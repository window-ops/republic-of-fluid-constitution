Discipline

The Inspectorate opens proceedings. A panel of the Council hears them, with the members drawn by lot in the majority. The Constitutional Court decides an appeal finally.

Article 206(1): disciplinary liability arises for a breach of the duties of Article 204, for a failure to decide within the periods fixed, for a breach of Article 201, for concealment of an interest, and for conduct incompatible with the office. It arises in no case for the content of a decision, for its reversal on appeal, or for a legal opinion expressed.

Article 206(3): proceedings are heard by a disciplinary panel of the Council, composed for each case by lot from its members, in which the members drawn by lot under Article 210 hold a majority.

The course of a proceeding

Step What happens Provision
Opening By the Judicial Inspectorate, upon complaint by any person or on its own motion Article 206(2) and Article 214(4)
Panel Composed for the case by lot from the members of the Council, with the members drawn by lot in the majority Article 206(3)
Hearing The judge is heard, has access to the file, and is assisted by counsel at public cost Article 206(4)
Publicity Hearings and decisions are public, with the redactions Article 51 requires for the protection of complainants Article 206(5)
Sanction Warning, reprimand, transfer, suspension or removal, proportionate to the breach established Article 206(6)
Appeal To the Constitutional Court, which decides finally Article 206(4)

Removal

Removal follows only upon a grave breach established by this procedure or for conviction of an offence by final judgment. Article 203(3) allows a judge to be transferred, suspended or removed in the course of a term only by this procedure.

Limits of discipline

Article 206(1): liability arises in no case for the content of a decision, for its reversal on appeal, or for a legal opinion expressed.

Article 204(6): judges may teach, write, research and take part in public debate upon questions of law, and this in no case engages disciplinary liability.

Article 214(6): the Inspectorate in no case examines the content of a decision, and an inspection touching the reasoning of a judgment is void.
