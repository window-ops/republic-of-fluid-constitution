Jurisdiction

This page sets out the matters assigned to the Court and the bodies that may bring each of them.

What belongs to another court

Article 200(4): the National Court decides no question of the conformity of a norm with this Constitution, which belongs to the Constitutional Chamber under Article 195.

The division runs the other way as well. Cassation upon a question of law, divergence between regional courts, and manifest breach of procedure belong to the National Court under Article 200(2). The Court decides no such question, and a party raising one before it is directed to the court that has competence over it.

Ecological actions under Article 19 may be brought at first instance before any competent court. Article 198(4) gives the Ecological Chamber of the Court a concurrent first-instance jurisdiction in those matters, so an applicant may choose.

Guardianship of the distribution of functions

Article 230(1): the Court guards the distribution of functions established by Article 13 and enforces the limits this Constitution places upon the Assembly, upon the executive committees, upon the planning bodies, upon the territories and upon itself.

Article 230(2): the union of legislative and executive functions in the Assembly enlarges the powers of neither under Article 154(6), and the Court determines whether an act of the Assembly or of a committee has exceeded them.

The Republic places the executive function in committees of the Assembly and has no separate ministry. Article 154(6) provides that the combination adds nothing to either function, and the Court decides in a contested case whether a committee has done something the Assembly itself could not have done.

Acts void in every case

The Constitution lists the acts that are void whoever adopts them, by whatever majority and by whatever procedure. Article 265(3) allows any person to plead the nullity before any court at any time, so those matters reach the Court by referral and reach the ordinary courts directly. Article 266 extends the same nullity to a measure attaining such a result in substance, and to a series of measures attaining it cumulatively.

The Office of Democratic Integrity has two such referrals pending before the Court. Its register of referrals states each one.

Entrenchment

Article 223(1): the number of justices, the duration and staggering of their terms, the appointment by lot, the composition of the pool, the jurisdiction of the Court and the composition of the Judicial Council are fixed by this Constitution and by organic law adopted under it.

Article 223(2): an organic law altering any of those matters takes effect only after the first ordinary election of the Assembly following its promulgation, and applies in no case to a justice, a judge or a member of the Council in office.
