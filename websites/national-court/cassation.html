Cassation

The regional court decides finally. Cassation opens on three grounds and on no others.

Article 198(3): the regional court decides finally, except for cassation to the National Court under Article 200(2).

Article 200(2): the Court hears cassation upon a question of law, upon a divergence between regional courts, and upon a manifest breach of procedure.

Article 200(3): its judgments in cassation bind the court to which the case is remitted.

What cassation does not reopen

Every judgment carries an appeal to the regional court on the facts and on the law, and that appeal is where the facts are decided. Cassation examines the law applied to the facts as the regional court found them, and it reopens no finding of fact.

A judgment quashed in cassation is remitted, and the court to which it is remitted is bound by the judgment of this Court on the point of law.

A refusal states reasons

Judges decide within the periods fixed by law and state reasons. A refusal of cassation states which ground was invoked and why it was not made out, and the reasons are published with the decision. Two of the seven appeals above were refused.

Where a constitutional question arises

Questions of conformity with this Constitution are removed from this Court. Article 195 requires a reference to the Constitutional Chamber, and the case waits for the answer. One appeal of the current year took that route in March 2026.

The limits of this Court in full

Cost

Access to a prompt, effective and free remedy is given. Article 231(4) governs costs before the Constitutional Court, and Article 19(3) excludes fees, security for costs and adverse costs orders in ecological actions, except where a court finds an action vexatious.
