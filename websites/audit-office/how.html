How we audit

Three questions, asked in physical units, of whatever body has been consuming what belongs in common.

The three questions an audit asks

Did resources reach the purposes the plan assigned to them
Purpose
Were the ceilings for the period observed
Ceiling
Were the accounts kept truthfully
Truth

The units

Energy, materials, water, land, emissions, labour time and the useful life of goods are the primary units of the plan, and every ceiling is expressed in the same terms. An audit therefore counts things, and it converts nothing into any common denominator, because the Republic has abolished the denominator that used to serve.

Units in which findings are stated.
Quantity Unit Source of the figure
Energy GWh Physical accounts of the body audited
Materials t Receipts, stock and discard records
Water Ml Abstraction and discharge meters
Land ha Spatial plan of the territory
Emissions t CO2e Ecological accounts
Labour time h Workplace records
Useful life years Plan entry against the retirement record

The course of an audit

Ordinary course, from opening to answer.
Stage What happens What becomes public
Opening Scope, questions and method published with the reference Everything, on the day
Collection Records requisitioned, systems inspected, testimony taken The list of what was required, and any refusal
Verification Physical count against the accounts, on the premises The method of counting
Draft finding Put to the body audited for correction of fact only Nothing. The draft leaves this office in no other form.
Publication Findings, quantities, persons responsible, remedies proposed The report in full, with dissents of the college
Answer Sixty days for a public written answer upon each point The answer, or the record of its absence

The draft is put for correction of fact alone. No organ receives an opportunity to alter a report, and a disagreement about a conclusion is answered after publication, never before it.

Auditing while a programme runs

An audit may be opened at any stage, and none is confined to the period after a programme has been completed. Four of the six open audits concern programmes still running, and two have produced corrections that changed a ceiling for the current period.
