CARD-ROM against four other standards

MIFARE Classic, DESFire EV3, FeliCa and Calypso are the cards the gates of the Bloc meet most often in a traveller's wallet. The figures below are the published ones for each standard, and the CARD-ROM column is measured on a revision 3 card at a Fluid intercity gate. A card of any of these standards is refused at the gate, and the panel says which one it is and where it is accepted.

Reading the tables

  • Speed at the gate

    DESFire and FeliCa are quicker on the air in systems with closed gates. A Fluid gate is semi-open, so the digital ticket is accounted for while a person walks through it, and 148 ms against 100 ms changes nothing a traveller can feel.

  • The purse module

    Most of these standards were built to store value and to deduct it at a gate. CARD-ROM has that module but it is not compiled in Fluid. The comparison of purse features is therefore irrelevant here.

  • Unit cost of an open mask

    A CARD-ROM chip is made by three factories in the Bloc to a published mask. The volumes are small, so the unit cost stands above a MIFARE Classic and near a DESFire. The card is free to the holder either way.

  • MIFARE Classic and Crypto-1

    Crypto-1 has been broken since 2008 and cloning takes a laptop. It is listed here because it is still in wallets across the region, including the STB cards that reach the Lindoma gates most often.

At the gate

The gate reads it and refuses passage. A Suica, a Card Activ, an Oyster or a Navigo is intact and readable, and contains no Bloc record and no Bloc identifier. The panel names the card, its issuer and its product, states where it is accepted, and states why passage is refused here.

No balance is touched. The gate reads the standard and the identifier, and stops. It takes nothing, writes nothing, and keeps nothing about the card presented.

The panel answers in the language of the card. A FeliCa card issued in Japan brings a panel in Japanese. A MIFARE card from Bucharest brings one in Romanian. The gate supports twelve languages and falls back to Fluidian and English if the language of the card is not supported.

Then it says what is needed. A Universal Bloc Transport Card, or a Universal Ticket, which is the paper form of the same record and covers one journey, are the only compatible devices that can be issued at the counter behind the gate or at every machine on the concourse.
