145 stations, four sizes

What stands on a platform

A station in the Republic is sized by the trains that call there, and every size has the same three things: a board with the times, shelter that is warm in winter, and a way to reach a person. Gates stand at the intercity platforms and nowhere else.

  • 145 stations
  • 22 town stations
  • 38 halts
  • 19 with intercity gates
  • 0 with a ticket window that sells anything

The four things on a concourse

Board, machine, gate, counter

  • The board

    Split-flap, driven from the timetable and the position feed together. It turns over on a change and again a minute before departure. Every board shows the same information as the application.

  • The machine

    Issues a card, reads one, writes the display language, prints the paper form, and lists the sections with their journey times. It asks for a language and nothing else.

    What the machine writes

  • The gate

    Stands open. It records that a journey was made, so each route group learns how many people use its lines. Nineteen stations have them, all on intercity platforms. Nothing is charged and no balance is read.

  • The counter

    A person who does everything the machine does, and the things it cannot: a card for someone whose hands will not work a panel, a section explained, a wheelchair space held on the train.

Around the platform

Things a station has that nobody announces

  • Warmth

    Every shelter on the network is heated between November and March, including the halts. The heating is on the station account, and a shelter that is cold is a fault worth reporting.

  • Water and a lavatory

    Drinking water on every staffed station, and a lavatory that is open whenever the station is. Neither is charged for anywhere.

  • Left luggage

    Lockers at the four central stations, taken by a card presented at the door, free for seventy-two hours. Beyond that a station worker moves the bag to the office and leaves a note in the locker.

  • Bicycles

    Racks under cover at every station, and a bicycle travels in the vestibule of any train without a reservation. The four night services hold a cycle space in the guard's van instead.
