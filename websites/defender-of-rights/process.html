What happens next

From the receipt of a complaint to an order of a court, with the point at which each step becomes public.

  1. Same day

    Receipt

    The complaint is registered and you get a reference. Your identity is separated from the file at this point and held under Article 253(5).

  2. Within twenty-one days

    Decision to investigate

    Every complaint gets an answer with reasons. A refusal to investigate is written down, states why, and may be reviewed. Article 253(2) leaves no complaint unanswered.

  3. As long as it takes

    Investigation

    This office may require documents, records, data, access to premises and systems, and the testimony of anyone holding a public or delegated function. Commercial confidentiality is a ground of refusal in no case. Refusal is itself an offence.

  4. On completion

    Finding, published in full

    Findings are published in full, in open form, free of charge, with the dissenting opinions of members. This office sends the finding to no organ before its publication and gives no organ a chance to alter it.

  5. Sixty days

    The body answers

    Where a violation is found, this office proposes the remedy and the body answers publicly within sixty days, upon each point. Silence is recorded in the following annual report.

  6. Where the remedy fails

    Court

    The matter is sent to the competent court, and Article 43 governs what the court may order: declaration, injunction, restoration, performance on a fixed timetable, annulment, and reparation.

What a court can order that this office cannot

Article 43(2): remedies comprise a declaration, an injunction, an order of restoration, an order to perform on a fixed timetable, annulment of the offending act, and reparation.

Article 43(3): reparation is owed by the public body, the enterprise or the establishment responsible, in goods, services, labour or restoration of position.

Article 43(4): persons who decided upon or executed a violation are personally liable, and the liability of the organisation extinguishes none of it.

Article 43(5): interim protection is granted where a credible risk of irreparable harm is shown.

Going to court in your name or its own

This office may bring proceedings in its own name or in support of a complainant, and Article 252(3) allows it to intervene in any proceeding concerning a right of Title III. The complainant chooses between the two, and a proceeding brought in the name of this office alone requires no further part from the complainant.

Interim protection under Article 43(5) moves fastest. Where provision has been cut off or a service withdrawn, an application for an interim order can be made within days, and the argument about the merits follows afterwards.
