Buying what the Republic does not make

The cooperative buys abroad on behalf of its members: a phone built to be repaired, a spare part for a laboratory instrument or a licence for a game whose seller cannot operate here.

Four steps to membership and a first request

  1. Joining at a communal office

    Membership is open to every resident and is free of charge. On proof of residence the account is opened and the member number is issued on the same day.

  2. Stating the request

    A request names the thing, the seller and the reason domestic supply is insufficient. Where a domestic equivalent exists and is available, the request is refused with reasons.

  3. Two ceilings are checked

    The first is personal: the residual units a member holds for the period, or the foreign currency already standing to their account. The second is collective: the materials and emissions the import carries, counted against the ecological budget of the Republic.

  4. Payment and delivery

    Payment leaves an account held in Ireland. Goods clear customs at Lindoma and travel on by rail. A licence or a key arrives by post to the communal office.

What the cooperative declines

  • Converting euro into residual units. The units are issued equally to every resident and cannot be bought.
  • Moving a balance from one member to another, lending against it, or paying interest upon it.
  • Holding a currency for gain. Currency is used to complete a purchase and held no longer than the payment takes.
  • Taking in money whose source a member cannot show. The origin of every incoming sum is recorded, as Irish law requires of any payment institution.

Where member balances are held

Member balances sit in a segregated account at a credit institution in Dublin, apart from the cooperative's own funds. Where the cooperative fails, those balances belong to the members and form no part of its estate. The arrangement is inspected annually and the auditor's report is published in full.
