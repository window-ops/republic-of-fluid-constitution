Accounts

The two balances a member may hold

A member may hold foreign currency they brought in themselves, and may assign the residual units issued to them for the period. The two follow different rules, and the cooperative keeps them apart on the books and on the teller slip.

The brought-in balance

This is euro that already belonged to the member: wages from a posting abroad, a fee for work done for a foreign institution, a gift carried home or receipts from a visitor. It sits in Ireland, in an account segregated from the cooperative's own funds, and it stays euro until the member spends it.

Inside the Republic the balance buys nothing. Food, water, sanitation, housing, energy, care, transport and communication are furnished free at the point of use, and no teller accepts euro for anything the plan supplies. The balance covers purchases that cross the border and the member's own use while travelling.

The translation entitlement

Every resident receives the same allocation of residual units at the opening of each period, and the units expire at its close. They are personal: a purported transfer is void and extinguishes the unit transferred. The cooperative therefore takes an assignment from the member holding the units, and takes nothing from anyone else on that member's behalf.

An assignment of units is a request that the cooperative make a purchase, paid from the common export pool. The units reach no foreign seller and become euro at no point.

Why euro cannot buy units

The unit is issued equally to every resident, and an issue that varied by contribution, by conduct, by function or by office would be void. A member able to buy units with euro would hold more of them by holding more euro, which defeats the equality of the issue.

The rule runs the other way as well: a member cannot sell units for euro.

The ceilings

Two ceilings apply to every request. The personal ceiling is what the member holds. The collective ceiling is physical: the materials and emissions the goods carry, counted against the ecological budget of the Republic on the same terms as domestic production.

When the collective ceiling nears its end the cooperative publishes the fact, and requests queue in the order they arrived. Repair parts take priority in that queue, on the ground that a part keeping a machine in use costs less in materials than a replacement.

Charges

Four euro for handling a request, whatever the request is worth. Carriage and duty at cost, shown on the slip before a member commits. No charge for holding a balance, for opening one or for closing one, and no margin on the rate.

The reference rate is the daily rate published by the European Central Bank, applied as published. A spread on the rate would amount to trading currency for gain, which the licence in the Republic excludes.

Any member may see the cooperative's own accounts, its dealings with its bank in Dublin, and the auditor's report, at any teller or on the page about the cooperative.
