What the three procedures mean

One step, euro area

The cooperative pays the seller by ordinary transfer from Dublin. No currency is converted and no customs frontier is crossed. On average, it takes nine days to deliver a resouce after the request has been approved.

Handling 4.00 EUR

Cover step, other EEA currency

The seller wants krona, zloty or krone. The cooperative buys exactly the sum owed at the reference rate on the day, holds it for as long as the payment takes, and holds none of it afterwards.

Handling 4.00 EUR + three days

Customs and screening, outside the EEA

A customs declaration, a check against the sanctions lists binding an Irish payment institution, and tarrifs at the rate the goods attract. Four to seven weeks is usual.

Handling 4.00 EUR + tarrifs

Grounds of refusal

Three grounds account for nearly every refusal:

  • Domestic capacity: Where the Republic makes the thing, or could make it with capacity standing idle, the import waits and the planning bodies are told that the need exists.
  • The intensity threshold: Goods heavy enough in energy, materials, land and emissions are held by the commune, the region or a public establishment and used by booking or rotation, so no personal allocation is possible whatever a member holds.
  • Repairability: A design without a minimum service life, without parts on sale and without a route of recovery fails the standard the Republic applies to its own production, and the same standard applies to imports.

A request for an item outside the catalogue may be made at any teller. It is examined on its own facts, and the answer usually comes within twenty-one days or less if possible.
