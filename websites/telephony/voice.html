Calling

Nothing is metered, so nothing is counted. Most calls are carried directly between the two people making them, which helps keeps the main lines clear and leaves the exchange with nothing to overhear.

The basisNo billing system exists

Free at the point of use

Calls are not metered, timed or counted. There is no billing system, which is worth saying plainly because it explains most of the rest of this site: a network that never had to answer the question "who called whom, and for how long" was never built to be able to.

Every number in the plan below is reachable from a handset with no SIM in the case of the short codes, and from any SIP client that can reach sip.telephony.rof.

How a call is carried94 per cent go direct

Peer to peer, for traffic reasons

Most calls never touch a server

When two people call each other, the exchange does the signalling and then gets out of the way. The two ends negotiate over WebRTC and carry the audio directly between themselves. The exchange knows the call was set up; it never carries a single packet of what was said.

The reason is traffic before it is anything else. A relayed call crosses the network twice and lands on lines that are thin in the countryside and cross a single gateway at the border. Routing calls directly halves that load, and on a bonded voiceband line it is the difference between a call working and not working.

turn.telephony.rof relays only when the two ends genuinely cannot reach each other, which happens on about 6 per cent of calls.

Older and strangerNine mechanical exchanges

The old exchanges

Nine mechanical exchanges are still in service, all of them in sub-regions that voted to keep them. They route about 8,000 lines between them and they interwork with everything else through a gateway at Sederna.

A resident on one of these will notice that dialling is slower and that the ring cadence is wrong. Nothing else differs, and the short codes work.

Voicemail, if you want it

There is no central mailbox and no message store on the network. If you want messages taken, your own equipment takes them: the TS-24 router contains an answering function, and any SIP client can do the same.

121 reaches whatever you are running, on your own line, in your own house. If you run nothing, an unanswered call is simply unanswered.
