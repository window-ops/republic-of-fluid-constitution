If you need help now

  • Emergency 112 Fire, ambulance, communal police, sea rescue and mountain rescue. One number, answered in the sub-region you are calling from.
  • Operator 100 A person, at any hour. They will connect you, look a number up, or read a page of this directory aloud.
  • Line faults 101 A dead line, a noisy line, an exchange that has dropped. Report it from any other line, including a neighbour's.
  • Text and speech relay 108 A relay assistant between text and speech, in both directions, on every service in this directory.

These four answer from every handset in the Republic. They answer from a phone with no SIM in it, from a line whose service has been stopped, and from a call box with no account attached to it at all.

What you already have Provision

Every resident is furnished with a computer, a telephone and a connection between them and everyone else. None of it is billed, metered or capped. There is no contract to sign, no plan to choose and no upgrade to be offered.

You hold one identity, and it works on every service listed below. You may hold as many further identities as you want under names of your own choosing, and you never have to say why. Telephony keeps no link between them.

The number plan First digit tells you the line

Reading a number

National numbers are nine digits. The first says what kind of line answers, the next two say which exchange holds it, and the last six are the subscriber.

First digit Kind of line Written
1 Short codes, three digits, free from everywhere 112
2 Fixed line, copper or fibre 2 18 044 125
3 ISDN, one number for each channel 3 04 811 260
5 SIP endpoint, no wire of its own 5 66 003 918
6 Mobile 6 21 447 052
8 Conference room, dialled to join a meeting 8 00 214 776
9 Cooperatives, communes and public bodies 9 12 000 340

Digits 4 and 7 are unassigned and held in reserve. There are no premium ranges, because there is nothing to charge for.

Standing The wire belongs to the commune

A federation, over communal plant

Telephony is a federation of the twenty sub-regional line cooperatives and the service cooperatives that run the machines. It holds a licence the Republic grants and it is audited in its use of common resources.

The copper, the fibre, the ducts and the poles are communal property. Each commune holds the plant inside its boundary and votes on what is done to it. The federation operates over that plant on terms the communes set, and it cannot dig, cut or reroute anything without them.

Everything here is published

Patents are prohibited, so every specification the federation writes is free to read, copy and build from. The modem in a village kitchen and the exchange it dials into were both built from drawings on this site.

The core network runs on software anyone can examine. Where a component would have to be trusted without being read, the federation does without the component.
