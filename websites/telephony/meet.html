Meeting

Rooms you can join from a browser, a handset or a telephone. Small calls are carried directly between the people in them, and larger ones are mixed as close to you as possible.

How a room is createdDirect below four

Peer to peer first, mixer second

Two or three people talking is not a conference and is not treated as one. Below four participants the room hands the call to WebRTC and the ends route between themselves, exactly as an ordinary call is carried. No mixer is involved and no server sees the media.

At four and above a mixer becomes cheaper than the mesh, and the room selects one: the one at your own exchange first, then the one in your sub-region, and only then anything further away. A meeting whose participants are all in one sub-region never leaves it.

Every room offers an audio-only join, and the assemblies use it routinely so that a household on a mountain pair can take part in a meeting of four hundred.

In practiceAssemblies and minutes

Assemblies meet here

Communal and sub-regional assemblies sit in rooms on meet.telephony.rof, with the dial-in number printed on the notice. Attendance by telephone counts the same as attendance in the hall.

Rooms opened for an assembly are announced in #assembly- channels and the room number appears in the Public Register alongside the papers.

Rooms are not kept

A room exists while somebody is in it. When the last participant leaves, the room and its number are gone, and the number returns to the pool after a day.

Nothing is recorded by the service. If an assembly wants a record, somebody in the room makes one and publishes it under their own name.
