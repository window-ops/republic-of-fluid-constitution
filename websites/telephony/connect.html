Getting a line

A connection is provision. There is nothing to buy, nothing to sign and nobody to negotiate with. What varies is the wire, and the wire belongs to your commune.

How it goesAbout a fortnight, unless you want fibre

Five steps

  1. Ask, at the commune office or on 100. A new household, a new building or a line that was never pulled. There is no form worth the name: your address, and whether anything is already in the wall.

  2. The line cooperative surveys. What is in the duct, how far the cabinet is, and whether the commune has to open anything. A survey takes under a week and the result is read back to you.

  3. If digging is needed, the commune votes. The land is communal property, so opening a lane is a decision for the people who live on that land. Village assemblies often say no, and the answer is then dial-up or ISDN on the copper already there.

  4. The drills are executed if voted and the equipment handed over. A modem or a terminal, built to a published specification, is left with you. It is yours, it has no lock on it, and the drawings for it are on the hardware pages.

  5. Your identity is already waiting. Nothing is created at this point. The identity you hold as a resident works on every service the moment the line comes up.

AfterwardsThe box on the shelf is yours

The equipment is yours

Nothing is rented and nothing is returned. You may open it, reflash it, replace it with something you built, or refuse it and use your own. The federation asks only that whatever you connect meets the line specification, since a modem out of specification degrades the pair for your neighbours.

Repair workshops within reach of every commune hold parts for at least ten years, and the tool libraries keep the jigs.

Changing your mind

Moving between line types costs nothing and needs no reason. Households on dial-up ask for ISDN most often, which is a same-copper upgrade and needs no digging at all.

When it breaks

Report a fault on 101 from any line, including a neighbour's. Because the network keeps no logs, the fault desk will ask you what you saw.
