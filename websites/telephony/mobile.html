The mobile network

LTE reaches 98 per cent of people and 71 per cent of the ground. The core runs on software you can read, the SIM is provision, and nothing about the handset is registered anywhere.

The coreOpen5GS at four sites

What runs the network

The core is Open5GS

The packet core, the subscriber register and the call session control all run on Open5GS on ordinary machines at four sites. The configuration is published alongside the software, so anyone can see exactly what the network does with a handset that attaches to it.

There is no vendor core here and there never has been. A core that cannot be read cannot be audited, and an unauditable core would fail the constitutional rule about software the user cannot examine.

VoLTE is how calls go

Voice runs over the data bearer. There is no circuit fallback to maintain, because there is no legacy 2G or 3G network under it: the Republic went from analogue trunking straight to LTE in 2013.

Calls between two handsets with a clear path between them are handed to WebRTC and carried directly, which keeps them off the core entirely.

Land coverage is low and stays low. Masts need a communal vote and objections are common, so coverage follows the roads and the valleys, and leaves the map alone.

5G31 per cent of people, 4 per cent of the ground

Standalone, and slow

5G is going in slowly and on purpose. Each site is a communal decision, each one costs materials against a ceiling the commune holds. The federation's position is that LTE already handles what most people do. The assembly reviews the programme yearly and has twice voted to slow it.

The rollout is standalone from the start. There is no non-standalone stage, because anchoring 5G to an LTE carrier would have meant a second core to audit.

What you get and what is never recorded

  • Getting a SIMAt any commune officeOne per resident, more on request, no reason asked and no name required for the extra ones.
  • LockingNone, anywhereNo handset lock, no network lock, no carrier profile. A SIM from here works in anything that will take it.
  • IMEI registerDoes not existThe network does not record handset identifiers and has no table in which to keep them.
  • BandsB1 B3 B7 B20 · n78Common European allocations. Almost any imported handset works on the radio side.
  • HandsetsAnything you can examineA handset running software you cannot inspect, replace or remove is prohibited from sale, and most foreign models fail on that alone.
  • RoamingAcross the BlocYour number and your data follow you into the Bloc states at no charge. Outside the Bloc there is no agreement to roam under.
