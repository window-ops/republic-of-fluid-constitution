Published specifications

Sixteen specifications, published whole, with schematics, parts lists, test procedures and firmware source. No fee, no licence and no prior approval for connection.

Why they are openNo patents exist here

Patents are prohibited and knowledge made with public resources belongs to everyone. Every specification below is published whole: schematics, board files, parts lists, test procedures and the firmware source. There is no fee, no licence and no authorisation to request.

A workshop, a tool library, a cooperative or an individual may build any of these and connect the result to the network. The only condition is that it meets the line specification, because equipment out of specification degrades the pair for the neighbours who share the cable.

Building oneFour steps, no approval

From drawing to connected

  1. Take the drawings. Every revision is on this site with its errata. Old revisions stay published, because a workshop repairing a thirty-year-old modem needs the drawing that modem was built to.

  2. Build it, anywhere. Boards, cases and magnetics are made by cooperatives in eleven sub-regions and by tool libraries in most communes.

  3. Test it against the published procedure. Each specification has its own test: what to inject, what to measure, what the pass limits are. Tool libraries hold the test sets and lend them.

  4. Connect it. A device that passes the test may be connected, and a device that fails will be found by the line survey and reported to the builder.

AfterwardsTen years at least

Parts stay available

Every specification comes with a parts guarantee of at least ten years from its last revision, and in practice the cooperatives hold stock for the working life of the design. TS-14 parts have been available continuously since 1996.

Foreign equipment

Equipment may be connected where it meets the line specification and where the software in the modem may be examined, replaced or removed, or where it is compatible with the published protocol.
