# Real-life implications

## Why the constitution was redrafted

Nothing inside the Republic happened. The Directorate, the Deadlock, the Long Winter and the void election of 2025 are inventions, and the history exists to carry an argument in a readable form.

The reasons for the redraft sit outside the fiction. The kind of socialism the 2024 text described has failure modes that are well documented in economics, in constitutional law and in the historical record of the twentieth century, and the earlier text walked into most of them. Each disaster in the narrative stands for one of those objections.

| Event in the narrative | Real problem |
| - | - |
| The Long Winter of 2024 | Money abolished with nothing named to allocate goods, and the shortage economy that follows |
| The Safeguard of September 2024 | Parties sorted by ideology by an administrative body |
| The void election of June 2025 | Turnout quorums and the boycott dynamic they reward |
| The Deadlock, 2019 to 2023 | A constitution of conventions with no procedure for its own failure |
| The Directorate, 1954 to 1974 | Planning conducted in secret, targets set in tonnage, and no way for the governed to argue with the plan |
| The one-page judgment of 2022 | A court appointed by the chamber it is supposed to review |

### The first two texts were wish lists

The 1890 text is short and procedural, and it survives in the fiction because a century of practice grew around it. The 2024 text is my own earliest attempt at writing a constitution, a simplified, somewhat utopian one that doesn't have any realistic account of class struggle.

Seven articles declared a moneyless economy, a classless society and a full catalogue of rights. None of them named an organ to deliver any of it, and none named a court to complain to.

### Abolishing money settles nothing

This is the central defect, and the reason for most of the length of the redraft.

When the quantity of a good that people ask for exceeds the quantity available, some mechanism decides which of them receives it. A market does it through price. A planned economy has to do it by a written rule, applied by identified people, open to challenge. The 2024 text removed the first mechanism and supplied nothing in its place.

Mises argued in 1920 and Hayek in 1945 that a price carries dispersed information no planning office can reassemble, much of it held by people who could never write it down. Lange and Lerner replied that a planner could iterate toward the right answer, and Hayek's counter was that the world changes faster than the iteration. Kornai's Economics of Shortage in 1980 made the empirical case that shortage in such systems is systemic, produced by enterprises whose budgets are soft because everyone knows they will be rescued.

Across the Eastern Bloc, queues, rationing and a second economy did the allocating that the plan failed to do, and access ran through acquaintance, position and favour. Romania in the 1980s rationed bread, sugar, cooking oil, meat and household energy.

The redraft answers the objection with a procedure. Residents register what they need and the planners are bound by the register. Allocation runs on published rules turning on urgency, length of wait and lots. Waiting lists are public and each person knows their place. Small discretionary goods run on a personal, equal, expiring token that cannot be saved, lent, pledged or transferred, so nothing accumulates. Resource-heavy goods sit in common holding and are booked.

### Cooperative ownership on its own leaves gaps

In Yugoslavia from the 1950s, investment behaviour, employment levels, and enterprise budgets softened whenever a firm was too important to close.

The 2024 text went further than any of that and prohibited every holder except a cooperative. Grids, hospitals, universities, water systems and railways were left with no lawful owner at all. The redraft settles the question by naming four social forms and closing the list, so each asset has a holder and a mode of governance appropriate to its scale.

### Rights without remedies are decoration

The Soviet constitution of 1936 had a generous catalogue of rights with no independent court, no standing and no remedy. Rights that nobody can invoke are a statement of intent.

The 2024 text repeated the structure in good faith, listing sixteen rights drawn from the Universal Declaration with no way to enforce any of them. The redraft makes rights invocable directly, gives standing to anyone, provides free and fast remedies, fixes a minimum core enforceable immediately, and creates an independent office to take complaints and publish findings.

### Banning ideologies is the wrong defence

The idea that a democracy may defend itself against parties hostile to it goes back to Loewenstein in 1937 and has a serious modern implementation in Germany, where the constitutional court may ban a party on proved conduct, a power used twice in seventy years and deliberately difficult to invoke.

The 2024 text handed an electoral commission a list of policy combinations and told it to sort parties by label. Whatever the intent, a screening power of that kind ends up aimed at whoever the government of the day finds inconvenient. In a socialist state the targets are the right and the moderate left.

The redraft defines prohibited acts, requires a court to find them on evidence, and forbids classification by opinion in terms.

### Turnout quorums reward boycotts

A validity threshold gives opponents a reason to stay at home, so abstention becomes a campaign tactic and the threshold decides the outcome.

The 2024 text applied the same device to parliamentary elections, which converts a quiet Sunday into a constitutional vacuum. Deciding by a majority of votes cast, with no threshold at all, removes the incentive and the vacuum.

### Nothing was secure

Fifty-five per cent of a single chamber could rewrite the 2024 text end to end, including the guarantees it declared most loudly. Guarantees of that kind last exactly as long as the next majority tolerates them.

The German Basic Law places human dignity, the federal order and the democratic form beyond amendment. The Indian Supreme Court developed the basic structure doctrine in 1973, holding that an amendment cannot destroy the constitution's essential features. The redraft borrows the technique: a referendum for every amendment, and parts that no procedure and no majority can reach.

### Emergency powers

Automatic extension of parliamentary terms during an emergency is one a reliable route to indefinite rule, and the history of the twentieth century has a lot of examples that will not be listed here. The 2024 text contained exactly that clause.

The redraft caps everything: a declaration lapses within a week without a reinforced vote and a judicial finding, renewals occur every thirty days, and six months is the outer limit in any two years, and elections, courts, provision and oversight are placed outside the reach of emergency powers entirely.

### What the third text is for

The redraft works through the realistic choices involved in building a socialist society, and publishes the cost of each one. Variety is traded for durability and some goods will queue. The calculation problem has an answer at one scale and no proof of an answer at any larger one. A residual token exists because pure moneylessness collapses on contact with scarcity.

The text concedes its own length as a cost, and requires the Republic to put the whole design before a body drawn by lot every ten years and answer for it.

## Legislation outside the economy

Outside the economic order, the Republic legislates much as the European Union does. Product safety, construction and accessibility standards, sanitary and food standards, electrical and network interconnection, measurement and calibration, and the rules of the public way are fixed centrally under Article 178 and hold identically throughout the territory.

Standards are published in full, free of charge, in open form, and are implementable and verifiable by any person. A territory may adopt a stricter standard for ecology, safety or accessibility, provided it obstructs no lawful movement from another territory.

Someone arriving from a member state would find the regulatory texture familiar: conformity requirements, standard technical specifications, data protection, reasons stated for administrative decisions, and a right of appeal.

## Why some would classify the Republic as an autarky

The classification is defensible, and the register addresses it directly in `economy/objections.html` and `questions/index.html`. Three features produce it.

**Incompatibility:** The economy has no counterpart among the economies it trades with. Exchange requires translation layers: a single external account under Article 117(1), settlement in goods, labour, technique and knowledge in physical units, and a unit of settlement used only where a counterparty insists, which circulates nowhere and is held by no resident under Article 295.

**Regulation of things normal in a market economy:** Arrangements that are ordinary elsewhere are prohibited here as undemocratic: profit enterprises, tradable claims, interest, insurance for gain, brokerage, letting for gain, and the appropriation of another person's product, under Articles 88(3) and 265(1)(b). Foreign firms operate on a revocable public licence under Article 21, with stricter duties than domestic cooperatives, mandatory transfer of technique to the knowledge commons, no rights under Title III, and no submission to arbitral jurisdiction. Eleven such licences exist, covering shipping, cargo certification, insurance and brokerage.

**Heavy regulation of trade in both directions:** Imports are counted against the ecological budgets and refused where they exceed them under Article 117(2). Costly imports require a project licence granted on a published test, and the Ecological Budget Authority may refuse one outright under Article 299(3). Article 298 requires needs to be met at home first and unused domestic capacity to be developed before importing. Exports proceed only where essential and general provision remain unimpaired.
