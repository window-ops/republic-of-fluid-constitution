Concepts explained

16 articles on the ideas the Constitution relies on, grouped by subject, with a plain glossary of 32 shorter terms and a table of the words people actually use.

These pages explain. They do not govern. Where an explanation differs from the Constitution, the Constitution governs, and the Office of Constitutional Revision should be told.

Property and the economy

How things are owned, made and shared out.

UsufructThe right to use and manage something you do not own, for as long as you are actually using it. The residual unit of accountA personal, equal, expiring token that allocates small luxuries in an economy with no money. How the plan is madeA five-year cycle in which councils state, a secretariat computes, and each level negotiates what the level below could not settle. Contribution without compulsionEveryone owes their capacity to the common life, everyone is presumed to give it, and nothing whatever depends on proving it. What happens when an enterprise failsSupport is allowed, but it is public, capped, dated, and ends in reorganisation if it has not worked.

Ecology and limits

The budgets, the rights of nature, and the cap on what the Republic may take.

Ecological budgetA set of published ceilings on what the Republic may take from the world in a given period, and floors on what it must put back. Rights of nature and universal standingEcosystems hold rights of their own, and any person at all may go to court on their behalf. Steady state and throughputThe Republic holds its total use of energy and materials constant, and spends every efficiency gain on sufficiency.

Government and participation

Who decides, how they are chosen, and how you take part.

Drawing by lotFilling an office by random selection from a defined pool, used here for courts, watchdogs and deliberation. General competence and subsidiarityCommunes may act on anything not expressly taken from them, and doubt is resolved in their favour. Participatory democracyThe name the Republic gives its own form, meaning that participation is not reducible to voting every five years.

Law, courts and procedure

The machinery that makes the rest of it enforceable.

Organic lawA reinforced class of statute that fills in the Constitution's detail, and cannot be amended by an ordinary majority. Declaration of incompatibilityWhat happens when the Constitutional Court finds that a law of the Assembly breaches the Constitution. The unamendable coreEleven things that no majority, no referendum and no constitutional convention may touch.

Rights and their enforcement

What you are owed, and what you can do when you do not get it.

Minimum coreThe level of provision below which a life of dignity becomes impossible, enforceable in court today, with nothing deferred to a later date.

The world outside

Treaties, unions, trade and the rules the Republic applies to other states.

Life-limiting regimeA legal finding about what a state does, used to govern trade and to open citizenship to people fleeing it.
